## Core Content

This curriculum's ORANGE track established that a sales commission structure shapes behavior according to its actual mechanics, regardless of leadership's stated intentions. Here's the "why" this same principle applies to leadership and organizational culture broadly, not just sales compensation specifically: employees generally learn what a leader actually values by observing what gets rewarded, promoted, and tolerated — not by listening to what a leader says matters in a company meeting or mission statement.

This is why a genuine gap can open between an organization's announced culture and its actual, lived culture: a leader who gives inspiring speeches about collaboration while promoting and rewarding only individual, competitive achievement is training the organization, through its actual incentive structure, to value exactly what gets measured and rewarded — regardless of how sincerely the collaborative message was delivered.

What does your organization's actual promotion and reward pattern say about what's genuinely valued, and does that match what your stated values and speeches claim to prioritize?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader regularly emphasizes teamwork and collaboration in company meetings, while the organization's promotion and bonus structure exclusively rewards individual performance metrics with no consideration of collaborative contribution. [ASSUMPTION] Employees, rationally responding to the actual incentive structure rather than the stated message, prioritize individual achievement over genuine collaboration, echoing exactly the ORANGE track's principle that people optimize for what's actually rewarded — the culture that emerges reflects the incentive design, not the speeches, regardless of how sincerely those speeches were delivered.
