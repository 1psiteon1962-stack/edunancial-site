By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Distinguish organizational succession planning from the personal estate planning covered in this curriculum's PURPLE track

- Explain why this is a distinct, ongoing leadership responsibility, not a one-time document

## Core Content

This curriculum's PURPLE and BLUE tracks covered personal estate planning and business succession planning for an owner's eventual exit. Here's the "why" this lesson addresses a related but distinct concept: organizational leadership succession — identifying and developing who could step into key leadership roles if a current leader leaves, is promoted, or becomes unexpectedly unavailable — is an ongoing leadership discipline, not a one-time legal document to be filed and forgotten.

Here's the "why" this matters even for a leader with no near-term plans to leave, echoing the estate planning lesson's point that unexpected events don't wait for convenient timing: a leader who hasn't actively developed potential successors leaves the organization vulnerable to exactly the kind of disruption a documented estate plan is meant to prevent at the personal level — except here, the vulnerability sits at the organizational level, affecting far more than one family's affairs.

Who in your organization could step into a key leadership role tomorrow if needed, and what specifically have you done, beyond simply hoping they'd figure it out, to actually prepare them for that possibility?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader in good health with no near-term plans to leave considers succession planning unnecessary for now, focusing entirely on current operational priorities. [ASSUMPTION] If this leader unexpectedly becomes unavailable — through illness, an unexpected departure, or a sudden opportunity elsewhere — the organization discovers no one has been actively developed or prepared to step into the leadership role, creating exactly the kind of disruption and uncertainty the personal estate planning lessons elsewhere in this curriculum warned against at the family level, now playing out across an entire organization and its employees.

## Key Takeaways
- Organizational leadership succession planning is distinct from personal estate planning, though both share the same underlying "unexpected events don't wait" principle.
- This is an ongoing leadership discipline requiring active development of potential successors, not a one-time document.
- Leadership succession matters even for leaders with no near-term plans to leave, given the genuine risk of unexpected unavailability.
