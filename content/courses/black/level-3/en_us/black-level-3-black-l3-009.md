## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why a leadership style well-suited to a small team can become a bottleneck as an organization scales
- Connect this concept to the systems-enable-scale principle from Lesson 7
- Explain why adapting leadership style requires deliberate effort, not just good intentions

## Core Content

Lesson 7 established that systems, not a single person's presence, are what enable a business to scale. Here's the specific "why" a leader's own management style needs to evolve alongside that scaling: a hands-on, deeply involved leadership approach — personally reviewing every decision, staying closely connected to every team member — genuinely works well, and may even be necessary, for a small team, where a leader can realistically maintain that level of direct involvement.

Here's the "why" this same style becomes a bottleneck as the organization grows: the number of decisions and relationships a leader can personally, directly manage doesn't scale proportionally with headcount — a leader who continues insisting on personal involvement in decisions that could now be delegated (echoing Lesson 2) becomes the limiting factor on the organization's pace and growth, exactly the presence-dependency ceiling Lesson 7 described, except now created by the leader's own unwillingness to adapt rather than by a lack of available systems.

What leadership habits served you well when your team was smaller that might now be creating exactly the bottleneck this lesson describes? What would you need to let go of, and what system or trusted person would need to take its place?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader who built a small team by personally reviewing every decision and staying closely involved in every project continues this same approach as the organization grows significantly larger. [ASSUMPTION] What once ensured quality and cohesion now creates a bottleneck — decisions queue up waiting for this leader's personal attention, team members feel disempowered to act without explicit approval, and the organization's pace slows to match what one person can personally review, rather than what the now-larger team could actually accomplish if empowered and trusted with appropriate systems in place.

- A hands-on leadership style well-suited to a small team can become a genuine bottleneck as the organization grows.

- Adapting leadership style requires deliberate effort to replace personal involvement with systems and empowered team members.
