## Core Content

This curriculum's GOLD track established that overconfidence bias doesn't diminish with experience or knowledge, and can even become more pronounced. Here's the "why" this bias carries outsized consequences specifically for a leader, beyond its effect on their own individual decisions: a leader's confidence and stated conclusions influence an entire team or organization's decisions, not just their own — team members often defer to a confident, experienced leader's judgment rather than independently and rigorously challenging it, meaning a leader's individual overconfidence can propagate through an organization's decisions without the natural check that a diverse set of independent viewpoints might otherwise provide.

This is why the investment committee structure discussed in the GOLD track — requiring multiple perspectives before a significant decision — matters with particular force for confident, experienced leaders specifically: the more a leader's judgment is trusted and deferred to by default, the more valuable it becomes to have a structural mechanism actively countering that same deference, rather than assuming the leader's confidence itself is evidence of being right.

Does your organization have anyone genuinely empowered to challenge your judgment on a significant decision, or does deference to your position and confidence quietly discourage that kind of pushback?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A confident, experienced leader makes a significant strategic decision, expressing strong conviction in a team meeting. [ASSUMPTION] Team members, deferring to the leader's confidence and position, don't voice their genuine reservations or independently challenge the underlying assumptions — echoing the GOLD track's overconfidence discussion, the leader's individual bias propagates through the team's decision-making without the natural check a genuinely empowered, diverse set of perspectives might have provided, illustrating why building structural mechanisms for real pushback matters more, not less, as a leader's confidence and authority grow.

## Key Takeaways
- A leader's overconfidence, unlike an individual investor's, propagates through an entire organization's decisions via deference to their authority.
- Team deference to a confident leader's judgment can amplify rather than check overconfidence bias.
- Structural mechanisms for genuine pushback, echoing the GOLD track's investment committee principle, matter more as a leader's confidence and authority grow, not less.
