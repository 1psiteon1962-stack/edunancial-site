## Core Content

This curriculum's ORANGE track walked through a hypothetical brand crisis, showing how it simultaneously tests legal, financial, and leadership systems. Here's the specific "why" leadership itself needs to operate differently during that kind of event, compared to steady-state operations: normal, steady-state leadership generally benefits from patience, extensive consultation, and building broad consensus before major decisions. A genuine crisis generally requires faster decision-making with incomplete information, clear and decisive communication even amid uncertainty, and tolerance for making a reasonable decision quickly rather than the theoretically optimal decision slowly.

Here's the "why" a leader who doesn't recognize this shift can make a crisis worse: applying steady-state patience and extensive consultation habits during a fast-moving crisis can create the appearance of paralysis or indecision at exactly the moment stakeholders — employees, customers, media — most need clear, confident leadership, even if that leadership is operating with less certainty than usual.

Have you thought through, in advance, how your own natural leadership style would need to shift during a genuine crisis? What specifically would you need to do differently than your normal, steady-state approach?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader accustomed to extensive consultation and consensus-building before any significant decision faces a fast-moving crisis requiring an immediate public response. [ASSUMPTION] Applying the same extensive consultation process used for normal strategic decisions, this leader delays a public response for days while gathering input from every relevant stakeholder — a pace appropriate for steady-state leadership but that, during an actual crisis, creates a vacuum other parties fill with their own narrative, illustrating why crisis leadership specifically requires faster, more decisive action even with less certainty than the leader's normal decision-making process would prefer.
