## Core Content

This curriculum has repeatedly emphasized that seeking expert help reflects competence, not weakness — the "business is a team sport" principle covered in the BLUE track. Here's a specific application of that principle to negotiation: a leader negotiating entirely alone, without a lawyer reviewing terms, an advisor providing an outside perspective, or a team member serving as a sounding board, often doesn't realize how much this isolation actually weakens their position, beyond just the practical risk of missing a poorly drafted contract term (echoing the PURPLE track's contract discussion).

Here's the "why" this weakens leverage specifically: a counterparty negotiating against a leader who has visibly consulted advisors or has a team backing a position generally has to account for the possibility that any concession or hard line reflects careful, collective judgment, not simply one person's individual, potentially reversible preference — solo negotiation removes this natural check on the counterparty's own aggressiveness, since there's no visible collective judgment for them to have to reckon with.

Before your next significant negotiation, who else should be involved — a legal advisor, a trusted colleague, an outside perspective — and what does negotiating entirely alone actually cost you, beyond the risk of missing something in the contract itself?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader negotiates a significant deal entirely alone, without consulting legal counsel or bringing in a trusted advisor, believing this demonstrates confidence and independence. [ASSUMPTION] The counterparty, recognizing they're negotiating against a single individual's judgment rather than a considered, collective position, pushes harder on specific terms than they might have against a team visibly backed by legal review and outside perspective — the leader's solo approach, intended to project strength, actually weakened their negotiating leverage in ways they may not have recognized, echoing the "business is a team sport" principle covered elsewhere in this curriculum.
