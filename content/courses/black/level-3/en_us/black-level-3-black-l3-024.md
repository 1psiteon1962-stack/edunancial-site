- Explain why leadership development spending should be evaluated as an investment, not a discretionary expense

## Core Content

This curriculum's ORANGE track established that marketing spend should be evaluated as a capital allocation decision, not a fixed expense to simply minimize. Here's the "why" leadership and management development spending deserves the exact same reframing: investing in developing leadership and management capability throughout an organization — training, coaching, deliberate skill-building — competes for the same limited resources as any other investment, and should be evaluated by its likely return relative to realistic alternatives, not treated as a nice-to-have easily cut when budgets tighten.

Here's the "why" this spending is often the first cut during difficult periods, and why that reasoning deserves scrutiny: leadership development's return is genuinely harder to measure directly than a marketing campaign's, echoing the "invisible value" theme covered elsewhere in this curriculum's PURPLE track discussion of proactive legal work — the disputes avoided, the better decisions made, and the retention improved because of stronger management are real but hard to point to directly, making this investment an easy target for cuts precisely because its value is less visible, not because its value is actually lower.

Before cutting leadership development spending during a tight budget period, what specific evidence do you actually have about its return, versus an assumption that it's simply a discretionary nice-to-have easier to cut than other line items?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] During a budget tightening period, a business cuts its leadership development program first, reasoning that its impact is harder to point to directly compared to other spending. [ASSUMPTION] Months later, management turnover increases and decision quality visibly declines in ways that, while difficult to attribute cleanly to the specific cut, echo exactly the kind of invisible value the PURPLE track described for proactive legal planning — the leadership development spending was providing real, if hard-to-measure, value that the easy assumption of "this is discretionary" failed to properly weigh against genuine alternatives.
