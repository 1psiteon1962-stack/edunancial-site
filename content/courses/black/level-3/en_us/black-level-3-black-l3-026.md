## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why leadership habits built for a shared physical space don't automatically transfer to remote teams
- Connect this concept to the systems-enable-scale principle covered in Lesson 7
- Explain why remote leadership requires more deliberate, explicit systems, not less structure

## Core Content

Lesson 7 established that systems, not a single person's presence, are what enable an organization to scale. Here's a related "why," specific to remote and distributed teams: many leadership habits built around informal, in-person presence — noticing a struggling team member's body language, having a quick hallway conversation to resolve a small issue, sensing team morale simply by being physically present — don't transfer to a remote environment, where those informal, presence-based signals simply aren't available.

Here's the "why" this requires more deliberate systems, not less structure, contrary to a common assumption that remote work means looser management: without the informal, ambient signals available in a shared physical space, a remote leader needs explicit systems — structured check-ins, clear documentation, deliberate communication norms — to accomplish what previously happened somewhat automatically through physical proximity, echoing Lesson 7's broader point that systems transfer capability out of what was previously informal and person-dependent.

What informal, presence-based habits has your leadership approach historically relied on that simply don't exist in your organization's remote or hybrid working arrangements? What explicit system would need to replace each one?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader accustomed to sensing team morale and catching small problems through informal, in-person observation transitions to leading a fully remote team, continuing to rely on the same informal approach. [ASSUMPTION] Without deliberate systems to replace what physical presence previously provided — structured regular check-ins, explicit documentation, clear communication norms — this leader misses signals they would have previously caught automatically, illustrating why remote leadership requires more deliberate structure, not simply a looser version of in-person management, echoing Lesson 7's systems principle applied specifically to distributed teams.
