## Core Content

This curriculum's ORANGE track distinguished leading indicators (signaling a likely future change) from lagging indicators (reflecting a result that already happened), using customer churn as the example. Here's the "why" employee turnover functions the same way, specifically as a signal of leadership quality: rising turnover, particularly among strong performers, often reflects an underlying leadership or management problem — poor communication, lack of psychological safety (Lesson 11), unaddressed conflict (Lesson 12), or the kind of tolerated bad behavior described in Lesson 22 — well before that same problem shows up in visible performance metrics like productivity or revenue.

This is why exit interviews, and turnover data more broadly, deserve the same leading-indicator attention this curriculum has emphasized for customer churn — but only if the findings are actually acted upon. An organization that collects exit interview data without ever addressing the patterns it reveals is treating the leading indicator exactly like a lagging one, waiting for the underlying problem to show up in more visible, harder-to-ignore metrics before responding, defeating the entire purpose of having caught the signal early.

What does your organization's turnover data, and specifically your exit interview findings, actually say about leadership and management quality — and has anything concrete changed as a result of what's been learned?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An organization notices rising turnover among strong performers over several months but doesn't investigate the underlying cause, waiting instead to see if overall productivity metrics decline before taking the trend seriously. [ASSUMPTION] By the time productivity does visibly decline — the lagging indicator finally catching up — the underlying leadership problem driving the turnover has had months to worsen and affect a wider group of employees, exactly the delayed-response pattern the ORANGE track warned against for customer churn, applied here to employee retention instead.
