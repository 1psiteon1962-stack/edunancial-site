## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain what identity fusion with a business generally means

- Connect this concept to the ego and sunk cost discussion from Lesson 17

## Core Content

Lesson 17 covered how a leader's ego can distort decision-making, connecting to the sunk cost fallacy. Here's a related, deeper pattern specific to founders: when a person's personal identity becomes fused with the business they built — "I am this company" rather than "I lead this company" — criticism of the business, or evidence the business needs significant change, can feel like a threat to personal identity itself, not simply feedback about a strategy or operation.

Here's the "why" this fusion creates specific, recognizable blind spots: a founder in this position may struggle to make genuinely objective decisions about the business's direction, since doing so requires the ability to view the business as separate from themselves — exactly the objectivity Lesson 17's ego discussion described as difficult, but here rooted in something deeper than simple pride in a specific decision, extending to the founder's fundamental sense of self.

Do you experience criticism of your business as feedback about a strategy, or does it feel like a more personal, identity-level threat? What would it take to create more separation between your sense of self and the business's ongoing performance?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A founder who has built their business over many years experiences any significant criticism of the company's direction as deeply personal, responding defensively even to well-intentioned, constructive feedback from trusted advisors. [ASSUMPTION] This identity fusion, extending beyond the specific ego-driven resistance to admitting a mistake described in Lesson 17, makes it genuinely difficult for this founder to evaluate the business's actual needs objectively — every piece of feedback about the company feels like feedback about themselves, creating a blind spot that a founder with more separation between personal identity and business performance would be less prone to.
