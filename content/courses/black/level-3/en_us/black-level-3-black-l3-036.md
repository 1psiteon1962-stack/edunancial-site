- Connect this concept to the board governance discussion from Lesson 18

## Core Content

Leadership is often discussed primarily in terms of managing "down" — leading direct reports and teams. Here's the "why" this is genuinely incomplete: a leader also needs to manage "up" (their own supervisors, a board, or investors, connecting directly to Lesson 18's board governance discussion) and "sideways" (peer leaders and departments the leader doesn't formally control but depends on for coordinated organizational success).

Here's the "why" neglecting any one of these three directions creates specific, recognizable risk: a leader who manages down well but neglects managing up may find themselves without the resources, support, or trust needed from oversight structures like the board discussed in Lesson 18, regardless of how well their own team performs. A leader who neglects managing sideways may find their team's work undermined by poor coordination with peer departments, since organizational success rarely depends on any single team's isolated performance alone.

Which of these three directions — up, down, or sideways — receives the least deliberate attention in your current leadership approach, and what specific risk has that created or is it likely to create?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader invests heavily in managing their direct team well, building strong morale and clear direction, while neglecting to actively manage relationships with peer department leaders. [ASSUMPTION] When a significant cross-departmental initiative requires coordination this leader hasn't cultivated, the team's excellent individual performance is undermined by poor sideways coordination — illustrating why managing down alone, however well executed, is insufficient without also deliberately managing sideways relationships that affect organizational outcomes beyond any single team's control.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does it mean to manage "up, down, and sideways" simultaneously?
2. What specific risk does neglecting to manage "up" create, connecting to Lesson 18?
3. What specific risk does neglecting to manage "sideways" create?

## Answer Key

1. Managing relationships with supervisors or oversight structures (up), direct reports and teams (down), and peer leaders or departments (sideways) all at once.
2. A leader may find themselves without the resources, support, or trust needed from oversight structures like the board, regardless of how well their own team performs, directly connecting to the board governance discussion in Lesson 18.
3. A leader's team's work can be undermined by poor coordination with peer departments, since organizational success rarely depends on any single team's isolated performance alone.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition for the lesson.
2. This explicitly connects the lesson back to Lesson 18's board governance discussion.
3. This reinforces the specific, practical risk of neglecting lateral coordination.

## Key Takeaways
- Effective leadership requires managing relationships in three directions simultaneously: up, down, and sideways.
- Neglecting to manage up can undermine the resources and trust needed from oversight structures, connecting to Lesson 18's board governance discussion.
- Neglecting to manage sideways can undermine even a well-led team's work through poor cross-departmental coordination.
