## Core Content

Lesson 23 established that negotiating with the support of advisors and outside perspective produces better outcomes than negotiating entirely alone. Here's a broader "why" this same principle extends to a leader's ongoing professional network generally: a genuine network — peers, mentors, former colleagues, industry contacts — provides exactly the kind of independent perspective and outside pushback this track has repeatedly identified as valuable (Lessons 10, 11, 18, 19), available on an ongoing basis, not just during a formal negotiation.

Here's the "why" a network built only reactively, once a crisis hits, is far less valuable than one built proactively over time: genuine trust and useful advice depend on an existing relationship, not one hastily formed under pressure — a leader reaching out to a near-stranger for advice during a crisis gets a much thinner, less candid response than one reaching out to a long-cultivated relationship built well before any specific need arose.

How much of your professional network has been built proactively, during calm periods, versus reactively, only when you needed something specific? What would change if you invested more deliberately in these relationships before you needed them?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader facing a difficult, unfamiliar strategic decision reaches out to a peer they've maintained a genuine, ongoing relationship with for years, receiving candid, well-informed advice grounded in real trust and context. [ASSUMPTION] A different leader facing a similar decision, having never invested in building genuine professional relationships during calmer periods, finds themselves without anyone to turn to for this kind of candid outside perspective exactly when it would be most valuable — illustrating why a network is a risk-mitigation asset that, like other assets covered throughout this curriculum, requires proactive investment well before the moment it's actually needed.
