## Core Content

Lesson 2 established that key person risk includes the danger of a leader's sudden unavailability disrupting organizational operations. Here's the "why" burnout deserves inclusion in this same risk category, not treated as a separate, purely personal matter: a leader operating in a state of chronic burnout is at meaningfully elevated risk of the kind of sudden unavailability — through health crisis, an abrupt departure, or a significant decline in decision quality — that this track has repeatedly identified as a genuine organizational vulnerability.

Here's the "why" addressing this is a leadership responsibility, not simply an individual self-care choice: an organization dependent on a burned-out leader is exposed to real, elevated risk, echoing exactly the presence-dependency concern from Lesson 7 and the key person risk from Lesson 2 — meaning sustainable pacing, appropriate delegation, and genuine attention to a leader's own capacity aren't indulgent luxuries, they're a form of organizational risk management with the same underlying logic as the other risk-reduction practices covered throughout this track.

Honestly, how sustainable is your current pace as a leader? If the answer is "not very," what specific organizational risk does that create, beyond the personal cost to you?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader pushes through an unsustainable pace for an extended period, treating rest and delegation as indulgences to be addressed only once the current pressing demands ease. [ASSUMPTION] This chronic burnout eventually manifests as a health crisis requiring an unexpected, extended absence — precisely the kind of sudden unavailability Lesson 2's key person risk discussion warned against, illustrating why a leader's own sustainable pacing is a genuine organizational continuity concern, not simply a personal wellbeing matter separate from the business risks covered throughout this track.
