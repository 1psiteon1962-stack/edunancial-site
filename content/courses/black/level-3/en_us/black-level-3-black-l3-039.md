## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Synthesize the ego, identity fusion, and falsifiability themes covered earlier in this track
- Apply this technique to a specific decision currently facing scrutiny

## Core Content

This track has covered a leader's ego (Lesson 17) and identity fusion with a business (Lesson 31) as sources of decision distortion, and referenced the GOLD track's falsifiability principle as a counter to unfalsifiable, ego-protecting reasoning. Here's a practical, applicable technique synthesizing these themes: when evaluating whether to continue or reverse a decision, ask "if someone else had made this exact decision, and I were evaluating it purely on its current evidence and merits, what would I recommend?" — deliberately separating the evaluation from personal attachment to having been the one who made the original call.

This technique works by directly interrupting the ego and identity-fusion patterns covered earlier in this track: it's genuinely harder to defend a failing decision purely to protect your own sense of having been right when you're explicitly asked to evaluate it as if someone else made it — the technique doesn't eliminate ego, but it creates enough distance to make the sunk cost and identity-protection patterns (Lessons 17 and 31) more visible and easier to resist.

Pick a decision you're currently defending or continuing to support. Apply this technique directly: if someone else had made this exact call, and you were evaluating it fresh today, what would you actually recommend?
