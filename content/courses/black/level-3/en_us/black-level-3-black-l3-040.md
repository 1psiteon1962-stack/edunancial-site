## Core Content

[HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO — ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED FACT] Imagine a business facing genuine financial pressure needs to make significant workforce reductions to remain viable. This is a hypothetical stress-test scenario, not a prediction about any specific real company or event.

Before reading further, think through this using everything covered across this curriculum, not just this track: What legal requirements (PURPLE track) govern notice periods, severance, and potential discrimination claims in how the reductions are selected? What does the business's cash flow and runway situation (BLUE track) actually require, versus what feels emotionally easier? How does the timing and manner of communication reflect the employer responsibility theme (Lesson 14) and avoid the conflict-avoidance trap (Lesson 12)? What does this decision, and how it's handled, signal to remaining employees about what leadership actually values (Lessons 3, 22)? And what does this test about the leader's own ego, identity fusion, and capacity to act decisively under genuine uncertainty (Lessons 17, 30, 31, 39)?

Map out your own answer before checking the reasoning below — this scenario is deliberately designed to require pulling from nearly everything this track, and this curriculum, has covered.

## Worked Example

[HYPOTHETICAL — ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT A PREDICTION] A leader facing this hypothetical situation works with legal counsel to ensure the selection criteria and process comply with applicable employment law (PURPLE track), confirms with finance exactly what reduction is genuinely necessary given the actual cash position rather than an emotionally-driven overcorrection (BLUE track), communicates directly and honestly with affected employees and the remaining team rather than avoiding the difficult conversation (Lesson 12) or leaving damaging silence (Lesson 35), and reflects honestly on whether their own ego or identity fusion with the business (Lessons 17, 31) is distorting the decision about scope or timing. Every one of these considerations operates simultaneously, not sequentially — exactly the kind of integrated judgment this entire curriculum has been building toward.
