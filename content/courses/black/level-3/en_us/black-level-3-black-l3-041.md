## Core Content

This curriculum references mentorship as a recurring, valued theme — including a specific mentorship relationship highlighted as a core curriculum anchor. Here's the "why" mentorship deserves this emphasis specifically within leadership development: unlike most individual accomplishments, which are typically bounded by a leader's own tenure, the people a leader has genuinely mentored carry forward what they learned well beyond that leader's own time in any specific role — mentorship's value compounds across time and across people in a way most other leadership contributions don't.

This connects directly to Lesson 24's argument that leadership development deserves investment-grade evaluation: mentorship is one of the highest-return forms of that investment specifically because its return isn't capped by the mentor's own remaining tenure — a well-mentored successor or team member continues generating value, and often mentors others in turn, long after the original mentoring relationship has ended, creating a compounding effect closer to the reinvestment principle covered in this curriculum's GOLD track than to a typical, bounded return.

Who has genuinely benefited from your deliberate mentorship, and have you thought about that investment the way this lesson frames it — as a compounding return that extends well beyond your own tenure, rather than simply a nice thing to do when time allows?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader invests significant time deliberately mentoring several team members over the years, teaching not just specific skills but the underlying judgment and reasoning this leader has developed. [ASSUMPTION] Years after this leader has moved on to a different role or organization, several of those mentored individuals are now mentoring others in turn, carrying forward and multiplying the original investment — a compounding return that continued generating value long after the original leader's own direct involvement ended, illustrating exactly why mentorship deserves the investment-grade evaluation Lesson 24 recommends for leadership development generally.
