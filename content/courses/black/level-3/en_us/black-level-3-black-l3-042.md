## Core Content

This curriculum's PURPLE track established that a written partnership agreement matters even between trusted partners, since circumstances change and clear terms prevent disputes. Here's the "why" this principle carries even more weight in a family business specifically: the personal and professional relationships are already deeply overlapping — a disagreement about business strategy can't be as easily separated from a family relationship the way it might be between unrelated business partners, since the two relationships are permanently intertwined regardless of how the business itself performs.

Here's the "why" this overlap makes deliberate structure — clear roles, documented decision-making authority, formal succession plans (echoing Lesson 8), and even the buy-sell agreement concept from the PURPLE track — more important, not less, contrary to a common assumption that family trust makes formal structure unnecessary: precisely because personal and professional stakes are so intertwined, the absence of clear structure risks damaging both the business and the family relationships simultaneously when a disagreement does eventually arise, since there's no "we can just stop working together" option that separates the two relationships cleanly.

If your business involves family members, what formal structures — clear roles, documented decision authority, a succession or buy-sell plan — are actually in place, versus relying on the assumption that family trust makes this kind of formality unnecessary?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A family business operates for years without formal documentation of roles, decision-making authority, or a succession plan, relying on the assumption that family trust makes this kind of structure unnecessary. [ASSUMPTION] When a significant disagreement about business direction eventually arises between family members, the absence of any predetermined structure or agreement — echoing exactly the risk the PURPLE track's partnership agreement discussion warned against — means the conflict now threatens both the business and the family relationships simultaneously, with no clear process to resolve it that either party had previously agreed to.
