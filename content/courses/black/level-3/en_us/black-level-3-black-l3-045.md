- Explain why neither excessive risk aversion nor excessive risk tolerance at the top serves an organization well

## Core Content

This track has established that leaders shape culture through incentives (Lesson 3) and through what they tolerate (Lesson 22). Here's a related "why," specific to risk itself: a leader's own personal relationship with risk — whether they're generally cautious or generally bold — tends to propagate through an organization's decisions at every level, since employees generally calibrate their own risk-taking, consciously or not, based on cues about what leadership seems to reward or punish.

Here's the "why" neither extreme serves an organization well, connecting to the risk-return relationship covered throughout this curriculum's GOLD track: a leader who is excessively risk-averse can create an organization that misses genuinely good opportunities out of reflexive caution, while a leader who is excessively risk-tolerant can create an organization that takes on risk without the discipline this curriculum has emphasized — risk-adjusted judgment, not risk avoidance or risk-seeking as a blanket disposition, is the actual goal a leader's own example should model.

Does your organization's actual risk-taking pattern reflect thoughtful, risk-adjusted judgment, or does it more closely mirror your own personal comfort or discomfort with risk, regardless of what any specific decision's actual risk-return profile would recommend?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader who is personally very risk-averse consistently rejects proposals involving any meaningful uncertainty, regardless of their actual risk-adjusted merit. [ASSUMPTION] Over time, employees learn that proposals framed as low-risk get approved and anything perceived as risky gets rejected, regardless of the underlying risk-adjusted return — the organization's actual risk-taking pattern comes to mirror this leader's personal disposition rather than reflecting the thoughtful, case-by-case risk-adjusted judgment this curriculum's GOLD track has emphasized throughout, causing the organization to systematically miss opportunities with genuinely favorable risk-adjusted profiles.
