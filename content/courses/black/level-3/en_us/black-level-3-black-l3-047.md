## Core Content

This track has repeatedly returned to the value of a falsifiable thesis (echoing the GOLD track), genuine team disagreement (Lesson 19), and psychological safety (Lesson 11). Here's the practical, synthesizing "why" behind actively seeking disconfirming evidence specifically, rather than simply remaining open to it if it happens to arise: confirmation bias means people naturally notice and remember evidence supporting their existing beliefs more readily than evidence contradicting them, so passively "remaining open" to disconfirming evidence generally isn't enough — a leader needs to actively look for it, asking specifically "what evidence would tell me this strategy is wrong, and have I actually gone looking for it?"

This is why the most effective version of this practice combines every earlier theme in this track: a leader who has built psychological safety (Lesson 11) and a team willing to disagree (Lesson 19) has created the conditions where disconfirming evidence is more likely to actually reach them, but still needs to personally, actively seek it out — the structural conditions enable this practice, but don't replace the leader's own deliberate effort to look for what might prove them wrong.

For your current top strategic priority, what specific evidence, if you found it, would tell you this strategy needs to change? Have you actually gone looking for that evidence, or only noticed evidence confirming you're on the right track?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader confident in a current strategic direction has built a team with genuine psychological safety and a track record of disagreement, per Lessons 11 and 19. [ASSUMPTION] Rather than simply waiting to see if disconfirming evidence happens to surface through this structure, this leader actively and specifically asks the team: "what would tell us this strategy isn't working, and have we actually checked for that specific evidence recently?" — combining the structural conditions this track has built toward with the leader's own deliberate, active effort to seek out disconfirmation, rather than passively waiting for it or unconsciously filtering it out.
