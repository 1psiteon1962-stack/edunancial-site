## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track established that the mythologized solo-founder narrative is inaccurate, and that seeking expert help reflects competence, not weakness. Here's the "why" this same principle applies with full, undiminished force at the very top of an organization, contrary to an intuition that greater seniority should mean greater self-sufficiency: this track has repeatedly shown, from different angles, that even the most senior leader depends on others for sound judgment — delegation to reduce key person risk (Lesson 2), genuine board oversight (Lesson 18), team members willing to disagree (Lesson 19), outside perspective in negotiation (Lesson 23), an ongoing professional network (Lesson 37), and a personal board of advisors distinct from corporate governance (Lesson 43).

Here's the "why" the mythologized solo leader narrative becomes more, not less, inaccurate at higher levels: the stakes of a senior leader's decisions are generally larger, meaning the cost of operating without the checks and support this track has described compounds at exactly the level where it's least visible and most dangerous, since fewer people are positioned to challenge or even fully see a senior leader's individual judgment.

Reflecting on everything covered in this track, where in your own leadership are you still operating more like the mythologized solo leader than the genuinely supported, team-dependent leader this entire track has described?
