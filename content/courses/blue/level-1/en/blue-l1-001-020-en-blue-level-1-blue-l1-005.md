# What Is an LLP, and How Is It Different from an LLC?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define an LLP in plain terms.
- Explain who typically uses this structure.
- Explain a key difference between LLP and LLC liability protection.

## Core Content

### What LLP Stands For

LLP stands for Limited Liability Partnership — a structure built for
multiple owners (partners) rather than a single member, commonly used by
professional practices like law or accounting firms.

### Who Typically Uses It

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: availability and rules vary by state/country
and profession.)* LLPs are often used by licensed professionals who are
required or choose to operate as partners, where each partner still
carries direct responsibility for their own professional work.

### The Key Liability Difference

Ask yourself: if one partner in the firm makes a costly error, should
every other partner's personal assets be at risk for it? In an LLP, a
partner is generally protected from personal liability for the
malpractice or wrongdoing of other partners — a distinction that matters
specifically in multi-partner professional settings.

## Worked Example

Picture a small law firm with three partners structured as an LLP. One
partner makes a serious error in a client matter. Ask yourself: are the
other two partners' personal homes at risk because of that one partner's
mistake? Under an LLP, generally not — each partner's liability
exposure for others' actions is limited, even though they share the
business.

## Practice Quiz

1. LLP stands for:
   A. Limited Liability Practice
   B. Limited Liability Partnership
   C. Legal Licensed Professional
   D. Local Liability Program

2. True or False: LLPs are typically structured for a single owner.

3. What does LLP liability protection generally address?
   A. Protection from all business debts of any kind
   B. Protection from personal liability for other partners' malpractice
      or wrongdoing
   C. Protection from paying any taxes
   D. Protection from ever being sued

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. LLP stands for Limited Liability Partnership.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. LLPs are structured for multiple partners, not a
single owner.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. LLPs specifically address liability for other
partners' actions.

## Key Takeaways

- An LLP is built for multiple partners, often in licensed professions.
- It generally protects a partner from liability for other partners'
  malpractice or wrongdoing.
- This is a different protection than an LLC's general liability
  shield.
