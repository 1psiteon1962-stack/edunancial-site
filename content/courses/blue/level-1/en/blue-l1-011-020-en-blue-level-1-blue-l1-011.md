## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Revenue Is
Revenue is the total amount of money a business brings in from sales, before subtracting any costs. It's often called the "top line," because it's the first number on a financial statement, before anything gets taken out.

### Topic 2: What Profit Is
Profit is what's left after subtracting all costs — cost of goods, labor, rent, and everything else — from revenue. It's often called the "bottom line," for the same reason revenue is the top line.

### Topic 3: How a Business Can Have Revenue Without Profit
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business does $500,000 in sales (revenue) in a year, but spends $520,000 on inventory, staff, rent, and other costs. That business has strong revenue and a $20,000 loss — negative profit — at the same time.

### Topic 4: Why This Confusion Causes Real Damage
Owners who track only revenue can feel like the business is thriving while it's actually losing money every month. Decisions about hiring, spending, and growth made on revenue alone, without checking profit, are one of the most common causes of businesses running into serious financial trouble.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Revenue:** The total money a business brings in from sales, before subtracting any costs.
- **Profit:** What remains after subtracting all costs from revenue.
- **Loss:** Negative profit — when costs exceed revenue.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A clothing boutique brings in $10,000 in sales in a month (revenue). After paying $4,000 for inventory, $3,000 for rent and staff, and $2,000 for other costs, $1,000 remains — that $1,000 is profit, not the original $10,000.

## Common Mistakes
- Announcing revenue figures as if they represent take-home earnings
- Making spending decisions based on revenue growth without checking whether profit is also growing
- Assuming high revenue always means a healthy business

## FAQ
**Q: Is it possible to have high revenue and still go out of business?**
A: Yes — if costs consistently exceed revenue, a business can have impressive sales numbers and still fail. This is a very common real-world pattern.

**Q: Which number matters more, revenue or profit?**
A: [OPINION] Both matter, for different reasons — revenue shows demand and scale, profit shows whether the business is actually sustainable. Neither alone tells the full story.

## Key Takeaways
- Revenue is total sales before costs; profit is what remains after costs
- A business can have strong revenue and still lose money
- Confusing the two leads to poor spending and growth decisions
- Both numbers matter, but they answer different questions

## Practical Exercise
Take a hypothetical business with $50,000 in monthly revenue and $45,000 in monthly costs. Calculate the profit, and explain in one sentence what that number tells you that revenue alone doesn't.
