## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Break-Even Means
The break-even point is the level of sales at which total revenue exactly equals total costs — no profit, no loss. Below that point, the business operates at a loss; above it, the business begins generating profit.

### Topic 2: The Basic Calculation Concept
[ESTIMATE] At a conceptual level, break-even is reached when enough units are sold that the revenue from those sales covers both the overhead (from BLUE-L1-013) and the direct costs of producing those units. The exact formula involves fixed costs, price per unit, and variable cost per unit, which later lessons will cover in more depth.

### Topic 3: Why It Matters
Knowing roughly where break-even sits tells an owner how many sales are needed just to stop losing money — a critical number before assessing whether a business idea, pricing plan, or growth target is realistic.

### Topic 4: Break-Even Isn't a Finish Line
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Reaching break-even means the business has stopped losing money on an ongoing basis — it doesn't mean the business is thriving, paying the owner well, or building any cushion for tough months. It's the starting line for profit, not the goal itself.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Break-Even Point:** The level of sales at which total revenue exactly equals total costs, resulting in neither profit nor loss.
- **Fixed Costs:** Costs (like overhead) that don't change with sales volume, relevant to the break-even calculation.
- **Variable Costs:** Costs (like direct costs) that change with sales volume, also relevant to the break-even calculation.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE, ESTIMATE] A small business with $5,000/month in overhead needs to sell enough units, after covering the direct cost of each unit, to generate $5,000 in leftover revenue just to reach break-even — anything sold beyond that point starts contributing to actual profit.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming a business is doing well simply because it's no longer losing money
- Not knowing the break-even point before setting sales or growth targets
- Confusing break-even with "profitable"

## FAQ
**Q: Is reaching break-even a good milestone?**
A: [OPINION] It's a meaningful and necessary milestone, but not a success milestone on its own — it means the business is no longer bleeding money, not that it's thriving.

**Q: Does break-even ever change?**
A: Yes — if fixed costs (overhead) or variable costs (direct costs per unit) change, the break-even point shifts accordingly.

## Key Takeaways
- Break-even is where total revenue exactly equals total costs
- Below it, the business loses money; above it, profit begins
- It depends on both fixed costs and variable costs
- Reaching break-even is a starting point for profit, not a finish line
