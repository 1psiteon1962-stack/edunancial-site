## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Cash Flow Means
Cash flow refers to the actual movement of money into and out of a business over a period of time — when cash is really received and really paid, not just when a sale is recorded on paper.

### Topic 2: Profit vs. Cash Flow — A Timing Problem
Profit (from BLUE-L1-011) can be recorded the moment a sale happens, even if the customer hasn't actually paid yet. Cash flow only counts money once it actually lands in or leaves the bank account. These two pictures can diverge significantly.

### Topic 3: Why Profitable Businesses Still Run Out of Cash
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business delivers $100,000 of work in a month (recorded as profit) but its customers don't pay for 60 days under agreed payment terms. Meanwhile, the business still has to pay its own staff and suppliers this month. On paper, it's profitable — but if it doesn't have enough cash cushion to cover this month's bills while waiting on those 60-day payments, it can run out of money despite being profitable.

### Topic 4: The Practical Takeaway
Tracking profit alone isn't enough — a business also needs to track when money actually arrives and leaves, especially if there's a gap between delivering work and getting paid for it.

## Key Definitions
- **Cash Flow:** The actual movement of money into and out of a business over a period of time.
- **Cash Flow Timing Gap:** The delay between when revenue is earned (and profit recorded) and when the corresponding cash is actually received.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A construction company completes a $50,000 project in March (recorded as profit that month) but the client, per contract terms, doesn't pay until May. If the company has payroll and supplier bills due in April, it needs cash on hand or short-term financing to bridge that gap — despite being profitable on the March project.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming a profitable month automatically means enough cash is available to pay bills that month
- Not tracking payment timing separately from sales/profit recording
- Growing sales aggressively without ensuring enough cash cushion to cover the resulting timing gaps

## FAQ
**Q: Can a business be profitable and still go bankrupt?**
A: Yes — this is a well-documented and common real-world pattern, precisely because of cash flow timing problems rather than a lack of underlying profitability.

**Q: How do businesses manage this timing gap?**
A: [ESTIMATE] Common approaches include maintaining a cash reserve, negotiating faster payment terms with customers, negotiating slower payment terms with suppliers, or using short-term financing — specific strategies are covered in later lessons.

## Key Takeaways
- Cash flow is the actual movement of money, not the recorded moment of a sale
- Profit and cash flow can diverge significantly due to payment timing
- A profitable business can still run out of cash if it can't cover bills during the gap
- Tracking cash flow separately from profit is essential to avoiding this trap
