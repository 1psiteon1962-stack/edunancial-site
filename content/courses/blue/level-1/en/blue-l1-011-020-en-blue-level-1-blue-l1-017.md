## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Startup Capital Is Used For
Startup capital is the money needed to get a business up and running before it can support itself through sales — covering things like initial inventory, equipment, licensing, initial overhead, and other pre-revenue costs.

### Topic 2: Personal Savings
[ESTIMATE] For many new businesses, personal savings is the most common first source of startup capital — it's fully within the owner's control and avoids taking on debt or giving up ownership, though it also puts the owner's own financial cushion at direct risk.

### Topic 3: Friends and Family
Some owners raise startup capital from friends or family, often as informal loans or investments. [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] This can be faster and more flexible than formal financing, but mixing money and personal relationships carries its own risk if the business struggles or expectations aren't clearly documented.

### Topic 4: Credit and Small Loans
Personal or business credit cards, small personal loans, or lines of credit are another common early-stage source, [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] though terms, interest rates, and availability vary significantly by lender and jurisdiction. These sources typically come with real repayment obligations regardless of how the business performs.

## Key Definitions
- **Startup Capital:** Money needed to launch and sustain a business before it generates enough revenue to support itself.
- **Bootstrapping (preview):** Funding a business primarily through personal resources and early revenue rather than outside investment — covered in depth in the next lesson.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A new food truck business might draw $15,000 in startup capital from a combination of $8,000 in personal savings, a $5,000 loan from a family member, and $2,000 on a personal credit card — three different sources, each with different risk and repayment implications.

## Common Mistakes
- Underestimating how much startup capital is actually needed before revenue arrives
- Treating friends-and-family money as if it carries no real obligation or risk to the relationship
- Ignoring the interest and repayment terms attached to credit-based funding

## FAQ
**Q: Is outside investment (like venture capital) the most common source of startup capital?**
A: [ESTIMATE] No — most small businesses start with personal savings, friends/family, or credit rather than outside investors, who typically become relevant at a later stage or for specific types of high-growth businesses.

**Q: Is it risky to use personal savings for a business?**
A: [OPINION] Yes, meaningfully — it puts personal financial security directly at stake, which is why understanding the amount needed and the risk involved matters before committing those funds.

## Key Takeaways
- Startup capital covers pre-revenue costs needed to get a business running
- Personal savings is often the first and most common source
- Friends and family funding is common but carries relationship risk if not clearly documented
- Credit-based sources come with fixed repayment obligations regardless of business performance
