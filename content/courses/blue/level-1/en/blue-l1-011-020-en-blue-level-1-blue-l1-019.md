## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What an Invoice Is
An invoice is a formal document a business sends to a customer requesting payment for goods or services already delivered, typically listing what was provided, the amount owed, and by when.

### Topic 2: Payment Terms Explained
Payment terms specify when payment is due — common examples include "Net 30" (payment due 30 days after the invoice date) or "Due on receipt" (payment expected immediately). [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] Standard terms and typical negotiating norms vary by industry and location.

### Topic 3: Why Payment Terms Directly Affect Cash Flow
Recall from BLUE-L1-016 that the gap between earning revenue and actually receiving cash is exactly what creates cash flow risk. Payment terms are the mechanism that sets the size of that gap — "Net 30" creates a longer wait than "Due on receipt," directly shaping how much of a cash cushion a business needs.

### Topic 4: Negotiating Terms Strategically
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business might negotiate shorter payment terms with its own customers (to get paid faster) while negotiating longer payment terms with its own suppliers (to hold onto cash longer) — actively managing the cash flow gap from both directions rather than just accepting whatever terms are offered.

## Key Definitions
- **Invoice:** A formal document requesting payment for goods or services already delivered, listing what was provided, the amount owed, and payment due date.
- **Payment Terms:** The agreed timeframe and conditions under which an invoice must be paid.
- **Net 30 (example term):** A common payment term meaning payment is due 30 days after the invoice date.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A consultant sends a $5,000 invoice with "Net 30" terms on June 1st. Payment isn't technically due until July 1st, meaning the consultant must cover a full month of their own expenses before that cash arrives — directly illustrating the timing gap from BLUE-L1-016.

## Common Mistakes
- Sending invoices without clear payment terms, leaving payment timing ambiguous
- Accepting long payment terms from customers without considering the resulting cash flow strain
- Not tracking which invoices are overdue, allowing cash flow gaps to widen unnoticed

## FAQ
**Q: Are payment terms negotiable?**
A: Often yes — [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, ESTIMATE] terms can frequently be negotiated between businesses, though norms vary significantly by industry and relationship leverage.

**Q: What happens if a customer doesn't pay by the term deadline?**
A: This becomes a late or overdue invoice, which can require follow-up, and in some cases, may involve late fees or collections processes depending on the original agreement and jurisdiction.

## Key Takeaways
- An invoice formally requests payment already-delivered goods or services
- Payment terms specify exactly when that payment is due
- Payment terms directly determine the size of the cash flow timing gap
- Businesses can negotiate terms with both customers and suppliers to actively manage that gap
