## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What a Balance Sheet Shows
A balance sheet is a financial document showing what a business owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities), and the resulting owner's equity, all as of one specific date — a financial "snapshot" rather than a summary of activity over time.

### Topic 2: The Core Equation
[VERIFIED FACT] The fundamental balance sheet equation is: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity. This must always balance — hence the name — because everything the business owns was financed either by debt (liabilities) or by the owner's own investment and retained profit (equity).

### Topic 3: Snapshot vs. Period — The Key Difference From a P&L
Recall from BLUE-L1-024 that a P&L covers a period of time (a month, a quarter, a year). A balance sheet, by contrast, is a snapshot as of one specific date — like a photograph rather than a video of financial activity.

### Topic 4: Why Both Documents Matter Together
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business could show strong profit on its P&L for the year while also carrying significant debt shown on its balance sheet — the P&L alone wouldn't reveal that debt burden, and the balance sheet alone wouldn't reveal how that profit was generated. Together, they give a much fuller financial picture.

## Key Definitions
- **Balance Sheet:** A financial snapshot as of one specific date, showing assets, liabilities, and owner's equity.
- **Assets:** What a business owns (cash, equipment, inventory, property, etc.).
- **Liabilities:** What a business owes (loans, unpaid bills, other debts).
- **Owner's Equity:** The owner's remaining stake in the business, calculated as assets minus liabilities.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business has $100,000 in assets (equipment, cash, inventory) and $40,000 in liabilities (a business loan). Owner's equity is $60,000 — the portion of the business's value that belongs to the owner after accounting for what's owed.

## Common Mistakes
- Confusing a balance sheet's snapshot nature with a P&L's period-based nature
- Assuming high assets alone mean a business is financially healthy, without checking liabilities
- Forgetting that owner's equity isn't cash sitting in an account — it's a calculated figure

## FAQ
**Q: Which is more important, the P&L or the balance sheet?**
A: [OPINION] Neither alone tells the full story — the P&L shows performance over time, the balance sheet shows financial position at a moment; both are generally needed together for a complete picture.

**Q: Does a balance sheet always literally "balance"?**
A: Yes — by definition, assets must always equal liabilities plus owner's equity; if it doesn't balance, there's an error somewhere in the underlying records.

## Key Takeaways
- A balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and owner's equity as of one specific date
- The core equation is: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity
- Unlike the P&L's period-based view, a balance sheet is a single-moment snapshot
- Both documents together give a fuller financial picture than either alone
