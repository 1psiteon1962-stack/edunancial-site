## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What a Supplier Relationship Involves
A supplier provides the raw materials, components, or products a business needs to operate — the relationship typically involves ongoing orders, agreed pricing, and expectations around delivery timing and quality.

### Topic 2: Why the Cheapest Option Feels Appealing
It's natural to compare suppliers primarily on price, since price is the easiest number to compare directly across options.

### Topic 3: What Unreliability Actually Costs
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A cheaper supplier that frequently delivers late, delivers incomplete orders, or delivers inconsistent quality can cause a business to miss its own customer deadlines, hold emergency backup inventory (adding holding costs from BLUE-L1-026), or lose customer trust — costs that often exceed whatever was saved on price.

### Topic 4: What Reliability Actually Buys
A reliable supplier, even at a somewhat higher price, provides predictability — the business can plan production, inventory, and customer commitments with confidence, reducing the kind of operational risk that's much harder to price into a simple cost comparison.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Supplier:** A business or individual that provides raw materials, components, or products another business needs to operate.
- **Reliability:** A supplier's consistency in delivering on time, in full, and at the agreed quality.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A furniture maker switches to a cheaper wood supplier to cut costs, but the new supplier's shipments are frequently delayed. The furniture maker starts missing delivery promises to its own customers, damaging its reputation — a cost far larger than the money saved on wood.

## Common Mistakes
- Choosing a supplier based on price alone, without evaluating reliability
- Not having a backup plan if a primary supplier fails to deliver
- Underestimating how much unreliability can damage relationships further down the chain, with the business's own customers

## FAQ
**Q: Should price never factor into choosing a supplier?**
A: [OPINION] Price still matters — the point isn't to ignore it, but to weigh it against reliability rather than treating price as the only variable that matters.

**Q: How can a business evaluate a supplier's reliability before committing?**
A: [ESTIMATE] Common approaches include checking references from other customers, starting with smaller trial orders, and reviewing any track record or reviews available before relying on a supplier for critical, ongoing needs.

## Key Takeaways
- A supplier relationship involves ongoing orders, pricing, and delivery/quality expectations
- The cheapest price isn't automatically the best value once reliability is considered
- Unreliable suppliers can cause missed deadlines, extra holding costs, and lost customer trust
- Reliability provides predictability that's often worth more than a lower price alone

## Practical Exercise
Describe a real or hypothetical situation where choosing a cheaper, less reliable supplier ended up costing more than a more expensive, reliable one.
