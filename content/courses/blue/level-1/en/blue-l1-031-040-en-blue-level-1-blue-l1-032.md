## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What a Marketing Plan Is, Stripped Down
A marketing plan, at its core, is a plan for how a business will reach and persuade its target market to buy — everything more elaborate builds on this basic foundation.

### Topic 2: Core Question One — Who?
This directly draws on BLUE-L1-031: a marketing plan has to start with a clearly defined target market. Without that, none of the following questions can be answered meaningfully.

### Topic 3: Core Question Two — What Message?
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] What specific value or problem-solving benefit does the product offer to that target market, and how should that be communicated? A message that resonates with one target market may fall flat with another.

### Topic 4: Core Questions Three and Four — Where and How Much?
Where will the target market actually see this message (which channels — social media, local advertising, word of mouth, etc.), and what budget is realistically available to reach them there? A basic marketing plan answers all four questions together, even in a very simple form.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Marketing Plan:** A plan for how a business will reach and persuade its target market to buy, built on answers to who, what message, where, and how much.
- **Marketing Channel:** A specific medium or platform (such as social media, local advertising, or word of mouth) used to reach a target market.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A local bakery's basic marketing plan: Who — nearby residents interested in fresh baked goods. What message — freshly baked daily, made with quality ingredients. Where — local social media groups and in-store signage. How much — a modest monthly budget for social media promotion.

## Common Mistakes
- Starting a marketing plan without first answering the target market question
- Choosing marketing channels the target market doesn't actually use
- Setting a budget without connecting it to the size and reach needed for the chosen channels

## FAQ
**Q: Does a marketing plan need to be complicated to be effective?**
A: [OPINION] No — a simple, clear answer to these four basic questions can be far more effective than an elaborate plan built on an unclear target market or message.

**Q: Can a small business with a very limited budget still have a marketing plan?**
A: Yes — the "how much" question simply reflects whatever budget is realistically available; a marketing plan scales to the resources at hand, even if that's very modest.

## Key Takeaways
- A basic marketing plan answers who, what message, where, and how much
- It builds directly on having a clearly defined target market first
- The message should be tailored specifically to that target market
- Channel and budget choices should align with where the target market can actually be reached

## Socratic Teaching Guide
`BLUE-L1-032-SOCRATIC` (Level 1 — "What")

2. **Concept 1 — Foundation:** What core question must be answered before any of the others?
3. **Follow-up:** Why does this connect directly back to BLUE-L1-031?
4. **Concept 2 — Message:** What does the "what message" question ask?
5. **Follow-up:** Why might the same product need a different message for different target markets?
6. **Concept 3 — Channels:** What does "where" refer to in a marketing plan?
7. **Follow-up:** Why does the channel choice depend on the target market?
8. **Concept 4 — Budget:** Why does the "how much" question matter alongside the other three?
9. **Synthesis:** Walk through all four questions for a business you're familiar with.
10. **Reflection:** Why might a very simple marketing plan sometimes outperform a complicated one?
