## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Customer Retention Means
Customer retention refers to a business's ability to keep existing customers buying or subscribing over time, rather than losing them to competitors, disinterest, or dissatisfaction.

### Topic 2: Why Retention Is Usually Cheaper Than Acquisition
Recall from BLUE-L1-034 that acquiring a new customer costs money (CAC) — marketing, advertising, and outreach. An existing customer, already familiar with and trusting the business, typically requires far less spending to keep buying than a brand-new customer requires to be found and convinced in the first place.

### Topic 3: The Compounding Value of Retention
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A customer who buys repeatedly over years generates far more total revenue than a one-time buyer, without the business needing to spend CAC again and again for each purchase — the initial acquisition cost gets "spread out" over a longer relationship.

### Topic 4: What Drives Retention
[ESTIMATE] Common drivers of retention include product quality, customer service experience, consistency, and the overall brand relationship discussed in BLUE-L1-033 — retention isn't just a marketing function, it's closely tied to the actual experience a customer has.

## Key Definitions
- **Customer Retention:** A business's ability to keep existing customers buying or subscribing over time.
- **Customer Lifetime Value (preview):** The total revenue a business can expect from a single customer over the full duration of their relationship — a concept building directly on retention, covered further at higher levels.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A $50 CAC to acquire a customer becomes far more worthwhile if that customer buys $30 worth of product every month for two years, versus a customer who buys once for $35 and never returns — the retained customer generates dramatically more value relative to the same acquisition cost.

## Common Mistakes
- Focusing marketing spend entirely on new customer acquisition while neglecting existing customer experience
- Assuming retention "just happens" without deliberate effort
- Underestimating how much repeat customers contribute relative to one-time buyers

## FAQ
**Q: Does this mean acquiring new customers isn't important?**
A: [OPINION] No — new customer acquisition is still necessary for growth; the point is that retention efforts are often comparatively cost-effective and shouldn't be neglected in favor of acquisition alone.

**Q: How does retention connect to the brand concept from BLUE-L1-033?**
A: Since brand is built through consistent, positive experience over time, strong brand perception and strong retention tend to reinforce each other directly.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer retention means keeping existing customers buying over time
- It's usually cheaper than acquiring new customers, since existing customers already trust the business
- Retained customers generate compounding value relative to the CAC already spent on them
- Retention is driven by actual experience — product quality, service, and consistency — not marketing alone
