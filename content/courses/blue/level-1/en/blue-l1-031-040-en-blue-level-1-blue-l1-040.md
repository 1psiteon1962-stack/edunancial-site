## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What Staffing Involves
Staffing refers to the process of bringing people into a business to perform work — a decision that involves not just finding someone, but having the systems (BLUE-L1-039), cash flow (BLUE-L1-016), and clear tasks needed for that person to succeed.

### Topic 2: The Risk of Hiring Too Early
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Hiring before cash flow can reliably support a salary, or before tasks are clearly defined enough to hand off, can strain a business financially and leave a new hire confused about what's actually expected of them.

### Topic 3: The Risk of Hiring Too Late
Recall from BLUE-L1-038 that staying entirely self-employed caps growth at the owner's personal hours. Delaying a needed first hire too long can mean turning away business, burning out, or simply staying capped at that hours-based ceiling indefinitely.

### Topic 4: Practical Signals It Might Be Time
[ESTIMATE] Common signals include consistently turning away work due to lack of time, spending significant time on tasks that don't require the owner's specific expertise, and having cash flow that can reliably support a salary even during a slower period, not just the current busiest month.

## Key Definitions
- **Staffing:** The process of bringing people into a business to perform work, requiring adequate systems, cash flow, and clearly defined tasks.
- **Role Clarity:** How clearly defined a job's tasks and expectations are before hiring for that role.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A landscaping business owner who's turning away new clients every week, and has cash reserves that could cover a new hire's salary through a slow season, shows several signals it may be time to hire — versus an owner who's occasionally busy but has no documented processes and unpredictable cash flow, where hiring might be premature.

## Common Mistakes
- Hiring based on a single unusually busy period rather than a sustained pattern
- Hiring without first building at least basic systems (BLUE-L1-039) for the role
- Waiting too long out of fear, staying capped at the self-employed hours ceiling unnecessarily

## FAQ
**Q: Is there a universal "right" number of clients or revenue that signals it's time to hire?**
A: [OPINION] No — the right signals depend heavily on the specific business and role; the practical signals in Topic 4 are meant as general guidance, not a fixed formula.

**Q: Does the first hire need to be a full-time employee?**
A: Not necessarily — [ESTIMATE] part-time help, contractors, or specific task-based support can sometimes be an appropriate first step before committing to a full-time role, depending on the business's specific needs.

## Key Takeaways
- Staffing requires more than just finding a person — it needs systems, cash flow, and role clarity
- Hiring too early can strain finances and confuse a new hire without clear tasks
- Hiring too late traps an owner at the self-employed hours ceiling unnecessarily
- Practical signals include sustained turned-away work, non-expertise task burden, and reliable cash flow
