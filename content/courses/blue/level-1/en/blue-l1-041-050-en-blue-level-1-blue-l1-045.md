## Executive Summary

A pivot isn't giving up — it's changing direction while keeping what's already been learned and built. This lesson explains what a pivot actually is and distinguishes it from simply starting over or quitting.

## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What a Pivot Is
A pivot is a significant change in a business's approach — often its business model (BLUE-L1-030), product, or target market (BLUE-L1-031) — made in response to what's been learned, while retaining and building on other elements that are already working.

### Topic 2: What Typically Stays Constant
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A pivot often keeps core assets like accumulated customer relationships, brand reputation (BLUE-L1-033), team expertise, or underlying technology, while changing the specific product, business model, or market focus built around those assets.

### Topic 3: How a Pivot Differs From Giving Up
Giving up means abandoning the effort entirely. A pivot means recognizing that specific aspects of the current approach aren't working, while preserving and redirecting what's been learned and built toward a better-fitting direction.

### Topic 4: How a Pivot Differs From Stubbornly Refusing to Change
[OPINION] Refusing to adjust course despite clear evidence something isn't working is a different mistake entirely — a pivot represents a deliberate, informed change based on real signals, not stubbornness, and also not panic-driven abandonment of a fundamentally sound idea.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Pivot:** A significant, deliberate change in a business's approach — often its model, product, or target market — made in response to what's been learned, while retaining other working elements.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A company originally selling a photo-sharing app to consumers discovers that businesses, not individual consumers, are the ones actually finding real value in the underlying technology. Pivoting to sell the same core technology as a business tool retains the technology and team expertise while changing the target market and business model.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating every difficulty as a signal to pivot, rather than distinguishing real signals from normal early-stage struggle
- Refusing to pivot despite clear, repeated evidence the current approach isn't working
- Confusing a pivot with abandoning everything and starting completely from scratch

## FAQ
**Q: Is pivoting a sign of failure?**
A: [OPINION] Not inherently — many successful businesses pivoted significantly from their original concept; the ability to recognize and act on real signals is often viewed as a strength, not a weakness.

**Q: How do you know when it's actually time to pivot versus just pushing through a normal difficult period?**
A: [OPINION] This is a genuinely difficult judgment call with no universal formula — it typically involves weighing repeated, consistent signals (like the market research signals from BLUE-L1-037) against the natural ups and downs any new business experiences.

## Key Takeaways
- A pivot is a deliberate change in approach, made in response to what's been learned
- It typically retains valuable elements (relationships, brand, expertise) while changing others
- A pivot differs from giving up, since it redirects rather than abandons effort
- It also differs from stubborn refusal to change despite clear evidence

## Practical Exercise
Think of a real or hypothetical business pivot. Identify what stayed the same and what changed in that pivot.
