## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What OPM Means, at a Basic Level
OPM refers to funding a business using money that belongs to other people or institutions — investors, lenders, or partners — rather than exclusively the owner's own personal resources.

### Topic 2: The Connection to Startup Capital
Recall from BLUE-L1-017 that startup capital can come from several sources — personal savings, friends and family, credit. OPM specifically refers to sources beyond the owner's own money and immediate personal circle — institutional investors, formal lenders, or larger-scale outside capital.

### Topic 3: The Connection to Bootstrapping
Recall from BLUE-L1-018 that bootstrapping means relying primarily on personal resources and reinvested revenue, trading growth speed for retained control. OPM represents essentially the opposite end of that same spectrum — trading some control or repayment obligation for access to potentially larger and faster capital.

### Topic 4: Why This Lesson Stops Here
[OPINION] Using OPM effectively involves real risk, legal complexity, and strategic tradeoffs that go well beyond Level 1 awareness — this introductory lesson exists only to establish the basic concept and its relationship to what's already been covered, with the full method, risk analysis, and strategic application reserved for BLUE-L3.

## Key Definitions
- **OPM (Other People's Money):** Funding a business using money belonging to outside investors, lenders, or partners, rather than exclusively the owner's own personal resources.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business bootstrapped for its first two years using personal savings and reinvested revenue (BLUE-L1-018) later takes on a bank loan and an outside investor to fund a major expansion — shifting from primarily personal capital toward OPM as the business's needs and opportunities grow.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming OPM and personal credit/loans (from BLUE-L1-017) are the same thing — OPM at this introductory level refers more broadly to any outside capital source, though the boundary can blur
- Assuming this lesson provides enough depth to actually pursue OPM strategically — it does not, by design
- Confusing OPM with "free money" — outside capital typically comes with repayment obligations or ownership stakes attached

## FAQ
**Q: Is OPM inherently better or worse than bootstrapping?**
A: [OPINION] Neither inherently — as discussed in BLUE-L1-018, this is a trade-off between control/growth speed, and the right choice depends on the specific business and owner's goals.

**Q: Where can I learn the full strategic method for using OPM?**
A: This introductory lesson deliberately stops at basic awareness — the complete method, including risk analysis and strategic application, is covered at BLUE-L3.
