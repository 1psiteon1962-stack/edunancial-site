# BLUE-L1-001: What Is a Business, Really?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a business in plain terms.
2. Identify the core function every business performs.
3. Distinguish a business from a hobby or a job.

## Core Content
A business is an organised effort to provide goods or services to customers in exchange for payment, with the intention of generating a profit. *VERIFIED FACT:* At its core, every business performs the same basic function — it solves a problem or meets a need for someone else, and that someone pays for the solution.

*ASSUMPTION:* The line between a hobby and a business isn't always the activity itself, but the intent and structure behind it — a hobby that starts generating regular income, is run with a profit motive, and is treated as an ongoing operation typically becomes a business, though the exact legal threshold varies by jurisdiction.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Maria bakes cakes for friends as a hobby, accepting occasional payment to cover ingredients. When she starts advertising, pricing her cakes to include a profit margin, and treating orders as a regular source of income, she has crossed from hobby into business — even though the activity itself (baking cakes) hasn't changed at all.

1. This is the universal function underlying every business, regardless of industry.
2. This is the core definition used throughout this lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- A business solves a problem or meets a need in exchange for payment.
- The hobby-to-business line depends on intent and structure, not just the activity.
- Every business, regardless of size or industry, shares this same core function.
