# BLUE-L1-002: Revenue vs. Profit — The Critical Difference

## Learning Objectives
1. Define revenue and profit separately.
2. Explain why high revenue doesn't guarantee a healthy business.
3. Calculate simple profit given revenue and expenses.

## Core Content
Revenue is the total amount of money a business brings in from sales, before any costs are subtracted. *VERIFIED FACT:* Profit = Revenue − Expenses — it's what's left over after everything the business had to spend to generate that revenue.

*ASSUMPTION:* A business can have very high revenue and still lose money if its expenses are high enough — revenue alone says nothing about whether a business is actually financially healthy.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A shop brings in $200,000 in sales revenue this year — a headline-grabbing number. But after paying for stock, staff wages, rent, and other costs totalling $210,000, the business actually lost $10,000. High revenue told only part of the story; profit revealed the real picture.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is revenue?
2. What is the formula for profit?
3. True/False: High revenue always means a business is profitable.
4. If a business has $150,000 in revenue and $120,000 in expenses, what is its profit?

## Answer Key
1. The total money brought in from sales, before costs   2. Revenue − Expenses   3. False   4. $30,000

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of revenue as a top-line figure.
2. This is the standard profit calculation.
3. A business can have high revenue and still operate at a loss if expenses are high enough.
4. $150,000 − $120,000 = $30,000.

## Key Takeaways
- Revenue is the total money in; profit is what's left after expenses.
- Profit = Revenue − Expenses is the standard formula.
- High revenue doesn't guarantee profitability — expenses matter just as much.
