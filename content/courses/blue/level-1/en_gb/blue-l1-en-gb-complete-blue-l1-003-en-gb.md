# BLUE-L1-003: Understanding Expenses

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a business expense.
2. Distinguish operating expenses from one-off expenses.
3. Identify common categories of business expenses.

## Core Content
A business expense is any cost incurred in the process of running the business and generating revenue. *VERIFIED FACT:* Expenses are commonly split into operating expenses (recurring costs like rent, wages, and utilities) and one-off expenses (irregular costs like a new piece of equipment or a legal settlement).

Common expense categories include: cost of goods sold (materials, stock), staff wages, rent and utilities, marketing, insurance, and professional fees (accountants, solicitors).

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A small café's monthly operating expenses include $2,000 rent, $3,500 in staff wages, $800 for ingredients and stock, and $200 for utilities — recurring costs it can predict each month. When the oven breaks and needs replacing for $1,500, that's a one-off expense — unpredictable and not part of the regular monthly pattern.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a business expense?
2. What's the difference between operating and one-off expenses?
3. True/False: Staff wages are typically classified as a one-off expense.
4. Name two common categories of business expenses.

## Answer Key
1. Any cost incurred in running the business and generating revenue   2. Operating expenses recur regularly; one-off expenses are irregular and unpredictable   3. False   4. Cost of goods sold, staff wages (among others)

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a business expense.
2. This distinguishes the two general categories of business costs.
3. Wages are a classic recurring operating expense, not a one-off.
4. These are among the standard expense categories most businesses track.

## Key Takeaways
- A business expense is any cost of running the business.
- Operating expenses recur regularly; one-off expenses are irregular.
- Tracking expense categories helps a business understand where its money actually goes.
