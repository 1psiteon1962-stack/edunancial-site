# BLUE-L1-005: Fixed Costs vs. Variable Costs

## Learning Objectives
1. Define fixed costs and variable costs.
2. Identify examples of each.
3. Explain why this distinction matters for planning.

## Core Content
Fixed costs stay roughly the same regardless of how much a business sells — rent, insurance, and a manager's salary are common examples. *VERIFIED FACT:* Variable costs rise and fall with sales volume — raw materials, packaging, and sales commissions are typical variable costs, since more sales generally require more of these inputs.

*ASSUMPTION:* Understanding this split matters for planning because fixed costs must be paid even during a slow month with little revenue, while variable costs naturally shrink when sales slow down.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A bakery pays $1,500 rent every month regardless of how many loaves it sells — a fixed cost. Flour, however, costs roughly $0.50 per loaf baked; the more loaves sold, the more flour purchased. In a slow month with fewer sales, the flour bill shrinks accordingly, but the $1,500 rent is due all the same.

## Practice Quiz
1. What are fixed costs?
2. What are variable costs?
3. True/False: Rent is typically a variable cost.
4. Why does the fixed/variable distinction matter for planning?

## Answer Key
1. Costs that stay roughly the same regardless of sales volume   2. Costs that rise and fall with sales volume   3. False   4. Fixed costs must be paid even in slow months; variable costs naturally adjust with sales

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the standard definition of a fixed cost.
2. This is the standard definition of a variable cost.
3. Rent is a classic fixed cost, not variable.
4. This distinction helps a business predict cash needs during slower periods.

## Key Takeaways
- Fixed costs stay constant regardless of sales volume.
- Variable costs rise and fall with how much a business sells.
- Fixed costs create risk in slow periods since they must be paid regardless of revenue.
