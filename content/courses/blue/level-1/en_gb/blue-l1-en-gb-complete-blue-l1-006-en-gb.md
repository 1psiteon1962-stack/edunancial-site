# BLUE-L1-006: The Break-Even Point Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define the break-even point.
2. Calculate a simple break-even point in units.
3. Explain why knowing this figure matters for a new business.

## Core Content
The break-even point is the level of sales at which a business's total revenue exactly equals its total costs — no profit, no loss. *VERIFIED FACT:* Break-Even Point (in units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit).

*ASSUMPTION:* Knowing the break-even point matters because it tells a business owner the minimum sales volume needed just to avoid losing money, before any profit begins.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A small candle business has $1,000 in monthly fixed costs. Each candle sells for $20 and costs $5 in materials (variable cost). Break-Even Point = $1,000 ÷ ($20 − $5) = 1,000 ÷ 15 ≈ 67 candles. The business must sell about 67 candles a month just to cover its costs — anything beyond that becomes profit.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is the break-even point?
2. What is the formula for break-even point in units?
3. True/False: At the break-even point, a business is making a profit.
4. If fixed costs are $500, price per unit is $25, and variable cost per unit is $10, what is the break-even point in units?

## Answer Key
1. The sales level where total revenue equals total costs   2. Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)   3. False   4. Approximately 33 units

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of break-even.
2. This is the standard break-even calculation.
3. At break-even, revenue equals costs exactly — there's no profit or loss yet.
4. $500 ÷ ($25 − $10) = 500 ÷ 15 ≈ 33.3, rounded to about 33 units.

## Key Takeaways
- The break-even point is where revenue exactly covers total costs.
- It's calculated as Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per Unit).
- Knowing this figure tells an owner the minimum sales needed before profit begins.
