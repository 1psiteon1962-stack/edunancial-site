# BLUE-L1-007: What Is a Business Plan?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a business plan and its general purpose.
2. Identify the core sections a business plan typically includes.
3. Explain who a business plan is usually written for.

## Core Content
A business plan is a written document that describes what a business does, how it intends to make money, and how it plans to operate and grow. *VERIFIED FACT:* Common sections include an executive summary, a description of the product or service, market research, a marketing and sales plan, an operations plan, and financial projections.

*ASSUMPTION:* Business plans generally serve two audiences: the owner, as a planning and thinking tool, and external parties like lenders or investors, who use it to evaluate whether the business is viable enough to fund.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Before opening a food truck, Sam writes a business plan estimating startup costs, projecting monthly sales based on similar local businesses, and outlining a marketing plan for social media. When he later applies for a small business loan, the bank asks to see exactly this document — the same plan that helped him think through the business now helps convince a lender it's worth funding.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a business plan.
2. These are standard sections found in most business plans.
3. Business plans are also valuable internally, as a planning and thinking tool for the owner.
4. Business plans commonly serve both internal planning and external funding purposes.

## Key Takeaways
- A business plan describes what a business does and how it intends to operate and grow.
- Common sections include market research, operations, and financial projections.
- Business plans serve both the owner's own planning and external audiences like lenders.
