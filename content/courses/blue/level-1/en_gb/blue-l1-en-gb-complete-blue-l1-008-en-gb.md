# BLUE-L1-008: Sole Trader vs. Partnership vs. Limited Company

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish a sole trader, a partnership, and a limited company.
2. Identify the key liability difference between these structures.
3. Recognise that structure names and rules vary by jurisdiction.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Business structure names and rules vary significantly by country; this lesson describes common UK terminology, and equivalents differ elsewhere (for example, an LLC in the United States, or a Pty Ltd in Australia).

A **sole trader** is one person running a business, with no legal separation between the owner and the business — the owner is personally liable for all debts. A **partnership** is similar but involves two or more people sharing ownership and liability. A **limited company** is a separate legal entity from its owners, meaning the owners' personal assets are generally protected if the business runs into debt, though this protection can have exceptions.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Priya runs her graphic design work as a sole trader. When the business takes on an unpaid invoice dispute that turns into debt, her personal savings and assets are, in principle, exposed to cover it, since there's no legal separation between her and the business. Had she structured it as a limited company, her personal assets would generally be protected, with the company's own assets bearing that risk instead.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This defines the sole trader structure specifically.
2. This defines the partnership structure specifically.
3. Limited companies generally do provide this separation, with some exceptions.
4. This is the central distinction driving structure choice for many businesses.

## Key Takeaways
- Sole trader: one owner, no legal separation, full personal liability.
- Partnership: multiple owners, shared liability.
- Limited company: separate legal entity, generally protecting personal assets — structure names vary by country.
