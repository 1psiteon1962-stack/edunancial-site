# BLUE-L1-009: Business Entities — Liability and Structure

## Learning Objectives
1. Define what a business entity is.
2. Explain the general concept of limited liability.
3. Identify why choosing a structure is a meaningful early decision.

## Core Content
A business entity is the legal structure under which a business operates, determining how it's taxed, how liability is handled, and how it's regulated. *VERIFIED FACT:* Limited liability means the owners' personal assets are generally separate from the business's debts and legal obligations — a key reason many growing businesses choose a limited company structure over operating as a sole trader.

*ASSUMPTION:* Choosing a structure early matters because changing it later can involve legal and administrative cost and complexity — though it's rarely impossible, getting it broadly right from the outset avoids unnecessary friction.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two friends start a small consultancy. They choose to register as a limited company from day one, even though it involves more paperwork than simply operating informally, because they anticipate hiring staff and taking on contracts where liability protection matters. This upfront structural choice shapes how the business is taxed and how it's protected from that point forward.
