# BLUE-L1-010: What Is Equity in a Business?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define business equity in plain terms.
2. Distinguish equity from revenue and profit.
3. Explain how equity can change over time.

## Core Content
Equity in a business represents the owner's stake in the company — essentially what would be left over if all assets were sold and all debts paid off. *VERIFIED FACT:* Equity = Assets − Liabilities.

*ASSUMPTION:* Equity generally grows when a business retains profits (rather than distributing them all to owners) or when owners inject additional capital, and can shrink when the business takes on losses or debt exceeding its assets.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A small business has $80,000 in assets (equipment, stock, cash) and $30,000 in outstanding debts. Equity = $80,000 − $30,000 = $50,000. If the business has a strong year and retains $10,000 in profit rather than paying it all out, equity grows to $60,000 — the owner's stake has increased even without selling anything.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is the formula for business equity?
2. What does equity represent, in plain terms?
3. True/False: Equity is the same thing as revenue.
4. If a business has $100,000 in assets and $40,000 in liabilities, what is its equity?

## Answer Key
1. Assets − Liabilities   2. The owner's stake in the business   3. False   4. $60,000

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the standard equity calculation.
2. This is the core meaning of equity in a business context.
3. Revenue is money coming in from sales; equity is the owner's overall stake in the business.
4. $100,000 − $40,000 = $60,000.

## Key Takeaways
- Equity = Assets − Liabilities, representing the owner's stake in the business.
- Retained profit and additional capital investment generally grow equity.
- Equity is distinct from revenue and profit — it reflects overall ownership value, not income flow.
