# BLUE-L1-011: Startup Costs — What to Expect

## Learning Objectives
1. Define startup costs.
2. Identify common categories of startup costs.
3. Explain why underestimating startup costs is a common early mistake.

## Core Content
Startup costs are the one-off expenses incurred to get a business up and running before it opens or launches. *VERIFIED FACT:* Common categories include equipment, initial stock, legal and registration fees, initial marketing, deposits (for premises or utilities), and a cash buffer for the early months before revenue stabilises.

*ASSUMPTION:* Underestimating startup costs is a common early mistake, often because new owners focus on obvious costs like equipment while overlooking smaller recurring costs that add up before meaningful revenue arrives.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* When budgeting to open a small salon, Aisha accounts for equipment and initial stock, but initially forgets to budget for the months before the client base builds up — rent, wages, and utilities that must be paid regardless of how few clients walk in during the first few months. Adding a cash buffer for that ramp-up period turns out to be as important as the equipment itself.

## Practice Quiz
1. What are startup costs?
2. Name two common categories of startup costs.
3. True/False: Startup costs only include one-off purchases like equipment.
4. Why is underestimating startup costs a common mistake?

## Answer Key
1. One-off expenses to get a business running before it launches   2. Equipment, initial stock (among others)   3. False   4. New owners often overlook smaller recurring costs during the early ramp-up period

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of startup costs.
2. These are among the standard startup cost categories.
3. Startup costs also include a cash buffer for early operating expenses before revenue stabilises.
4. Obvious costs get budgeted; the cumulative cost of surviving the early months is often overlooked.

## Key Takeaways
- Startup costs are the one-off expenses needed before a business can launch.
- Common categories include equipment, stock, legal fees, and an early cash buffer.
- A cash buffer for the ramp-up period is as important as the obvious upfront purchases.
