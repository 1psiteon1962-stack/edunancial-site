# BLUE-L1-012: Working Capital Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define working capital.
2. Calculate simple working capital.
3. Explain why positive working capital matters for day-to-day operations.

## Core Content
Working capital is the money a business has available to cover its short-term obligations and day-to-day operations. *VERIFIED FACT:* Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities, where "current" generally means due or convertible to cash within a year.

*ASSUMPTION:* Positive working capital generally means a business can comfortably meet its near-term obligations, while negative working capital can signal a cash crunch is approaching, even if the business is profitable on paper.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A business has $40,000 in current assets (cash, stock, money owed by customers) and $25,000 in current liabilities (bills due, short-term loans). Working Capital = $40,000 − $25,000 = $15,000, suggesting the business can cover its near-term obligations comfortably. If liabilities instead exceeded assets, the business could be profitable overall yet still struggle to pay its bills on time.

## Key Takeaways

- Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.
- Negative working capital can signal a cash crunch even in an otherwise profitable business.
