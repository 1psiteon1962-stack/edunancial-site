# BLUE-L1-014: Customer Acquisition Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define customer acquisition.
2. Identify common channels through which businesses find customers.
3. Explain why acquiring a customer generally has a cost.

## Core Content
Customer acquisition is the process of gaining new customers for a business. *VERIFIED FACT:* Common acquisition channels include word of mouth, advertising, social media, search engines, referrals, and direct outreach — most businesses use a mix rather than relying on a single channel.

*ASSUMPTION:* Acquiring a customer generally costs something, whether that's money spent on advertising, time spent on outreach, or discounts offered to attract first-time buyers — this is commonly referred to as customer acquisition cost, and understanding it matters for pricing and profitability.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A new bakery spends $200 on local social media adverts over a month and gains 40 new customers from those adverts. Its customer acquisition cost from that channel is $200 ÷ 40 = $5 per customer. If the average customer spends $15 on their first visit, the bakery is still spending a meaningful share of that first sale just to acquire the customer, before any cost of ingredients or overheads.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is customer acquisition?
2. Name two common acquisition channels.
3. True/False: Acquiring a customer is generally free.
4. If a business spends $500 on adverts and gains 100 customers from them, what is the acquisition cost per customer?

## Answer Key
1. The process of gaining new customers   2. Word of mouth, advertising (among others)   3. False   4. $5

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of customer acquisition.
2. These are commonly cited acquisition channels.
3. Acquisition generally carries some cost, whether monetary or in time and effort.
4. $500 ÷ 100 = $5 per customer.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer acquisition is the process of gaining new customers through various channels.
- Most businesses use a mix of channels rather than relying on just one.
- Acquiring a customer generally carries a cost, which matters for pricing and profitability.
