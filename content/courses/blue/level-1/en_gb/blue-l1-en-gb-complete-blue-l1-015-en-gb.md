# BLUE-L1-015: Customer Retention Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define customer retention.
2. Explain why retaining customers is generally less costly than acquiring new ones.
3. Identify common ways businesses encourage repeat custom.

## Core Content
Customer retention is the ability of a business to keep customers buying from it over time, rather than making a single purchase and never returning. *ESTIMATE:* Acquiring a new customer is commonly cited as costing significantly more than retaining an existing one, though the exact ratio varies widely by industry and business model.

*ASSUMPTION:* Common retention approaches include good customer service, loyalty programmes, consistent quality, and staying in touch through channels like email — the specific mix depends on the type of business and its customers.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A hairdresser could spend money on adverts to attract a new client for a single visit, or focus on quality and friendliness so that same client books a follow-up appointment and refers friends. The second path generally costs less over time and builds a more stable base of repeat custom, rather than requiring constant spending just to keep the appointment book full.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is customer retention?
2. Is acquiring a new customer generally more or less costly than retaining an existing one?
3. Name two common retention approaches.
4. True/False: Retention and acquisition are unrelated to each other in practice.

## Answer Key
1. The ability to keep customers buying over time   2. Generally more costly   3. Good customer service, loyalty programmes (among others)   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of retention.
2. This is a commonly cited general pattern, though the exact ratio varies by industry.
3. These are commonly cited retention approaches.
4. A business with strong retention can rely less heavily on constant new acquisition to sustain revenue.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer retention keeps existing customers buying over time.
- Retaining a customer is generally less costly than acquiring a new one.
- Service quality, loyalty programmes, and consistent communication commonly support retention.
