# BLUE-L1-016: Pricing Strategy — An Introduction

## Learning Objectives
1. Define pricing strategy.
2. Identify the three broad approaches to pricing.
3. Explain why price affects more than just revenue.

## Core Content
Pricing strategy is how a business decides what to charge for its product or service. *VERIFIED FACT:* Three broad approaches are commonly used: cost-based pricing (starting from what it costs to produce, then adding a margin), value-based pricing (starting from what the customer believes the offering is worth), and competitive pricing (setting price relative to what competitors charge).

*ASSUMPTION:* Price affects more than revenue alone — it also shapes how customers perceive quality, who the business attracts as customers, and how it's positioned relative to competitors, so pricing decisions are rarely just a matter of arithmetic.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two coffee shops sell nearly identical coffee. One prices low to compete on affordability and attract high volume; the other prices high, investing in décor and service to position itself as a premium experience. Neither price is "wrong" — each reflects a different strategy for who the business wants as its customer and how it wants to be perceived.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is pricing strategy?
2. Name the three broad pricing approaches.
3. True/False: Price only affects revenue, not customer perception.
4. What can two businesses selling a similar product be doing differently through their pricing?

## Answer Key
1. How a business decides what to charge   2. Cost-based, value-based, competitive   3. False   4. Positioning themselves differently and attracting different types of customers

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of pricing strategy.
2. These are the three commonly cited broad pricing approaches.
3. Price also shapes perception of quality and market positioning.
4. Different pricing reflects different strategic choices about positioning and target customer.

## Key Takeaways
- Pricing strategy determines what a business charges and why.
- Cost-based, value-based, and competitive pricing are the three broad approaches.
- Price shapes customer perception and market positioning, not just revenue.
