# BLUE-L1-018: Value-Based Pricing

## Learning Objectives
1. Define value-based pricing.
2. Distinguish value-based pricing from cost-plus pricing.
3. Identify why value-based pricing can be harder to apply.

## Core Content
Value-based pricing sets price according to how much the customer believes the product or service is worth to them, rather than starting from the cost to produce it. *ASSUMPTION:* This approach generally requires a strong understanding of the customer — their alternatives, their budget, and how much value they place on the specific problem being solved — which can make it harder to apply than cost-plus pricing.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Value-based pricing can result in prices well above production cost when customers place high value on an outcome, such as speed, exclusivity, or expertise, and well below what cost-plus might suggest when the market simply won't bear a higher price.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A specialist consultant who can solve a client's costly problem in one day might charge $5,000 for that day, even though their actual costs (time, minor materials) are a small fraction of that. The price reflects the value of the outcome to the client — avoiding a much larger loss — rather than the consultant's cost to deliver it.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is value-based pricing?
2. How does value-based pricing differ from cost-plus pricing?
3. True/False: Value-based pricing always results in lower prices than cost-plus pricing.
4. Why can value-based pricing be harder to apply than cost-plus pricing?

## Answer Key
1. Setting price according to what the customer believes the offering is worth   2. Value-based starts from customer perception; cost-plus starts from production cost   3. False   4. It requires a strong understanding of the customer's alternatives and how much value they place on the outcome

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of value-based pricing.
2. This is the key distinction between the two pricing approaches.
3. Value-based pricing can result in prices higher or lower than cost-plus, depending on perceived value.
4. Estimating customer-perceived value is generally harder than calculating internal costs.

## Key Takeaways
- Value-based pricing sets price according to customer-perceived worth, not production cost.
- It can result in prices well above or below what cost-plus pricing would suggest.
- Applying it well requires strong understanding of the customer and their alternatives.
