# BLUE-L1-019: Competitive Pricing

## Learning Objectives
1. Define competitive pricing.
2. Identify the general reasoning behind pricing relative to competitors.
3. Recognise a risk of relying solely on competitive pricing.

## Core Content
Competitive pricing means setting a price primarily by reference to what competitors charge for similar products or services, rather than starting from internal cost or customer-perceived value alone. *ASSUMPTION:* This approach is commonly used in markets where products are similar across providers, and customers can easily compare prices, making relative price a significant factor in the purchase decision.

*VERIFIED FACT:* A commonly cited risk of competitive pricing is that it can lead to a race to the lowest price, squeezing margins across an entire market if businesses compete primarily on price rather than differentiating on quality, service, or brand.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Several petrol stations on the same road generally price within a few cents of each other, since customers can easily see and compare prices while driving past. A station that prices significantly higher without offering something different — like a shop, car wash, or better location — would likely lose customers to nearby competitors.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is competitive pricing?
2. In what kind of market is competitive pricing commonly used?
3. True/False: Competitive pricing carries no risk to profit margins.
4. What can help a business avoid a pure price race under competitive pricing?

## Answer Key
1. Setting price primarily by reference to competitors' prices   2. Markets with similar products where customers easily compare prices   3. False   4. Differentiating on quality, service, or brand rather than competing purely on price

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of competitive pricing.
2. This describes the market conditions where competitive pricing is commonly applied.
3. Competing primarily on price can squeeze margins across an entire market.
4. Differentiation reduces the pressure to compete purely on price.

## Key Takeaways
- Competitive pricing sets price mainly by reference to what competitors charge.
- It's commonly used where products are similar and prices are easy to compare.
- Relying on it alone risks a margin-squeezing race to the lowest price.
