# BLUE-L1-020: Cash Flow vs. Profit

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish cash flow from profit.
2. Explain why a profitable business can still run out of cash.
3. Identify why cash flow matters day to day.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Profit is revenue minus expenses over a period, measured on paper according to accounting rules. Cash flow is the actual movement of money in and out of the business — what's in the bank account right now, and when it arrives or leaves.

*ASSUMPTION:* A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash if, for example, customers are slow to pay their invoices, or a large amount of cash is tied up in inventory — this mismatch between recorded profit and actual available cash is a commonly cited reason otherwise sound businesses fail.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A consultancy completes $50,000 of work in a month and books it as revenue and profit. But if clients don't pay their invoices for 60 days, the business might not have enough cash on hand to cover payroll and rent this month, despite being profitable on paper for the work already delivered.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is profit?
2. What is cash flow?
3. True/False: A profitable business can never run out of cash.
4. What commonly causes a mismatch between profit and available cash?

## Answer Key
1. Revenue minus expenses over a period, on paper   2. The actual movement of money in and out of the business   3. False   4. Slow customer payments or cash tied up in inventory (among other causes)

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental accounting definition of profit.
2. This is the fundamental definition of cash flow.
3. Profitable businesses can and do run out of cash due to timing mismatches.
4. These are commonly cited causes of the profit-cash flow mismatch.

## Key Takeaways
- Profit is a paper measure of revenue minus expenses; cash flow is actual money movement.
- A business can be profitable and still run short of cash due to timing.
- Managing cash flow day to day is distinct from, and as important as, tracking profit.
