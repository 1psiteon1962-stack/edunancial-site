# BLUE-L1-021: Reading a Simple Profit and Loss Statement

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify the basic structure of a profit and loss statement.
2. Explain what each major line represents.
3. Calculate net profit from a simple example.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A profit and loss statement (also called an income statement) generally shows: revenue (total sales), minus cost of goods sold (direct costs of producing what was sold), equals gross profit; minus operating expenses (rent, salaries, marketing, and similar), equals net profit.

*ASSUMPTION:* This statement generally covers a specific period — a month, quarter, or year — and is one of the most commonly reviewed documents for understanding whether a business is fundamentally profitable over that period.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A small shop has $100,000 in revenue for the year. Cost of goods sold is $40,000, giving a gross profit of $60,000. Operating expenses (rent, wages, utilities) total $45,000. Net profit = $60,000 − $45,000 = $15,000. This figure tells the owner how much the business earned after all costs for the year.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does a profit and loss statement generally show?
2. What is gross profit?
3. If revenue is $80,000, cost of goods sold is $30,000, and operating expenses are $35,000, what is net profit?
4. What period does a profit and loss statement typically cover?

## Answer Key
1. Revenue, costs, and resulting profit over a period   2. Revenue minus cost of goods sold   3. $15,000   4. A specific period, such as a month, quarter, or year

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general structure of a profit and loss statement.
2. This is the standard definition of gross profit.
3. $80,000 − $30,000 = $50,000 gross profit; $50,000 − $35,000 = $15,000 net profit.
4. Profit and loss statements are period-specific, not a snapshot of a single moment.

## Key Takeaways
- A profit and loss statement shows revenue, costs, and resulting profit over a period.
- Gross profit subtracts cost of goods sold; net profit further subtracts operating expenses.
- It's a key document for understanding a business's fundamental profitability.
