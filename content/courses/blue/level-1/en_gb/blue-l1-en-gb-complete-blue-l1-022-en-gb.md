# BLUE-L1-022: Reading a Simple Balance Sheet

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify the basic structure of a balance sheet.
2. Explain the fundamental balance sheet equation.
3. Distinguish assets from liabilities.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A balance sheet shows a business's financial position at a single point in time, organised around the equation: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity. Assets are what the business owns (cash, inventory, equipment); liabilities are what the business owes (loans, unpaid bills); owner's equity is what's left over for the owner after liabilities are subtracted from assets.

*ASSUMPTION:* Unlike a profit and loss statement, which covers a period, a balance sheet is commonly described as a "snapshot" — it reflects the business's position on one specific date.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A business has $80,000 in assets (cash, inventory, equipment) and $30,000 in liabilities (a loan and unpaid bills). Owner's Equity = Assets − Liabilities = $80,000 − $30,000 = $50,000. This $50,000 represents what the owner would theoretically have left if the business sold everything and paid off everything it owed.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does a balance sheet show?
2. What is the fundamental balance sheet equation?
3. If assets are $100,000 and liabilities are $40,000, what is owner's equity?
4. True/False: A balance sheet covers a period of time, like a profit and loss statement.

## Answer Key
1. A business's financial position at a single point in time   2. Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity   3. $60,000   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental purpose of a balance sheet.
2. This is the foundational balance sheet equation.
3. $100,000 − $40,000 = $60,000 owner's equity.
4. A balance sheet is a snapshot at one point in time, not a period-based statement.

## Key Takeaways
- A balance sheet shows financial position at a single point in time.
- The core equation is Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity.
- Assets are what's owned; liabilities are what's owed; equity is what remains for the owner.
