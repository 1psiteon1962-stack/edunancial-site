# BLUE-L1-025: Basic Bookkeeping

## Learning Objectives
1. Define bookkeeping.
2. Distinguish bookkeeping from accounting.
3. Identify why consistent bookkeeping matters for a small business.

## Core Content
Bookkeeping is the ongoing process of recording a business's financial transactions — sales, purchases, payments, and receipts. *ASSUMPTION:* Bookkeeping is generally distinguished from accounting: bookkeeping is the day-to-day recording of transactions, while accounting involves interpreting those records to produce financial statements, analysis, and tax filings.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Consistent bookkeeping is commonly cited as essential for knowing whether a business is profitable, preparing accurate tax filings, and having reliable records if the business seeks financing or is ever audited.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A market trader who records every sale and expense in a simple spreadsheet each day can tell, at any point, roughly how the month is going. A trader who keeps receipts in a shoebox and sorts them out once a year at tax time has no reliable sense of their financial position until it's too late to act on it.

## Key Takeaways
- Bookkeeping is the ongoing recording of a business's financial transactions.
- It's generally distinct from accounting, which interprets those records.
- Consistent bookkeeping supports profitability tracking, tax preparation, and financing readiness.
