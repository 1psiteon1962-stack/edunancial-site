# BLUE-L1-026: Commercial Credit Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define commercial credit.
2. Distinguish commercial credit from personal credit.
3. Explain why commercial credit matters for a growing business.

## Core Content
Commercial credit refers to a business's ability to borrow money or obtain goods and services now, with an agreement to pay later, evaluated separately from the owner's personal credit. *ASSUMPTION:* For newer or smaller businesses, lenders commonly rely partly on the owner's personal credit history and guarantee alongside the business's own track record, since the business itself may not yet have an established credit history.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Commercial credit is generally used for purposes like purchasing inventory on payment terms, financing equipment, or securing a line of credit to smooth out cash flow — distinct from the day-to-day cash a business generates from sales.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A growing shop wants to buy a large batch of inventory ahead of a busy season but doesn't have the cash on hand yet. With good commercial credit, a supplier might extend 30-day payment terms, letting the shop receive and sell the inventory before the payment is due — effectively using credit to bridge the timing gap.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of commercial credit.
2. Newer businesses commonly lack their own credit track record, so personal credit fills the gap.
3. Commercial and personal credit are evaluated separately, even when related for newer businesses.
4. These are commonly cited uses of commercial credit.

## Key Takeaways
- Commercial credit is a business's ability to borrow or delay payment, evaluated separately from personal credit.
- Newer businesses commonly rely partly on the owner's personal credit until an independent history is built.
- Commercial credit commonly helps bridge timing gaps in cash flow and inventory purchasing.
