# BLUE-L1-027: Business Credit Scores

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a business credit score.
2. Distinguish it from a personal credit score.
3. Identify factors that commonly influence a business credit score.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Business credit scoring systems and the agencies that produce them vary by country; the general concept described here applies broadly, but specific scoring models and thresholds differ.

A business credit score is a rating that reflects a business's creditworthiness, based on its own payment history, financial stability, and credit usage — separate from the owner's personal credit score. *ASSUMPTION:* Factors that commonly influence it include payment history with suppliers and lenders, the age of the business, credit utilisation, and public financial filings, where applicable.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A business that consistently pays suppliers on time, maintains a reasonable level of debt relative to its size, and has been operating for several years will generally build a stronger business credit score than a brand-new business with no payment history at all — even if both are run by owners with excellent personal credit.

## Key Takeaways
- A business credit score reflects a business's own creditworthiness, separate from the owner's personal score.
- Payment history, business age, and credit usage commonly influence it.
- Scoring systems and specific models vary by country.
