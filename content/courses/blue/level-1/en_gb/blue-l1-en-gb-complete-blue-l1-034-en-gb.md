# BLUE-L1-034: Funding Sources Overview

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify the common broad categories of business funding.
2. Distinguish debt funding from equity funding.
3. Explain why the right funding source depends on the business's situation.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Common broad categories of business funding include personal savings (bootstrapping), debt (loans that must be repaid with interest), equity (selling a share of ownership in exchange for capital), and grants (funding that generally doesn't need to be repaid, often from government or specific organisations).

*ASSUMPTION:* The right funding source generally depends on factors like how much control the owner wants to retain, how much risk they're willing to take on personally, and the stage and needs of the business — there's no single "best" funding source across all situations.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* One founder takes out a small business loan to buy equipment, keeping full ownership but committing to fixed repayments regardless of how the business performs. Another founder sells 20% of their company to an investor, giving up some ownership and control but avoiding fixed repayment obligations if the business struggles early on. Neither choice is universally better — each trades control for risk differently.

## Practice Quiz
1. Name the common broad categories of business funding.
2. What is the key difference between debt and equity funding?
3. True/False: There is one universally best funding source for every business.
4. What does a founder generally give up when raising equity funding?

## Answer Key
1. Personal savings, debt, equity, grants   2. Debt must be repaid with interest; equity trades ownership for capital   3. False   4. Some ownership and control

## Answers and Explanations
1. These are the commonly cited broad categories of business funding.
2. This is the fundamental distinction between debt and equity.
3. The right funding source depends on the business's specific situation and the owner's priorities.
4. Equity funding generally involves giving up a share of ownership and some control.

## Key Takeaways
- Common funding categories include personal savings, debt, equity, and grants.
- Debt requires repayment with interest; equity trades ownership for capital.
- The right funding source depends on the owner's priorities around control, risk, and business stage.
