# BLUE-L1-035: Bootstrapping a Business

## Learning Objectives
1. Define bootstrapping.
2. Identify a key advantage and a key limitation of bootstrapping.
3. Recognise common bootstrapping tactics.

## Core Content
Bootstrapping means funding and growing a business using personal savings and the business's own revenue, rather than outside debt or investment. *ASSUMPTION:* A commonly cited advantage of bootstrapping is retaining full ownership and control, since no outside party holds equity or a claim on repayment; a commonly cited limitation is that growth is generally slower, constrained by however much cash the business itself generates.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A founder starts a small online shop using $2,000 of personal savings, reinvesting every bit of early profit back into more inventory rather than taking outside funding. Growth is gradual, tied directly to how much the business itself earns, but the founder retains complete ownership and answers to no investors or lenders.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is bootstrapping?
2. Name a commonly cited advantage of bootstrapping.
3. Name a commonly cited limitation of bootstrapping.
4. True/False: Bootstrapping involves taking on outside debt or investment.

## Answer Key
1. Funding a business through personal savings and its own revenue   2. Retaining full ownership and control   3. Generally slower growth, limited by available cash   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of bootstrapping.
2. Full ownership retention is a commonly cited bootstrapping advantage.
3. Slower growth is a commonly cited bootstrapping limitation.
4. Bootstrapping specifically avoids outside debt and investment.

## Key Takeaways
- Bootstrapping funds a business through personal savings and its own revenue.
- It commonly offers full ownership retention but slower growth.
- It's one of several funding approaches, each with different trade-offs.
