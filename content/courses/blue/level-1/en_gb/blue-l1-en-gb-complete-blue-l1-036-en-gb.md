# BLUE-L1-036: Small Business Loans

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a small business loan.
2. Identify common factors lenders consider.
3. Explain what a personal guarantee generally means.

## Core Content
A small business loan is borrowed capital that a business agrees to repay over time, generally with interest. *ASSUMPTION:* Lenders commonly consider factors such as the business's credit history (or the owner's, for newer businesses), revenue and cash flow, time in operation, and the purpose of the loan.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Many small business loans, particularly for newer businesses, require a personal guarantee — meaning the owner agrees to be personally responsible for repaying the loan if the business itself cannot, which can put personal assets at risk even for a limited company structure.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A business applies for a loan to buy new equipment. The lender reviews the business's two years of revenue history and cash flow, but because the business is still relatively young, also requires the owner to sign a personal guarantee. If the business later fails and can't repay, the lender can pursue the owner's personal assets to recover the debt, despite the business being a limited company.

## Key Takeaways
- A small business loan is borrowed capital repaid over time, generally with interest.
- Lenders commonly weigh credit history, cash flow, time in operation, and loan purpose.
- A personal guarantee can put personal assets at risk, overriding some liability protection.
