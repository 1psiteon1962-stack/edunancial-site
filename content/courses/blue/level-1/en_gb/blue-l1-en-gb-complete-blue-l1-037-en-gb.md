# BLUE-L1-037: Investors — Equity vs. Debt Financing

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish equity financing from debt financing in the context of investors.
2. Explain what an investor generally receives in exchange for equity.
3. Identify a key trade-off of taking on equity investors.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Equity financing involves selling a share of ownership in the business in exchange for capital — the investor becomes a part-owner and generally shares in future profits or gains, but the business owes no fixed repayment. Debt financing involves borrowing capital that must be repaid, generally with interest, regardless of how the business performs.

*ASSUMPTION:* A key trade-off of equity financing is that the founder gives up some ownership and, often, some decision-making control — in exchange for capital that doesn't require fixed repayments, which can matter greatly for a business without steady early revenue.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A founder raises $100,000 by selling 25% of their company to an investor. The business owes no fixed monthly repayment on that money, but the investor now owns a quarter of the company and may expect a say in major decisions, and a share of any future profits or sale proceeds.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does an investor generally receive in exchange for equity financing?
2. How does debt financing differ from equity financing?
3. True/False: Equity financing requires fixed monthly repayments regardless of performance.
4. What does a founder generally give up in exchange for equity financing?

## Answer Key
1. A share of ownership in the business   2. Debt must be repaid with interest regardless of performance; equity doesn't require fixed repayment   3. False   4. Some ownership and, often, some decision-making control

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental nature of equity financing.
2. This is the key distinction between debt and equity.
3. Equity financing specifically avoids fixed repayment obligations.
4. This is the commonly cited trade-off founders accept for equity capital.

## Key Takeaways
- Equity financing exchanges ownership for capital; debt financing requires repayment with interest.
- Equity investors generally share in future profits or control, without requiring fixed repayments.
- The founder's trade-off with equity is giving up some ownership and control.
