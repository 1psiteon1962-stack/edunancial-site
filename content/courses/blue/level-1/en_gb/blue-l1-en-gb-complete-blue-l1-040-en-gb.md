# BLUE-L1-040: Branding Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a brand in business terms.
2. Distinguish a brand from a logo.
3. Explain why consistency matters in branding.

## Core Content
*ASSUMPTION:* A brand is generally the overall perception customers hold of a business — how it looks, sounds, and feels, and what it stands for — while a logo is just one visual element within that broader brand, not the brand itself.

*OPINION:* Many marketing educators commonly emphasise that brand consistency across visuals, tone of voice, and customer experience helps a business become recognisable and trusted over time, whereas an inconsistent presentation can make a business feel less credible, even if the product itself is strong.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two shops sell similar products. One consistently uses the same colours, tone, and style across its shopfront, packaging, and social media, so customers immediately recognise it wherever they see it. The other changes its look and messaging frequently, so customers struggle to form a clear, consistent impression — even if both shops sell equally good products.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a brand, in business terms?
2. How does a brand differ from a logo?
3. True/False: A logo and a brand are the same thing.
4. Why does consistency matter in branding?

## Answer Key
1. The overall perception customers hold of a business   2. A logo is one visual element within the broader brand   3. False   4. It helps a business become recognisable and trusted over time

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general definition of a brand.
2. This is the key distinction between a brand and a logo.
3. A logo is a component of a brand, not the whole of it.
4. Consistency builds recognition and trust, according to commonly cited marketing guidance.

## Key Takeaways
- A brand is the overall perception customers hold of a business, not just its logo.
- A logo is one visual element within a much broader brand identity.
- Consistency across visuals, tone, and experience commonly builds recognition and trust.
