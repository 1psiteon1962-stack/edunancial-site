# BLUE-L1-041: Marketing Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define marketing in business terms.
2. Distinguish marketing from sales.
3. Identify commonly cited marketing channels.

## Core Content
*ASSUMPTION:* Marketing is generally the set of activities a business uses to build awareness, generate interest, and attract potential customers — distinct from sales, which is the process of actually converting that interest into a completed purchase.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Commonly cited marketing channels include social media, content such as blogs or videos, email, search engine advertising, and traditional media like print or radio — the right mix generally depends on where a business's specific customers spend their attention.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A local bakery posts photos of its products on social media, builds an email list of regular customers, and occasionally advertises in a local newsletter — these are marketing activities, building awareness and interest. When a customer walks in and a staff member helps them choose and pay for a cake, that's sales — converting the interest marketing generated into an actual purchase.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is marketing, in business terms?
2. How does marketing differ from sales?
3. Name a commonly cited marketing channel.
4. True/False: Marketing and sales are the same activity.

## Answer Key
1. Activities that build awareness, generate interest, and attract potential customers   2. Marketing builds interest; sales converts that interest into a completed purchase   3. Social media, email, or search engine advertising (among others)   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general definition of marketing.
2. This is the key distinction between marketing and sales.
3. These are commonly cited marketing channels.
4. Marketing and sales serve distinct, complementary roles.

## Key Takeaways
- Marketing builds awareness and interest; sales converts that interest into a purchase.
- Common marketing channels include social media, content, email, and advertising.
- The right channel mix depends on where a business's specific customers spend their attention.
