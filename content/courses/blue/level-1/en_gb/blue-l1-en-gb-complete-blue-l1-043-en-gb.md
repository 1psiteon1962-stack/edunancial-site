# BLUE-L1-043: Customer Service Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define customer service in business terms.
2. Explain why customer service affects retention and reputation.
3. Identify a commonly cited principle of good customer service.

## Core Content
Customer service is how a business supports and interacts with customers before, during, and after a purchase. *ASSUMPTION:* Good customer service is commonly linked to customer retention and word-of-mouth reputation — customers who feel well treated are more likely to return and recommend the business, while poor service can drive customers away even when the underlying product is good.

*OPINION:* A commonly cited principle of good customer service is resolving problems promptly and treating the customer's concern as legitimate, rather than becoming defensive — how a business handles a complaint often shapes the customer relationship more than the original problem itself.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A customer receives a faulty product. One business responds quickly, apologises, and replaces it without argument — the customer, despite the initial inconvenience, comes away with a positive impression and continues to shop there. Another business argues with the customer over the fault, delaying resolution — even if eventually resolved, the experience damages the relationship and the customer may not return.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer service covers all support and interaction around a purchase.
- Good service is commonly linked to stronger retention and word-of-mouth reputation.
- Prompt, respectful problem resolution is a commonly cited principle of good service.
