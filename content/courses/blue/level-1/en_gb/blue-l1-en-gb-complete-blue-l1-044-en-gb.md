# BLUE-L1-044: Inventory Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define inventory in business terms.
2. Explain why holding too much or too little inventory both carry risk.
3. Identify a commonly cited inventory management approach.

## Core Content
Inventory is the stock of goods a business holds for sale or for use in producing what it sells. *ASSUMPTION:* Holding too much inventory generally ties up cash and can lead to waste, damage, or obsolescence, while holding too little can lead to lost sales when a business can't fulfil customer demand — effective inventory management generally aims to balance these two risks.

*VERIFIED FACT:* A commonly cited approach is "just-in-time" inventory management, where a business orders stock to arrive close to when it's needed, reducing the amount of cash tied up in stock sitting unsold — though this approach carries its own risk if supply is disrupted.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A clothing shop that orders a full season's stock all at once ties up a large amount of cash in inventory, some of which may not sell before styles change. A shop that orders smaller batches more frequently, closer to when items are needed, keeps less cash tied up — but risks running short if a particular item suddenly becomes popular and reorder lead times are long.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is inventory, in business terms?
2. What risk does holding too much inventory generally carry?
3. What risk does holding too little inventory generally carry?
4. What is "just-in-time" inventory management?

## Answer Key
1. The stock of goods a business holds for sale or production   2. Tied-up cash, waste, damage, or obsolescence   3. Lost sales when demand can't be met   4. Ordering stock to arrive close to when it's needed, reducing cash tied up in stock

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general definition of inventory.
2. Excess inventory ties up cash and carries waste/obsolescence risk.
3. Insufficient inventory risks lost sales from unmet demand.
4. This is the commonly cited definition of just-in-time inventory management.

## Key Takeaways
- Inventory is the stock a business holds for sale or production.
- Both excess and insufficient inventory carry distinct business risks.
- Just-in-time management is a commonly cited approach to balancing these risks.
