# BLUE-L1-046: Scaling a Business

## Learning Objectives
1. Define scaling in business terms.
2. Distinguish scaling from simply growing.
3. Identify a commonly cited requirement for scaling successfully.

## Core Content
*ASSUMPTION:* Scaling generally refers to growing revenue significantly faster than costs increase, often by building systems, processes, or products that can serve many more customers without a proportional increase in effort — this is generally distinguished from simple growth, where revenue and costs might increase roughly together.

*OPINION:* Many business educators commonly cite documented systems and processes as a requirement for successful scaling — a business entirely dependent on one person's direct involvement in every transaction generally struggles to scale, since that person's time is a hard limit on growth.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A consultant who personally delivers every hour of service can grow their income somewhat, but is capped by the hours in a day — this is growth, not scaling. A consultant who builds a course, hires and trains other consultants using documented methods, or creates software that delivers similar value can serve far more customers without their personal time being the limiting factor — this is scaling.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does scaling generally refer to?
2. How does scaling differ from simple growth?
3. True/False: A business entirely dependent on one person's direct involvement generally scales easily.
4. What do many business educators commonly cite as a requirement for scaling?

## Answer Key
1. Growing revenue significantly faster than costs, often via systems serving many more customers   2. Scaling grows revenue disproportionately to effort; growth may increase revenue and costs together   3. False   4. Documented systems and processes

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general definition of scaling.
2. This is the key distinction between scaling and simple growth.
3. Dependence on one person's time generally limits scaling potential.
4. This is commonly cited advice from business educators.

## Key Takeaways
- Scaling means growing revenue significantly faster than costs, often via systems.
- It's distinct from simple growth, where costs may rise alongside revenue.
- Documented systems and processes are commonly cited as necessary for scaling beyond one person's capacity.
