# BLUE-L1-048: Profit Reinvestment vs. Owner Pay

## Learning Objectives
1. Define profit reinvestment.
2. Explain the general trade-off between reinvesting profit and paying the owner.
3. Recognise that the right balance depends on the business's stage and goals.

## Core Content
Profit reinvestment means using a business's earnings to fund further growth — such as new equipment, inventory, marketing, or hiring — rather than distributing that money to the owner. *ASSUMPTION:* The general trade-off is that reinvesting profit can accelerate future growth, but reduces what the owner takes home now, while paying the owner more now can reduce the pace of reinvestment-driven growth.

*OPINION:* Many business advisors commonly suggest that early-stage businesses reinvest a larger share of profit to build momentum, while more established businesses might shift toward distributing a larger share to the owner — though the right balance genuinely depends on the specific business's goals and circumstances.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A new business owner takes a modest salary and reinvests most remaining profit into marketing and additional stock, aiming to grow the business faster in its early years. A more established owner of the same type of business, having already built a stable customer base, might instead take a larger share of profit as personal income, since further reinvestment offers diminishing returns at that stage.

## Key Takeaways
- Profit reinvestment funds further growth instead of paying the owner directly.
- The trade-off is between accelerating growth and current owner income.
- The right balance depends on the business's specific stage and goals, not a fixed rule.
