# BLUE-L1-050: Building Your First Business Plan

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify the core components of a simple business plan.
2. Explain why writing a plan clarifies thinking even for a small business.
3. Apply the concepts from this level to outline a basic plan.

## Core Content
*ASSUMPTION:* A simple business plan generally covers: what the business does and for whom, how it makes money (its pricing and revenue model), what it will cost to start and run, how it will find and keep customers, and what the founder expects to happen financially over the first year or so.

*OPINION:* Many business educators commonly argue that the value of writing a business plan lies less in the finished document and more in the thinking it forces — working through pricing, costs, and customer acquisition on paper commonly surfaces gaps and weak assumptions before they cost real money.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An aspiring bakery owner, working through a simple plan, realises while calculating costs that their planned pricing (set using cost-plus pricing from Lesson 017) barely covers ingredients once rent and staff time are properly accounted for. Catching this on paper, before signing a lease, lets them adjust pricing or reconsider the concept — a far cheaper lesson than discovering it after opening.

## Practice Quiz
1. Name a core component of a simple business plan.
2. Where do many business educators commonly say the real value of a business plan lies?
3. True/False: A business plan's main value is impressing outside readers.
4. What did the aspiring bakery owner discover by working through their plan on paper?

## Answer Key
1. What the business does, how it makes money, its costs, or its customer strategy   2. In the thinking it forces, not just the finished document   3. False   4. Their planned pricing barely covered costs once rent and staff time were included

## Answers and Explanations
1. These are commonly cited core components of a simple business plan.
2. This reflects commonly cited advice from business educators.
3. The process of planning is commonly valued as much as, or more than, the document itself.
4. This illustrates how planning can surface a costly problem before it happens in reality.

## Key Takeaways
- A simple business plan covers the offering, revenue model, costs, and customer strategy.
- The process of planning commonly surfaces gaps and weak assumptions before they cost real money.
- Concepts from earlier lessons — pricing, costs, customers — come together directly in building a plan.
