## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain the difference between asset value and business (going concern) value
- Explain why a profitable business is typically worth more than the sum of its physical assets
- Explain why an unprofitable business might be worth close to only its asset value

## Core Content

It's tempting to think a business's value is simply the sum of what it owns: equipment, inventory, cash, property. This is called **asset value**, and it's a real, calculable figure — but for most operating businesses, it's not the primary driver of what a buyer would actually pay.

Here's the "why" a business is usually valued differently: a profitable, operating business (a "going concern") has earning power — the ability to generate ongoing profit into the future — that has value well beyond what its physical assets alone would fetch if simply sold off individually. A buyer is typically purchasing that future earning power, not primarily the physical equipment.

[VERIFIED FACT] This relationship can also run the other way: a business that isn't profitable, or has an uncertain future, may be valued close to its liquidation or asset value alone, since a buyer isn't willing to pay much for uncertain or negative future earning power — in some cases, an unprofitable business's assets might even be worth more sold individually than the business is worth as a continuing operation.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Business A owns $200,000 in equipment and inventory but generates strong, consistent annual profit. A buyer might reasonably pay significantly more than $200,000, because they're primarily purchasing the ongoing earning power the business demonstrates, not just the physical items. Business B owns the same $200,000 in equipment and inventory but has been losing money with no clear path to profitability. [ASSUMPTION] A buyer for Business B might only be willing to pay close to the $200,000 asset value, or even less after accounting for any liabilities, since the ongoing operations aren't demonstrating earning power worth paying an additional premium for.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is asset value, as defined in this lesson?
2. Why is a profitable business typically worth more than the sum of its physical assets?
3. Why might an unprofitable business be valued close to only its asset value?

## Answer Key

1. The value of what a business owns — equipment, inventory, cash, property — calculated independent of its ongoing operations.
2. Because a buyer is primarily purchasing the business's future earning power, which has value beyond the physical assets used to generate it.
3. Because uncertain or negative future earning power gives a buyer little reason to pay a premium above what the physical assets themselves would be worth.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition contrasted with going-concern value.
2. This is the central "why" explaining most business valuations.
3. This shows the concept working in the opposite direction, reinforcing that earning power — not asset ownership — is usually the primary driver.

## Key Takeaways
- Asset value reflects what a business owns; going-concern value reflects its earning power as an operating business.
- A profitable business is typically valued well above its asset value, since buyers are paying for future earning power.
- An unprofitable or uncertain business may be valued close to, or even below, its asset value alone.
