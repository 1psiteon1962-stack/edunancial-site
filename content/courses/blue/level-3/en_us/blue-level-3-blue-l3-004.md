## Core Content

When acquiring a business structured as a corporation or similar entity, there are generally two structural approaches: an **asset purchase**, where the buyer acquires specific assets (and sometimes specific liabilities) of the business directly, or a **stock purchase**, where the buyer acquires ownership shares of the company itself, taking on the entire entity — assets, liabilities, contracts, and history — as it exists.

Here's the "why" buyers and sellers often prefer opposite structures: in an asset purchase, the buyer can typically choose which specific assets and liabilities to acquire, generally avoiding unknown or unwanted liabilities that might exist within the company itself (potential undisclosed lawsuits, for example) — this is a major reason buyers often prefer this structure. In a stock purchase, the seller transfers the entire entity, including all its liabilities, whether known or unknown, which is why sellers often prefer this structure: it provides a cleaner, more complete exit from every aspect of the business, known and unknown.

[VERIFIED FACT] Tax treatment often also differs significantly between the two structures for both buyer and seller, and can be a major factor in which structure is ultimately negotiated — the specific tax consequences depend on jurisdiction-specific rules and should be evaluated with professional guidance for any actual transaction.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A buyer wants to acquire a manufacturing business but is concerned about the possibility of undisclosed environmental liabilities tied to the company's history. Structuring the deal as an asset purchase would let the buyer specifically acquire the equipment, inventory, and customer contracts he wants, without automatically inheriting any unknown liabilities tied to the company entity itself. The seller, however, might prefer a stock purchase specifically to achieve a complete, clean exit from the business, including any liabilities the buyer's asset-purchase structure would otherwise leave behind with the seller.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is the structural difference between an asset purchase and a stock purchase?
2. Why do buyers often prefer an asset purchase structure?
3. Why do sellers often prefer a stock purchase structure?

## Answer Key

1. An asset purchase acquires specific assets (and sometimes liabilities) directly; a stock purchase acquires ownership of the entire company entity, including all its assets, liabilities, contracts, and history.
2. Because it generally lets them choose which specific assets and liabilities to acquire, avoiding unknown or unwanted liabilities that might exist within the company entity itself.
3. Because it provides a cleaner, more complete exit from the entire business, including liabilities, rather than leaving unwanted liabilities behind with the seller.

## Key Takeaways
- An asset purchase acquires specific assets directly; a stock purchase acquires the entire company entity, including all liabilities.
- Buyers often prefer asset purchases to avoid unknown or unwanted liabilities.
- Sellers often prefer stock purchases for a cleaner, more complete exit, including all liabilities.
