## Core Content

This lesson closely parallels a concept covered in this curriculum's RED track regarding personal guarantees on real estate LLC loans. The same underlying logic applies here: lenders financing a small business acquisition or operating loan almost universally require a **personal guarantee** from the individual business owner or owners, even when the loan is technically made to a corporation or LLC.

Here's the "why" this requirement is so consistent for small businesses specifically: a newly acquired or smaller business often doesn't have an extensive independent financial track record or substantial assets of its own to reassure a lender. A personal guarantee lets the lender pursue the individual owner personally if the business defaults and can't repay the loan, providing the lender additional security beyond the business entity's own limited financial history.

[VERIFIED FACT] This means that forming a corporation or LLC for liability protection purposes, while still valuable for many other reasons, does not automatically shield an owner's personal assets from a loan that specifically includes a personal guarantee — the guarantee creates a direct, personal obligation separate from the entity's own liability.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An entrepreneur forms an LLC specifically to acquire a small business, expecting the LLC structure to protect her personal assets from any business liabilities. When she applies for financing to fund the acquisition, the lender requires her to personally guarantee the loan, given the business's limited independent track record under her new ownership. If the business later struggles and defaults on the loan, the lender can pursue her personally under the guarantee, despite the LLC structure — the liability protection she expected from the entity doesn't extend to this specific, personally guaranteed debt.
