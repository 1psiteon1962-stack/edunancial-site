## Core Content

The **cash conversion cycle** measures roughly how long it takes a business to convert cash spent on inventory and operations back into cash collected from customers — combining the time inventory sits before being sold, the time it takes to collect payment from customers after a sale, offset by the time the business itself takes to pay its own suppliers.

Here's the "why" this cycle length matters directly, connecting to Lesson 13's discussion of the profit-cash flow gap: the longer this cycle, the more cash a business needs on hand (or available through financing) to bridge the gap between paying for inventory and operations and actually collecting cash from customers. A business with a long cash conversion cycle needs a larger working capital cushion (Lesson 12) than an otherwise similar business with a short cycle, even if both are equally profitable on paper.

[VERIFIED FACT] Businesses can actively work to shorten their cash conversion cycle — collecting customer payments faster, managing inventory more efficiently, or negotiating longer payment terms with their own suppliers — each of which reduces the amount of cash tied up in the gap between spending and collecting.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A retail business buys inventory, holds it for an average of 60 days before selling it, and then collects payment from customers roughly 30 days after each sale — while paying its own suppliers within 20 days of receiving inventory. [ASSUMPTION] This results in a combined cash conversion cycle of roughly 70 days (60 days holding inventory plus 30 days collecting payment, minus the 20 days of supplier payment terms) — meaning the business needs enough cash or financing to cover its operations for about 70 days before the cash from a given inventory purchase cycle actually comes back in, regardless of how profitable each individual sale looks on paper.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does the cash conversion cycle measure?
2. Why does a longer cash conversion cycle increase a business's working capital needs?
3. Name one way a business could actively shorten its cash conversion cycle.

## Answer Key

1. Roughly how long it takes a business to convert cash spent on inventory and operations back into cash collected from customers.
2. Because a longer cycle means more time — and more cash — is needed to bridge the gap between paying for inventory/operations and actually collecting payment from customers.
3. Any one of: collecting customer payments faster, managing inventory more efficiently, or negotiating longer payment terms with suppliers.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition for the lesson.
2. This is the central "why" connecting cycle length directly to working capital needs.
3. This reinforces that the cycle length isn't fixed and can be actively managed.

## Key Takeaways
- The cash conversion cycle measures the time between spending cash on inventory/operations and collecting cash from customers.
- A longer cycle increases a business's working capital needs, since more cash must bridge that gap.
- Businesses can actively shorten this cycle through faster collections, more efficient inventory management, or better supplier payment terms.
