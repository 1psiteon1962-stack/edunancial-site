## Core Content

**Gross margin** is the percentage of revenue remaining after subtracting the direct cost of producing or delivering a product or service (cost of goods sold), before accounting for other operating expenses like marketing, rent, or administrative overhead.

Here's the "why" revenue alone can be a misleading headline figure: two businesses could each generate $1 million in annual revenue, but if one has a 70% gross margin and the other has a 20% gross margin, they have dramatically different amounts of money left over to cover other expenses and eventually generate profit — the higher-margin business retains far more of each revenue dollar before other costs are even considered.

[VERIFIED FACT] Gross margin also matters significantly for how well a business scales: a business with a high gross margin can generally add revenue with each additional sale contributing more toward covering fixed costs and eventual profit, while a low-margin business needs substantially higher sales volume to achieve the same dollar impact, since so much of each sale is consumed by direct costs.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Business A sells software with minimal direct delivery costs, achieving a 75% gross margin — for every $100 in revenue, $75 remains after direct costs to cover other expenses and profit. Business B sells physical products with substantial material and manufacturing costs, achieving only a 25% gross margin — for every $100 in revenue, only $25 remains after direct costs. [ASSUMPTION] Even if both businesses report the same total annual revenue, Business A has far more resources remaining to invest in growth, cover overhead, or generate profit, illustrating why revenue alone, without considering gross margin, can be a misleading measure of a business's underlying economic strength.

## Key Takeaways
- Gross margin measures the percentage of revenue remaining after direct production or delivery costs.
- Businesses with identical revenue can have very different underlying economics depending on their gross margin.
- Higher gross margins generally support easier, more profitable scaling than lower margins.
