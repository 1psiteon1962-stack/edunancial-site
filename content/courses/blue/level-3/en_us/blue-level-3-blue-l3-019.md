## Core Content

**Burn rate** is the rate at which a business (often one not yet profitable, such as a newer venture) spends its available cash, typically measured monthly. **Runway** is the amount of time remaining before that cash runs out entirely, given the current burn rate — calculated simply as available cash divided by monthly burn rate.

Here's the "why" tracking these figures precisely matters so much: runway tells a business owner or founder exactly how much time remains to either become profitable, secure additional funding, or otherwise change course, before the business runs out of cash entirely. [VERIFIED FACT] Because raising additional funding (through investors, loans, or other means) itself typically takes meaningful time — often months — many experienced founders and advisors recommend beginning that process well before runway is nearly exhausted, rather than waiting until a cash crisis is already underway, since a business fundraising from a position of urgency typically has much less negotiating leverage than one fundraising with a comfortable amount of runway still remaining.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business has $300,000 in available cash and is spending $50,000 per month more than it brings in (its burn rate) — giving it a runway of 6 months ($300,000 divided by $50,000). [ASSUMPTION] If securing additional funding typically takes 3 to 4 months from start to finish, waiting until only 2 months of runway remain to begin that process would leave the business in serious jeopardy — starting the funding process with a full 6 months of runway remaining, instead, gives it comfortable time to secure funding on more favorable terms rather than from a position of desperation.
