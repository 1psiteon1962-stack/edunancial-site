## Core Content

A **vesting schedule** determines when someone who has been granted equity (a founder, employee, or sometimes an advisor) actually earns full ownership of those shares over time, rather than receiving full ownership immediately upon the initial grant.

Here's the "why" this structure exists, connecting back to the cap table discussion in Lesson 22: without vesting, someone could receive a significant equity grant and then leave the company almost immediately, walking away with a meaningful ownership stake despite having contributed very little to the company's actual success. Vesting protects against this specific scenario by having ownership accrue gradually, typically over several years, tied to continued involvement with the company.

[VERIFIED FACT] A common vesting structure includes a **cliff** — often one year — during which no shares vest at all; if the person leaves before reaching the cliff, they receive no equity whatsoever, even though time has passed since the grant. After the cliff, remaining shares often vest gradually, sometimes monthly or quarterly, over the remaining vesting period (commonly totaling four years, though specific structures vary by company and agreement).

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A startup grants a new co-founder equity subject to a four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff. If that co-founder leaves the company after just eight months — before reaching the one-year cliff — she receives no equity at all under the vesting schedule, despite having been granted shares initially. [ASSUMPTION] If she had instead stayed for two full years before leaving, she would have vested (earned) roughly half of her total granted shares, reflecting her actual period of contribution, while the unvested remaining half would typically return to the company rather than staying with her.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does a vesting schedule determine?
2. Why does vesting protect a company if a founder or employee leaves early?
3. What is a "cliff" in a typical vesting schedule?

## Answer Key

1. When someone granted equity actually earns full ownership of those shares over time, rather than immediately upon the grant.
2. Because without vesting, someone could leave almost immediately after receiving a grant while still keeping a significant ownership stake, despite minimal actual contribution to the company.
3. A period, often one year, during which no shares vest at all — if the person leaves before reaching the cliff, they receive no equity, even though time has passed since the grant.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition for the lesson.
2. This is the central "why" explaining vesting's protective purpose.
3. This explains a specific, common feature within typical vesting structures.

## Key Takeaways
- Vesting schedules have equity accrue gradually over time, rather than being fully owned immediately upon grant.
- This protects a company from someone leaving early while still keeping a significant, largely unearned ownership stake.
- A common structure includes a one-year cliff followed by gradual vesting, often over a total four-year period.
