Title: Why Dilution Is the Price of Raising Outside Capital

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain what dilution means when a company raises new equity capital
- Explain why dilution reduces existing owners' percentage stake but not necessarily the value of their stake
- Explain why dilution is a natural, expected consequence of raising outside capital, not inherently a mistake

## Core Content

**Dilution** occurs when a company issues new shares — typically to raise capital from new investors — which reduces the percentage ownership of existing shareholders, since the total number of shares outstanding increases while each existing shareholder's number of shares stays the same.

Here's the "why" this reduction in percentage doesn't necessarily mean existing owners are worse off: if the capital raised is used effectively to grow the company's overall value, existing shareholders can end up owning a smaller percentage of a much larger, more valuable company — potentially resulting in a larger total value for their reduced stake than a larger percentage of a smaller, less capitalized company would have been worth.

[VERIFIED FACT] This connects directly back to Lesson 20's bootstrapping-versus-raising-capital discussion: dilution is the direct, quantifiable mechanism through which the "give up some ownership" side of that trade-off actually happens — it's an expected, natural consequence of raising outside equity capital, not evidence that something has gone wrong, though excessive or poorly negotiated dilution across multiple funding rounds is a legitimate concern worth managing carefully.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A founder owns 100% of her company when it's valued at $1 million. She raises capital from investors, issuing new shares that dilute her ownership down to 70% of the company. [ASSUMPTION] If that capital is used effectively and the company's value grows to $10 million as a result, her 70% stake ($7 million) is worth far more than her original 100% stake of the $1 million company ever was — illustrating why dilution, in this specific case, wasn't actually a loss for her despite her percentage ownership decreasing.

## Key Takeaways
- Dilution reduces existing shareholders' percentage ownership when new shares are issued to raise capital.
- A smaller percentage of a company that grows significantly in value can still result in greater total value than a larger percentage of a smaller company.
- Dilution is the direct mechanism behind the ownership trade-off of raising outside capital, and is expected, not inherently a mistake — though it should still be managed carefully across funding rounds.
