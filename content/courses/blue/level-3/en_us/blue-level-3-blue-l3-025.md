## Core Content

A **term sheet** in an equity fundraising context outlines the key proposed terms of an investment — valuation, amount being raised, type of shares being issued, investor rights, and other significant provisions — before the full, detailed legal investment agreements are drafted.

Here's the "why" this closely parallels Lesson 6's discussion of a Letter of Intent in a business acquisition: like an LOI, a term sheet is generally mostly non-binding on its substantive terms, allowing both the company and the investor to align on the major deal points and proceed to due diligence and detailed legal documentation, without either side being fully locked into the deal until final agreements are signed — while, similarly, certain provisions (like confidentiality or a limited exclusivity period) are commonly structured to be binding even within an otherwise non-binding term sheet.

[VERIFIED FACT] Common terms found in an equity term sheet include the company's valuation (often distinguishing "pre-money" and "post-money" valuation), the amount of capital being raised, the type of equity being issued (often preferred shares with specific rights), and various investor protections and governance rights — each a significant point of negotiation before the final legal documents are prepared.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A startup and a potential investor negotiate and sign a term sheet outlining a proposed investment amount, an agreed valuation, and the type of preferred shares to be issued, along with a binding 45-day exclusivity period. [ASSUMPTION] During that period, both sides work through legal due diligence and draft the full investment agreements. If a significant issue arises during that process, the non-binding substantive terms of the term sheet — valuation, amount, structure — can still be renegotiated or the deal can be walked away from, mirroring exactly the same non-binding flexibility described for an LOI in Lesson 6, applied here to an equity investment context instead of a business acquisition.
