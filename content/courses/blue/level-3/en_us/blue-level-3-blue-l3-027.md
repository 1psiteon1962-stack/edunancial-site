## Core Content

**Revenue-based financing (RBF)** provides capital to a business in exchange for a percentage of future revenue, typically paid until a predetermined multiple of the original investment has been repaid, rather than through fixed monthly payments (like traditional debt) or an ongoing equity ownership stake (like venture capital, Lesson 26).

Here's the "why" this is often described as a middle path between the two, connecting to Lesson 21's debt-versus-equity discussion: like debt, RBF has a defined total repayment amount (typically the investment amount times a set multiple) rather than an open-ended equity stake in the business's entire future value. But unlike traditional debt, the actual payment amount flexes with the business's revenue — during a slower revenue period, the payment is smaller; during a stronger period, it's larger — reducing the fixed monthly repayment risk pure debt financing carries.

[VERIFIED FACT] RBF is often called **non-dilutive** capital, since it doesn't require giving up any equity ownership in the business, unlike venture capital or other equity financing — this is a significant part of its appeal for founders wanting to raise growth capital without diluting their ownership stake (Lesson 24) or giving up governance control.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A growing business receives $200,000 in revenue-based financing, agreeing to repay a total of $260,000 (a 1.3x multiple) through a percentage of monthly revenue until that total is repaid. [ASSUMPTION] During a strong sales month, the business pays a larger amount toward that repayment total; during a slower month, the payment automatically shrinks along with revenue, reducing strain on cash flow compared to a fixed monthly debt payment that wouldn't adjust regardless of how sales performed. Throughout this process, the founder retains full ownership of the business, unlike an equity financing arrangement.

## Practice Quiz

1. How does revenue-based financing work?
2. Why is RBF often described as a middle path between debt and equity?

## Answer Key

1. A business receives capital in exchange for a percentage of future revenue, typically paid until a predetermined multiple of the original investment is repaid.
2. Because, like debt, it has a defined total repayment amount rather than an open-ended equity stake, but like a flexible arrangement, the actual payment amount adjusts with the business's revenue rather than being a fixed monthly obligation.
3. Because it doesn't require giving up any equity ownership in the business, unlike venture capital or other equity financing arrangements.

## Key Takeaways
- Revenue-based financing provides capital in exchange for a percentage of future revenue, up to a predetermined repayment multiple.
- It combines debt's defined repayment total with a flexible, revenue-linked payment structure, unlike a fixed monthly debt payment.
- RBF is non-dilutive, meaning it doesn't require giving up any equity ownership in the business.
