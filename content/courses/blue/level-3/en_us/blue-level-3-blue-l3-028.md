## Core Content

**Crowdfunding** generally involves raising smaller amounts of money from a large number of individual backers, often in exchange for early access to a product, a reward, or (in certain regulated forms) a small equity stake in the business, typically conducted through an online platform.

Here's the "why" crowdfunding serves a dual purpose beyond simply raising capital: a successful campaign directly demonstrates that real people are willing to put actual money behind a product or idea before it's even fully built or launched — a form of market validation that's more meaningful than survey responses or expressed interest alone, since backers are making a genuine financial commitment.

[VERIFIED FACT] This means a crowdfunding campaign's outcome, whether it succeeds or fails to reach its funding goal, provides genuine market information: a strong response suggests real demand worth pursuing further, while a weak response — even if disappointing from a pure funding perspective — can be valuable information suggesting the product, pricing, or positioning needs rethinking before committing further resources to it.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An entrepreneur launches a crowdfunding campaign for a new product idea, setting a funding goal to cover initial production costs. The campaign significantly exceeds its goal within days, with a large number of backers committing real money before the product even exists yet. [ASSUMPTION] This response gives the entrepreneur confidence, beyond just the raised funds themselves, that there's genuine market demand worth investing further resources into — a different kind of confirmation than simply asking potential customers whether they'd be interested, since the backers have already put actual money behind their interest.

## Key Takeaways
- Crowdfunding raises smaller amounts from many individual backers, often through an online platform.
- A successful campaign provides genuine market validation, since backers commit real money before the product exists.
- Even a weak campaign can provide valuable information about product, pricing, or positioning before further resources are committed.
