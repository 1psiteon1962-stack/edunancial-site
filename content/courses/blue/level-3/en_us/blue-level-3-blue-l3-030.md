## Core Content

A **Merchant Cash Advance (MCA)** provides a lump sum of cash to a business in exchange for a percentage of future daily (or sometimes weekly) sales, typically calculated using a **factor rate** (for example, 1.3) rather than a traditional interest rate — meaning the business agrees to repay the advance amount multiplied by that factor.

Here's the "why" this can become the most expensive financing option covered in this track: [VERIFIED FACT] because MCAs are typically repaid very quickly — often within several months, through frequent, sometimes daily, deductions from sales — the factor rate, when converted into an equivalent annual percentage rate for comparison purposes, often works out to a much higher effective cost than it might initially appear, frequently exceeding the effective cost of options like invoice factoring (Lesson 29) or traditional loans by a wide margin.

Despite this high cost, MCAs are often used by businesses that can't qualify for other, less expensive financing options — perhaps due to limited credit history, urgent timing needs, or an inability to meet traditional lending requirements — making an MCA, despite its cost, sometimes the only readily available option for immediate cash needs.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business receives a $50,000 merchant cash advance with a 1.3 factor rate, agreeing to repay a total of $65,000 through daily deductions from its sales over an expected repayment period of roughly 6 months. [ASSUMPTION] When that repayment structure is converted into an equivalent annual percentage rate for comparison to other financing options, the effective cost could be dramatically higher than a traditional bank loan's stated interest rate would suggest, given the flat $15,000 total cost is being repaid over such a short period — illustrating why MCAs are generally considered a financing option of last resort rather than a first choice, despite their accessibility and speed.

## Key Takeaways
- A merchant cash advance provides upfront cash in exchange for a percentage of future sales, using a factor rate rather than a traditional interest rate.
- Because MCAs are typically repaid quickly, their effective annual cost is often very high compared to other financing options.
- MCAs are often used as a financing option of last resort by businesses unable to qualify for less expensive alternatives.
