## Core Content

In the United States, a **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)** is a legally required document that franchisors must provide to prospective franchisees before any franchise sale is finalized, containing detailed, standardized information about the franchise opportunity. [VERIFIED FACT] Specific FDD requirements are set by federal and, in some cases, additional state regulations, and the required categories of disclosure are periodically updated — current requirements should be verified directly rather than assumed.

Here's the "why" this document exists: prospective franchisees, without independent access to a franchisor's internal information, would otherwise be relying almost entirely on the franchisor's own sales pitch to evaluate the opportunity. The FDD is specifically designed to counter that imbalance by requiring standardized disclosure of information including the franchisor's litigation history, initial and ongoing fees, total investment ranges, and — in some cases — specific financial performance representations, if the franchisor chooses to make any.

This connects directly to the due diligence concept from Lesson 7: reviewing an FDD carefully is, in effect, a franchise-specific form of due diligence, giving a prospective franchisee independently required information to evaluate before committing, rather than relying solely on what the franchisor chooses to volunteer in conversation.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A prospective franchisee is excited about a franchise opportunity after an enthusiastic sales presentation from the franchisor. Before signing any agreement, she carefully reviews the legally required FDD, which reveals the franchisor's litigation history with previous franchisees and the actual range of total investment costs reported across existing locations — information not necessarily emphasized during the sales conversation itself. [ASSUMPTION] This careful FDD review, similar in spirit to the due diligence process described in Lesson 7, gives her a more complete, independently required picture before making a significant financial commitment.
