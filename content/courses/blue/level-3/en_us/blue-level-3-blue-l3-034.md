## Core Content

A **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** is the simplest version of a product that still allows a business to test its core assumptions about customer demand, usage, or value, without investing the time and resources needed to build a fully featured, polished product.

Here's the "why" the word "minimum" is deliberate and important: the purpose of an MVP is specifically to test whether a core assumption — will customers actually want and use this? will they pay for it? — holds up in reality, as cheaply and quickly as possible, before committing significant further resources. Building a more elaborate, polished version than strictly necessary to test that core assumption defeats the purpose: it consumes more time and capital before the business has actually confirmed whether its foundational assumption is even correct.

[VERIFIED FACT] This concept connects directly back to Lesson 33's TAM/SAM/SOM discussion and the general theme of testing assumptions before committing significant resources — an MVP is the product-development version of the same underlying discipline: validate the core assumption cheaply first, then invest further resources once that validation exists, rather than before.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An entrepreneur believes there's demand for a specific new service. Rather than spending months and significant capital building a fully featured platform, she creates a simple landing page describing the service and a basic manual process to actually deliver it to the first handful of customers who sign up, testing whether real people are actually willing to pay for the core idea. [ASSUMPTION] If this minimal version confirms genuine customer demand and willingness to pay, she has validated her core assumption cheaply and can then justify investing in a more polished, scalable version — but if demand doesn't materialize even at this minimal level, she has avoided months of wasted development on a more elaborate product nobody actually wanted.
