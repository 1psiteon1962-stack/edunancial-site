## Core Content

A **buy-sell agreement** is a contract between business partners or co-owners that establishes, in advance, what happens to an owner's share of the business if a specific triggering event occurs — a partner wanting to leave voluntarily, a partner's death or disability, divorce, or a serious, unresolvable dispute between partners.

Here's the "why" this closely parallels a concept covered in this curriculum's RED track around partnership and joint venture debt structures: partners often start a business with strong trust and aligned goals, but circumstances change over time, and without a predetermined agreement, a triggering event can create serious conflict — disagreement about what a departing partner's share is actually worth, who has the right (or obligation) to buy it, and on what terms.

[VERIFIED FACT] A well-drafted buy-sell agreement typically addresses valuation methodology (how the business, or a departing partner's share, will be valued when a trigger occurs), funding mechanisms (how the buyout will actually be paid for, sometimes including life insurance specifically for a death-triggered buyout), and the specific process to be followed — all negotiated calmly, in advance, before any actual triggering event creates emotional or financial pressure that makes fair negotiation much harder.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two business partners start a company together with a strong personal friendship and no formal buy-sell agreement in place. Years later, one partner unexpectedly wants to exit the business for personal reasons. Without a predetermined agreement, they find themselves in a difficult, emotionally charged negotiation over what the departing partner's share is actually worth and how the remaining partner will fund the buyout — a dispute that could have been avoided entirely had they established clear valuation and funding terms in a buy-sell agreement years earlier, before either partner's specific circumstances or incentives were at stake in that particular negotiation.

## Key Takeaways
- A buy-sell agreement establishes in advance what happens to a partner's share of a business if a specific triggering event occurs.
- Common triggers include a partner leaving voluntarily, death, disability, divorce, or an unresolvable dispute.
- Negotiating this agreement calmly in advance avoids the difficult, high-pressure negotiations that arise if a trigger occurs without a predetermined plan.
