## Core Content

**Key person insurance** is a life (or sometimes disability) insurance policy a business takes out on a critical individual within the company — often a founder, top salesperson, or someone with highly specialized knowledge or relationships that would be genuinely difficult to quickly replace — with the business itself as the beneficiary if that person dies or becomes disabled.

Here's the "why" this specific risk deserves dedicated insurance coverage: some businesses are disproportionately dependent on one or a small handful of individuals — the sole person with deep relationships across major clients, the technical founder with critical, hard-to-replace knowledge of a key system. The sudden, unexpected loss of that person can create both a genuine operational disruption and a real financial gap, at exactly the moment the business can least afford to absorb it.

[VERIFIED FACT] The insurance proceeds are typically intended to help the business cover the financial impact of that loss — funding the cost of finding and training a replacement, covering lost revenue during a disruption period, or, connecting directly to Lesson 38, potentially funding a buy-sell agreement obligation if the key person was also a business partner whose ownership share needs to be bought out following their death.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A small business relies heavily on its technical founder, who holds specialized knowledge critical to the company's core product with no other employee currently able to fully replace that expertise. The business takes out a key person insurance policy on the founder, naming the business itself as the beneficiary. [ASSUMPTION] If the founder unexpectedly passes away, the resulting insurance proceeds could help the business fund the search for and training of a replacement, cover potential revenue disruption during that transition period, and — if the founder was also a co-owner — help fund the buyout of her ownership share under a buy-sell agreement (Lesson 38), rather than leaving the business in immediate financial crisis on top of the operational disruption.

## Key Takeaways
- Key person insurance protects a business against the financial impact of losing a critical individual to death or disability.
- Businesses heavily dependent on one or a few individuals with hard-to-replace knowledge or relationships face especially acute exposure to this risk.
- Insurance proceeds can help fund a replacement search, cover revenue disruption, or fund a buy-sell agreement buyout obligation.
