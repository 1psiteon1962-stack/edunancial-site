- Identify factors that generally drive higher or lower multiples

## Core Content

A **valuation multiple** applies a specific factor to a financial metric — commonly EBITDA (Lesson 3) — to estimate a business's overall value; for example, a business valued at a "4x EBITDA multiple" with $500,000 in EBITDA would be roughly valued at $2 million.

Here's the "why" this multiple varies so significantly across different industries, even for businesses with identical EBITDA: multiples generally reflect the market's collective view of a business's future growth potential, risk level, and the general desirability of that specific type of business as an investment. [VERIFIED FACT] Industries perceived as having stronger growth prospects, more predictable and recurring revenue (a topic covered in the next lesson), or lower overall risk generally command higher valuation multiples than industries perceived as slower-growing, cyclical, or higher-risk — meaning a dollar of EBITDA in one industry is not treated as equivalent to a dollar of EBITDA in a different industry.

This is why comparing a specific business's valuation to a generic, industry-agnostic multiple can be seriously misleading — meaningful comparisons should be made against multiples typical for that specific industry and business type, based on recent comparable transactions where available.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Business A, in an industry perceived as high-growth with strong recurring revenue, might command a valuation multiple of 8x EBITDA. Business B, with identical $500,000 EBITDA but operating in a slower-growing, more cyclical industry, might only command a 3x multiple. [ASSUMPTION] This means Business A could be valued around $4 million, while Business B, despite having the exact same underlying EBITDA figure, might be valued around $1.5 million — illustrating why EBITDA alone, without considering the appropriate industry-specific multiple, provides an incomplete picture of a business's likely sale value.

## Key Takeaways
- A valuation multiple applies a factor to a metric like EBITDA to estimate overall business value.
- The same EBITDA figure can result in very different valuations depending on the industry's perceived growth, revenue predictability, and risk.
- Valuation comparisons should be made against industry-specific multiples, not a generic, industry-agnostic figure.
