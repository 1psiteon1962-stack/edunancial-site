## Core Content

**Recurring revenue** refers to revenue a business can reasonably expect to receive again in future periods from existing customers — subscription fees, ongoing service contracts, or repeat business under an established agreement — as opposed to **one-time revenue**, which comes from a single transaction with no built-in expectation of repeating.

Here's the "why" recurring revenue commands a valuation premium, directly connecting to Lesson 45's discussion of industry-driven multiples: predictability reduces risk from a buyer's or investor's perspective. A business generating $1 million primarily through recurring subscription revenue offers much more confidence about next year's likely revenue than a business generating the same $1 million primarily through one-time project sales that must each be individually won again from scratch.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is a significant part of why industries built around subscription or recurring-contract models often command higher valuation multiples than otherwise comparable businesses relying primarily on one-time sales — the market is specifically rewarding the reduced uncertainty about future revenue that recurring models provide.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Business A generates $2 million in annual revenue almost entirely through recurring monthly subscriptions with a low customer cancellation rate, giving reasonable confidence that a similar revenue level will continue next year. Business B generates the same $2 million in annual revenue, but entirely through one-time project work that must be newly won, client by client, each year, with no guaranteed continuation. [ASSUMPTION] Despite generating identical current-year revenue, Business A would likely command a meaningfully higher valuation multiple than Business B, reflecting the market's reward for the greater predictability and reduced risk of its recurring revenue model.
