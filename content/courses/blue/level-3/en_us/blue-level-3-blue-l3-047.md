## Core Content

**Customer concentration risk** describes a situation where a large percentage of a business's total revenue comes from one customer, or a small handful of customers, rather than being spread across a broad, diversified customer base.

Here's the "why" this is treated as a serious red flag during due diligence (Lesson 7): this parallels, at the level of a single business, the diversification and correlation concepts covered in this curriculum's WHITE track — just as an investment portfolio concentrated in one holding carries outsized risk if that one holding struggles, a business overly dependent on one customer carries outsized risk if that customer leaves, renegotiates unfavorable terms, or experiences its own financial difficulties. [VERIFIED FACT] A single lost major customer can devastate an otherwise healthy business's revenue and profitability far more severely than the loss of any one customer would affect a business with a broad, diversified customer base.

This is why a buyer evaluating an acquisition specifically investigates customer concentration during due diligence, and why a business heavily dependent on one or a few customers will generally be valued at a meaningfully lower multiple (Lesson 45) than an otherwise comparable business with a diversified customer base, reflecting this specific, quantifiable risk.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business generates 60% of its total revenue from a single long-standing customer relationship. During due diligence, a prospective buyer identifies this as a significant risk: if that one customer were to leave, get acquired by a competitor, or simply decide to switch suppliers, the business's revenue and profitability could be devastated almost overnight. [ASSUMPTION] Even though the relationship has been stable for years and the seller expresses confidence it will continue, the buyer will likely factor this concentration risk directly into a lower offered valuation multiple, or request specific contractual protections, reflecting the real, quantifiable risk this dependency creates regardless of the relationship's past stability.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer concentration risk exists when a large share of revenue depends on one or a few customers.
- This mirrors the diversification risk concept from investing — concentration creates outsized vulnerability to a single point of failure.
- Buyers specifically screen for this risk during due diligence, and it typically results in a lower valuation multiple for an affected business.
