## Core Content

This track has covered many distinct financing tools and concepts: seller financing, SBA loans, equity financing, revenue-based financing, factoring, merchant cash advances, and more. This lesson revisits them together through two consistent lenses that have run underneath nearly every one: **control** (how much decision-making authority and ownership you retain) and **runway** (how much time and cash cushion a given choice buys you).

Debt-based options (SBA loans, Lesson 10; revenue-based financing, Lesson 27) generally preserve full ownership control but require repayment regardless of performance, directly affecting runway if the business struggles. Equity-based options (Lesson 21) extend runway without a fixed repayment obligation, but directly reduce ownership control through dilution (Lesson 24). Fast, expensive options (factoring, Lesson 29; merchant cash advances, Lesson 30) can quickly extend short-term runway but at a real, sometimes very high, ongoing cost that itself affects longer-term runway if used repeatedly.

Here's the "why" tying every decision back to these two variables leads to more consistent choices: none of these tools are inherently "good" or "bad" — each represents a different trade-off point between preserving control and extending runway, at a different cost. A founder or business owner who evaluates each financing decision by asking "how much control does this cost me, and how much runway does this actually buy me?" is applying a consistent framework across very different-looking tools, rather than evaluating each financing option as an isolated, unrelated choice.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A founder needs additional capital and is considering both an equity investment offer and a revenue-based financing option covering the same dollar amount. Evaluating through the control-and-runway framework: the equity offer preserves cash flow flexibility (payments aren't tied to revenue in the same way) and extends runway without a fixed repayment schedule, but costs meaningful ownership control and future upside (Lesson 21). The revenue-based financing option preserves full ownership control and doesn't dilute her stake, but requires ongoing repayment tied to revenue that could constrain cash flow and effectively use up runway faster if revenue is strong. [ASSUMPTION] Neither option is objectively "better" — the right choice depends on how she weighs preserving control against the specific runway and cash flow implications of each, the same trade-off examined throughout this track in different specific forms.
