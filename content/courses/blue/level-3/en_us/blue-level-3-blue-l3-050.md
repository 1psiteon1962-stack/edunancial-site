## Core Content

This track opened with the idea that a business's earning power, not just its assets, drives its value (Lesson 2), and closes with a related, deliberate reframe: mastering business finance isn't primarily about learning every available financing tool — it's about developing the judgment to recognize which growth opportunities are actually worth financing, and having the discipline to decline the ones that aren't.

Look back across this track: unit economics (Lesson 17) showed that growth with negative per-customer economics accelerates losses, not profits. Burn rate and runway (Lesson 19) showed that growth funded by fundraising still depends on that fundraising succeeding on a specific timeline. The control-and-runway framework (Lesson 49) showed that every financing choice trades away something — ownership, flexibility, or cost — in exchange for growth capital. Customer concentration risk (Lesson 47) and the TAM/SAM/SOM discipline (Lesson 33) both warned against pursuing an opportunity's most flattering framing rather than its most honest one.

[VERIFIED FACT] None of the tools covered in this track — seller financing, SBA loans, equity, revenue-based financing, and the rest — are inherently good financing choices. Each is a tool well-suited to funding growth that has already been honestly evaluated as worth pursuing, and poorly suited to disguising or funding growth that hasn't been.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An entrepreneur evaluates an opportunity to significantly scale her business, using the frameworks from across this track: she calculates honest unit economics (Lesson 17) and a real CAC-to-LTV ratio (Lesson 18), confirms she has adequate runway to test the growth plan (Lesson 19), and considers the control-and-runway trade-off (Lesson 49) of the specific financing option available to fund it. The analysis confirms the growth opportunity is genuinely sound. A different opportunity, evaluated with the same honest rigor, reveals negative unit economics and a customer base concentrated in a single, fragile relationship (Lesson 47). Rather than searching for a way to finance that second opportunity anyway, she declines it — the same disciplined judgment, applied consistently, leading to two different, appropriate decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- True mastery of business finance means judging which growth opportunities are genuinely worth financing, not just knowing every available tool.
- Frameworks like unit economics, CAC-to-LTV, runway analysis, and honest market sizing help make that judgment consistently.
- The financing tools covered in this track are neutral; real skill lies in the discipline to finance growth that's been honestly evaluated, and decline growth that hasn't.
