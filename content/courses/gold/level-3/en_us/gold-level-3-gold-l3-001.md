## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain the general relationship between risk and potential return
- Explain why an investment promising high return with little risk should raise questions
- Explain why evaluating return without considering risk gives an incomplete picture

## Core Content

A foundational principle running through this entire GOLD track: risk and potential return are generally linked, not independent variables an investor can freely optimize separately. Here's the "why" behind this relationship: investments carrying greater uncertainty about their outcome — greater risk — generally need to offer greater potential return to attract capital at all, since investors require compensation for bearing that additional uncertainty. An investment offering high potential return with very little actual risk would, in a reasonably functioning market, attract so much capital seeking that favorable combination that its price would typically rise until the excess return largely disappeared.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is why claims of high return with minimal risk should be treated with particular skepticism — either the risk is being understated or misunderstood, or something else about the situation doesn't fit the normal pattern this relationship describes. This doesn't mean every risky investment automatically delivers high returns — many risky investments simply lose money — but it does mean that genuinely low-risk opportunities rarely offer outsized returns for a sustained period.

This principle underlies why evaluating an investment's return in isolation, without asking what risk was taken on to generate it, gives an incomplete and potentially misleading picture — a topic developed further throughout this track.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor is presented with two opportunities: one offering modest, steady returns with a well-understood, limited risk profile, and another promising dramatically higher returns with the same claimed low risk level. [ASSUMPTION] Given the general risk-return relationship, the second opportunity's claim should prompt careful scrutiny — either its actual risk is higher than represented, its return claims are unrealistic or exaggerated, or there's some other issue (potentially even fraud) that doesn't fit the normal pattern this relationship would predict for a legitimate, sustainable opportunity.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is the general relationship between risk and potential return?
2. Why should an investment claiming high return with minimal risk raise questions?
3. Does this principle mean every risky investment automatically delivers high returns?

## Answer Key

1. Investments carrying greater uncertainty generally need to offer greater potential return to attract capital, since investors require compensation for bearing that additional risk.
2. Because in a reasonably functioning market, genuinely low-risk, high-return opportunities would attract so much capital that the excess return would typically disappear — an unusual claim suggests misunderstood risk or another issue.
3. No — many risky investments simply lose money; the relationship describes a general pattern of required compensation for risk, not a guarantee that risk-taking pays off.

## Key Takeaways
- Risk and potential return are generally linked, not independently optimizable variables.
- Claims of high return with minimal risk should be treated with particular skepticism.
- This relationship describes required compensation for risk-bearing, not a guarantee that taking risk produces high returns.
