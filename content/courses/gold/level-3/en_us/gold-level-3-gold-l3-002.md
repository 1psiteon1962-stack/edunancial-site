## Core Content

Lesson 1 established the general relationship between risk and return. Here's the "why" this lesson pushes further: "risk" itself isn't a single, universally fixed concept — it means genuinely different things depending on an investor's specific circumstances, goals, and time horizon.

For one investor, risk might mean the chance of a permanent, unrecoverable loss of capital. For another, closer to needing the money for a specific near-term goal, risk might mean short-term volatility that could force a poorly timed sale (connecting to the volatility-versus-risk distinction covered in this curriculum's WHITE track). For someone focused on retirement income, risk might primarily mean the chance that their income doesn't keep pace with inflation over decades. [VERIFIED FACT] These different framings of risk aren't contradictory — they reflect the fact that risk is fundamentally about the chance of not achieving a specific goal, and different investors have different goals.

This is why the same specific investment can be genuinely risky for one investor and entirely reasonable for another: a volatile, long-term growth investment might be reasonable risk for a young investor decades from retirement, but genuinely risky for someone who needs that specific money in the next year.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A young investor with decades until retirement and a diversified income source views a volatile, growth-oriented investment as a reasonable risk, since she has ample time to recover from short-term downturns and her primary goal is long-term growth. A retiree relying on that same portfolio for near-term living expenses would view an identical investment very differently — the same volatility that's a reasonable risk for the younger investor represents a genuine threat to the retiree's near-term financial stability. [ASSUMPTION] Neither investor is wrong about the investment's risk; they're accurately assessing risk relative to their own specific goals and time horizons, which genuinely differ.

## Practice Quiz

1. Because it means different things depending on an investor's specific circumstances, goals, and time horizon — permanent loss, short-term volatility, or inflation-adjusted income shortfall, among other framings.
2. The chance of not achieving a specific goal, meaning risk assessment depends on what that goal actually is.
3. Because their specific goals and time horizons genuinely differ, changing what "risk" practically means for each of them.

## Key Takeaways
- "Risk" isn't a single, universal concept — it takes different practical forms depending on an investor's specific goals.
- Risk is fundamentally about the chance of not achieving a specific goal, whatever that goal is for a given investor.
- The same investment can be a reasonable risk for one investor and a genuine risk for another, based on differing time horizons and goals.
