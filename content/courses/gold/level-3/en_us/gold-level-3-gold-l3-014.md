## Core Content

Economies generally move through cycles — periods of expansion (growth, rising employment, increasing business activity) and periods of contraction (slowdown, rising unemployment, reduced business activity), influenced by many interacting factors. [VERIFIED FACT] The specific timing, length, and severity of any given cycle isn't reliably predictable in advance, though the general pattern of cycles occurring over time is a well-established economic observation.

Here's the "why" different asset classes tend to respond differently to the same point in this cycle: stocks generally reflect expectations about future business profits, often performing well during expansion and struggling during contraction. Bonds, particularly high-quality ones, sometimes perform relatively better during contractions, as investors seek relative safety and interest rates often move in ways that can benefit existing bond prices (connecting to the interest-rate-and-bond-price relationship covered in the WHITE track). Real estate often responds to its own more localized supply and demand factors alongside broader economic conditions. Gold, given its role discussed in earlier lessons, sometimes performs relatively well during periods of significant economic or currency stress.

This is the underlying reason behind the asset-class diversification concept covered in the WHITE track: because different asset classes tend to respond differently to the same economic conditions, holding a mix across these classes can smooth overall portfolio performance across the full cycle, rather than being fully exposed to whichever phase currently disadvantages a single, concentrated asset choice.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] During a period of economic expansion, an investor's stock holdings perform strongly, reflecting growing business profits and optimism. During a subsequent economic contraction, those same stock holdings struggle, but her separate allocation to high-quality bonds and a modest gold position perform comparatively better during that same period, partially offsetting the stock decline. [ASSUMPTION] Across the full cycle, her diversified mix of asset classes likely delivers a smoother overall experience than being concentrated entirely in stocks would have, illustrating the practical value of the cross-asset-class diversification concept this lesson connects to.
