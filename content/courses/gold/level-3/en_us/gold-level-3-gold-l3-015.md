## Core Content

Lesson 14 established that different asset classes respond to economic conditions through different mechanisms. Interest rates provide a particularly clear illustration of this: a single change in prevailing interest rates ripples through nearly every asset class at once, though through genuinely different channels for each.

Here's the "why" this happens: bond prices move inversely with interest rates through the direct mechanism covered in the WHITE track. Real estate, heavily dependent on mortgage financing as covered throughout the RED track, becomes more or less affordable as rates change, affecting property values and cap rate comparisons (also covered in the RED track). Stocks are affected because higher rates increase the cost of business borrowing and can make future company earnings less attractive on a present-value basis relative to now higher-yielding, safer bonds. Gold, having no yield itself, can become relatively less attractive when interest-bearing alternatives offer a higher yield, though this specific relationship, like others involving gold, can be influenced by other simultaneous factors, such as broader economic stress.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is exactly why interest rate decisions and expectations are among the most closely watched broad economic indicators by investors across every asset class — a single rate change genuinely does affect nearly everything, even though the specific mechanism, direction, and magnitude of that effect varies meaningfully by asset type.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A significant change in prevailing interest rates simultaneously affects an investor's diversified holdings: her bond prices move due to the direct interest rate relationship, her real estate holdings are affected by changed mortgage affordability, her stock holdings react to changed borrowing costs and relative attractiveness compared to now-different bond yields, and her gold holdings respond to gold's changed relative appeal compared to interest-bearing alternatives. [ASSUMPTION] Each of these four responses happens through a genuinely different mechanism, but all are triggered by the same single underlying rate change — illustrating why this one economic factor is watched so closely across every part of a diversified portfolio at once.
