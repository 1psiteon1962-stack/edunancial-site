## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define strategic asset allocation and tactical asset allocation
- Explain why these two approaches operate on genuinely different time horizons and purposes
- Explain the risk of frequently second-guessing a strategic allocation with tactical adjustments

## Core Content

**Strategic asset allocation** refers to a long-term target mix of asset classes — a specific percentage allocated to stocks, bonds, real estate, alternatives, and other categories — designed based on an investor's overall goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance, and generally intended to remain relatively stable over long periods, adjusted only as major life circumstances change. **Tactical asset allocation** refers to shorter-term, deliberate deviations from that strategic target, based on a current view about near-term market conditions or opportunities.

Here's the "why" confusing these two approaches, or excessively favoring tactical adjustments, can undermine a sound long-term plan: a strategic allocation is built on the durable, thoughtful reasoning covered throughout this track — time horizon (Lesson 3), risk tolerance (Lesson 2), and the different roles various asset classes play (Lessons 6 and 14). Frequent tactical deviations, based on short-term market predictions, [VERIFIED FACT] essentially reintroduce the market-timing challenge discussed elsewhere in this curriculum's WHITE track — betting on short-term market movements that are notoriously difficult to predict reliably and consistently.

This is why many disciplined, long-term investors rely primarily on their strategic allocation, using rebalancing (as covered in the WHITE track) to maintain it, and treat tactical adjustments, if used at all, as a limited, carefully considered exception rather than the primary driving force behind portfolio decisions.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor establishes a strategic asset allocation based on her specific time horizon and risk tolerance, intending to maintain it over many years through periodic rebalancing. If she frequently deviates from this target based on short-term market predictions — increasing her stock allocation because she feels optimistic about near-term market direction, then reducing it again based on a subsequent feeling of pessimism — she's essentially engaging in market timing under a different name. [ASSUMPTION] Over time, this pattern of frequent tactical deviation, if her short-term predictions aren't reliably accurate (a difficult standard to meet consistently, as discussed elsewhere in this curriculum), is likely to produce a worse outcome than simply maintaining her carefully considered strategic allocation and rebalancing back to it periodically.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is the difference between strategic asset allocation and tactical asset allocation?
2. Why is a strategic allocation generally intended to remain relatively stable over long periods?
3. Why can frequent tactical deviations from a strategic allocation undermine a sound long-term plan?

## Answer Key

1. Strategic allocation is a long-term target mix based on overall goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance; tactical allocation involves shorter-term, deliberate deviations based on a current view of near-term conditions.
2. Because it's built on durable factors like time horizon and risk tolerance that don't typically change frequently, unlike short-term market conditions.
3. Because frequent tactical deviations essentially reintroduce the market-timing challenge, betting on short-term market movements that are difficult to predict reliably and consistently.
