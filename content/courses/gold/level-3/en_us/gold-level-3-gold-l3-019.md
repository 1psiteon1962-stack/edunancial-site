## Core Content

This curriculum's WHITE track explained dollar-cost averaging (DCA) as a way to reduce timing risk by investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, rather than all at once. Here's the "why" this principle isn't limited to stocks: DCA addresses timing risk generally, and timing risk exists for any volatile asset an investor plans to accumulate over time — including gold and other precious metals, which, as covered earlier in this track, can be genuinely volatile despite their non-productive, store-of-value role.

[VERIFIED FACT] Because gold's price, like any asset without a clear, calculable "fair value" based on earnings, can be particularly difficult to judge as objectively cheap or expensive at any given moment (echoing Lesson 8's valuation framework discussion), accumulating a position gradually over time through regular purchases — rather than attempting to time a single large purchase at what feels like the "right" moment — applies the same timing-risk-reduction logic covered for stocks equally well to precious metals accumulation.

This connects directly to a biographical case study referenced elsewhere in this curriculum: gradually accumulating silver in ounces from an early age, using a dollar-cost-averaging-style approach over time, illustrates exactly this principle applied specifically to precious metals rather than stocks.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Rather than attempting to determine the single "best" moment to buy a large amount of gold all at once — a genuinely difficult judgment given the valuation challenges discussed in Lesson 8 — an investor instead purchases a smaller, fixed amount of gold on a regular schedule over an extended period. [ASSUMPTION] Some purchases happen at relatively higher prices, some at relatively lower prices, and her average cost smooths out over the full accumulation period — the same timing-risk-reduction benefit DCA provides for stock purchases, applied here to a volatile, difficult-to-value alternative asset instead.
