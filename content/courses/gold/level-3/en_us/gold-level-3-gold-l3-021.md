## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why gold, unlike bonds or dividend-paying stocks, generates no yield
- Explain why this lack of yield represents a genuine opportunity cost
- Explain why this trade-off is directly connected to gold's intended role, rather than an oversight

## Core Content

Lessons 4 and 5 established gold as a non-productive asset. Here's the specific "why" behind its complete lack of yield, and why this isn't simply an unfortunate gap: gold doesn't pay interest like a bond, dividends like some stocks, or rent like real estate, because it doesn't generate any underlying economic activity — it just sits, physically unchanged, whether held for one year or fifty.

Here's the "why" this represents a genuine, ongoing opportunity cost worth acknowledging honestly: [VERIFIED FACT] money held in gold isn't simultaneously earning interest, dividends, or rental income the way it could be if invested in a productive asset instead — this is a real cost of holding gold, not something to dismiss, and it becomes more significant the higher available yields on alternative, productive assets happen to be at a given time (connecting directly to Lesson 15's discussion of interest rates affecting gold's relative appeal).

This trade-off is directly connected to gold's intended role, not an oversight in its design: precisely because gold generates no yield, it also carries none of the risks tied to that yield — no dependence on a company's continued profitability, no dependence on a borrower's continued ability to pay interest. The lack of yield and the lack of yield-related risk are two sides of the same underlying characteristic.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holds a portion of her portfolio in gold rather than in a bond offering a meaningful yield. Over several years, she forgoes the interest income she would have earned from the bond — a real, quantifiable opportunity cost of her gold allocation. [ASSUMPTION] In exchange, she also avoids the specific risk tied to that bond — the possibility of the issuer defaulting or failing to make interest payments — since her gold holding carries no equivalent dependence on any specific borrower's ongoing ability to pay. She's traded yield, and the risk tied to that yield, for a different set of characteristics gold offers instead.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why does gold generate no yield, unlike a bond or dividend-paying stock?
2. Why does this lack of yield represent a genuine, ongoing opportunity cost?
3. Why is the lack of yield directly connected to gold's intended role, rather than simply a flaw?

## Answer Key

1. Because gold doesn't generate any underlying economic activity — it doesn't produce interest, dividends, or rent the way a productive asset does.
2. Because money held in gold isn't simultaneously earning income it could be earning in a productive alternative, a real, ongoing cost that becomes more significant when available yields elsewhere are higher.
3. Because the absence of yield also means the absence of yield-related risk — no dependence on a company's profitability or a borrower's ability to pay — these are two sides of the same underlying non-productive characteristic.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This connects directly back to gold's non-productive classification from Lessons 4 and 5.
2. This is the central, honest acknowledgment of gold's real cost.
3. This reframes the lack of yield as a deliberate trade-off tied to gold's role, not an oversight.

## Key Takeaways
- Gold generates no yield because it produces no underlying economic activity, unlike bonds, dividend stocks, or rental property.
- This represents a genuine, ongoing opportunity cost, which grows larger when available yields elsewhere are higher.
- The absence of yield and the absence of yield-related risk are directly connected — a deliberate trade-off tied to gold's intended role, not a flaw.
