## Core Content

This curriculum's RED track introduced the general concept that fiat currency's purchasing power can shrink over time through inflation. **Currency debasement** describes a related, more specific concern: a meaningful, sometimes deliberate, reduction in a currency's value or purchasing power, historically sometimes associated with significant increases in a currency's supply relative to underlying economic activity.

Here's the "why" some investors specifically respond to this concern by holding gold or other assets viewed as existing outside the traditional currency system, connecting directly to Lesson 20's "safe haven" discussion: gold's long history as a widely recognized, physically tangible store of value — not directly created or controlled by any single government's monetary policy decisions — makes it one asset some investors turn to specifically as a hedge against the risk that any particular currency's purchasing power could decline meaningfully over time.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is a genuinely debated area among economists and investors, with different perspectives on the actual likelihood, severity, and appropriate response to currency debasement concerns in any specific economic environment — this lesson presents the general reasoning behind why some investors hold this view and act on it, not an endorsement of any particular economic or political position on monetary policy.
