## Core Content

**Real assets** — physical, tangible assets like real estate, commodities, and precious metals — derive value from their physical characteristics and utility. **Financial assets** — stocks, bonds, and similar instruments — derive value from a contractual or ownership claim on future cash flows or value, without necessarily having intrinsic physical substance themselves.

Here's the "why" this classification connects directly to the differential inflation response discussed in the WHITE track: real assets often maintain their fundamental utility and scarcity regardless of what happens to the value of any specific currency, since their value isn't purely a function of a monetary promise. [VERIFIED FACT] Financial assets, particularly those with fixed nominal payments (like traditional bonds), are more directly exposed to a currency's changing purchasing power, since their value is tied to a specific, fixed monetary amount that inflation can erode over time.

This is why real assets — including real estate (RED track), commodities, and gold (this GOLD track) — are commonly discussed as a category with generally different inflation-response characteristics than financial assets, though, as covered in Lesson 30 of this curriculum's WHITE track equivalent discussion, no single asset category responds identically or guarantees protection in every specific inflationary period.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] During a period of significant inflation, a real asset like a rental property may see both its value and its rental income tend to rise along with broader prices over time, reflecting its physical, tangible nature and ongoing utility. A traditional bond with a fixed nominal interest payment, by contrast, sees that fixed payment's real purchasing power directly eroded by the same inflation, since the payment amount itself doesn't adjust. [ASSUMPTION] This illustrates the general classification distinction this lesson describes, while also reflecting the appropriate caveat that individual assets within each broad category can still behave differently based on their own specific circumstances.

## Key Takeaways
- Real assets derive value from physical characteristics and utility; financial assets derive value from a contractual claim on future cash flows.
- Real assets often maintain value differently during inflation than financial assets with fixed nominal payments, which are more directly eroded by inflation.
- No single asset category guarantees protection in every specific inflationary period, even within these broad classifications.
