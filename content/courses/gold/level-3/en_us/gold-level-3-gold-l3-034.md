## Core Content

**Overconfidence bias** describes a well-documented tendency for people to overestimate their own abilities, knowledge, or the accuracy of their predictions, relative to their actual, objectively measured performance. Applied to investing, this can show up as overestimating one's ability to consistently pick winning individual investments, correctly time market entry and exit points, or accurately predict future economic conditions.

Here's the "why" this bias doesn't simply disappear with more knowledge or experience: [VERIFIED FACT] behavioral finance research has found that overconfidence can actually be more pronounced among individuals with more knowledge or experience in a domain, since greater familiarity with a subject can paradoxically increase confidence in one's judgments beyond what that increased knowledge alone would objectively justify — meaning investment experience and education, including everything covered throughout this curriculum, doesn't automatically make someone immune to this specific bias, and can even make it more likely without deliberate awareness and counter-effort.

One documented practical consequence: [VERIFIED FACT] research analyzing real investor trading behavior has generally found that more frequent trading, often associated with higher investor confidence in one's own predictive ability, has been associated with lower net returns on average, compared to less frequent trading — connecting directly to the transaction costs and the difficulty of consistently outperforming the market discussed elsewhere in this curriculum's WHITE track.
