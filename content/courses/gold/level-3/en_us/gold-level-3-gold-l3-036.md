## Core Content

This curriculum's WHITE and BLUE tracks both cover position sizing — limiting how much of a portfolio is committed to any single investment, so a single poor outcome doesn't become catastrophic. Here's the "why" this principle applies with equal, and arguably greater, force to the alternative and illiquid investments covered throughout this GOLD track: given the reduced liquidity (Lesson 10), more difficult valuation (Lesson 8), and often complex fee structures (Lesson 25) covered throughout this track, a poorly sized position in one of these asset types can be considerably harder to correct or exit than an oversized position in a liquid, publicly traded stock.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is directly connected to the fractional shares case study referenced elsewhere in this curriculum: sizing any single investment — whether a stock, a gold allocation, or a private fund commitment — so that a disappointing outcome is a real, felt setback but never a catastrophic one, is a universal principle, not something specific to any one asset category covered in this curriculum's various tracks.

This matters especially given this track's emphasis on illiquid, harder-to-value alternative investments: since exiting a poor decision is considerably harder in these categories than with a liquid stock, getting the position size right upfront carries even more weight than it does for more easily adjustable liquid holdings.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor sizes her positions carefully across her entire portfolio: a modest allocation to individual stocks, a modest allocation to gold, and a modest, carefully considered commitment to an illiquid private fund — each sized so that even a significantly disappointing outcome in any single position wouldn't threaten her overall financial position. [ASSUMPTION] If her private fund investment underperforms significantly, her careful position sizing means this is a real, felt setback, but not one that jeopardizes her broader financial stability — precisely because she applied the same disciplined sizing principle to this illiquid, harder-to-exit position that she applied to her more easily adjustable liquid holdings.
