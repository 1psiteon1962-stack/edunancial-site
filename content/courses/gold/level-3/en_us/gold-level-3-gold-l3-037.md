## Core Content

Lesson 35 established that genuine contrarian conviction requires a specific, articulated basis, not just disagreement with the crowd. Here's a related, more general principle: a sound investment thesis should be **falsifiable** — meaning it should specify, in advance, what kind of evidence or outcome would indicate the thesis was wrong.

Here's the "why" this matters: a thesis that can absorb any outcome as confirming evidence — rising prices confirm it, falling prices are dismissed as "temporary noise" or "the market not yet recognizing the true value" — isn't actually providing useful, testable information at all. [VERIFIED FACT] This kind of unfalsifiable reasoning is a documented pattern in behavioral finance, sometimes connected to confirmation bias, where evidence is interpreted in whatever way supports an already-held belief, rather than being genuinely weighed as potential support or refutation.

This is why disciplined investors generally try to articulate, before making a significant investment, specific conditions or evidence that would change their mind — not because they expect to be wrong, but because this discipline forces a more honest, complete evaluation of the thesis upfront, and provides a genuine basis for reevaluating the position later if that specific evidence actually materializes.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor develops a thesis for a specific alternative investment, and before committing capital, explicitly identifies several specific conditions that, if they occurred, would indicate her thesis was wrong and prompt her to reconsider the position. [ASSUMPTION] Months later, when one of those specific conditions actually does occur, she has a clear, previously-established basis for reevaluating her position honestly, rather than reflexively dismissing the unfavorable development as irrelevant noise — a discipline that a less falsifiable, more flexible thesis wouldn't have provided her.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does it mean for an investment thesis to be falsifiable?
2. Why is an unfalsifiable thesis considered a warning sign rather than a sign of strong conviction?
3. Why does articulating specific disconfirming conditions in advance provide genuine practical value?

## Answer Key

1. It specifies, in advance, what kind of evidence or outcome would indicate the thesis was wrong, rather than being able to absorb any outcome as confirmation.
2. Because a thesis that interprets any outcome as confirming isn't providing useful, testable information — this pattern is connected to confirmation bias, interpreting evidence to support an already-held belief rather than genuinely weighing it.
3. It forces a more honest, complete evaluation upfront and provides a clear, previously-established basis for reevaluating the position later if that specific disconfirming evidence actually materializes.

## Key Takeaways
- A sound investment thesis should be falsifiable, specifying in advance what evidence would indicate it was wrong.
- An unfalsifiable thesis that absorbs any outcome as confirmation is a warning sign, often connected to confirmation bias.
- Articulating specific disconfirming conditions in advance provides a genuine, honest basis for reevaluating a position later.
