## Learning Objectives

- Define opportunity cost in an investment context
- Explain why this cost is easy to overlook compared to more visible costs like fees
- Explain why every investment decision, including holding cash, carries some opportunity cost

## Core Content

**Opportunity cost** refers to the value of the next-best alternative given up when a specific choice is made. In an investment context, choosing to put capital into one specific investment means giving up whatever return that same capital might have earned in the next-best available alternative.

Here's the "why" this cost, while genuinely real, is easy to overlook compared to more visible costs like fees (Lesson 25) or taxes: opportunity cost doesn't appear as a line-item deduction on any account statement — it's an invisible, counterfactual cost, reflecting something that didn't happen rather than something that visibly did. [VERIFIED FACT] This invisibility doesn't make it any less real: capital committed to one specific investment, including gold or any alternative asset covered throughout this track, genuinely cannot simultaneously be earning whatever return was available in the next-best alternative during that same period.

This is why even holding cash carries a genuine opportunity cost — the potential return that same cash might have earned if invested elsewhere — a cost worth acknowledging honestly (as Lesson 21 did specifically for gold's lack of yield) rather than treating cash or any other choice as somehow "costless" simply because no visible fee or loss appears on a statement.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holds a significant portion of her portfolio in a specific investment for several years. [ASSUMPTION] Even if that investment performs reasonably well in absolute terms, if a different, readily available alternative would have performed meaningfully better over that same period, her actual investment decision carried a real opportunity cost — the difference between what she actually earned and what she could have earned in that alternative — even though no fee or visible loss reflecting this specific cost appears anywhere on her account statement.
