## Core Content

Lesson 21 discussed gold's lack of yield as an opportunity cost. Here's a related, additional cost specific to holding a **physical**, tangible version of an asset like gold, rather than a paper or digital equivalent (a distinction explored further in the next lesson): physical possession introduces genuine, ongoing costs that a purely financial asset — a stock, a bond, or even a paper claim on gold — simply doesn't carry.

[VERIFIED FACT] These costs generally include secure storage (a safe deposit box, a home safe, or a professional storage facility, each with its own cost and trade-offs) and insurance against theft, loss, or damage — both genuine, ongoing expenses that reduce the physical asset's actual net return, beyond the opportunity cost already discussed in Lesson 21.

This is why an honest evaluation of physical gold's true, all-in cost of ownership needs to include these ongoing expenses, not just the metal's own price appreciation or depreciation — two investors holding what looks like an identical amount of gold could have meaningfully different actual net returns, depending on how much they're each spending on storage and insurance for their specific holdings.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holds physical gold in a professional secure storage facility, paying an annual fee for that storage along with a separate insurance premium protecting against theft or loss. [ASSUMPTION] Over time, these ongoing costs, while individually modest, meaningfully reduce her actual net return on the gold holding compared to the metal's raw price appreciation alone — an honest accounting of her actual investment performance needs to subtract these real, ongoing costs, not just track the metal's headline price movement.
