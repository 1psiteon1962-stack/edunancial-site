## Core Content

[HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO — ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED FACT] Imagine a significant geopolitical conflict disrupts a major global shipping route, sharply increasing transportation costs and creating uncertainty across global markets. This is a hypothetical stress-test scenario, not a prediction about any specific real event, country, or conflict.

In this hypothetical, the disruption would likely touch several asset classes covered across this curriculum simultaneously, each through a different mechanism. In the WHITE track's terms: markets might see sharp volatility as uncertainty about supply chains and corporate earnings increases, and options strategies like a straddle (betting on movement rather than direction, WHITE track) might become more attractive given the genuine uncertainty about which direction markets will move. In the RED track's terms: real estate values and financing costs in directly affected regions might shift, while regions positioned to benefit from rerouted trade might see the opposite effect. In the BLUE track's terms: a startup offering an alternative shipping or logistics solution might suddenly find funding easier to raise, given increased investor interest in solutions to the disruption. Within this GOLD track specifically: gold, per Lesson 20's "safe haven" discussion, might see increased demand as investors seek assets outside the directly affected system, while broader commodities might see prices shift based on the specific goods affected by the disrupted shipping route.

## Worked Example

[HYPOTHETICAL — ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT A PREDICTION] An investor holding a diversified portfolio across the asset types covered throughout this curriculum experiences this hypothetical disruption differently across her various holdings: her gold allocation, consistent with Lesson 20's "safe haven" discussion, holds up or even increases in value; her real estate holdings in the affected region see reduced values, while a separate real estate holding in a region benefiting from rerouted trade performs better; her stock holdings experience broad volatility, with companies dependent on the disrupted route underperforming and companies offering alternatives outperforming. Rather than being surprised by any single asset's specific response, her understanding of each asset class's differential sensitivity — built throughout this curriculum's various tracks — helps her interpret what's happening across her full portfolio coherently.
