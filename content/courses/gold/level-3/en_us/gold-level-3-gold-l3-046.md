## Core Content

This curriculum's WHITE track and Lesson 18 of this track both covered rebalancing as a mechanical discipline for maintaining a target asset allocation. Here's the "why" this becomes more complicated once illiquid alternative assets (Lessons 10-13) are part of a portfolio: rebalancing a stock-and-bond portfolio simply requires selling some of one and buying more of the other, both easily done in a liquid market. Selling a portion of an illiquid alternative investment to rebalance isn't nearly as simple — as covered throughout this track, these positions often can't be partially or quickly sold at all, given their structural illiquidity.

[VERIFIED FACT] Investors managing portfolios that include meaningful alternative asset allocations generally address this in a few ways: treating the illiquid allocation as a longer-term, less frequently adjusted component of the overall strategic allocation (connecting to Lesson 17's strategic-versus-tactical distinction) rather than something rebalanced on the same frequent schedule as liquid holdings; using new contributions or liquid asset adjustments to gradually shift the overall portfolio's balance back toward target over time, rather than needing to sell the illiquid position directly; and building the illiquid allocation's eventual, planned liquidity events (a fund's scheduled distributions, for example) into the broader rebalancing plan.

This is why maintaining rebalancing discipline with alternative assets in the mix requires more thoughtful, longer-term planning than the more mechanical, straightforward rebalancing possible with a purely liquid stock-and-bond portfolio, though the underlying goal — maintaining an appropriate overall allocation — remains the same.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor's portfolio includes a meaningful allocation to an illiquid private fund alongside her liquid stock and bond holdings. When her overall allocation drifts from target due to market movements, she can't simply sell a portion of the illiquid fund the way she could sell some stocks. [ASSUMPTION] Instead, she adjusts her new contributions and her liquid holdings' rebalancing to gradually shift her overall portfolio back toward target over time, and factors in the fund's eventually scheduled distributions as a planned future rebalancing opportunity — a more patient, longer-term approach than the straightforward, immediate rebalancing possible with her purely liquid holdings.

## Key Takeaways
- Rebalancing an illiquid alternative asset allocation is more complicated than rebalancing liquid stocks and bonds, since illiquid positions can't be easily or partially sold.
- Investors generally address this through longer-term strategic treatment, gradual adjustment via new contributions, and planning around scheduled liquidity events.
- The underlying goal of rebalancing — maintaining an appropriate overall allocation — remains the same, even though the approach requires more thoughtful, longer-term planning.
