## Core Content

Lesson 1 explained that only income within a given bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. The **effective tax rate** takes this a step further: it's the average rate you actually pay across your entire income, calculated as total tax paid divided by total income.

Here's the "why" this figure is almost always meaningfully lower than your top marginal rate: because lower brackets are taxed at lower rates on the way up to your highest bracket, blending all of those rates together into a single average always pulls the overall effective rate below the top marginal rate that applies only to your last dollars earned.

[VERIFIED FACT] This is why effective tax rate is often considered a more useful figure for real financial planning and for comparing tax burden across different income levels or time periods: it reflects your actual overall tax burden, rather than just the rate that happens to apply to your highest-earning dollar, which can make a person's tax situation look more severe than it actually is if the marginal rate is mistaken for the overall rate.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Using the same simplified bracket structure from Lesson 1 (10% on the first $10,000, 20% above that), a person earning $15,000 pays $1,000 (10% of $10,000) plus $1,000 (20% of the remaining $5,000), for a total of $2,000 in tax. Their top marginal rate is 20%, but their effective tax rate is $2,000 divided by $15,000, or about 13.3% — meaningfully lower than the 20% marginal rate that might make their tax situation seem more severe than it actually is.

2. Why is effective tax rate always lower than or equal to your top marginal rate?
3. Why is effective tax rate often considered more useful for financial planning than marginal rate?

## Answer Key

1. The average tax rate actually paid across your entire income, calculated as total tax paid divided by total income.
2. Because lower brackets taxed at lower rates are blended into the average along with the top bracket, always pulling the overall average below the top marginal rate.
3. Because it reflects actual overall tax burden rather than just the rate applying to the highest-earning dollar, giving a more accurate picture for real planning and comparison purposes.

## Key Takeaways
- Effective tax rate is the average rate paid across total income, distinct from the top marginal bracket rate.
- It's always lower than or equal to the top marginal rate, since lower-taxed brackets are blended into the average.
- Effective tax rate is often more useful for real financial planning, since it reflects actual overall tax burden.
