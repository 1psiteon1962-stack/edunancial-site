## Core Content

**Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** are mandatory minimum withdrawal amounts that owners of certain tax-deferred retirement accounts (Lesson 11) must begin taking once they reach a specific age, generally continuing each year afterward. [VERIFIED FACT] The specific starting age and calculation method for RMDs are set by tax law and have changed over time — current rules should be verified directly rather than assumed.

Here's the "why" this requirement exists, connecting directly to Lesson 11's tax-deferred concept: tax-deferred accounts represent postponed, not eliminated, tax liability — the government is owed tax on that money eventually. Without a mandatory withdrawal requirement, a taxpayer could theoretically defer withdrawing (and therefore paying tax on) these funds indefinitely, well beyond their own lifetime in some cases, delaying that tax revenue far longer than the tax-deferred structure was originally intended to allow. RMDs ensure that deferred tax liability is eventually actually paid, on a defined schedule.

[VERIFIED FACT] Roth accounts have historically been treated differently regarding RMDs, given their different tax structure (tax paid upfront, rather than deferred) — since there's no remaining deferred tax liability to collect from a Roth account's growth, the government's rationale for requiring mandatory withdrawals doesn't apply in the same way, though specific rules and any changes to this treatment should be verified directly and can vary depending on the specific type of account.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A retiree with a substantial balance in a tax-deferred Traditional account reaches the age at which RMDs begin. Even if she doesn't need the withdrawn funds for her current living expenses, she's required to withdraw at least the calculated minimum amount each year, and that withdrawal is taxed as ordinary income, just as any other Traditional account withdrawal would be. [ASSUMPTION] This ensures the government eventually collects the deferred tax on this money, rather than allowing it to remain untaxed and inaccessible to tax authorities indefinitely, which connects directly back to the tax-deferred (not tax-eliminated) nature of the account established in Lesson 11.

## Practice Quiz

1. What are Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)?
2. Why do RMDs exist, connecting back to the tax-deferred concept from Lesson 11?
3. Why have Roth accounts historically been treated differently regarding RMDs?

## Answer Key

1. Mandatory minimum withdrawal amounts that owners of certain tax-deferred retirement accounts must begin taking once they reach a specific age.
2. Because tax-deferred accounts represent postponed, not eliminated, tax liability, and RMDs ensure that liability is eventually actually collected on a defined schedule, rather than deferred indefinitely.
3. Because Roth accounts have tax paid upfront rather than deferred, meaning there's no remaining deferred tax liability on growth for the government to collect, removing the primary rationale for a mandatory withdrawal requirement.

## Key Takeaways
- RMDs require mandatory minimum withdrawals from certain tax-deferred retirement accounts beginning at a specific age.
- This ensures the government eventually collects the deferred tax liability those accounts represent, rather than allowing indefinite deferral.
- Roth accounts have historically been treated differently, since their upfront tax payment removes the deferred tax liability RMDs are designed to collect.
