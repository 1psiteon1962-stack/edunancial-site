## Core Content

Lesson 5 established that withholding functions as an ongoing estimate of tax liability, paid throughout the year rather than in one lump sum at filing time. Income not automatically subject to withholding — self-employment income, certain investment income, and other sources — doesn't have this automatic mechanism in place. **Quarterly estimated tax payments** exist to extend the same underlying principle to this kind of income.

Here's the "why" this requirement exists: the overall tax system in many jurisdictions is generally designed around a **pay-as-you-go** principle — taxes are meant to be paid throughout the year as income is earned, not saved up and paid entirely at the time of filing a return. [VERIFIED FACT] For income subject to withholding, this happens automatically through the paycheck deduction process; for income without automatic withholding, quarterly estimated payments are the mechanism used to achieve the same pay-as-you-go effect, generally due at specific intervals throughout the year rather than all at once.

Failing to make adequate estimated payments throughout the year, when required, can result in the underpayment penalties discussed in the next lesson — this system exists specifically to avoid taxpayers going an entire year without paying anything toward their tax liability on this kind of income.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A self-employed consultant earns income throughout the year with no employer withholding taxes automatically on her behalf. To comply with the pay-as-you-go principle, she calculates and pays estimated taxes at specific intervals throughout the year, based on her projected annual income and tax liability, rather than waiting to pay her entire tax obligation in one lump sum when she files her return the following year. [ASSUMPTION] This mirrors, for her non-withheld income, the same ongoing payment pattern that an employee's paycheck withholding achieves automatically for wage income, keeping her generally in compliance with the underlying pay-as-you-go system requirement.
