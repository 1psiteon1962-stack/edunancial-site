## Core Content

A **pass-through entity** — common examples include sole proprietorships, partnerships, and, in many jurisdictions, LLCs that elect this treatment — doesn't pay income tax as a separate entity itself. Instead, the business's profit "passes through" directly to the owners, who report and pay tax on that profit as part of their own personal tax returns.

Here's the "why" this avoids the double taxation scenario introduced in Lesson 17: because the business itself isn't taxed as a separate entity, there's no first layer of tax at the business level before the profit even reaches the owner — profit is taxed exactly once, at the owner's personal tax rate, regardless of whether that profit is actually distributed to the owner in cash or simply retained in the business.

[VERIFIED FACT] This single-layer taxation is a significant reason pass-through structures are common for many smaller and mid-sized businesses, though the specific tax rules, including how self-employment tax (Lesson 14) applies and whether a specific business qualifies for certain deductions like the Qualified Business Income deduction (covered in a later lesson), vary by the specific type of pass-through entity and jurisdiction.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A small business organized as an LLC electing pass-through tax treatment generates $100,000 in annual profit. That $100,000 is reported on the owner's personal tax return and taxed once, at the owner's individual tax rates, regardless of whether the owner actually withdrew that profit as cash or reinvested it back into the business. [ASSUMPTION] There's no separate corporate-level tax applied to this $100,000 before it reaches the owner's personal return — illustrating the single-layer taxation that distinguishes pass-through treatment from the double-taxation possibility discussed for traditional corporations in the next lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- A pass-through entity's profit is taxed once, directly on the owners' personal tax returns, not at the business entity level.
- This avoids the double taxation possibility that can apply to traditional corporations.
- Common pass-through structures include sole proprietorships, partnerships, and LLCs electing this treatment.
