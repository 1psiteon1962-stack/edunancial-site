## Core Content

A **C-corporation** (a common term for a traditional corporate structure taxed as its own separate entity) generally pays income tax on its own profit at the entity level. If that after-tax profit is later distributed to shareholders as a dividend, the shareholders generally owe tax again on that dividend income at the individual level — creating the **double taxation** scenario introduced in Lesson 17, in direct contrast to Lesson 18's single-layer pass-through treatment.

Here's the "why" this structure can still make sense for certain businesses, despite this apparent tax disadvantage: C-corporations offer specific advantages in other areas — potentially easier access to certain types of investment capital and equity structures (connecting to concepts covered in this curriculum's BLUE track around venture capital and multiple classes of stock), potentially advantageous treatment for retained earnings not distributed as dividends, and specific benefits or requirements tied to certain business models or growth plans.

[VERIFIED FACT] Additionally, a business's actual effective double-taxation burden depends significantly on how much profit is retained within the business versus distributed as dividends, and on the specific tax rates applicable at both the corporate and individual level — meaning the practical impact of double taxation varies considerably by specific business circumstances, rather than being a fixed, universal penalty.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A rapidly growing company anticipates needing significant outside investment capital and plans to reinvest most profit back into growth rather than distributing dividends in its early years. Structuring as a C-corporation may better support its capital-raising strategy (connecting to venture capital concepts covered elsewhere in this curriculum), and since it's not distributing significant dividends currently, the practical double-taxation impact is minimal in these early years. [ASSUMPTION] A smaller, more mature business primarily interested in distributing most of its profit directly to its owner-operators each year might instead find pass-through treatment (Lesson 18) more tax-efficient for its specific situation, illustrating why the "better" structure genuinely depends on each business's specific circumstances and plans.

## Key Takeaways
- C-corporations can face double taxation: once at the corporate level, and again on dividends at the individual level.
- Despite this, C-corporation structure can still make sense for businesses prioritizing capital-raising flexibility or retaining most profit for reinvestment.
- The actual, practical double-taxation impact depends significantly on how much profit is distributed as dividends versus retained.
