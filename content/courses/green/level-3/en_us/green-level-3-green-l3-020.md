## Core Content

In the U.S. context, a **Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction** generally allows eligible owners of pass-through businesses (Lesson 18) to deduct a portion of their qualified business income when calculating their personal taxable income. [VERIFIED FACT] Specific eligibility requirements, income thresholds, and calculation rules for this type of deduction are set by tax law, are notably complex, vary by business type and income level, and are subject to legislative change — current rules should be verified directly rather than assumed.

Here's the general "why" a deduction like this might be introduced: it's sometimes discussed as a way to help address the tax rate gap between pass-through business income (taxed at individual rates, as covered in Lesson 18) and corporate income (which may be taxed at a different rate structure, as covered in Lesson 19) — providing pass-through business owners a form of tax relief intended to bring their effective tax treatment somewhat closer in line with corporate tax treatment.

This lesson deliberately doesn't attempt to specify exact eligibility thresholds or calculation formulas, since these types of provisions are especially prone to legislative change and vary significantly by jurisdiction — the conceptual purpose (partial relief specifically for pass-through business income) is the durable, transferable lesson here, not any specific numeric detail.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A small business owner operating as a pass-through entity generates qualified business income and, if she meets the applicable eligibility requirements in effect for her specific situation, may be able to deduct a portion of that income before calculating her personal taxable income — providing some relief intended to help address the tax rate gap between her pass-through treatment and the treatment a similarly profitable corporation might receive. [ASSUMPTION] Because eligibility and calculation details for this kind of deduction are complex and can change, she works with a tax professional each year to confirm exactly how the rules in effect apply to her specific business and income level, rather than relying on a general understanding alone.
