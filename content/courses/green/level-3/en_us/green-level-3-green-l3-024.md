## Core Content

This curriculum's WHITE track covers tax-loss harvesting and the wash-sale rule from an investing-strategy perspective. Here's the same concept from a specifically tax-focused angle: a **realized capital loss** — one actually locked in through a sale, not just an unrealized paper loss — can generally be used to offset capital gains for tax purposes, and in many tax systems, a limited amount of any remaining net loss can also offset a portion of ordinary income, with any further excess potentially carried forward to offset gains in future years.

Here's the "why" the **wash-sale rule** specifically limits this: [VERIFIED FACT] many tax systems disallow a claimed loss if the same or a substantially identical security is repurchased within a specific window around the sale, since allowing this would let a taxpayer claim a tax loss while effectively maintaining the exact same investment position — treated as an abuse of the loss-realization concept, and disallowed accordingly.

This is why the tax-focused view of this strategy emphasizes precise timing and documentation: a taxpayer needs to track not just that a loss was realized, but also confirm no wash-sale-triggering repurchase occurred within the relevant window, to ensure the claimed loss will actually be allowed when the tax return is filed.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor realizes a loss by selling a declining investment, intending to use that loss to offset capital gains elsewhere in her portfolio for tax purposes. If she repurchases the same or a substantially identical investment within the wash-sale window, tax authorities may disallow the claimed loss for that tax year, despite her genuine intention to harvest it. [ASSUMPTION] Careful tracking of both the sale and any subsequent purchases, relative to the applicable wash-sale window, is essential to ensure the loss she's counting on to offset her gains is actually allowed when she files her tax return.

## Key Takeaways
- A realized capital loss can generally offset capital gains, and a limited amount of ordinary income, for tax purposes.
- The wash-sale rule disallows a claimed loss if the same or a substantially identical security is repurchased within a specific window.
- This requires careful tracking of both sales and subsequent purchases to ensure a claimed loss is actually allowed at filing time.
