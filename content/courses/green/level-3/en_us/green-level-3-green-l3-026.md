## Core Content

**Sales tax nexus** generally refers to the connection a business must have with a specific jurisdiction before that jurisdiction can require the business to collect and remit sales tax on sales made to customers there. Historically, this connection was often based on **physical presence** — having a store, office, warehouse, or employees physically located in that jurisdiction.

Here's the "why" this traditional approach struggled as e-commerce grew: a business could sell significant volumes of goods to customers in a jurisdiction entirely online, with no physical presence there at all, generating substantial sales while collecting no sales tax under the old physical-presence standard — creating both a real revenue gap for jurisdictions and, some argued, an uneven playing field between local, physically present businesses required to collect sales tax and remote online sellers who weren't.

[VERIFIED FACT] In response, many jurisdictions have adopted **economic nexus** standards, generally requiring a business to collect sales tax once it exceeds certain sales volume or transaction count thresholds in a jurisdiction, regardless of physical presence — a legal and policy shift specifically driven by the rise of e-commerce. Specific thresholds and rules vary significantly by jurisdiction and continue to evolve, making this an area requiring ongoing, current guidance rather than static assumptions.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An online retailer with no physical stores, warehouses, or employees in a particular state sells a substantial volume of goods to customers there each year. Under an older, purely physical-presence nexus standard, this retailer might not have been required to collect sales tax on those sales at all. Under an economic nexus standard adopted by that state, once the retailer's sales volume or transaction count to customers in that state exceeds the applicable threshold, it becomes required to collect and remit sales tax there, despite having no physical presence — reflecting the policy shift this lesson describes.

## Key Takeaways
- Sales tax nexus determines when a jurisdiction can require a business to collect and remit sales tax.
- Traditional physical-presence standards struggled to address e-commerce, since online sellers could operate with no physical presence in a jurisdiction.
- Many jurisdictions have adopted economic nexus standards, based on sales volume or transaction thresholds, regardless of physical presence.
