## Core Content

Lesson 22 explained that a 1031 exchange defers, rather than eliminates, capital gains and depreciation recapture liability, carrying it forward attached to the replacement property's cost basis. The **step-up in basis** rule addresses what can eventually happen to that carried-forward liability upon the owner's death.

Here's the "why" this rule can meaningfully change the outcome: [VERIFIED FACT] in many jurisdictions, including the U.S. under current rules (which have changed over time and could change again), an inherited asset's cost basis is generally "stepped up" to its fair market value as of the date of the original owner's death, rather than carrying over the original owner's historical cost basis — meaning much or all of the capital gain that had built up (and, in some cases, been repeatedly deferred through prior 1031 exchanges) during the original owner's lifetime can effectively be eliminated for the heir, rather than being inherited along with the asset.

This connects directly to the deferred tax liability concept from Lesson 22: while a lifetime of deferral through repeated 1031 exchanges builds up an increasingly large deferred liability, a step-up in basis at death can be one of the ways that liability is ultimately resolved — not through eventually paying the deferred tax, but through the heir receiving a fresh, reset basis that erases the previously accumulated gain for tax purposes going forward.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor has completed several 1031 exchanges over her lifetime, accumulating a substantial deferred capital gains and depreciation recapture liability attached to her current property's low cost basis, as described in Lesson 22. Rather than ever selling in a fully taxable transaction, she holds the property until her death. [ASSUMPTION] If a step-up in basis rule applies under the specific laws in effect at that time, her heir inherits the property with its basis reset to its fair market value at her death — meaning the heir could potentially sell the property soon after inheriting it with little or no capital gains tax due, despite the substantial deferred gain that had built up over the original owner's lifetime.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does a step-up in basis generally mean?
2. Why can this rule effectively eliminate previously accumulated capital gain for an heir?

## Answer Key

1. An inherited asset's cost basis is generally reset to its fair market value as of the original owner's date of death, rather than carrying over the original owner's historical cost basis.
2. Because the reset basis means much or all of the gain that had built up during the original owner's lifetime is no longer reflected in the asset's basis for the heir's future tax purposes.
3. It represents one way a lifetime of deferred tax liability, built up through mechanisms like repeated 1031 exchanges, can ultimately be resolved — not through eventual payment, but through basis reset at death, under current rules in relevant jurisdictions.

## Key Takeaways
- A step-up in basis resets an inherited asset's cost basis to its fair market value at the original owner's death, in jurisdictions where this rule applies.
- This can effectively eliminate previously accumulated capital gain for tax purposes going forward, for the heir.
- This is one way a lifetime of deferred tax liability, such as from repeated 1031 exchanges, can ultimately be resolved rather than eventually paid.
