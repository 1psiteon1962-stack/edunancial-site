## Core Content

Charitable donations to qualifying organizations can generally be claimed as an itemized deduction (Lesson 9), reducing taxable income. Here's the "why" this specific type of deduction comes with documentation requirements that scale based on donation size: tax authorities need a way to verify that claimed charitable deductions reflect genuine donations, actually made, to genuinely qualifying organizations — without proper documentation, this deduction category would be especially vulnerable to inflated or entirely fabricated claims.

[VERIFIED FACT] Documentation requirements typically become more rigorous as donation amounts increase — smaller cash donations might only require a bank record or receipt, while larger donations, and particularly non-cash donations of property, often require more detailed contemporaneous written acknowledgment from the receiving organization, and in some cases, formal appraisals for significant non-cash property donations — the exact thresholds and requirements are set by tax law and should be verified directly for a specific situation.

This is why maintaining organized records of charitable giving throughout the year — receipts, bank statements, and written acknowledgments from organizations — matters significantly for anyone planning to itemize and claim charitable deductions, rather than attempting to reconstruct this documentation after the fact at filing time.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A taxpayer makes several charitable donations throughout the year: some smaller cash donations to a local charity, and one larger non-cash donation of property to another organization. [ASSUMPTION] For the smaller cash donations, bank records or simple receipts are likely sufficient documentation. For the larger non-cash donation, she would likely need a more detailed written acknowledgment from the receiving organization, and possibly a formal appraisal, given the donation's size and non-cash nature — illustrating why documentation requirements scale with the type and size of the donation, and why keeping organized records throughout the year, rather than scrambling at filing time, is the more reliable approach.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why do charitable deductions require specific supporting documentation?
2. Why do documentation requirements generally become more rigorous as donation size increases?
3. Why does this lesson recommend maintaining organized records throughout the year, rather than reconstructing them later?

## Answer Key

1. To verify that claimed deductions reflect genuine donations actually made to genuinely qualifying organizations, protecting against inflated or fabricated claims.
2. Because larger claims represent a greater potential tax impact and risk, so more rigorous documentation (detailed acknowledgments, appraisals for significant non-cash donations) helps verify their legitimacy.
3. Because reconstructing documentation after the fact, at filing time, is less reliable and more difficult than maintaining organized records as donations are actually made throughout the year.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the central "why" explaining the documentation requirement's purpose.
2. This explains the scaling logic behind increasingly rigorous requirements for larger donations.
3. This reinforces the practical, proactive recordkeeping habit the lesson recommends.

## Key Takeaways
- Charitable deductions require documentation to verify that claimed donations were genuinely made to qualifying organizations.
- Documentation requirements generally become more rigorous as donation size and complexity (especially for non-cash gifts) increase.
- Maintaining organized records of charitable giving throughout the year is more reliable than reconstructing documentation at filing time.
