## Core Content

The rise of remote and flexible work arrangements has created genuinely new complications connecting directly to two concepts covered earlier in this track: tax residency (Lesson 25) and nexus (Lesson 26).

Here's the "why" remote work specifically complicates these existing frameworks: an employee who lives in one jurisdiction but works remotely for an employer based in a different jurisdiction — or who travels and works from multiple jurisdictions over the course of a year — may trigger tax obligations, or create nexus for their employer, in jurisdictions that wouldn't have been relevant under more traditional, single-location employment arrangements. [VERIFIED FACT] Some jurisdictions have specific rules addressing where income is taxed based on where work is actually physically performed, rather than solely where the employer or employee is otherwise based, and these rules — along with any reciprocity agreements between specific jurisdictions reducing the resulting double-taxation burden — vary significantly and continue to evolve as remote work arrangements become more common.

This is why both remote employees and the employers who hire them increasingly need to carefully track where work is actually being performed, rather than assuming a single, simple "home jurisdiction" applies to all of the resulting tax obligations.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An employee lives and is a tax resident of one state but works remotely for an employer based in a different state, and also spends several months of the year working from a third state while traveling. [ASSUMPTION] Depending on the specific rules and any reciprocity agreements applicable to these particular jurisdictions, she could potentially owe tax, or her employer could face nexus obligations, in more than one of these states — a complication that simply didn't exist in the same way for a traditional employee working entirely from a single physical office location, illustrating why remote work arrangements require more careful tax planning than traditional employment historically did.
