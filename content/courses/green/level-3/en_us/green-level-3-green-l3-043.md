## Core Content

A **home office deduction** generally allows a portion of home-related expenses to be deducted for a space used regularly for business purposes. [VERIFIED FACT] In many tax systems, this deduction generally requires the specific space to be used **exclusively** for business — not used for both business and significant personal purposes — along with being used regularly for business activity.

Here's the "why" this exclusive-use requirement exists: without it, distinguishing legitimate business-related home expenses from ordinary personal living expenses would become genuinely difficult to verify and enforce — a dining room table used occasionally for work but primarily for family meals, for example, mixes personal and business use in a way that makes calculating a clean, defensible business deduction difficult, and would be especially prone to abuse if allowed without a clear dividing line.

This is why a room or clearly defined space used exclusively and regularly for business — even if physically small — is more likely to qualify than a much larger, shared-use space that's occasionally used for work but primarily serves other, personal household functions, regardless of how much actual work happens to occur there.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A self-employed consultant converts a spare bedroom into a dedicated office space, used exclusively for client meetings and business work, with no other personal use of that specific room. This space would likely qualify for a home office deduction under a typical exclusive-use requirement. [ASSUMPTION] A different self-employed person who works from her kitchen table, which is also used daily for family meals and other household activities, would likely not qualify for the same deduction for that space, despite genuinely doing real business work there, because the space isn't used exclusively for business — illustrating why the specific, dedicated nature of the space matters as much as the actual work performed there.

3. Because the space isn't used exclusively for business — its mixed personal and business use fails the exclusive-use requirement, regardless of how much genuine work occurs there.

## Key Takeaways
- A home office deduction generally requires the specific space to be used exclusively, not just regularly, for business purposes.
- This requirement exists to create a clear, verifiable dividing line between business and personal expenses.
- A shared-use space, even with genuine business activity, typically doesn't qualify if it's also used for significant personal purposes.
