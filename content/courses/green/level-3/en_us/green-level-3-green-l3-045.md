## Core Content

Lesson 12 established that choosing between Traditional and Roth accounts is fundamentally a bet on future tax rates, with genuine uncertainty about which choice will ultimately prove more favorable. **Tax diversification** applies a familiar risk-management principle from this curriculum's WHITE track — spreading exposure across different, not-perfectly-correlated positions — to this specific tax uncertainty.

Here's the "why" holding a mix of account types hedges against this uncertainty rather than resolving it outright: by holding some assets in Traditional (tax-deferred), some in Roth (generally tax-free at qualifying withdrawal), and some in a standard taxable brokerage account, a person creates flexibility to draw from whichever type of account makes the most tax sense in any given future year, rather than being locked entirely into one bet about future tax rates made years or decades in advance.

[VERIFIED FACT] This flexibility can be genuinely valuable in retirement specifically: a retiree with tax-diversified accounts can strategically choose which accounts to draw from each year based on their actual tax situation at that time — potentially drawing more from taxable or Roth accounts in a year when additional Traditional withdrawals would push them into a higher bracket, for example — a level of tax planning flexibility not available to someone holding only one account type.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A retiree with a mix of Traditional, Roth, and taxable account balances can choose, each year, which accounts to draw from based on her actual tax situation that year. [ASSUMPTION] In a year when she has other significant taxable income, she might draw more from her Roth or taxable accounts to avoid pushing herself into a higher bracket through additional Traditional withdrawals; in a lower-income year, she might draw more from her Traditional account instead, taking advantage of her lower bracket that year — a flexible, year-by-year optimization simply not available to a retiree whose savings sit entirely within a single account type.
