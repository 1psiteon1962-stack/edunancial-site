## Core Content

**Tax filing** is the annual process of reporting income and calculating tax liability for a specific past year. **Tax planning** is a distinct, forward-looking activity, aimed at structuring financial decisions in advance to achieve a more favorable tax outcome — and here's the "why" these are fundamentally different activities with different timelines: many of the most effective tax strategies covered throughout this track require decisions made well before, sometimes years before, an eventual filing deadline, not adjustments squeezed in during the filing process itself.

[VERIFIED FACT] Consider several examples already covered in this track: choosing between Traditional and Roth accounts (Lesson 12) requires an upfront decision, not one made retroactively at filing time; a 1031 exchange (Lesson 22) has strict timing requirements that must be initiated at the time of a property sale, not discovered as an option months later while filing taxes; tax-loss harvesting (Lesson 24) depends on transactions executed during the actual tax year, not decisions made after the year has already closed; and business structure choice (Lesson 17) is typically established at formation, not something easily or costlessly changed retroactively.

This is why the most effective tax outcomes generally come from ongoing, proactive planning throughout the year — and ideally across multiple years, given decisions like retirement account strategy and business structure that play out over long horizons — rather than treating tax season as the primary or only occasion to think about taxes.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A taxpayer who only thinks about taxes during the annual filing process discovers, while preparing her return, that a 1031 exchange could have deferred significant tax on a property sale completed months earlier — but by the time she's filing, the strict timing windows for initiating that exchange have already passed, making the strategy unavailable to her for that transaction. [ASSUMPTION] A taxpayer who instead engages in ongoing tax planning throughout the year would have identified and executed on that opportunity at the time of the actual sale, illustrating why waiting until filing season to think about tax strategy can mean missing opportunities that needed to be acted on much earlier.

## Key Takeaways
- Tax filing reports past events; tax planning proactively structures future decisions for a better tax outcome.
- Many effective strategies covered in this track require decisions made well before, sometimes years before, a filing deadline.
- Ongoing, multi-year tax planning captures opportunities that would otherwise be missed if taxes are only considered during the annual filing process.
