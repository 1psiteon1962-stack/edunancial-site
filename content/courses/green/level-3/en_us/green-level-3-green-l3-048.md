## Core Content

A **charitable trust** is a legal structure through which assets are contributed for eventual charitable benefit, often while providing the original donor (or other named beneficiaries) with some form of ongoing benefit — such as income — for a specified period before the remaining assets ultimately pass to the designated charity. [VERIFIED FACT] Various types of charitable trusts exist with different structures, income arrangements, and tax treatments, and the specific rules governing them are complex and vary by jurisdiction.

Here's the "why" this structure can combine genuine philanthropic intent with tax planning benefits: contributing appreciated assets to a properly structured charitable trust can potentially provide the donor a charitable deduction (per Lesson 30's documentation requirements) while also, in some structures, avoiding immediate capital gains tax on the appreciated asset's value that would otherwise apply if it were simply sold outright — and, connecting to Lesson 28's estate and gift tax discussion, can also serve as part of a broader estate planning strategy, potentially reducing the size of a taxable estate while still providing income to the donor or other named beneficiaries during their lifetime.

This is why charitable trusts are generally used by donors who have both a genuine philanthropic goal and significant appreciated assets or estate planning needs — the structure is specifically designed to serve both purposes together, not as a purely tax-motivated maneuver disconnected from actual charitable intent.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A donor holds a significantly appreciated asset and wants to support a cause she cares about while also managing her overall estate planning and tax situation. She contributes the asset to a properly structured charitable trust, potentially receiving an income stream from the trust during her lifetime, a charitable deduction reflecting the future value that will ultimately go to charity, and avoiding an immediate capital gains tax event on the asset's full appreciated value. [ASSUMPTION] Upon her eventual passing (or after the trust's specified term), the remaining trust assets pass to the designated charity, fulfilling her philanthropic goal while having also provided meaningful tax planning benefits throughout the arrangement — illustrating how this structure serves both purposes together.
