## Core Content

This track has covered many distinct tax concepts: marginal rates, deductions, tax-deferred accounts, business structure, capital gains, international income, and more. This lesson revisits them together through a single, recurring theme: **timing**.

Withholding and estimated taxes (Lessons 5, 15) are entirely about matching payment timing to income timing. The Traditional-versus-Roth choice (Lesson 12) is a bet about the timing of tax rates — now versus later. A 1031 exchange (Lesson 22) and depreciation recapture (Lesson 21) both involve deferring tax to a later point in time, with specific timing requirements governing exactly when and how that deferral works. Capital gains holding periods (Lesson 23) directly reward the passage of time. Tax-loss harvesting (Lesson 24) depends on realizing a loss within a specific tax year, avoiding a specific timing window around any repurchase. The step-up in basis (Lesson 29) resolves a lifetime of deferred timing at a single, final point.

Here's the "why" tying every decision back to timing leads to better decisions: none of these concepts are really about avoiding tax altogether — they're about influencing *when* tax is paid, at what rate, and under what specific rules in effect at that time. A taxpayer who asks, for any given decision, "what does this do to the timing of my tax liability, and is that timing advantageous given what I know and can reasonably plan for?" is applying a single, consistent lens across very different-looking tools.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A taxpayer facing several simultaneous decisions — whether to realize a capital loss before year-end (Lesson 24), whether to contribute to a Traditional or Roth account this year (Lesson 12), and whether to accelerate or defer a business expense into the current or following tax year — evaluates each through the same timing lens: what does each choice do to when income is recognized, when deductions are claimed, and at what rate the resulting tax liability will ultimately be calculated? [ASSUMPTION] Rather than treating these as three unrelated decisions, she recognizes they're all variations on the same underlying question, allowing her to reason about them consistently rather than starting from scratch with each one.
