## Core Content

This curriculum's PURPLE track established that an enforceable contract requires offer, acceptance, and consideration, and that ambiguous terms create the conditions for disputes. Here's the "why" this applies directly, and with real financial stakes, to sales agreements specifically: a sales contract — whether a simple purchase order or a complex, negotiated deal — is a legally binding contract, and every principle covered in the PURPLE track (breach remedies, liquidated damages, indemnification, dispute resolution) applies to it exactly as it would to any other agreement.

A sales team focused primarily on closing the deal can treat contract terms as a formality to get through quickly, rather than as the actual document that will govern what happens if something goes wrong — payment terms, delivery obligations, what happens if the customer doesn't pay, and how disputes get resolved all deserve the same careful attention as any other contract covered throughout this curriculum.

What happens, under your current sales contract template, if a customer simply doesn't pay on time? If you don't know the specific answer, what does that suggest about how carefully that document was actually drafted?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A sales team uses a simple, informally drafted sales agreement for a significant transaction, focused on closing the deal quickly rather than on the specific contract language. [ASSUMPTION] When the customer later fails to pay on time, the business discovers its sales agreement doesn't clearly specify late payment consequences, interest, or a dispute resolution process — exactly the kind of ambiguity the PURPLE track warned creates disputes that are harder to resolve, turning what should have been a straightforward collections matter into a much more difficult, costly situation.
