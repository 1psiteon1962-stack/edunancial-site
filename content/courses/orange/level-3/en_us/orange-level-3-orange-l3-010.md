## Learning Objectives

## Core Content

A **loss leader** strategy sells one product at or below cost specifically to drive traffic or purchases of other, more profitable products or services. Here's the "why" this can be a genuinely sound strategy or a genuinely dangerous one, depending entirely on discipline: connecting directly to the unit economics concept covered in this curriculum's BLUE track, a loss leader only makes sense if the business can actually demonstrate, with real data, that customers acquired through the loss-leading product reliably go on to purchase the profitable products at a rate and margin that more than compensates for the initial loss.

Here's the risk: it's easy to assume this cross-sell effect is happening without ever rigorously measuring it — a business can convince itself a loss leader is "working" because the loss-leading product is popular, without confirming that the assumed downstream profitable purchases are actually materializing at the rate the strategy depends on. Popularity of the loss leader itself is not evidence the strategy is profitable overall.

Before running a loss leader promotion, what specific data would you need to track to confirm customers are actually completing the profitable follow-on purchase, and at what rate would that need to happen to make the whole strategy worthwhile?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business sells a specific product at a loss, assuming customers will then purchase a separate, profitable add-on at a high enough rate to make the overall strategy worthwhile. [ASSUMPTION] The loss-leading product becomes very popular, and the business treats this popularity as evidence the strategy is succeeding — without ever actually measuring what percentage of those customers go on to buy the profitable add-on. If that follow-on purchase rate is actually much lower than assumed, the business could be running a strategy that's popular but genuinely unprofitable, precisely because it never tracked the unit economics the strategy depends on.
