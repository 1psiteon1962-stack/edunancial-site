## Core Content

**Word of mouth** — customers recommending a business to others without a paid incentive — is often the cheapest acquisition channel a business has, since it carries no direct media spend. Here's the "why" this doesn't make it the obviously best channel to build a strategy around: unlike paid advertising, which can be scaled, tested, and reasonably forecasted (within the probability bounds covered in Lesson 3), word of mouth is genuinely difficult to control, predict, or deliberately scale on demand — it depends on customer satisfaction and social dynamics largely outside the business's direct control.

This connects to the risk-return framework covered throughout the GOLD track: word of mouth offers an attractive "return" (low cost per acquired customer) but comes with genuine uncertainty and lack of control as its specific risk — a business that depends heavily on word of mouth for growth is making a bet on a channel it can influence (through great product and service) but can't directly control or reliably scale up when it needs growth on a specific timeline.

If your business currently depends heavily on word of mouth, what would happen to your growth plans if that channel simply slowed down for reasons outside your control? What controllable channel would you need to activate to compensate?
