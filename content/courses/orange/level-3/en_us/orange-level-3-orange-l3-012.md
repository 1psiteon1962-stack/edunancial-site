## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why different marketing channels carry meaningfully different risk profiles, not just different costs
- Connect this concept to the diversification principle covered in this curriculum's WHITE and GOLD tracks
- Explain why relying entirely on one channel concentrates risk, regardless of its apparent efficiency

## Core Content

This curriculum's WHITE and GOLD tracks both cover diversification as a way to reduce reliance on any single, potentially volatile source of risk. Here's the "why" this same principle applies directly to a marketing channel mix: each channel — paid search, social media advertising, word of mouth (Lesson 11), content marketing, partnerships — carries its own distinct risk profile, including sensitivity to platform algorithm changes, rising competition for ad space, or regulatory shifts affecting a specific channel.

A business that concentrates its acquisition entirely in one channel, even one currently performing efficiently, is taking on concentration risk directly analogous to holding a single stock or a single asset class covered elsewhere in this curriculum — a single platform policy change, algorithm update, or cost increase can suddenly and significantly disrupt that channel's performance, with no other channel positioned to absorb the impact.

If your business currently gets most of its customers from one dominant channel, what specifically could disrupt that channel, and how quickly could you meaningfully ramp up an alternative if it did?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business drives the vast majority of its customer acquisition through a single advertising platform, appreciating its currently low cost per acquisition. [ASSUMPTION] When that platform significantly changes its algorithm or pricing structure, the business's acquisition cost spikes and volume drops sharply, with no other established channel mature enough to absorb the gap — a concentration risk directly analogous to holding a single asset class, as covered in the WHITE and GOLD tracks, that a more diversified channel mix would have helped mitigate.
