## Core Content

**Attribution** generally means determining which specific marketing touchpoint (an ad, an email, a social post) should get credit for a resulting sale. Here's the "why" this is genuinely harder than it sounds: most customers interact with a business through multiple touchpoints before purchasing — seeing an ad, later reading a review, then clicking an email link, then finally buying — and a simple attribution approach has to somehow decide which of these gets the credit, even though the sale likely depended on the combination.

A common but limited approach, **last-click attribution**, credits only the final touchpoint before purchase — [VERIFIED FACT] this systematically undervalues earlier-stage touchpoints (like the initial ad that created awareness) that may have been essential to the sale happening at all, even though they weren't the final click. Different attribution models (first-click, multi-touch, time-decay, and others) will generally produce different conclusions about which channels deserve credit and budget, from the exact same underlying customer behavior data.

If your business currently relies on last-click attribution, what earlier-stage marketing activity might be systematically undervalued and at risk of having its budget cut, even though it's quietly doing essential work?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A customer sees a social media ad, later reads an online review, and finally clicks an email link before completing a purchase. Under last-click attribution, the email gets full credit for the sale. [ASSUMPTION] If the business, based on this attribution, decides to cut spending on the social media ads that actually created the initial awareness, it may unknowingly be reducing an essential part of its customer journey — the email only worked because the earlier touchpoints had already built interest, a contribution last-click attribution entirely fails to capture.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why is attributing a sale to a single marketing touchpoint genuinely difficult?
2. What is the general problem with last-click attribution specifically?
3. Why can different attribution models produce different, equally defensible conclusions from the same data?

## Answer Key

1. Because most customers interact with multiple touchpoints before purchasing, and the sale likely depended on the combination, not any single one.
2. It systematically undervalues earlier-stage touchpoints that may have been essential to the sale happening at all, crediting only the final click.
3. Because each model makes a different assumption about how credit should be distributed across the customer journey, and there's no single objectively "correct" way to divide that credit.

## Answers and Explanations

2. This is the specific, commonly cited limitation the lesson focuses on.
3. This reinforces that attribution involves genuine interpretive choices, not a single objective answer.
