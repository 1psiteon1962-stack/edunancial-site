## Core Content

This track's Lesson 7 covered legal exposure from marketing claims; here's the same principle applied specifically to individual sales conversations. A salesperson eager to close a deal may promise something — a delivery date, a specific outcome, a level of support — that goes beyond what the business can actually reliably deliver. Here's the "why" this creates real exposure, connecting directly to the PURPLE track's contract and misrepresentation principles: a specific promise made during a sales process, even verbally, can potentially become part of the actual agreement, or at minimum, evidence supporting a claim if the promise isn't honored.

Beyond the legal risk, overpromising creates a second, distinct risk: reputational damage (Lesson 4) that occurs even when no legal claim is ever filed — a customer who feels misled will generally share that experience, and unlike a legal dispute that might be resolved privately, a damaged reputation from a broken sales promise can spread well beyond the specific customer affected.

Before a salesperson makes a specific promise to close a deal, is that promise something the business can actually, reliably deliver — and if not, what's the honest alternative to say instead?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A salesperson, eager to close a deal, verbally promises a delivery timeline the business's operations team has not actually confirmed it can meet. [ASSUMPTION] When the delivery is delayed well beyond that promised timeline, the customer feels misled, potentially raising a legal claim connecting to the PURPLE track's misrepresentation principles, and separately, sharing their negative experience publicly — creating both a legal exposure and a reputational one from the same single overpromise, illustrating why sales integrity carries stakes well beyond the individual deal being closed.
