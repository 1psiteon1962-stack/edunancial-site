## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track covered customer concentration risk in the context of business valuation — a business overly dependent on one customer sells for a lower multiple. Here's the "why" this same risk matters well before any eventual sale, as a live, present-day sales and revenue risk: a business generating a large share of revenue from one or a few customers is exposed, right now, to the possibility that any one of those relationships ends, is renegotiated unfavorably, or is disrupted by that customer's own financial troubles — with consequences for cash flow, staffing, and operations that arrive immediately, not just at a future valuation event.

This is why a sales team's growth strategy should actively track customer concentration as an ongoing metric, not treat closing one large account as an unambiguous win without also considering what dependency that account creates. Landing a single large customer that comes to represent a majority of revenue creates a form of concentration risk directly analogous to an undiversified investment portfolio, discussed elsewhere in this curriculum.

If your largest customer left tomorrow, what would happen to your revenue, your team, and your ability to make payroll? What would need to be true about your other customers or your reserves to weather that?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A sales team closes a single large account that quickly grows to represent 50% of the business's total revenue, and the team celebrates this as a major win. [ASSUMPTION] Months later, that customer experiences its own financial difficulties and significantly reduces its orders — the business's revenue is immediately and severely affected, a live consequence of the concentration risk that was building the whole time, well before any future sale or valuation event would have made that risk visible on paper as the BLUE track describes.
