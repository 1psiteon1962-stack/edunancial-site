## Core Content

Partnering with an influencer or brand ambassador to promote a business is, at its core, a contractual relationship — connecting directly to Lesson 8's point that sales and marketing agreements need the same rigor as any other contract covered in the PURPLE track. This includes clear terms about content approval, exclusivity, required disclosures (many jurisdictions require paid partnerships to be clearly disclosed to the audience), and what happens if either party doesn't fulfill their obligations.

Here's the "why" this partnership also carries a distinct reputational risk beyond a typical vendor contract, connecting to Lesson 4's discussion of brand vulnerability: a business effectively ties a portion of its own reputation to the influencer's ongoing personal conduct and public perception, which the business doesn't control. If the influencer becomes involved in an unrelated controversy, that reputational damage can transfer to the partnering business by association, regardless of anything the business itself did.

Before entering an influencer partnership, what contractual protections would you want in place if the influencer's own conduct later created a reputational problem for your business, and how would you disclose the partnership properly to comply with applicable rules?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business partners with a popular influencer to promote its product, without a clearly drafted contract specifying content standards, disclosure requirements, or an exit provision if the relationship needs to end. [ASSUMPTION] The influencer later becomes involved in an unrelated public controversy, and the business finds its own reputation affected by association, with no clear contractual mechanism to quickly end the partnership or distance itself — illustrating both the contractual gap this lesson warns about and the specific reputational tie-in risk unique to this type of marketing partnership.

## Key Takeaways
- Influencer partnerships are contractual relationships requiring the same rigor covered for sales contracts generally.
- These partnerships also tie a business's reputation to someone outside its direct control, a distinct risk beyond a typical vendor agreement.
- Clear contract terms and disclosure compliance should be established before entering this type of partnership.
