## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track covered the MVP concept — testing a core assumption cheaply before committing significant resources. Market research serves a related, earlier-stage purpose: gathering information about customer needs, preferences, and likely demand before finalizing a product or campaign decision. Here's the "why" this genuinely reduces risk without eliminating it entirely: research methods — surveys, focus groups, market analysis — provide real, useful signal about likely customer response, but customers describing hypothetical future behavior in a research setting don't always behave the same way when actually facing a real purchase decision with real money and real competing options.

This is why market research should inform, not replace, the kind of real-world testing an MVP provides — research reduces uncertainty and helps avoid obviously poor decisions, but the genuinely reliable test of actual demand is still real customers making real purchase decisions, echoing exactly why the BLUE track emphasized testing assumptions with real, if minimal, market exposure rather than relying entirely on research or assumption alone.

Before treating market research results as confirmation that a product or campaign will succeed, what would still need to be true when real customers face the actual purchase decision, not just the hypothetical one posed in a research setting?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business conducts extensive market research before launching a new product, with survey respondents expressing strong interest and stated willingness to purchase. [ASSUMPTION] Upon actual launch, real purchase behavior falls meaningfully short of what the research suggested — a common pattern reflecting the genuine gap between hypothetical stated intentions and real purchase decisions involving actual money, illustrating why the research was valuable for reducing risk and informing the launch, but never eliminated the underlying uncertainty the way an actual market test, echoing the BLUE track's MVP concept, would have more reliably revealed.

## Key Takeaways
- Market research genuinely reduces risk by providing real signal about customer needs and preferences before a major decision.
- It can never fully eliminate uncertainty, since hypothetical research behavior doesn't always match real purchase decisions.
