## Core Content

**Positioning** generally refers to how a business wants customers to perceive its product relative to alternatives — as premium, as budget-friendly, as a specialized solution for a specific need. Here's the "why" this perception, not the product's actual category, determines who a business is really competing against in the customer's mind: a specific product might technically belong to one industry category, but if customers perceive and evaluate it against a different set of alternatives, that perceived competitive set — not the technical category — is what actually shapes the customer's purchase decision.

This is why a business's assumed competitive set can differ meaningfully from its actual one: a company might believe it competes against other providers in its narrow technical category, while customers are actually comparing it against a broader or different set of alternatives that solve the same underlying customer need in a different way. Pricing and messaging built around the wrong competitive set can miss the actual decision customers are making entirely.

Who do your customers actually compare you against when deciding whether to buy — not who you think your competitors are based on your industry category, but who they're mentally weighing you against when making the decision?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business assumes its main competitors are other companies offering a technically similar product, and prices and messages accordingly. [ASSUMPTION] Customer research reveals that many customers are actually comparing this product against a different, broader category of solutions that address the same underlying need in a different way — meaning the business's actual competitive set, as perceived by customers, is different from the one it assumed, and its pricing and messaging strategy, built around the wrong comparison set, may be missing the actual decision customers are making.
