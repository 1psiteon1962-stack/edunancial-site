## Core Content

This curriculum's GREEN track covered economic nexus — a threshold, based on sales volume or transaction count, that can trigger a sales tax collection obligation regardless of physical presence in a jurisdiction. Here's the "why" this connects directly to marketing strategy, not just to the sales that eventually result from it: a deliberate marketing push into a new geographic market, if successful, can generate enough sales volume in that jurisdiction to cross the economic nexus threshold relatively quickly — meaning a marketing expansion decision can create tax compliance obligations well before the business has built out any other operational presence there.

This is why marketing expansion into new geographic markets shouldn't be planned in isolation from tax compliance: a marketing team celebrating strong results in a newly targeted region might be unaware that those same results have quietly triggered a sales tax collection obligation the business isn't yet prepared to fulfill, creating a compliance gap that grows more costly the longer it goes unaddressed.

Before launching an aggressive marketing push into a new geographic region, has anyone checked what sales volume in that region would trigger a tax nexus obligation, and is the business prepared to comply once that threshold is likely to be crossed?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business launches a successful, deliberate marketing campaign targeting a specific new state, generating strong sales growth there within a few months. [ASSUMPTION] That same sales growth, driven directly by the marketing campaign, crosses the state's economic nexus threshold for sales tax collection, discussed in the GREEN track — but because marketing and tax compliance weren't coordinating on this expansion, the business doesn't realize it needs to begin collecting and remitting sales tax in that state until well after the obligation has already begun, creating a retroactive compliance issue that a simple pre-expansion check would have avoided.
