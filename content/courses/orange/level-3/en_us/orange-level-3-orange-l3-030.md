## Core Content

This curriculum's GOLD track established that a sound investment thesis should be falsifiable — specifying in advance what evidence would indicate it's wrong. Here's the "why" a marketing plan benefits from the exact same discipline: a plan built with specific, predetermined checkpoints ("if conversion rate is below X by week 4, we pause and reassess") forces an honest evaluation of whether the underlying assumptions are actually holding up, rather than continuing to execute a plan on faith simply because it was the original plan.

This differs meaningfully from a rigid plan that simply proceeds through its predetermined steps regardless of early results — a marketing plan without falsifiable checkpoints can absorb disappointing early results as "just needing more time," the same unfalsifiable reasoning pattern the GOLD track specifically warned against, continuing to spend against an assumption that may already be disproven.

Before launching your next campaign, what specific, predetermined result at what specific checkpoint would tell you the underlying assumption isn't working, prompting a genuine pause and reassessment rather than continued spending?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A marketing team launches a new campaign with an assumed conversion rate baked into the budget, but without any predetermined checkpoint for reassessing that assumption. [ASSUMPTION] When early results come in well below the assumed rate, the team continues spending, treating the shortfall as temporary and expecting it to improve — the same unfalsifiable reasoning pattern the GOLD track warned against, absorbing disconfirming evidence rather than treating it as a genuine signal to pause, a mistake a predetermined checkpoint ("reassess if conversion is below X by week 2") would have specifically prevented.
