## Core Content

This curriculum's PURPLE track covered trademark protection and its continued-use requirement. Here's the "why" the timing of securing this protection matters as much as the protection itself: a business that invests heavily in a brand name, logo, and marketing campaign before confirming that mark is actually available and can be legally protected risks discovering, only after significant investment, that another business already holds conflicting trademark rights — potentially forcing an expensive rebrand after substantial marketing spend has already gone toward building recognition for a name the business may not be able to keep using.

This is why a trademark search and application process should generally happen before, not after, committing significant marketing budget to a new brand identity — the cost of this upfront legal step is generally far smaller than the cost of rebranding after a conflict is discovered, echoing the broader proactive-versus-reactive legal planning theme covered throughout the PURPLE track.

Before launching a significant marketing campaign around a new brand name or logo, has a trademark search actually confirmed the mark is available, or is the marketing team building recognition for a name that hasn't yet been legally secured?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business develops a new brand name and invests significantly in marketing and customer recognition before ever conducting a trademark search. [ASSUMPTION] After this substantial investment, the business discovers another company already holds trademark rights to a confusingly similar name in the same industry — forcing an expensive rebrand, discarding the marketing investment already made, and illustrating why the modest cost of an upfront trademark search and application, completed before the branding investment, would have been far cheaper than the actual outcome.
