## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track established that growth with negative unit economics doesn't solve a business's problems — it accelerates them, since each new customer added is a loss, not a gain. Here's the "why" this exact caution applies directly to marketing-driven growth strategies specifically: a marketing team focused on growth metrics — new customers, new revenue, expanding market share — can be scaling a customer base whose underlying unit economics don't actually support profitable growth, celebrating the growth itself as success while the business's true financial position quietly worsens with every new customer acquired.

This is why "we're growing" is not, by itself, evidence that a strategy is working — connecting directly to the BLUE track's core caution — growth is only genuinely positive if the underlying unit economics are sound; growth built on negative unit economics is simply a faster path to the same problem, dressed up as a marketing success story.

Before celebrating a growth marketing win, have you confirmed the unit economics behind each new customer are actually positive, or is the growth itself being treated as sufficient evidence of success on its own?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A marketing team celebrates a successful quarter of rapid customer growth, driven by an aggressive, expensive acquisition campaign. [ASSUMPTION] A closer look at the unit economics behind this specific growth reveals that each new customer, once fully accounted for acquisition cost and expected lifetime value, is actually a net loss for the business — meaning this "successful" growth quarter, exactly as the BLUE track warned, has accelerated the business's underlying financial problem rather than solved it, even though every surface-level marketing metric looked positive.
