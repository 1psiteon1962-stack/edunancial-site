## Core Content

Lesson 19 established that a commission structure shapes salesperson behavior according to its specific mechanics, not the intentions behind it. Here's the "why" designing that structure is ultimately a leadership decision, not something to be delegated entirely to finance or sales operations without broader input: the specific behaviors a compensation plan encourages — collaboration versus internal competition, short-term closing versus long-term relationship building, individual performance versus team performance — directly shape the sales organization's culture, not just its output.

This is why leadership needs to weigh compensation design against the kind of sales culture and long-term customer relationships the business actually wants, not just against short-term revenue targets — a plan that maximizes immediate sales performance while quietly encouraging behavior that erodes team cohesion or customer trust reflects a leadership choice about trade-offs, whether or not that trade-off was made deliberately.

What kind of sales culture do you actually want to build — and does your current compensation structure genuinely encourage that culture, or does it just happen to reward whatever behavior maximizes short-term numbers?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business designs its sales compensation plan purely around maximizing individual short-term sales performance, delegating the decision entirely to a finance team optimizing for immediate revenue targets. [ASSUMPTION] Over time, this structure encourages internal competition that undermines collaboration, and a focus on quick closes that damages long-term customer relationships — outcomes that reflect a genuine leadership choice about organizational culture and priorities, even though no one explicitly decided "let's undermine collaboration," illustrating why compensation design deserves leadership-level consideration of its cultural consequences, not just its immediate incentive mechanics.
