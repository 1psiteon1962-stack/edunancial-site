## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Trace how a single hypothetical brand crisis simultaneously activates legal, financial, and leadership considerations
- Identify which specific concepts from earlier in this curriculum become relevant during this kind of event
- Practice recognizing relevant cross-color connections without being told which ones apply

## Core Content

[HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO — ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED FACT] Imagine a business faces a sudden, public controversy — a product issue, a viral customer complaint, or an executive's public misstep — that generates significant negative attention within days. This is a hypothetical stress-test scenario, not a prediction about any specific real company or event.

Rather than walking through every implication for you, consider what you've learned throughout this curriculum and identify: What legal exposure might this create, and which specific concepts from the PURPLE track would apply? What happens to cash flow if customers pause or cancel orders, and how does the business's current working capital position (BLUE track) affect how much time it has to respond? What does this do to the goodwill and reputation asset discussed earlier in this track, and how quickly could that value erode? What decisions fall specifically to leadership, versus what can be handled operationally? And what role does the business's existing insurance coverage (BLUE and PURPLE tracks) play in whether some of the financial impact is actually covered?

Try to map out the full cascade yourself before checking the worked example — which of these did you identify, and which didn't occur to you at first?

## Worked Example

[HYPOTHETICAL — ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT A PREDICTION] A business facing this kind of hypothetical crisis finds itself needing to simultaneously: consult legal counsel about potential liability exposure and any required public statements (PURPLE track); assess how a sudden drop in near-term sales affects its cash position and whether it has adequate reserves (BLUE track's working capital concept); recognize that its brand and reputation — its least liquid asset (Lesson 4) — is being damaged in real time, with recovery likely to take far longer than the crisis itself; and rely on leadership to make fast, high-stakes decisions with incomplete information, weighing legal advice, financial constraints, and reputational considerations against each other simultaneously, rather than treating any single department's perspective as sufficient on its own.
