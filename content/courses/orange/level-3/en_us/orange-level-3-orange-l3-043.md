## Core Content

A customer referral program — offering existing customers a reward for bringing in new business — is designed and discussed primarily as a marketing tactic. Here's the "why" it's simultaneously a legal offer, connecting directly to the contract formation principles covered in this curriculum's PURPLE track: when a business publishes specific referral program terms ("refer a friend, get $50"), and a customer acts on those terms by actually making a qualifying referral, this can form the elements of an enforceable agreement — offer, and acceptance through the customer's performance — obligating the business to actually provide the promised reward under the terms as stated.

This is why vague or poorly specified referral program terms create genuine dispute risk, echoing the PURPLE track's broader caution about ambiguous contract language: if the program doesn't clearly define what counts as a "qualifying" referral, when the reward is actually paid, or what happens in edge cases (a referred customer who cancels shortly after signing up, for example), the business may face disputes with customers who reasonably interpreted the ambiguous terms in their own favor.

Before launching a referral program, are its terms specific enough to clearly define what qualifies, when the reward is paid, and how edge cases are handled — the same clarity standard the PURPLE track emphasized for any other contract?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business launches a referral program with a simple, vague promise: "refer a friend, get rewarded." A customer refers several friends, some of whom sign up briefly and then cancel almost immediately. [ASSUMPTION] Without clear terms specifying whether a reward is earned upon signup or only after a minimum retention period, the business faces a genuine dispute with the customer about whether these particular referrals actually qualify — a problem a more specifically drafted program, applying the same contract clarity principles covered in the PURPLE track, would have avoided from the outset.
