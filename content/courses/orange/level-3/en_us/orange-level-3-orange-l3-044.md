## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track established that gross margin and cost structure determine how much room a business actually has to absorb price competition or reinvest in growth. Here's the "why" inferring a competitor's likely cost structure, even without their internal financial data, provides genuine strategic insight: a competitor with a fundamentally lower-cost operating model can sustain lower prices indefinitely, while a competitor matching that same low price despite a higher underlying cost structure is likely doing so unsustainably, perhaps to win market share temporarily or because they're under other financial pressure.

This is why a low competitor price shouldn't be assumed to reflect a stable, permanent market rate — connecting to Lesson 9's discounting-math discussion, a competitor cutting prices below what their own cost structure can sustainably support is often signaling either a temporary strategic push or genuine financial strain, both of which suggest that price level may not hold, rather than something your own business necessarily needs to match indefinitely.

What do you actually know or can reasonably infer about a key competitor's cost structure — their likely supplier relationships, scale advantages, or overhead — and does their current pricing look sustainable given that inferred cost structure, or does it look like a temporary or desperate move?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A competitor drops its prices significantly below what appears to be a sustainable level given their likely cost structure — similar supplier relationships, similar scale, no obvious cost advantage. [ASSUMPTION] Rather than assuming this new, lower price represents the new permanent market rate and matching it immediately (with the margin-eroding consequences discussed in Lesson 9), a business that reasons through the competitor's likely unsustainable cost position might instead hold its own price, correctly anticipating that the competitor's aggressive pricing is a temporary push that their own cost structure won't allow them to sustain indefinitely.
