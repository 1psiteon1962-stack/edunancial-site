- Explain why attempting every channel and tactic simultaneously usually produces a worse outcome than focused execution

## Core Content

Lesson 26 established that a niche strategy deliberately trades broad market size for defensibility. Here's the broader "why" behind that same principle, applied to strategy generally: a genuine strategy is defined as much by what it deliberately excludes as by what it includes — a plan that tries to pursue every available channel, every possible customer segment, and every competitive response simultaneously isn't really a strategy at all, it's an attempt to avoid the hard trade-offs a real strategy requires.

This is why resource and attention constraints make exclusion necessary, not optional: a business with limited budget and team capacity that spreads itself across every available marketing channel and customer segment typically executes each one worse than a business that deliberately excludes most options to focus intensely on the few it believes matter most — echoing directly the niche defensibility principle from Lesson 26, but applied here to channel and tactic choice generally, not just market segment.

What is your current sales and marketing strategy deliberately choosing not to do? If the honest answer is "we're trying to do everything," what does that suggest about how well any single part of it is likely being executed?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business attempts to maintain an active presence across every available marketing channel, targeting every plausible customer segment simultaneously, believing this maximizes its total opportunity. [ASSUMPTION] With limited budget and team capacity spread this thin, no single channel or segment receives the focused attention needed to actually execute well, and overall results underperform a competitor who deliberately excluded most channels and segments to focus intensely on the few that mattered most for their specific customers — directly echoing the defensibility-through-focus principle established for niche market strategy in Lesson 26.
