## Core Content

This track has covered CAC and financing (Lesson 1), pricing and margin (Lesson 2), discounting math (Lesson 9), legal exposure (Lessons 7-8, 16), channel risk (Lesson 12), attribution difficulty (Lesson 14), and much more. This closing synthesis revisits the single question running underneath nearly all of it: what does this specific decision actually cost, completely and honestly, and what does it actually return, adjusted for risk and compared against real alternatives?

"Cost" here means more than the sticker price of a campaign — it includes the opportunity cost (Lesson 38), the legal exposure a claim or contract might create (Lessons 7-8), the reputational risk a partnership might carry (Lessons 4, 22), and the cash flow timing mismatch a spending decision might create (Lesson 18). "Return" means more than the immediate metric a dashboard reports — it requires tracing that metric back to an actual business outcome (Lesson 45), weighted by realistic probability (Lessons 3, 15), and net of the true, fully-loaded costs involved (Lesson 38).

Take any sales or marketing decision currently in front of you and apply this question fully and honestly — not just the obvious cost and the obvious return, but every cost and every return this track has taught you to look for. What changes about the decision once you do?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business evaluating a new sponsorship opportunity applies the full version of this question: the cost includes not just the sponsorship fee but the due diligence effort on the partner's stability (Lesson 36) and the reputational risk of association (Lesson 4); the return includes not just estimated audience reach but the realistic conversion probability of that reach into actual paying, retained customers (Lessons 3, 6, 15), compared honestly against what that same budget could achieve elsewhere (Lesson 38). This complete version of the question produces a genuinely different, more rigorous evaluation than a narrower one asking only "what's the sponsorship fee and how many people will see it."
