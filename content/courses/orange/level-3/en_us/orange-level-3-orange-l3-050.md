## Core Content

This track opened by showing that CAC is inseparable from financing (Lesson 1), and has spent 49 lessons demonstrating that pattern across pricing, forecasting, legal exposure, channel strategy, and leadership. It closes with a deliberate reframe: mastering sales and marketing isn't primarily about becoming better at any single tactic — it's about seeing clearly how every sales and marketing decision ripples into finance, law, tax, and leadership, and vice versa.

Look back across this track: pricing determines margin (Lesson 2); marketing claims create legal exposure (Lesson 7); commission structures are leadership decisions (Lesson 37); a growth strategy is only as sound as its unit economics (Lesson 35); international marketing raises tax, legal, and currency questions all at once (Lesson 29). None of these connections were incidental — they reflect the reality that a real business decision, as the broader curriculum direction behind this track emphasizes, almost never belongs to only one category.

This is why the final measure of mastery in this track isn't whether you can recite these connections when asked, but whether you now instinctively ask, for any sales or marketing decision in front of you: what else does this affect, what assumptions am I making, and what would I need to know from finance, law, or leadership before I could evaluate this completely?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A marketer who has internalized this entire track, facing a new campaign proposal, doesn't evaluate it purely on its own marketing merits. She instinctively asks what it would do to CAC and cash flow timing (Lessons 1, 18), whether any claims involved need legal substantiation (Lesson 7), what the commission or incentive implications are for the sales team (Lessons 19, 37), and whether the underlying unit economics support the growth it's meant to produce (Lesson 35) — not because a checklist told her to, but because she now sees a marketing decision as inseparable from the rest of the business it's meant to serve.
