# PURPLE-L1-001: What Is Law, Really?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define law in simple terms.
2. Recognise the general purpose law serves in a society.
3. Distinguish law from morality or custom.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Law is a system of rules created and enforced by governmental institutions, designed to regulate behaviour within a society. These rules generally cover everything from how business is conducted to how disputes are resolved, and which conduct counts as a crime.

*ASSUMPTION:* Law generally differs from morality or custom in that law is formally enforced by the state — with specific consequences for breaking it — while moral or cultural norms rely on social pressure rather than governmental enforcement, though the two frequently overlap.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Many cultures consider it morally wrong to lie to a friend, but this is generally not illegal. Lying under oath in a court (perjury), however, is illegal, with specific legal consequences. This distinction helps illustrate that, while law and morality often coincide, they are not the same thing.

## Practical Quiz
1. What is law, in simple terms?
2. What generally distinguishes law from morality or custom?
3. True/False: Everything considered morally wrong is also illegal.
4. Why is perjury a useful example for distinguishing law from morality?

## Answer Key
1. A system of rules created and enforced by governmental institutions   2. Formal enforcement by the state   3. False   4. It shows a rule that is both morally questionable and legally enforceable with specific consequences

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of law.
2. This is the general, commonly cited distinction between law and other social norms.
3. Many morally questionable behaviours are not illegal.
4. Perjury illustrates a point where morally questionable conduct also carries formal legal consequences.

## Key Points to Remember
- Law is a system of rules created and enforced by government.
- Law generally differs from morality through its formal enforcement by the state.
- Law and morality often overlap, but are not identical.
