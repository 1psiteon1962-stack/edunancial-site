# PURPLE-L1-002: Civil Law vs. Criminal Law

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish civil law from criminal law.
2. Recognise who brings each type of case.
3. Explain the general difference in possible consequences.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Criminal law involves the state prosecuting someone for conduct considered an offence against society, with possible consequences including fines, probation, or imprisonment. Civil law involves disputes between private individuals or entities (or organisations) over rights, obligations, or damages, generally resolved with financial compensation or another remedy rather than punishment.

*ASSUMPTION:* The same set of facts can sometimes give rise to both a criminal case and a separate civil case — someone might, for example, face criminal charges for a harmful act while also being sued civilly by the victim seeking compensation, since these are distinct legal processes with different purposes.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* If someone drives recklessly and crashes into another person's car, the state might bring criminal charges for reckless driving. Separately, the person whose car was damaged might file a civil claim seeking payment for the repairs. These are two separate cases with different rules and possible outcomes, even though they arise from the same incident.
