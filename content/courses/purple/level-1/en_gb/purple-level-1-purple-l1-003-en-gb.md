# PURPLE-L1-003: What Is a Contract?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a contract in simple terms.
2. Recognise the general purpose of a contract.
3. Recognise that a contract can be written, verbal, or even implied.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties that creates obligations each party must fulfil. The general purpose of a contract is to make each party's expectations explicit and to give each party a legal remedy if the other fails to keep to what was agreed.

*ASSUMPTION:* Although many people think of contracts as formal written documents, a contract can generally also form verbally or even be implied through the parties' conduct, though written contracts are generally easier to enforce because they provide clear evidence of the agreed terms.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* When someone hires a painter to paint their house and both verbally agree a price and completion date, this can form a valid verbal contract. However, if a dispute later arises over exactly what was agreed, neither party has a clear written record to refer back to — which illustrates why many people prefer to put important agreements in writing.

## Key Points to Remember
- A contract is a legally binding agreement that creates obligations between the parties.
- Its general purpose is to clarify expectations and provide a legal remedy.
- A contract can form in writing, verbally, or by implication, though written ones are generally easier to enforce.
