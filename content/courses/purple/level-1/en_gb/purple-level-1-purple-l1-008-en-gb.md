# PURPLE-L1-008: Negligence Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define negligence in legal terms.
2. Recognise the elements generally required to prove negligence.
3. Explain the concept of "reasonable care".

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Negligence occurs when someone fails to exercise the level of care a reasonable person would exercise in similar circumstances, causing harm to another person. Proving negligence generally requires showing four elements: a duty of care, a breach of that duty, causation (the breach actually caused the harm), and actual damage.

*ASSUMPTION:* "Reasonable care" is generally judged against what a hypothetical, prudent person would have done in the same situation — it is not a standard of perfection, but a standard of reasonably careful conduct given the circumstances.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A driver who checks their phone while driving and therefore fails to see a pedestrian crossing lawfully probably did not exercise the reasonable care a prudent person would exercise while driving. If that distraction causes injury to the pedestrian, all four elements of negligence would generally be present: a duty to drive carefully, breach of that duty by checking the phone, causation between the distraction and the injury, and the actual harm suffered by the pedestrian.

## Practical Quiz
1. What is negligence?
2. Name two of the four elements generally required to prove negligence.
3. True/False: "Reasonable care" requires perfect conduct.
4. In the driver example, what action constituted the breach of the duty of care?

## Answer Key
1. Failing to exercise the level of care a reasonable person would exercise, causing harm   2. Duty of care, breach of that duty (among others)   3. False   4. Checking the phone while driving

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of negligence.
2. These are among the four generally required elements.
3. Reasonable care is a standard of prudent conduct, not perfection.
4. This action departed from what a reasonably careful person would have done.

## Key Points to Remember
- Negligence occurs when someone fails to exercise reasonable care, causing harm to another person.
- Proving it generally requires duty, breach, causation, and actual damage.
- Reasonable care is a standard of prudence, not perfection.
