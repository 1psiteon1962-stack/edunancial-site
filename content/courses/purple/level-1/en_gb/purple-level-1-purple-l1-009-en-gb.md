# PURPLE-L1-009: Defamation — Libel and Slander

## Learning Objectives
1. Define defamation in general terms.
2. Distinguish written defamation from spoken defamation.
3. Recognise why truth is generally a defence against defamation.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Defamation is a false statement made to a third party that damages someone's reputation. **Libel** generally refers to written or published defamation, while **slander** generally refers to spoken defamation.

*ASSUMPTION:* Truth is generally a complete defence against a defamation claim — if the statement is in fact true, it generally cannot be considered defamatory no matter how much damage it causes to someone's reputation, since defamation by definition requires the statement to be false.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* If someone posts online the false claim that a neighbour stole money, and that claim damages the neighbour's reputation, this could constitute libel, since it was published. If instead the person simply said the same thing verbally to a friend, this would generally be considered slander. If the claim turned out to be true, however, there would generally be no defamation at all, regardless of the harm caused.

## Key Points to Remember
- Defamation is a false statement that damages someone's reputation in front of third parties.
- Libel is generally written; slander is generally spoken.
- Truth is generally a complete defence against a defamation claim.
