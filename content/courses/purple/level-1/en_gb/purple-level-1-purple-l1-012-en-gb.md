# PURPLE-L1-012: What Is Intellectual Property?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define intellectual property in simple terms.
2. Recognise the broad categories of intellectual property.
3. Explain the general purpose of protecting intellectual property.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Intellectual property refers to creations of the mind — such as inventions, literary and artistic works, symbols, names, and images used in commerce — to which the law grants certain exclusive rights to their creators or owners. The broad categories generally include copyright, trademarks, patents, and trade secrets.

*ASSUMPTION:* The general purpose of protecting intellectual property is to encourage creativity and innovation, by giving creators the opportunity to profit from their work for a set period, rather than allowing others to copy it freely straight away.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* When an author writes a novel, copyright generally grants them the exclusive right to reproduce, distribute, and sell that work for a set period. Without this protection, anyone could copy and sell the novel immediately without compensating the author, which could discourage people from investing time and effort in creating new works.

## Key Points to Remember
- Intellectual property brings together creations of the mind protected by exclusive rights.
- The broad categories include copyright, trademarks, patents, and trade secrets.
- The general purpose is to encourage creativity and innovation.
