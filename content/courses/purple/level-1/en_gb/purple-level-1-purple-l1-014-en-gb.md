# PURPLE-L1-014: Trademarks Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define trademark in simple terms.
2. Explain the general purpose of a trademark.
3. Distinguish a trademark from copyright.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A trademark is a word, phrase, symbol, or design that identifies and distinguishes the source of one party's goods or services from those of others. The general purpose is to protect consumers from confusion over who provides a product or service, and to protect a business's reputation and brand identity.

*ASSUMPTION:* A trademark generally differs from copyright in that a trademark protects commercial identifiers (such as logos or names) used in commerce, while copyright protects broader creative works — a company's logo could potentially be protected by both in different ways.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* The name and distinctive logo of a coffee shop chain are generally protected as a trademark, preventing another business from using a confusingly similar name or logo to sell coffee. This helps customers know for certain which business they are supporting, and prevents others from unfairly benefiting from the reputation the original brand has built.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of a trademark.
2. This is the commonly cited general purpose of trademarks.
3. Trademarks protect commercial identifiers; copyright protects broader creative works.
4. Trademarks generally prevent consumer confusion over the source of goods or services.

## Key Points to Remember
- A trademark identifies and distinguishes the source of goods or services.
- Its general purpose is to protect consumers and a business's brand reputation.
- It differs from copyright, which protects broader creative works.
