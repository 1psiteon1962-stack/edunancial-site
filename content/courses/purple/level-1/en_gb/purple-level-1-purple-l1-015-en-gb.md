# PURPLE-L1-015: Patents Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define patent in simple terms.
2. Recognise what a patent can generally protect.
3. Explain the general trade-off behind the patent system.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A patent grants an inventor the exclusive right to make, use, or sell an invention for a limited period, generally in exchange for the inventor publicly disclosing how the invention works.

*ASSUMPTION:* The patent system generally represents a trade-off: society benefits from inventions being publicly disclosed (rather than kept secret), while the inventor receives temporary exclusive protection as an incentive to innovate and invest in research and development.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A pharmaceutical company that develops a new medicine can generally patent it, gaining the exclusive right to sell it for a set period. In exchange, the company must publicly disclose the formula and manufacturing process of the medicine, so that after the patent expires, others can manufacture generic versions using that disclosed information.

## Practical Quiz
1. What is a patent?
2. What must the inventor generally do in exchange for patent protection?
3. True/False: Patents grant permanent exclusive protection.
4. What is the general trade-off behind the patent system?

## Answer Key
1. The exclusive right to make, use, or sell an invention for a limited period   2. Publicly disclose how the invention works   3. False   4. Public disclosure in exchange for temporary exclusive protection

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of a patent.
2. Public disclosure is generally the requirement in exchange for patent protection.
3. Patents generally grant protection only for a limited period.
4. This is the fundamental, commonly cited trade-off of the patent system.

## Key Points to Remember
- A patent grants temporary exclusive rights over an invention.
- In exchange, the inventor generally must publicly disclose how the invention works.
- The system tries to balance the incentive to innovate with the public benefit of shared knowledge.
