# PURPLE-L1-016: Trade Secrets

## Learning Objectives
1. Define trade secret in simple terms.
2. Distinguish a trade secret from a patent.
3. Recognise why businesses sometimes prefer trade secrets over patents.

## Core Content
A trade secret is confidential business information — such as a formula, process, or method — that gives a business a competitive advantage because it is not publicly known. *VERIFIED FACT:* Unlike a patent, a trade secret requires neither public disclosure nor formal registration; protection instead depends on the business actively keeping the information secret.

*ASSUMPTION:* Businesses sometimes prefer trade secrets over patents because protection of a trade secret can last indefinitely as long as the secret is kept, while patents generally expire after a set period — the risk, however, is that if the secret is revealed or independently discovered, protection is generally lost.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* The exact formula of a popular soft drink has reportedly been kept as a trade secret for over a century, rather than being patented. This allows the company to avoid publicly disclosing the formula, though it also means that if someone else independently discovers the same formula, the company generally has no legal remedy against them, unlike what would happen with a patent.
