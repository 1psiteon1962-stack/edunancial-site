# PURPLE-L1-019: Power of Attorney Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define power of attorney in simple terms.
2. Distinguish a general power of attorney from a limited one.
3. Explain when a power of attorney generally ends.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A power of attorney is a legal document authorising one person (the "agent") to act on behalf of another person (the "principal") in specific legal or financial matters. A **general power of attorney** generally grants broad authority over many matters, while a **limited power of attorney** generally restricts authority to a specific task or period.

*ASSUMPTION:* A standard power of attorney generally ends if the principal becomes incapacitated or dies, unless the document is specifically designated as a "durable" power of attorney, generally designed to remain valid even if the principal becomes incapacitated.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Someone travelling abroad for several months might grant a limited power of attorney to a family member, authorising them only to handle payment of specific bills while they're away. Someone concerned about possible future age-related incapacity, on the other hand, might grant a broader durable power of attorney, so a trusted loved one can manage their financial affairs if they themselves become unable to.

## Key Points to Remember
- A power of attorney authorises someone to act on behalf of another person in legal or financial matters.
- It can be general (broad authority) or limited (authority restricted to a task or period).
- A durable power of attorney is designed to remain valid even during the principal's incapacity.
