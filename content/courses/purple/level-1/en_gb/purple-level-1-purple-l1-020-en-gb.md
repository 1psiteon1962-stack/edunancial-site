# PURPLE-L1-020: What Is a Trust?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a trust in simple terms.
2. Recognise the general roles involved in a trust.
3. Explain a common reason people set up trusts.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A trust is a legal arrangement in which one person (the "settlor") transfers property to be managed by another person or entity (the "trustee") for the benefit of a third person (the "beneficiary"), according to terms the settlor sets.

*ASSUMPTION:* A commonly cited reason for setting up a trust is to avoid the court probate process, generally the public process of administering someone's estate through the courts — property placed in a trust can generally be distributed to beneficiaries without going through that court process, though the exact rules vary by legal system.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A parent might set up a trust, transferring funds to a trusted trustee (such as a bank or family member) to manage on behalf of their child until the child turns twenty-five. The trustee has a legal duty to manage those funds according to the terms the parent set, rather than at their own discretion, and the child receives the benefits without the funds having to go through the court probate process.

## Key Points to Remember
- A trust transfers property to a trustee to manage for the benefit of a beneficiary.
- The three general roles are settlor, trustee, and beneficiary.
- A common reason to set one up is to avoid the public court probate process.
