# PURPLE-L1-023: What Is a Lawsuit?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a lawsuit in simple terms.
2. Recognise the general stages of a civil case.
3. Recognise the terms "claimant" and "defendant".

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* A lawsuit is a formal legal process in which one party (the "claimant") seeks a legal remedy from a court against another party (the "defendant"). The general stages include filing a formal claim, serving notice on the defendant, exchanging information between the parties (known as "disclosure"), and finally a trial or a settlement before reaching that stage.

*ASSUMPTION:* Many lawsuits never reach trial — they are often resolved through a settlement between the parties at some point in the process, since a trial generally involves significant cost, time, and uncertainty for both parties.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A person injured through another's negligence might bring a lawsuit as claimant, seeking financial compensation from the defendant. During disclosure, both parties generally exchange evidence and take statements. Often, seeing the strength of the other party's evidence, both sides decide to settle rather than risk an uncertain outcome at trial.

## Key Points to Remember
- A lawsuit is a formal legal process in which the claimant seeks a remedy against the defendant.
- The general stages include the claim, service, disclosure, and trial or settlement.
- Many lawsuits are settled before reaching a full trial.
