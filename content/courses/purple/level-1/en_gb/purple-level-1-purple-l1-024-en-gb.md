# PURPLE-L1-024: Mediation vs. Arbitration vs. Litigation

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish mediation, arbitration, and litigation.
2. Recognise who makes the final decision in each process.
3. Explain a general advantage of resolving disputes outside the courts.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* In **mediation**, a neutral third party helps the parties negotiate their own agreement, but has no power to impose a decision. In **arbitration**, a neutral third party (the arbitrator) hears both sides and makes a binding decision, similar to a judge but generally outside the traditional court system. In **litigation**, the dispute is resolved through the formal court system, generally with a judge or jury making the final decision.

*ASSUMPTION:* Mediation and arbitration are generally preferred in some cases for being faster, more private, and cheaper than litigation, though arbitration generally limits appeal options much more compared with ordinary litigation.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two businesses in a contract dispute might first try mediation, where a mediator helps them find a mutually acceptable compromise. If that fails and their contract includes an arbitration clause, they might be required to take the dispute to an arbitrator instead of a court, where the arbitrator would hear both sides and issue a binding decision with generally very limited appeal options.

## Key Points to Remember
- In mediation, the parties negotiate their own agreement with the help of a neutral third party.
- In arbitration, an arbitrator makes a binding decision outside the traditional court system.
- Litigation resolves disputes within the formal court system, generally with broader appeal options.
