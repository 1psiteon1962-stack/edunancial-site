# PURPLE-L1-028: Basic Constitutional Rights

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a constitutional right in general terms.
2. Recognise common categories of constitutional rights.
3. Recognise that specific rights vary by country.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Specific constitutional rights vary significantly by country; this lesson describes only general categories, not the specific rights of any particular country.

*VERIFIED FACT:* A constitutional right is a fundamental protection generally enshrined in a country's highest legal document, which typically limits what the government can do to people. Common categories generally include freedom of speech, freedom of religion, protection against unreasonable searches and seizures, and the right to a fair trial.

*ASSUMPTION:* Constitutional rights generally differ from other laws in that they are harder to change — a special process beyond ordinary legislation is generally required — reflecting their status as fundamental protections.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* In many countries, the right to a fair trial generally includes protections such as the right to know the charges against you, the right to present a defence, and the right to an impartial judge or jury. These protections generally exist because they limit the government's power to act arbitrarily against people.
