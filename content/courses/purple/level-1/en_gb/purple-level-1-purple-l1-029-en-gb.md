# PURPLE-L1-029: Due Process Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define due process in simple terms.
2. Distinguish the procedural aspect from the substantive aspect.
3. Explain why due process is generally important even for clearly guilty people.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* Due process is a legal principle that generally requires the government to follow fair procedures before depriving someone of life, liberty, or property. **Procedural** due process generally refers to the fair procedures themselves (such as adequate notice and an opportunity to be heard), while **substantive** due process generally refers to the protection of certain fundamental rights from government interference, regardless of how fair the procedure is.

*ASSUMPTION:* Due process is generally important even for people who appear clearly guilty, because the principle protects the system as a whole — without consistently fair procedures, there would be a risk that innocent people could also be wrongly convicted, since "apparent guilt" doesn't always turn out to be accurate.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Before the government can imprison someone for a crime, procedural due process generally requires that person to receive notice of the charges, have the opportunity to present a defence, and be tried by an impartial court. Even if the evidence against someone seems overwhelming, following these steps generally remains mandatory, since the purpose is to protect the integrity of the system for everyone, not just to secure individual outcomes.

4. What is substantive due process?

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of due process.
2. This is the fundamental definition of procedural due process.
3. Due process protects the integrity of the system for everyone, not just for those who appear innocent.

## Key Points to Remember
- Due process requires fair procedures before the government deprives someone of life, liberty, or property.
- Procedural due process concerns fair procedures; substantive due process protects certain specific fundamental rights.
- Due process matters for everyone because it protects the integrity of the whole system.
