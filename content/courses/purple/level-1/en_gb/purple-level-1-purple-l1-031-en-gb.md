# PURPLE-L1-031: Employment Discrimination

## Learning Objectives
1. Define employment discrimination in general terms.
2. Recognise commonly protected categories against workplace discrimination.
3. Recognise that specific protected categories vary by legal system.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Specific protected categories against workplace discrimination vary by country and region; this lesson describes only commonly cited categories.

Employment discrimination occurs when an employer treats a worker or applicant unfavourably based on a protected characteristic rather than their suitability for the role. *VERIFIED FACT:* Commonly protected categories in many legal systems include race, sex, age, religion, and disability, though the exact list varies.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* If an employer systematically refuses to hire qualified applicants of a certain age, preferring younger applicants with similar qualifications, this could constitute age discrimination in many legal systems that protect this category. The law generally requires that employment decisions be based on suitability for the role, not on protected characteristics unrelated to performance.

## Practical Quiz
1. What is employment discrimination?
2. Name a commonly protected category against workplace discrimination.
3. True/False: Protected categories are identical across all legal systems.
4. What should an employment decision generally be based on, according to the law?

## Answer Key
1. Treating someone unfavourably based on a protected characteristic rather than suitability for the role   2. Race, sex, age, religion, or disability (among others)   3. False   4. Suitability for the role

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the fundamental definition of employment discrimination.
2. These are commonly cited categories protected in many legal systems.
3. Specific protected categories vary by country and region.
4. The law generally requires decisions to be based on suitability for the role, not protected characteristics.

## Key Points to Remember
- Employment discrimination involves treating someone unfavourably because of a protected characteristic.
- Commonly protected categories include race, sex, age, religion, and disability.
- Specific protected categories vary significantly by legal system.
