# PURPLE-L1-040: Criminal Law — The Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define criminal law in general terms.
2. Distinguish a summary offence from an indictable offence.
3. Explain the general standard of proof in a criminal case.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Specific offence classifications and their consequences vary significantly by country; this lesson describes only general concepts.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Criminal law is the area of law that determines which conduct is considered a crime against society and sets the corresponding consequences. Many legal systems generally classify offences into categories by seriousness — commonly summary offences (generally less serious, with lighter consequences) and indictable offences (generally more serious, with harsher consequences, including potentially lengthy imprisonment).

*ASSUMPTION:* The standard of proof in a criminal case is generally "beyond reasonable doubt" — a considerably stricter standard than that used in civil cases — reflecting the seriousness of the possible consequences, such as loss of liberty.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* In many legal systems, minor theft of a low-value item might be classified as a summary offence with relatively light consequences, while armed robbery would generally be classified as an indictable offence with far harsher consequences. In both cases, the prosecution would generally need to prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt to secure a conviction.

- Offences are generally classified into summary and indictable by seriousness.
- The standard of proof in a criminal case is generally beyond reasonable doubt.
