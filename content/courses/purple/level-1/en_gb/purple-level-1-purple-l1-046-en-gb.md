# PURPLE-L1-046: Regulatory Compliance — The Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define regulatory compliance in general terms.
2. Explain why regulatory compliance is generally important for organisations.
3. Recognise that specific regulations vary widely by industry and legal system.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Specific regulations vary enormously by industry, country, and region; this lesson describes only the general concept.

Regulatory compliance refers to an organisation's efforts to follow the laws, regulations, standards, and ethical rules relevant to its operations. *ASSUMPTION:* Regulatory compliance is generally important because breaches can lead to fines, legal sanctions, loss of licences, or damage to the organisation's reputation — which is why many organisations invest significant resources in programmes and staff specifically dedicated to monitoring compliance.

## Practical Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A company handling customers' financial information generally must comply with specific regulations on how that information is protected and stored. If the company fails to implement the required safeguards and a data breach occurs, it could generally face significant regulatory sanctions in addition to possible civil claims from affected customers — illustrating why proactive compliance is generally considered wiser than addressing problems after they occur.

## Key Points to Remember
- Regulatory compliance brings together an organisation's efforts to follow relevant laws and regulations.
- It matters because breaches can bring fines, sanctions, and reputational damage.
- Specific regulations vary enormously by industry, country, and region.
