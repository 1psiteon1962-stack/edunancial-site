## Core Content

A **contract** is generally a legally enforceable agreement between parties. [VERIFIED FACT] While specific requirements vary by jurisdiction and contract type, most legal systems generally require three basic elements for a contract to be enforceable: an **offer** (one party proposes specific terms), **acceptance** (the other party agrees to those specific terms), and **consideration** (something of value exchanged between the parties, explored further in the next lesson).

Here's the "why" each element serves a distinct purpose: the offer and acceptance requirement ensures there's a genuine, identifiable meeting of the minds — both parties actually agreeing to the same specific terms, not just a vague, general understanding. The consideration requirement ensures the agreement is a genuine exchange, not simply one party's unenforceable promise of a gift or favor with nothing given in return.

This is why an agreement missing one of these elements may not hold up as a legally enforceable contract, even if both parties genuinely intended to be bound by it — a casual promise with no clear offer and acceptance, or an arrangement with nothing of value actually exchanged, may not meet the basic legal threshold for enforceability, regardless of the parties' sincere intentions.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] One business proposes specific terms to another — a specific price for a specific quantity of goods delivered by a specific date (the offer). The second business agrees to exactly those terms (the acceptance). Money changes hands in exchange for the goods (the consideration). All three elements are present, forming a legally enforceable contract. [ASSUMPTION] If instead one business had simply expressed a vague, general intention to "probably do some business together sometime" without specific terms ever being proposed and accepted, and without anything of value actually promised in exchange, this looser arrangement likely wouldn't meet the basic threshold for an enforceable contract, regardless of both parties' good intentions.
