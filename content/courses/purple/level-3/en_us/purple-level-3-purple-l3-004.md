## Learning Objectives

## Core Content

Lesson 3 established that a breach's severity determines what remedy is available. Here's the "why" behind the general nature of that remedy itself: [VERIFIED FACT] contract law generally aims to put the non-breaching party in the position they would have been in had the contract been properly performed — commonly called putting them in their "expectation" position — rather than to punish the breaching party for having broken their promise.

This is why **compensatory damages** — an amount intended to cover the actual, provable financial harm caused by the breach — are the default, standard remedy, and why **punitive damages** — damages specifically intended to punish the wrongdoer beyond compensating the other party's actual harm — are generally not available in a typical breach of contract case, even for a deliberate, intentional breach, in most legal systems.

This general approach reflects a specific legal philosophy: contract law generally treats a broken promise as a civil, economic matter to be made right through fair compensation, rather than as wrongdoing requiring separate legal punishment — a different philosophy than criminal law, or than certain other specific areas of civil law where punitive damages may be available under more limited, specific circumstances.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business breaches a contract, causing the other party to suffer a specific, provable financial loss. The non-breaching party can generally recover compensatory damages reflecting that specific financial harm, putting them roughly in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed as agreed. [ASSUMPTION] Even if the breach was deliberate and could be characterized as a calculated business decision to break the promise, the non-breaching party generally cannot recover additional punitive damages simply to punish that deliberate choice, beyond the actual compensatory amount reflecting their genuine financial harm — reflecting contract law's general focus on compensation rather than punishment.

## Key Takeaways
- Contract damages are generally intended to compensate the non-breaching party for actual harm, putting them in their expected position.
- Compensatory damages are the standard remedy; punitive damages, intended to punish, are generally unavailable in typical breach of contract cases.
- This reflects contract law's general philosophy of treating a broken promise as a civil matter to be made right, not wrongdoing requiring separate punishment.
