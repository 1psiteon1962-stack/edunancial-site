## Core Content

An **indemnification clause** (sometimes paired with "hold harmless" language) generally obligates one party to compensate the other for certain specified losses, damages, or claims that may arise, often connected to specific risks related to the contract's subject matter.

Here's the "why" this clause functions differently from the damages remedies covered in earlier lessons: rather than determining compensation after a breach or dispute has already occurred, an indemnification clause proactively allocates responsibility for certain categories of future risk in advance, as part of the original contract negotiation — deciding upfront which party will bear the cost if a specific type of loss or third-party claim later materializes, regardless of whether either party actually breaches the contract itself.

[VERIFIED FACT] The specific scope of an indemnification clause — exactly which types of losses or claims are covered, and any limits or exceptions — matters enormously, since a broadly worded clause could obligate one party to cover a much wider range of situations than a narrowly, carefully drafted one, making the specific language of this clause a frequent and important point of negotiation in significant contracts.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A contract between two businesses includes an indemnification clause obligating one party to cover certain specified losses if a third party brings a claim related to a specific aspect of the contracted work. [ASSUMPTION] If that specific type of claim later arises from a third party, the indemnification clause determines, based on terms negotiated well in advance, which of the two contracting parties bears that cost — a decision made proactively during contract negotiation, not something left to be litigated and decided only after the specific dispute has already occurred.
