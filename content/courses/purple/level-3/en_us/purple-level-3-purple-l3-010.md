## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track covers non-compete clauses specifically in the context of protecting goodwill in a business acquisition. Here's the broader legal "why" behind their enforceability generally: [VERIFIED FACT] simply having a non-compete clause signed by both parties doesn't automatically make it enforceable — many jurisdictions apply a **reasonableness test**, generally evaluating the clause's scope across several dimensions: the geographic area covered, the length of time the restriction lasts, and the specific scope of activities actually restricted.

Here's the "why" reasonableness matters so much: courts generally balance the legitimate business interest a non-compete is meant to protect (like the goodwill discussed in the BLUE track) against the restricted party's own legitimate interest in earning a living and pursuing their profession or trade — an overly broad clause, covering an unreasonably large area, an unreasonably long time period, or an unreasonably wide range of activities, may be found to tip that balance too far against the restricted party and be deemed unenforceable, or reduced in scope by a court, in the specific jurisdiction's approach.

[VERIFIED FACT] Some jurisdictions apply particularly strict limits on non-compete enforceability generally, in some cases significantly restricting or even prohibiting them for certain categories of workers — reinforcing that enforceability is genuinely jurisdiction-specific, not a matter of simply having appropriate contract language.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A non-compete clause restricts a former employee from working in a similar role anywhere in the world for ten years. [ASSUMPTION] Given the extremely broad geographic scope and lengthy time period, a court applying a reasonableness test might find this clause unenforceable as overly broad, potentially reducing its scope to something more reasonable, or striking it down entirely depending on the specific jurisdiction's approach — illustrating why simply having the clause signed doesn't guarantee it will be enforced as written.

## Key Takeaways
- A signed non-compete clause isn't automatically enforceable — many jurisdictions apply a reasonableness test.
- This test generally evaluates geographic scope, duration, and the range of restricted activities.
- Enforceability is genuinely jurisdiction-specific, with some jurisdictions applying particularly strict limits on non-competes generally.
