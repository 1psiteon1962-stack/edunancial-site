## Core Content

Forming an LLC (Limited Liability Company) is generally intended to create a legal separation between the business entity and its owners, protecting the owners' personal assets from most business liabilities. Here's the "why" this protection isn't automatically guaranteed simply by the act of formation: [VERIFIED FACT] many legal systems generally expect an LLC (and similar entities) to be maintained and operated as a genuinely separate entity from its owners, through certain ongoing formalities, in order for courts to continue respecting that legal separation.

These formalities generally include keeping business and personal finances genuinely separate (a topic explored further in Lesson 13), maintaining adequate business records, following the entity's own governing documents and any required internal procedures, and generally treating the business as a distinct legal entity in its dealings, rather than as a mere extension of the owner's personal affairs.

This is why simply filing paperwork to form an LLC, without maintaining these ongoing formalities afterward, doesn't guarantee the liability protection an owner might assume comes automatically with formation — the protection depends on ongoing conduct, not just the initial formation, setting up directly the "piercing the corporate veil" concept covered in the next lesson.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business owner forms an LLC, expecting this to automatically protect her personal assets from any business liabilities going forward. [ASSUMPTION] If she subsequently fails to maintain adequate business records, doesn't follow the entity's own required internal procedures, and generally treats the business's finances and affairs as indistinguishable from her own personal affairs, a court evaluating a future liability claim might find that the LLC wasn't actually being maintained and operated as a genuinely separate entity — potentially undermining the very liability protection she assumed the initial formation had automatically and permanently secured.

## Key Takeaways
- LLC liability protection isn't automatically guaranteed by formation alone — it depends on maintaining ongoing formalities.
- These formalities generally include separate finances, adequate records, and treating the entity as genuinely distinct from its owners.
- Failing to maintain these formalities can undermine the liability protection an owner might have assumed was automatic and permanent.
