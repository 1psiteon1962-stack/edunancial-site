## Core Content

Lesson 11 explained that an LLC's liability protection depends on maintaining certain ongoing formalities. **Piercing the corporate veil** is the specific legal remedy that results when those formalities have been significantly disregarded: a court disregards the entity's separate legal status and holds the owner personally liable for the business's debts or obligations, despite the LLC or corporate structure.

Here's the "why" courts apply this remedy: [VERIFIED FACT] if an owner hasn't actually treated the entity as genuinely separate — commingling personal and business funds (Lesson 13), failing to maintain adequate records, using the entity to commit fraud, or treating the business as a mere "alter ego" of themselves personally rather than a distinct entity — a court may conclude that the liability protection shouldn't be respected either, since the owner didn't actually operate the business in the separate manner that protection assumes.

This directly connects back to Lesson 11: piercing the corporate veil is essentially the legal consequence of failing to maintain the formalities discussed there — it's not a separate, unrelated risk, but the specific outcome that results when those formalities have been significantly neglected.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business owner forms an LLC but consistently uses the business's bank account for personal expenses, fails to maintain separate business records, and generally treats the business as indistinguishable from his own personal finances. [ASSUMPTION] If the business later faces a significant liability claim, a court evaluating whether to pierce the corporate veil might find that this owner never actually operated the LLC as a genuinely separate entity — concluding that the liability protection shouldn't apply, and holding him personally liable for the business's obligations despite the LLC structure, precisely because he failed to maintain the formalities described in Lesson 11.

## Key Takeaways
- Piercing the corporate veil is when a court disregards an entity's liability protection and holds the owner personally liable.
