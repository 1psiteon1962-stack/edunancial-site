## Core Content

Lesson 12 identified commingling personal and business funds as one factor courts consider when deciding whether to pierce the corporate veil. Here's the "why" this specific practice carries particular weight in that analysis, beyond just being one item on a broader list: [VERIFIED FACT] using a single, shared bank account for both personal and business expenses, or otherwise failing to keep clear financial separation between an owner and their entity, is often considered strong, direct evidence that the entity isn't actually being operated as a genuinely separate legal person — precisely the finding that supports piercing the veil, as Lesson 12 explained.

This is why maintaining a completely separate business bank account, keeping business and personal expenses clearly and consistently distinguished in the entity's own records, and generally avoiding using business funds for personal purposes (or vice versa) without proper documentation, are among the most practical, concrete, and commonly emphasized steps for protecting an entity's liability shield — this specific practice is one of the more visible, easily proven factors a court can point to when evaluating whether an entity has genuinely been operated separately.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A business owner maintains a completely separate bank account for her LLC, consistently paying all business expenses from that account and depositing all business income into it, while paying herself a clearly documented salary or distribution from the business account into her personal account, rather than freely moving money back and forth without documentation. [ASSUMPTION] This clear, consistent financial separation provides strong evidence, if ever needed, that her LLC has genuinely been operated as a separate entity — directly protecting against the specific veil-piercing risk this lesson describes, compared to an owner who freely mixes personal and business expenses through a single, shared account.

## Key Takeaways
- Commingling personal and business funds means failing to keep clear financial separation between an owner and their entity.
