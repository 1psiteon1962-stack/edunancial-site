## Core Content

A **sole proprietorship** is generally the default business structure that exists automatically when an individual operates a business without forming a separate legal entity. Here's the "why" this structure offers no liability separation at all, fundamentally differently from the LLC concepts covered in Lessons 11 through 13: [VERIFIED FACT] because no separate legal entity was ever formed, there's no corporate veil to pierce in the first place — the owner and the business are, legally, the same person, meaning the owner is personally liable for all business debts and obligations without any entity-based protection whatsoever.

This is a fundamentally different situation than the LLC veil-piercing risk discussed in the previous lessons: an LLC owner who fails to maintain formalities risks losing protection they otherwise would have had. A sole proprietor never had that protection to begin with — there's simply no separate entity standing between personal assets and business liabilities.

This is why a sole proprietorship, while often the simplest and least expensive structure to operate, generally carries the highest personal liability exposure among common business structures — a business creditor or a claim arising from the business's operations can generally reach the owner's personal assets directly, with no entity-based liability shield of any kind standing in the way.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An individual operates a small business as a sole proprietorship, without forming an LLC or any other separate legal entity. If the business faces a significant liability claim or debt it cannot cover, [ASSUMPTION] the individual's personal assets — savings, personal property, and other personal wealth — are generally directly exposed to satisfy that claim, since there's no legal separation between the owner and the business at all. This is a fundamentally different risk profile than an LLC owner facing potential veil-piercing, since the sole proprietor never had entity-based liability protection to lose in the first place.
