## Core Content

This curriculum's BLUE track covers contract essentials generally, and buy-sell agreements specifically for handling partner exit scenarios. Here's the specific legal "why" behind addressing partnership terms comprehensively in a written agreement, beyond just the buy-sell scenario: [VERIFIED FACT] absent a written agreement specifying otherwise, many jurisdictions' default partnership laws will govern the relationship — and these default rules may not reflect what the specific partners would have actually wanted, particularly regarding profit and loss sharing, decision-making authority, and dispute resolution procedures.

A well-drafted partnership agreement typically addresses ownership percentages and profit/loss sharing, decision-making authority (including which decisions require unanimous consent versus majority approval), each partner's specific roles and responsibilities, procedures for admitting new partners or handling a partner's exit (connecting to the buy-sell agreement concept from the BLUE track), and a specific process for resolving disputes between partners.

This is why addressing these topics in a written agreement, negotiated calmly before any actual dispute arises, matters even between partners who trust each other completely — echoing the same "why" behind buy-sell agreements discussed in the BLUE track: circumstances change, and a clear, pre-negotiated agreement prevents a difficult situation from becoming a legal crisis on top of already-strained personal relationships.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two business partners begin operating together with a strong personal relationship but no written partnership agreement specifying their respective roles, profit-sharing arrangement, or decision-making authority. [ASSUMPTION] If a significant disagreement later arises about how profits should be split or who has authority to make a major business decision, they may find themselves relying on default partnership laws that don't reflect what either of them actually intended or expected — a dispute that a clear, proactively negotiated written agreement, covering these specific topics from the start, could have prevented or at least provided a clear, pre-agreed process for resolving.
