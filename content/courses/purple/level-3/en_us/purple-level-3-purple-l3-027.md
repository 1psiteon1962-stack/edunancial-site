## Core Content

Lesson 26 established that a revocable trust offers flexibility during the creator's lifetime. An **irrevocable trust** takes the opposite approach: once created and funded, it generally cannot be easily modified, amended, or revoked by the creator, except in very limited circumstances depending on the trust's specific terms and applicable law.

Here's the "why" a creator might deliberately choose this more restrictive structure: [VERIFIED FACT] because the creator has genuinely given up control over the assets placed in an irrevocable trust — no longer able to freely reclaim or modify them — those assets are, in many circumstances, generally treated as no longer belonging to the creator for certain legal purposes, potentially providing protection from the creator's own future creditors and, connecting to the estate and gift tax discussion covered in this curriculum's GREEN track, potentially reducing the size of the creator's taxable estate.

This is why the choice between a revocable and an irrevocable trust represents a genuine, deliberate trade-off: a revocable trust offers flexibility and probate avoidance, while an irrevocable trust offers asset protection and potential tax benefits specifically because the creator has given up that same flexibility — neither structure is universally "better," and the right choice depends on which specific goals a person is prioritizing.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A person concerned specifically about potential future creditor claims or wanting to reduce the size of their eventual taxable estate (connecting to the GREEN track's estate tax discussion) creates an irrevocable trust, transferring specific assets into it and permanently giving up the ability to freely modify or reclaim them. [ASSUMPTION] In exchange for this loss of flexibility, those specific assets may be better protected from the creator's own future creditors and may be excluded from the creator's taxable estate — a deliberate trade-off, chosen specifically because this person's particular goals (asset protection, tax planning) outweighed the flexibility a revocable trust would have otherwise provided.

## Key Takeaways
- An irrevocable trust generally cannot be easily modified or revoked once created and funded.
- This loss of flexibility can provide asset protection from future creditors and potential estate tax benefits.
- The choice between a revocable and irrevocable trust represents a genuine, deliberate trade-off, not a universally "better" option.
