## Core Content

Lesson 28 established that a power of attorney's authority is limited to whatever scope the document actually specifies. A **healthcare directive** (sometimes called a medical power of attorney, healthcare proxy, or advance directive, with specific terminology varying by jurisdiction) is generally a distinct type of document, specifically authorizing a named person to make medical and healthcare decisions on the principal's behalf if they become unable to make those decisions themselves.

Here's the "why" this is generally kept as a separate document from a financial power of attorney, directly applying Lesson 28's scope principle: financial and medical decision-making are genuinely different categories of authority, often with different legal requirements, different appropriate agents (a person might reasonably trust one family member with financial matters and a different one with medical decisions), and different specific considerations. [VERIFIED FACT] Some jurisdictions and some individuals' specific planning documents combine these into a single, broader document, but treating them as conceptually distinct — and often practically separate — documents is common and often recommended, ensuring each type of authority is clearly and specifically addressed.

This is why comprehensive personal planning generally addresses both types of authority separately and deliberately, rather than assuming a single document (or assuming that financial authority alone implies healthcare authority) adequately covers both very different categories of potential future need.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A person grants a financial power of attorney to one trusted family member specifically to handle financial matters if needed, and separately grants a healthcare directive to a different family member, specifically chosen based on that person's particular comfort discussing and making difficult medical decisions. [ASSUMPTION] If the person later becomes unable to make their own medical decisions, only the specifically named healthcare agent has the legal authority to make those decisions — the financial power of attorney granted to the other family member generally doesn't extend to medical matters at all, illustrating exactly why these represent genuinely separate categories of authority requiring their own specific documents.

## Key Takeaways
- A healthcare directive specifically authorizes medical decision-making authority, distinct from financial authority.
- These are generally treated as separate documents, since they represent genuinely different categories of authority, often best suited to different agents.
- Comprehensive planning should address both financial and healthcare authority separately and deliberately.
