## Core Content

Lesson 31 established that landlord-tenant relationships are governed by mandatory statutory protections. Here's a specific, significant application of that principle: **eviction** — the legal process of removing a tenant from a rental property — generally requires following a specific, formal legal process, not simply the landlord's own unilateral decision that a tenant should leave.

[VERIFIED FACT] A lawful eviction process generally requires proper legal notice to the tenant (with specific requirements varying by jurisdiction and the reason for eviction), and if the tenant doesn't voluntarily leave after that notice, generally requires the landlord to file a formal court proceeding and obtain a court order before the tenant can actually be physically removed — a landlord generally cannot simply change the locks, remove a tenant's belongings, or shut off utilities to force a tenant out, actions sometimes called illegal **self-help eviction**.

Here's the "why" self-help eviction attempts create real legal risk for a landlord, rather than being a shortcut: [VERIFIED FACT] many jurisdictions specifically prohibit these self-help methods and can impose significant penalties on a landlord who attempts them, precisely because landlord-tenant statutes are designed to ensure tenants receive proper legal process before being removed from their home, regardless of how legitimate the landlord's underlying reason for wanting the tenant to leave might actually be.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A landlord believes a tenant has seriously violated their lease and wants them removed as quickly as possible. Rather than following the formal legal eviction process — proper notice, followed by a court proceeding if the tenant doesn't voluntarily leave — the landlord changes the locks while the tenant is away, attempting a self-help eviction. [ASSUMPTION] Even if the landlord's underlying belief about the lease violation is entirely correct, this self-help approach is likely illegal in many jurisdictions, potentially exposing the landlord to significant legal penalties, precisely because it bypassed the mandatory legal process this lesson describes — illustrating why the "correctness" of the underlying reason doesn't excuse skipping the required legal steps.

## Key Takeaways
- Eviction generally requires following a specific, formal legal process, not simply a landlord's unilateral decision.
- A lawful process typically requires proper notice and, if needed, a formal court proceeding before actual physical removal.
- Self-help eviction attempts (changing locks, removing belongings, shutting off utilities) are generally illegal and create real legal risk for a landlord, regardless of the underlying reason's legitimacy.
