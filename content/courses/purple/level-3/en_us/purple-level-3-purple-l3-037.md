## Core Content

**At-will employment** generally means an employer can terminate an employee for almost any reason, or no reason at all, without needing to demonstrate specific cause — and correspondingly, an employee is generally free to leave a job at any time as well, without needing to provide a specific reason. [VERIFIED FACT] This is the default employment relationship in many jurisdictions, absent a specific contract stating otherwise.

Here's the "why" this general rule doesn't mean literally any reason for termination is legal: even within an at-will employment relationship, certain specific reasons for termination remain illegal, carved out as exceptions to the general at-will rule. [VERIFIED FACT] These generally include terminating an employee based on a legally protected characteristic (a topic covered in more depth in the next lesson), terminating an employee in retaliation for exercising a legally protected right (such as filing a workplace safety complaint or participating in a legal investigation), or terminating an employee in violation of a specific contractual promise that overrides the general at-will presumption.

This is why "at-will employment" is better understood as "termination doesn't require a specific justification in most circumstances" rather than "any termination reason whatsoever is automatically legal" — the general flexibility at-will employment provides has real, specific, legally significant exceptions.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An employer terminates an at-will employee, citing general business reasons without providing extensive specific justification — generally permissible under the at-will employment doctrine. [ASSUMPTION] If instead the true underlying reason for that same termination was the employee's participation in a legally protected activity, such as reporting a workplace safety violation, the termination could be illegal despite the employment being at-will, since retaliation for exercising a legally protected right is a specific, recognized exception to the general at-will rule, regardless of how the employer characterizes the termination.

## Key Takeaways
- At-will employment generally allows termination without needing specific justification, and applies as the default absent a contrary contract.
