## Core Content

Lesson 9 covered NDAs in depth, focused on protecting confidential information. A **non-disparagement clause** serves a genuinely different purpose: rather than restricting the disclosure of confidential information, it generally restricts a party from making negative, disparaging statements about the other party, regardless of whether those statements involve any confidential information at all.

Here's the "why" these are distinct provisions, even though they commonly appear together in the same agreement, particularly in employment separation contexts: [VERIFIED FACT] a statement could violate a non-disparagement clause without disclosing any confidential information whatsoever — a former employee publicly criticizing a former employer's general business practices or leadership, using only publicly known or personally observed information, could violate a non-disparagement clause without ever touching on genuinely confidential information covered by a separate NDA.

This is why a comprehensive separation or settlement agreement often includes both clauses separately and specifically: an NDA protecting against disclosure of genuinely confidential information, and a non-disparagement clause separately protecting against negative public statements, since a party could potentially violate one without the other, depending on the specific content of what's said or disclosed.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A former employee, after leaving a company under a separation agreement including both an NDA and a non-disparagement clause, makes public statements criticizing the company's general leadership style and business decisions, based entirely on things she personally observed and that weren't specifically confidential. [ASSUMPTION] This could violate the non-disparagement clause, since it involves negative, disparaging statements about the company, even though it doesn't violate the separate NDA at all, since no genuinely confidential information was disclosed — illustrating why these two clauses address genuinely different types of restricted conduct.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is the general difference between a non-disparagement clause and an NDA?
2. Why can a statement violate a non-disparagement clause without disclosing any confidential information?
3. Why do comprehensive agreements often include both clauses separately?

## Answer Key

1. A non-disparagement clause restricts negative, disparaging statements about the other party; an NDA restricts disclosure of specifically confidential information, regardless of whether it's positive or negative.
2. Because negative or critical statements can be made using only publicly known or personally observed information, without touching on anything genuinely confidential.
3. Because a party could potentially violate one clause without violating the other, depending on the specific content of what's said or disclosed, so both types of protection are often needed together.

## Key Takeaways
- A non-disparagement clause restricts negative statements about a party; an NDA restricts disclosure of confidential information — these are genuinely different concerns.
- A statement can violate one clause without violating the other, since disparagement doesn't require confidential information.
- Comprehensive agreements, especially in employment separations, often include both clauses separately to address each distinct concern.
