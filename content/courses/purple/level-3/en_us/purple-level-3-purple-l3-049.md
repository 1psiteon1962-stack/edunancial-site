## Core Content

This track has covered contracts, business entities, intellectual property, estate planning, property law, employment law, and dispute resolution — genuinely different legal domains. This closing synthesis revisits the single theme running underneath nearly all of it: clarity about exactly who has which specific rights and obligations, and under what conditions.

A contract's enforceability depends on clearly establishing offer, acceptance, and consideration (Lesson 1). An indemnification clause's value depends on its specifically defined scope (Lesson 7). An LLC's liability protection depends on clearly maintaining the entity's separateness (Lesson 11). IP ownership depends on clearly established agreements, absent which a default rule fills the gap (Lessons 18-19). An estate plan's effectiveness depends on clearly and consistently documenting intentions across wills, trusts, and beneficiary designations together (Lessons 23-30). A power of attorney's authority depends entirely on its clearly specified scope (Lesson 28). Even at-will employment's exceptions depend on clearly defined protected categories (Lessons 37-38).

Here's the "why" tying every concept back to this lens leads to more consistent legal judgment: ambiguity — about what a contract's terms actually mean, about whether an entity was genuinely operated separately, about who actually owns a piece of intellectual property, about what an estate plan actually intended — is where legal disputes most commonly arise and are hardest to resolve predictably. An individual or business that consistently asks "have I clearly defined the relevant rights and obligations here, in writing, in a way that would hold up if specifically challenged" is applying a single, coherent framework across every legal domain covered in this track.
