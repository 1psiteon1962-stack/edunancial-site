## Core Content

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. It's a strategy built entirely on a concept from the previous two lessons: forcing equity through renovation, then accessing that equity through a cash-out refinance to fund the next deal.

Here's how the steps connect, and why the order matters:

- **Buy** a property below market value, usually because it needs work.
- **Rehab** it — the renovation is what forces appreciation; the property is now worth more than the purchase price plus repair costs.
- **Rent** it to a tenant, establishing income.
- **Refinance** based on the new, higher appraised value — pulling out cash close to (ideally covering) the original investment.
- **Repeat** — using the recovered capital to buy the next property.

The "why" behind BRRRR's appeal is capital efficiency: if executed well, an investor can get most or all of their initial cash back through the refinance step, meaning the same starting capital can be redeployed into property after property, rather than being permanently tied up in the first deal.

[VERIFIED FACT] This strategy depends on the property appraising for enough, after rehab, to support a refinance loan that returns a meaningful portion of the investor's capital — and appraisals are conducted by independent, licensed appraisers, not guaranteed to match an investor's own projections.

This is exactly where BRRRR most commonly breaks: the **Refinance** step. If the post-rehab appraisal comes in lower than projected — due to a soft market, an overly optimistic renovation budget, or comparable sales that don't support the expected value — the investor may not recover as much capital as planned, leaving money tied up longer than intended and disrupting the "Repeat" step entirely.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor buys a distressed property for $100,000 and spends $40,000 on rehab, for a total of $140,000 invested. [ASSUMPTION] They project the property will appraise for $190,000 after rehab, allowing an 80% LTV refinance loan of $152,000 — enough to recover their full $140,000 investment plus a small buffer.

If the appraisal instead comes in at $165,000 — lower than projected — the maximum refinance loan at 80% LTV would be $132,000, which is $8,000 short of the original $140,000 invested. The investor's capital isn't fully recycled, and the next deal in the "Repeat" step may need to be delayed or financed differently.

## Key Takeaways
- BRRRR (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat) allows capital to be recycled across multiple properties rather than staying tied up in one.
- The strategy depends on rehab creating real forced appreciation and on the refinance step confirming that value.
- The Refinance step is the most common point of failure, since it depends on an independent appraisal the investor doesn't control.
- A lower-than-projected appraisal can leave capital tied up longer than planned and delay the "Repeat" step.
