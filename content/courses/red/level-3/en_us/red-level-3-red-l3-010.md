## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why projected rent and collected rent are not the same number
- Explain why cash reserves are a form of risk management against leverage
- Calculate a simple vacancy-adjusted cash flow estimate

## Core Content

Every cash flow projection in earlier lessons assumed rent gets paid on time, every month. In reality, units sit vacant between tenants, tenants sometimes pay late, and unexpected repairs come up. Why does this matter so much here? Because your mortgage payment doesn't pause for any of that — the lender expects payment whether or not the unit is occupied.

This is why experienced investors build a **vacancy rate** and a **maintenance reserve** into their projections upfront, rather than treating them as afterthoughts. [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Budgeting for 5–8% vacancy means setting aside that percentage of expected rent, even in months when the unit is actually occupied, so the money is there when a vacancy eventually happens — because it will, eventually.

Reserves function as the buffer between a leveraged property's fixed obligation (the mortgage) and its variable reality (actual collected income). Without reserves, an investor is one bad month away from covering the mortgage out of pocket — turning strategic debt into consumer debt, as covered earlier in this track.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A property rents for $1,800/month. Naive cash flow math assumes $1,800 arrives every month. [ASSUMPTION] Budgeting a 7% vacancy reserve means setting aside $126/month, leaving $1,674/month as the "real" income used to test whether the mortgage and expenses are covered — a more honest number than the advertised rent.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why doesn't advertised rent equal reliable monthly income?
2. What is the purpose of a vacancy reserve?
3. What happens to an investor without reserves when a unit goes vacant?

## Answer Key

1. Because vacancies, late payments, and turnover between tenants reduce actual collected income below the advertised rent.
2. It sets aside a portion of income during occupied months so funds are available during inevitable vacant months.
3. They may have to cover the mortgage out of pocket, which can turn a strategic debt into an out-of-pocket liability.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This distinguishes a marketing number from an underwriting number, which is the core point of the lesson.
2. This confirms understanding of reserves as a proactive buffer, not a reaction to a crisis.
3. This ties the lesson back to the strategic vs. consumer debt distinction from Lesson 1.

## Key Takeaways
- Advertised rent and reliably collected income are not the same number.
- Vacancy reserves should be built into projections from the start, not treated as an afterthought.
- Reserves are what keep a leveraged property behaving like strategic debt during a rough month.
