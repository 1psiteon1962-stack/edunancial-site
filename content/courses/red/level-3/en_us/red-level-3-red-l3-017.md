## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain what hard money and private loans are
- Explain why these loans are faster to obtain than conventional financing
- Explain why speed and flexibility come at a higher cost

## Core Content

Conventional loans, as covered in earlier lessons, involve standardized underwriting: DTI, LTV, appraisals, and often weeks of processing time. **Hard money loans** (from lending businesses) and **private loans** (from individual investors) skip much of that process by lending primarily against the value of the property and the strength of the deal itself, rather than the borrower's full financial profile.

Here's the "why" behind their existence: some deals — a distressed property that needs fast cash to close, a short renovation timeline before a planned refinance — don't fit conventional underwriting timelines or property condition requirements. A property needing major repairs may not even qualify for a conventional loan until the repairs are done. Hard money and private lenders fill that gap, funding deals quickly based largely on the property's value and the exit plan.

[VERIFIED FACT] This speed and flexibility typically comes with meaningfully higher interest rates and shorter terms than conventional financing, along with higher fees — the cost reflects the lender's added risk and the reduced underwriting.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor finds a distressed property that needs $50,000 in repairs before it would qualify for a conventional loan, and a competing buyer is also interested. A hard money lender agrees to fund the purchase and rehab quickly, based mainly on the after-repair value, at a materially higher rate than a conventional mortgage. The investor's plan, consistent with the BRRRR strategy from Lesson 9, is to refinance into a conventional loan once the rehab is complete and the property qualifies — using the hard money loan only as a short-term bridge, not a long-term financing solution.

## Practice Quiz

1. What do hard money and private loans focus on instead of full conventional underwriting?
2. Why might a distressed property need this kind of financing instead of a conventional loan?
3. Why is this type of financing typically more expensive than a conventional loan?

## Answer Key

1. The value of the property and the strength of the deal or exit plan, rather than the borrower's complete financial profile.
2. Because the property's condition may not meet conventional loan requirements until repairs are completed, and the timeline for approval may be too slow for the deal.
3. Because the lender takes on more risk with less underwriting and typically expects the loan to be short-term, both of which are priced into higher rates and fees.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the defining feature distinguishing this loan type from conventional financing.
2. This grounds the abstract concept in the specific, common scenario where this financing is used.
3. This reinforces a pattern seen throughout this track: reduced underwriting friction is generally paired with a higher cost.

## Key Takeaways
- Hard money and private loans are underwritten primarily on property value and deal strength, not full borrower financials.
- They're often used for properties or timelines that don't fit conventional loan requirements.
- This speed and flexibility comes at the cost of higher rates, higher fees, and shorter terms — usually intended as a short-term bridge, not permanent financing.
