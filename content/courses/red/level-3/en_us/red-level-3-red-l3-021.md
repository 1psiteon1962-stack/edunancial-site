## Core Content

A **Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)** is a revolving credit line secured by a property's equity — similar in purpose to the cash-out refinance covered in Lesson 8, but structurally different. Rather than replacing your existing mortgage with a new, larger loan, a HELOC sits as a separate line of credit on top of your existing mortgage, which you can draw from, repay, and draw from again, up to a limit, during a set draw period.

Here's the "why" behind choosing a HELOC over a refinance: it leaves your original mortgage (and its rate) untouched, which matters a great deal if that original rate is favorable, as covered in Lesson 2's fiat-currency discussion. It also offers flexibility — you only pay interest on the amount actually drawn, not the full credit line, making it useful for funding a renovation or a down payment without committing to a large lump-sum loan upfront.

[VERIFIED FACT] HELOCs typically carry variable interest rates, meaning payments can rise if rates increase — connecting directly to the interest rate risk covered in Lesson 6, and requiring careful planning around how and when the balance will be repaid.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor with a favorable, low fixed-rate mortgage on an existing rental property wants to access $40,000 in equity for a down payment on a new property, without disturbing the original low-rate loan. She opens a HELOC instead of doing a cash-out refinance, draws $40,000 for the new down payment, and keeps her original mortgage's low rate intact. She pays variable interest only on the $40,000 drawn, and plans to pay it down using cash flow from the new property.

## Answer Key

1. A HELOC is a separate revolving credit line on top of the existing mortgage, while a cash-out refinance replaces the existing mortgage entirely with a new, larger one.
2. Because a HELOC leaves the original, favorable-rate mortgage untouched, rather than replacing it with a new loan potentially at a higher current rate.
3. HELOCs typically carry variable rates, so payments can rise if interest rates increase, similar to the risk discussed for adjustable-rate mortgages.

## Key Takeaways
- A HELOC is a separate, revolving credit line secured by home equity, distinct from a cash-out refinance.
- It preserves an existing favorable mortgage rate while still providing access to equity.
- HELOCs typically carry variable rates, introducing the same interest rate risk covered earlier in this track.
