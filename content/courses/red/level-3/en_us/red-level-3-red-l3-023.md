## Core Content

A **prepayment penalty** is a fee some loans charge if the borrower pays off the loan earlier than scheduled — including through a sale or a refinance. Lenders include this clause because early payoff means they lose out on interest income they expected to collect over the loan's full term; the penalty compensates them for that lost income.

Here's the "why" this matters directly for strategies covered earlier in this track: a plan built around refinancing quickly (Lesson 8's cash-out refinance, or the BRRRR strategy in Lesson 9) can be undermined if the original loan carries a steep prepayment penalty. The cost of that penalty has to be weighed against the benefit of refinancing — sometimes it still makes sense, sometimes it erases most of the advantage.

[VERIFIED FACT] Prepayment penalty terms — whether they exist, how they're calculated, and how long they last — vary significantly by loan type and lender, and are disclosed in loan documents, making them something to review carefully before signing, not something to discover later.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor plans a BRRRR-style refinance 12 months after purchase. Her original loan has a prepayment penalty that applies if the loan is paid off within the first 3 years. [ASSUMPTION] If that penalty would cost several thousand dollars, she needs to weigh that cost against the benefit of refinancing now versus waiting until the penalty period ends — a decision that directly affects the "Repeat" step of her strategy and how quickly she can redeploy capital.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is a prepayment penalty, and why do lenders include it?
2. Why does this clause matter specifically for a BRRRR-style strategy?
3. When should an investor find out whether a loan has a prepayment penalty?

## Answer Key

1. A fee charged for paying off a loan early, which compensates the lender for interest income they expected to receive but won't now collect.
2. Because BRRRR depends on refinancing relatively quickly, and a prepayment penalty on the original loan can add unexpected cost right at the point where the strategy plans to refinance.
3. Before signing the original loan, by carefully reviewing the loan documents — not after deciding to refinance or sell.

## Answers and Explanations

## Key Takeaways
- A prepayment penalty charges a fee for paying off a loan earlier than scheduled, compensating the lender for lost interest income.
- This clause can significantly affect the economics of an early refinance, especially for strategies like BRRRR that depend on refinancing quickly.
- Prepayment terms should be reviewed carefully in loan documents before signing, not discovered after the fact.
