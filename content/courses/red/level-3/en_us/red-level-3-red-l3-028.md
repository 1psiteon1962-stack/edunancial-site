## Core Content

Many investors hold rental properties inside an LLC (Limited Liability Company) partly for liability and asset protection purposes — separating personal assets from the property's risks. But when that LLC applies for a loan, especially with a smaller lender or a commercial loan (Lesson 16), the lender may still require a **personal guarantee** from the individual investor behind the LLC.

Here's the "why": lenders know that a newer or thinly capitalized LLC may not have much of a financial track record or many assets of its own. A personal guarantee lets the lender pursue the individual guarantor personally if the LLC defaults and can't cover the debt — similar in effect to a recourse loan (Lesson 27), even though the loan itself is technically made to the LLC.

This is an important, easy-to-miss point: an LLC's liability protection can be significantly undermined by a personal guarantee. If the investor personally guarantees the LLC's loan, a default can still expose personal assets, even though the property is held inside the "protected" entity.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor forms an LLC to hold a rental property, expecting the LLC structure to shield her personal assets from any liability tied to the property. The lender, evaluating the LLC's limited financial history, requires her to personally guarantee the loan as a condition of approval. If the LLC later defaults, the lender can pursue her personally for the shortfall under the guarantee — meaning the liability protection she expected from the LLC structure doesn't fully apply to this particular debt.

1. What is a personal guarantee?

## Answer Key

1. A commitment by an individual to be personally responsible for a loan if the primary borrower (often an LLC) can't repay it.
2. Because a newer or thinly capitalized LLC may not have enough of its own financial track record or assets to reassure the lender without additional backing.
3. It can significantly undermine that protection for the guaranteed debt specifically, since the lender can pursue the individual's personal assets despite the LLC structure.

## Key Takeaways
- A personal guarantee makes an individual personally responsible for a loan even when it's technically made to an LLC.
- Lenders often require this for LLCs without an established financial track record.
- A personal guarantee can significantly undermine the liability protection an LLC is otherwise meant to provide, for that specific debt.
