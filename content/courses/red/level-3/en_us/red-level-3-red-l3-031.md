## Core Content

A **bridge loan** is a short-term loan designed to "bridge" a gap between two financial events — most often between purchasing a new property and either selling an existing one or securing permanent long-term financing. It's a close relative of the hard money and private loans covered in Lesson 17, sharing the same emphasis on speed over standardized underwriting.

Here's the "why" bridge loans exist: real estate transactions rarely line up perfectly in time. An investor might find a great new deal before their current property has sold, or need to close quickly on a purchase before a conventional refinance (as in BRRRR, Lesson 9) can be arranged. A bridge loan provides temporary capital to close the timing gap, with the expectation that it will be paid off relatively soon through the sale or the permanent financing it's "bridging" toward.

[VERIFIED FACT] Bridge loans typically carry higher interest rates and shorter terms than conventional financing, reflecting their short-term, timing-focused purpose — and they depend on the anticipated event (a sale or refinance) actually happening on schedule.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor finds an excellent new property but her existing rental hasn't sold yet, and she doesn't want to lose the new deal waiting for that sale to close. She takes a short-term bridge loan to fund the new purchase, planning to pay it off once her existing property sells. [ASSUMPTION] If that sale takes longer than expected, or falls through, she's left holding an expensive short-term loan longer than planned — highlighting the risk of relying on an anticipated event to resolve short-term debt.

## Practice Quiz

1. What problem is a bridge loan designed to solve?
2. What is bridge financing meant to be replaced by, and on what timeline?
3. What is the main risk of using a bridge loan?

## Answer Key

1. A timing gap between two financial events, such as buying a new property before selling an existing one or before permanent financing is arranged.
2. It's meant to be replaced relatively soon by permanent financing or sale proceeds from the anticipated event.
3. If the anticipated event (a sale or refinance) is delayed or falls through, the borrower can be left holding expensive short-term debt longer than planned.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This establishes the specific purpose that distinguishes a bridge loan from other short-term financing types.
2. This clarifies that a bridge loan is inherently transitional, not a long-term solution.
3. This is the central risk of the lesson, dependent on an event outside the borrower's full control.

## Key Takeaways
- A bridge loan provides short-term capital to cover a timing gap between two financial events.
- It's meant to be paid off relatively quickly through a sale or permanent financing.
- The main risk is that the anticipated event doesn't happen on schedule, leaving the borrower with costly short-term debt for longer than planned.
