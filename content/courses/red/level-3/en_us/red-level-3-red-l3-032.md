## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why construction loans disburse funds in stages instead of all at once
- Explain what a "draw schedule" is and why inspections are tied to it
- Explain why a construction loan often converts into a different loan once building is complete

## Core Content

A standard purchase loan disburses the full loan amount at closing, against a property that already exists in its final form. Financing new construction or a major rehab is different, because the thing being financed doesn't fully exist yet — it's a plan and a budget, not a finished, appraisable asset.

Here's the "why" behind the structure: **construction loans** disburse funds in stages, called a **draw schedule**, tied to specific completed phases of the project (foundation, framing, plumbing and electrical, finishing, etc.). [VERIFIED FACT] Lenders typically require an inspection before releasing each draw, confirming the work described has actually been completed to the expected standard, before advancing more funds. This protects the lender from releasing money for work that hasn't happened, and gives the borrower funds matched to their actual progress and need.

Because construction loans are inherently short-term and tied to a project's completion, they often convert into a standard long-term mortgage (sometimes automatically, in a "construction-to-permanent" loan) once the building is finished and can be appraised as a completed asset — connecting back to the refinance concepts covered in Lesson 8.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor builds a new duplex using a construction loan with a draw schedule tied to five project phases. After the foundation is poured and inspected, the first draw is released. Subsequent draws follow the same pattern as framing, then plumbing/electrical, then interior finishing are each completed and inspected. Once the building passes final inspection and is appraised as a completed property, the construction loan converts into a standard long-term mortgage based on the finished property's appraised value — much like the "Refinance" step in a BRRRR strategy (Lesson 9), but for new construction rather than an existing property.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why don't construction loans disburse the full amount at closing, unlike a purchase loan?
2. What is a draw schedule, and why is an inspection typically required before each draw?
3. What commonly happens to a construction loan once the building is complete?

## Answer Key

1. Because the property being financed doesn't fully exist yet, so funds are released in stages matched to actual progress rather than all at once against a finished asset.
2. A draw schedule releases funds tied to specific completed project phases; inspections confirm the described work is actually done before more money is advanced, protecting the lender from funding work that hasn't happened.
3. It often converts into a standard long-term mortgage, sometimes automatically through a construction-to-permanent structure, once the building is complete and can be appraised.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational "why" of the entire lesson.
2. This explains the specific mechanism (draws plus inspections) that makes staged disbursement work in practice.

## Key Takeaways
- Construction loans disburse funds in stages (a draw schedule) rather than all at once, because the asset doesn't fully exist yet.
- Inspections are typically required before each draw to confirm the described work is actually complete.
- Construction loans often convert into a standard long-term mortgage once the building is finished and appraisable.
