## Core Content

When a borrower is in serious financial distress and default seems likely or has already occurred, foreclosure (Lesson 38) isn't the only possible outcome. Two common alternatives are a deed-in-lieu of foreclosure and a short sale.

A **deed-in-lieu of foreclosure** is an agreement where the borrower voluntarily transfers the property's title to the lender, in exchange for the lender releasing them from the remaining loan obligation, avoiding a formal foreclosure process.

A **short sale** happens when the property is sold for less than the remaining loan balance, with the lender's agreement to accept those proceeds as satisfying (or partially satisfying) the debt, rather than pursuing the full balance.

Here's the "why" both exist as alternatives: [VERIFIED FACT] foreclosure is generally costly and time-consuming for lenders, and both a deed-in-lieu and a short sale can potentially resolve the situation faster and with less cost to both parties, compared to a full foreclosure process — though outcomes, credit impact, and any remaining liability for the borrower can vary significantly by situation, lender, and jurisdiction.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor facing a property that's underwater (worth less than the loan balance) and unable to sustain payments contacts her lender before missing payments entirely. Rather than proceeding toward foreclosure, they negotiate a short sale: the property sells for less than the loan balance, and the lender agrees to accept those proceeds as resolving the debt under the negotiated terms. [ASSUMPTION] This path, while still a difficult outcome, is generally considered less damaging to the investor's overall financial position than allowing the situation to proceed all the way through a completed foreclosure.

## Key Takeaways
- A deed-in-lieu of foreclosure voluntarily transfers title to the lender in exchange for release from the remaining debt.
- A short sale sells the property for less than owed, with the lender agreeing to accept those proceeds toward the debt.
- Both are generally considered potentially less damaging alternatives to a completed foreclosure, though specific outcomes vary by situation.
