## Core Content

Interest rates don't stay fixed over the economy's history — they move in broader cycles influenced by inflation, central bank policy, and overall economic conditions. [VERIFIED FACT] These cycles have historically included periods of both rising and falling rates over multi-year spans, though the timing and magnitude of any specific cycle isn't reliably predictable in advance.

Here's the "why" this connects to strategy: a technique that performs well in one rate environment can underperform in another. Aggressive use of leverage tends to look most attractive in a positive-leverage environment (Lesson 24), when cap rates comfortably exceed borrowing costs. In a higher-rate environment, that same aggressive leverage can slide into negative-leverage territory (Lesson 25), and cash-out refinances (Lesson 8) or BRRRR-style strategies (Lesson 9) become more expensive to execute, since the refinance itself happens at prevailing, not historical, rates.

This is why experienced investors think of financing strategy as something to periodically re-evaluate against the current rate environment, rather than a fixed approach applied identically regardless of where rates currently sit in the broader cycle.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor built a portfolio successfully using an aggressive BRRRR strategy during a period of historically low rates, when refinances consistently produced favorable terms. [ASSUMPTION] As rates rise in a later part of the cycle, the same refinance step becomes more expensive and less reliably favorable, and cap-rate-versus-mortgage-rate math (Lesson 24) shifts toward negative leverage on new deals. Continuing to apply the exact same aggressive strategy without adjusting for the new rate environment could mean systematically overpaying for leverage relative to what the earlier part of the cycle allowed.

## Key Takeaways
- Interest rates move in broader cycles over time, not a fixed, unchanging level.
- A financing strategy well-suited to one rate environment can underperform as the cycle shifts.
- Financing strategy should be periodically re-evaluated against current rates, not applied identically regardless of the broader cycle.
