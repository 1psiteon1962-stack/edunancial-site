## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why many government-backed loan programs require owner-occupancy
- Explain why this limits their direct usefulness for a typical rental property investor
- Identify one indirect way these programs can still fit into an investment strategy

## Core Content

Certain government-backed loan programs offer advantages like lower down payments or more flexible qualification standards. Here's the "why" these usually don't apply directly to typical investment property purchases: many of these programs exist specifically to support owner-occupied housing, and generally require the borrower to live in the property as their primary residence for a specified period, not use it purely as a rental investment from day one.

This creates a real limitation for a straightforward buy-and-hold rental investor: these programs generally aren't designed as, or available for, pure investment property financing. [VERIFIED FACT] Owner-occupancy requirements, exact terms, and eligibility rules vary by specific program and change over time, so current program guidelines should always be verified directly rather than assumed.

However, this doesn't mean these programs are irrelevant to an investor's broader strategy. A common indirect approach some investors use — sometimes called "house hacking" — involves purchasing a small multi-unit property (within the 1–4 unit residential range from Lesson 16), living in one unit to satisfy an owner-occupancy requirement, and renting out the other units, gaining real estate investing experience and rental income while still meeting the program's occupancy rule.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor wants to use a government-backed loan program's favorable terms but knows it requires owner-occupancy. Rather than purchasing a pure rental property (which wouldn't qualify), she buys a duplex, moves into one unit to satisfy the occupancy requirement, and rents out the second unit. [ASSUMPTION] After meeting the required occupancy period, she may have options to move out and convert the property to a full rental, having used the program's terms as a stepping stone into her first investment property rather than a direct violation of its intended purpose.
