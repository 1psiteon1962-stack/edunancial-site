## Core Content

So far, this track has largely discussed leverage from the perspective of a single investor. In a **partnership** or **joint venture (JV)**, two or more parties combine capital, and sometimes combine liability, to acquire a property together — often used to access larger deals than one party could pursue individually.

Here's the "why" this changes the risk picture: how ownership is split and how loan liability is split aren't automatically the same thing, and need to be explicitly defined. [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] One partner might contribute most of the capital and hold a larger ownership share, while a lender might still require both partners to personally guarantee the loan (connecting back to Lesson 28) regardless of their ownership percentage — meaning a partner with a smaller ownership stake could still carry significant personal liability exposure if the deal defaults.

[VERIFIED FACT] Because these arrangements involve multiple parties with potentially different levels of involvement, capital, and risk tolerance, a clear written partnership agreement — addressing ownership splits, liability, decision-making authority, and what happens if one partner wants to exit — is generally considered essential, and typically benefits from legal guidance to draft properly.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two partners buy a property together: Partner A contributes 80% of the down payment and holds an 80% ownership share; Partner B contributes 20% and holds 20% ownership. The lender requires both partners to personally guarantee the loan in full, regardless of their ownership split. If the deal defaults, Partner B — despite a smaller ownership stake and smaller capital contribution — could still be personally pursued for the full guaranteed amount, not just 20% of it, unless the partnership agreement and loan documents specifically address and limit that exposure.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why don't ownership percentage and loan liability automatically match in a partnership?
2. In the worked example, what risk does Partner B face despite a smaller ownership share?
3. Why is a clear written partnership agreement especially important for these arrangements?

## Answer Key

1. Because a lender's personal guarantee requirements are separate from how the partners privately agree to split ownership — a lender can require full guarantees from all partners regardless of ownership percentage.
2. Being personally pursued for the full guaranteed loan amount if the deal defaults, not just an amount proportional to his 20% ownership stake, unless specifically addressed in the agreements.
3. Because it defines ownership splits, liability, decision-making, and exit terms explicitly, preventing disputes or unexpected exposure that could otherwise arise from unstated assumptions between partners.

## Key Takeaways
- Ownership percentage and loan liability in a partnership aren't automatically the same and must be explicitly defined.
- A partner with a smaller ownership stake can still carry full personal liability if a lender requires all partners to guarantee the loan.
- A clear, professionally drafted written partnership agreement is essential to define these terms and avoid disputes or unexpected exposure.
