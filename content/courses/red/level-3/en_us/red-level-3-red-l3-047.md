By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain the trade-off between paying down debt early and reinvesting available capital

- Explain why risk tolerance, not just math, plays a role in this choice

## Core Content

An investor with extra capital available often faces a real choice: pay down an existing mortgage faster than required, or reinvest that capital elsewhere (a down payment on another property, for instance). Neither answer is automatically correct — this lesson deliberately avoids treating either option as a default "right" choice.

Here's the "why" behind the trade-off: paying down debt reduces interest costs and increases the equity cushion (Lesson 25's negative leverage risk becomes less relevant as debt shrinks), and it's a guaranteed return equal to the loan's interest rate. Reinvesting instead bets that the reinvested capital can generate a return higher than the mortgage's interest rate — connecting back to the positive/negative leverage comparison from Lesson 24, but applied to the investor's next dollar of capital rather than the whole deal.

[VERIFIED FACT] This decision also depends on factors beyond pure math: an investor's risk tolerance, their need for payment flexibility, and their broader financial goals all reasonably factor into this choice, not just a comparison of expected returns.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor has $30,000 in available capital and a mortgage at a given interest rate. She could put that $30,000 toward extra principal payments, guaranteeing a return equal to the mortgage rate through reduced interest costs. Or she could use it as a down payment on another rental property, betting that the new property's return will exceed the mortgage rate she'd otherwise be "earning" by paying down debt. [ASSUMPTION] If she strongly values reduced risk and payment certainty, the guaranteed debt-reduction return may appeal to her even if the reinvestment option has a theoretically higher expected return — that's a legitimate, personal weighing of risk against return, not a math error.

## Practice Quiz

1. What guaranteed "return" does paying down debt early effectively provide?
2. What is the reinvestment option essentially betting on?
3. Besides pure math, what other factors reasonably influence this decision?

## Answer Key

1. A return equal to the loan's interest rate, through reduced interest costs over the remaining term.
2. That the capital, if redirected elsewhere, can generate a return higher than the mortgage's interest rate.
3. Risk tolerance, need for payment flexibility, and broader personal financial goals.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This frames debt paydown in the same "return" language used to evaluate other investment choices in this track.
2. This connects the decision back to the leverage comparison concepts from Lesson 24.
3. This reinforces that this decision isn't purely mathematical — personal factors are legitimately part of the analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- Paying down debt early versus reinvesting capital elsewhere is a genuine trade-off, not a decision with one universally correct answer.
- Debt paydown offers a guaranteed return equal to the loan's interest rate; reinvestment bets on a higher return elsewhere.
- Risk tolerance and personal financial goals are legitimate parts of this decision, not just a comparison of expected numeric returns.
