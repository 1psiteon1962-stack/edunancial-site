## Main Lesson

### 1. Sector and Industry Defined

A sector is a broad category of the economy (e.g., technology, healthcare, energy), while an industry is a more specific grouping within a sector (e.g., semiconductors within technology). [VERIFIED FACT] Standardized sector classification systems exist and are used by many index providers and analysts.

### 2. Sector Concentration Risk

A portfolio heavily concentrated in one sector, even if diversified across many individual companies within that sector, remains exposed to risks affecting that entire sector — for example, regulatory changes or a broad industry downturn. [VERIFIED FACT] This is a form of risk that individual-company diversification alone does not address.

### 3. Cyclical vs. Defensive Sectors

Cyclical sectors (e.g., consumer discretionary, industrials) tend to be more sensitive to broad economic growth and contraction, while defensive sectors (e.g., utilities, consumer staples) tend to be less sensitive, since demand for their products/services remains relatively stable regardless of economic conditions. [VERIFIED FACT] This classification is a general pattern, not a guarantee of any specific sector's behavior in a given cycle.

### 4. Using Sector Analysis in Practice

Investors sometimes review their portfolio's sector weightings against a broad benchmark to check for unintended concentration, adjusting holdings if one sector represents a disproportionate share of the total portfolio. [OPINION] Many financial educators recommend this as a periodic portfolio review practice, similar to the rebalancing check covered earlier.

## Key Definitions

- **Sector:** A broad category of the economy, such as technology or healthcare.
- **Industry:** A more specific grouping of companies within a sector.
- **Cyclical Sector:** A sector whose performance tends to be more sensitive to broad economic growth and contraction.
- **Defensive Sector:** A sector whose performance tends to be less sensitive to economic cycles due to relatively stable demand.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holding twenty different technology companies might feel diversified by company count, but remains concentrated in a single sector — if a broad technology-sector downturn occurs, most or all twenty holdings could be affected simultaneously.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing diversification across companies with diversification across sectors.
- Assuming defensive sectors are risk-free simply because they're less cyclical.
- Ignoring sector weightings when reviewing overall portfolio risk.
- Chasing whichever sector recently performed best without considering concentration risk.

## FAQ

**Q: How many sectors should my portfolio include?**
There's no single verified number — many broad index funds inherently include exposure across most sectors, which is one reason some investors use them as a diversification base.

**Q: Are defensive sectors always safer?**
They tend to be less sensitive to economic cycles specifically, but they still carry their own risks and are not risk-free.

**Q: How do I check my portfolio's sector weightings?**
Many brokerage platforms and portfolio analysis tools provide a sector breakdown; comparing this to a broad benchmark can reveal concentration.

## Key Takeaways

- A sector is a broad economic category; an industry is a more specific grouping within it.
- Sector concentration is a distinct risk from individual-company concentration.
- Cyclical sectors tend to be more sensitive to economic cycles; defensive sectors tend to be less so.
- Reviewing sector weightings periodically helps catch unintended concentration.

## Practical Exercise

Review your own portfolio (or a hypothetical one) and estimate what percentage falls into a single sector; note whether this feels appropriately diversified or overly concentrated.
