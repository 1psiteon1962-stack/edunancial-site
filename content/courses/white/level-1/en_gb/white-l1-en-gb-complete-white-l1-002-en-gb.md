# WHITE-L1-002: Shares — Owning a Piece of a Company

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a share/stock in plain terms.
2. Explain what shareholders are entitled to.
3. Identify the two main ways share ownership can generate returns.

## Core Content
A share (or stock) represents partial ownership in a company. *VERIFIED FACT:* Shareholders generally have a claim on a company's assets and earnings proportional to how many shares they own, and may have voting rights on certain company matters.

Share ownership can generate returns two ways: **price appreciation** (the share price rising) and **dividends** (a portion of profits distributed to shareholders, when a company chooses to pay them).

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Marcus buys 100 shares of a company at $50 each ($5,000 total, using illustrative round figures). A year later, the price rises to $60/share — his holding is now worth $6,000, a $1,000 gain on paper. If the company also paid a $1/share annual dividend, he'd have received an additional $100 in cash along the way, separate from the price change.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does owning a share represent?
2. Name the two main ways share ownership can generate returns.
3. True/False: All companies are required to pay dividends.
4. If you own 50 shares and the price rises $2/share, how much has your position's value increased?

## Answer Key
1. Partial ownership in a company   2. Price appreciation, dividends   3. False   4. $100

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a share as an ownership stake.
2. These are the two fundamental return sources for shareholders.
3. Dividends are a company choice, not a universal requirement.
4. 50 shares × $2 = $100 increase in position value.

## Key Takeaways
- A share represents proportional ownership in a company.
- Returns come from price appreciation and, optionally, dividends.
- Dividend payment is a company decision, not guaranteed.
