# WHITE-L1-003: Bonds — Lending Your Money for Interest

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a bond in plain terms.
2. Distinguish being a bondholder from being a shareholder.
3. Explain the relationship between bond prices and interest rates.

## Core Content
A bond is essentially a loan an investor makes to an issuer (a government or corporation), who agrees to pay periodic interest and return the principal at maturity. *VERIFIED FACT:* Unlike a shareholder, a bondholder is a creditor, not an owner — they generally have no ownership claim or voting rights in the issuing company.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Bond prices and interest rates generally move inversely — when market interest rates rise, existing bond prices tend to fall, and vice versa, because newer bonds offer more competitive rates.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Elena buys a $1,000 bond paying 4% annual interest, maturing in 10 years. Each year she receives $40 in interest, and at maturity she receives her original $1,000 back. If market interest rates rise to 6% before maturity, her 4% bond becomes less attractive compared to new bonds, and its resale price would typically fall if she wanted to sell before maturity.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a bond, in simple terms?
2. Is a bondholder an owner or a creditor?
3. True/False: Bond prices and interest rates typically move in the same direction.
4. On a $1,000 bond paying 4% annually, how much interest is paid each year?

## Answer Key
1. A loan from the investor to the issuer   2. A creditor   3. False   4. $40

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core lending relationship a bond represents.
2. Bondholders lend money; they don't own equity in the issuer.
3. Bond prices and rates generally move inversely, not together.
4. $1,000 × 0.04 = $40 annual interest.

## Key Takeaways
- A bond is a loan to an issuer, repaid with periodic interest.
- Bondholders are creditors, not owners.
- Bond prices and interest rates typically move inversely.
