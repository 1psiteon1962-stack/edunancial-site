# WHITE-L1-004: Mutual Funds Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a mutual fund.
2. Explain how pooling money benefits individual investors.
3. Identify how mutual fund shares are priced.

## Core Content
A mutual fund pools money from many investors to buy a diversified portfolio of shares, bonds, or other securities, managed by a professional fund manager. *VERIFIED FACT:* This pooling allows individual investors to access diversification and professional management they might not achieve efficiently on their own with smaller amounts of money.

Mutual fund shares are priced once per day at the fund's **Net Asset Value (NAV)** — the total value of the fund's holdings divided by the number of shares outstanding — unlike shares, which trade continuously throughout the day.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Tomás invests $2,000 in a mutual fund holding 500 different shares. Buying even one share of each of those 500 companies individually would require far more money and research than he has. Through the fund, his $2,000 gives him proportional exposure to all 500 holdings at once, managed by professionals who select and monitor those companies.

## Key Takeaways
- Mutual funds pool investor money into a diversified, professionally managed portfolio.
- Shares are priced once daily at NAV, not continuously like shares.
- Pooling gives smaller investors access to diversification and professional management.
