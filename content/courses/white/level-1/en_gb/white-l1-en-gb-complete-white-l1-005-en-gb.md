# WHITE-L1-005: ETFs vs. Mutual Funds

## Learning Objectives
1. Define an ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund).
2. Identify the key structural differences between ETFs and mutual funds.
3. Explain why trading flexibility differs between the two.

## Core Content
An ETF is similar to a mutual fund in that it holds a diversified basket of securities, but it trades on an exchange throughout the day like a share, rather than pricing once daily. *VERIFIED FACT:* This means ETF prices can fluctuate during the trading day, and investors can buy or sell at any point markets are open, unlike mutual funds priced only at day's end.

*ESTIMATE:* ETFs commonly (though not universally) have lower expense ratios than actively managed mutual funds, since many ETFs track an index passively rather than employing active share-picking.

## Worked Example
<br>*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Wei wants to buy into a diversified fund at 11am when she notices a market dip. With an ETF, she can execute that trade immediately at the current market price. Had she chosen an equivalent mutual fund instead, her order wouldn't execute until that day's NAV is calculated after market close — she loses the ability to react to intraday price movements.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the defining structural difference between the two vehicles.
2. ETF prices fluctuate throughout the trading day, unlike mutual funds.
3. Passive management commonly reduces costs compared to active management.
4. Intraday trading allows more responsive entry and exit timing.

## Key Takeaways
- ETFs trade throughout the day like shares; mutual funds price once daily.
- ETFs commonly carry lower expense ratios due to passive management, though not universally.
- Trading flexibility is a key practical difference investors should weigh.
