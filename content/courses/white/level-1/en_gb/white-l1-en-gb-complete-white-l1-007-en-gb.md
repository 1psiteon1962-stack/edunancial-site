# WHITE-L1-007: Market Orders vs. Limit Orders

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a market order and a limit order.
2. Explain the trade-off between execution certainty and price control.
3. Identify a scenario where each order type is appropriate.

## Core Content
A **market order** buys or sells a security immediately at the best currently available price — prioritizing speed of execution over exact price. A **limit order** sets a specific price at which the investor is willing to buy or sell — prioritizing price control, but execution isn't guaranteed if the market never reaches that price.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A share is trading around $50. Noah places a market order to buy — it executes almost instantly, but in a fast-moving market he might pay slightly more or less than $50 depending on the exact moment of execution. His friend Devi instead places a limit order to buy at $48 — she won't pay more than $48, but if the price never drops to $48, her order simply never executes.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does a market order prioritize?
2. What does a limit order prioritize?
3. True/False: A limit order guarantees execution.
4. Which order type would you use if getting the trade done immediately matters more than the exact price?

## Answer Key
1. Speed of execution   2. Price control   3. False   4. A market order

## Answers and Explanations
1. Market orders execute at the best available current price, prioritizing speed.
2. Limit orders execute only at the specified price or better, prioritizing control.
3. Limit orders execute only if the market reaches the set price — not guaranteed.
4. Market orders suit situations where immediate execution matters most.

## Key Takeaways
- Market orders prioritize speed; limit orders prioritize price control.
- Limit orders aren't guaranteed to execute if the price target isn't reached.
- Choose based on whether speed or price precision matters more for that trade.
