# WHITE-L1-008: Dividends — Getting Paid to Own

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a dividend.
2. Explain why not all companies pay dividends.
3. Calculate a simple dividend yield.

## Core Content
A dividend is a portion of a company's profits distributed to shareholders, usually paid quarterly. *ASSUMPTION:* Whether a company pays dividends is a business decision — growth-focused companies often reinvest profits instead of paying dividends, while more mature, stable companies more commonly pay them, though this isn't a strict rule.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Dividend Yield = Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Share Price, expressed as a percentage, showing dividend income relative to the investment's cost.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A share trades at $100/share and pays a $3 annual dividend per share. Dividend Yield = $3 ÷ $100 = 3%. An investor holding 100 shares ($10,000 invested) would receive $300/year in dividend income, separate from any price appreciation or decline in the shares themselves.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a dividend?
2. What is the formula for dividend yield?
3. True/False: All publicly traded companies are required to pay dividends.
4. If a share pays a $2 dividend and trades at $50, what is the dividend yield?

## Answer Key
1. A portion of company profits distributed to shareholders   2. Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Share Price   3. False   4. 4%

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a dividend payment.
2. This is the standard dividend yield calculation.
3. Dividends are a company choice, not a legal requirement.
4. $2 ÷ $50 = 0.04 = 4%.

## Key Takeaways
- Dividends distribute company profits to shareholders, typically quarterly.
- Dividend payment is a business choice, not guaranteed or required.
- Dividend yield measures dividend income relative to share price.
