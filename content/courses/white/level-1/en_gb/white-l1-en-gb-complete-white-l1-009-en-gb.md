# WHITE-L1-009: Capital Gains vs. Capital Losses

## Learning Objectives
1. Define capital gain and capital loss.
2. Distinguish realised from unrealized gains/losses.
3. Explain why the distinction matters for taxes.

## Core Content
A capital gain occurs when an investment is sold for more than its purchase price; a capital loss occurs when sold for less. *VERIFIED FACT:* Gains and losses are "unrealized" (only on paper) until the investment is actually sold — only realised gains/losses typically trigger tax consequences in most jurisdictions.

*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Tax treatment of capital gains (rates, holding period requirements) varies significantly by country and even by how long an asset was held — always verify current local tax rules.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Kenji buys a share for $2,000. It rises to $2,800 in value — an unrealized gain of $800 that exists only on paper and isn't taxed yet. He sells it, realizing an $800 capital gain, which may now be subject to tax depending on his jurisdiction and how long he held the investment.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a capital gain?
2. What does "unrealized" mean in this context?
3. True/False: Unrealized gains are typically taxed immediately.
4. What typically triggers a "realised" gain or loss?

## Answer Key
1. Profit from selling an investment above its purchase price   2. A gain/loss existing only on paper, not yet actualized through a sale   3. False   4. Actually selling the investment

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a capital gain.
2. Unrealized status reflects paper value changes before any sale occurs.
3. Most jurisdictions don't tax unrealized gains — only realised ones typically.
4. Selling the asset converts a paper gain/loss into a realised one.

## Key Takeaways
- Capital gains/losses occur when investments sell above/below purchase price.
- Gains and losses are "unrealized" until actually sold.
- Tax treatment varies significantly by jurisdiction and holding period — verify locally.
