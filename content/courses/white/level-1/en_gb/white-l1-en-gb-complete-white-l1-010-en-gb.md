# WHITE-L1-010: Risk and Reward — The Basic Relationship

## Learning Objectives
1. Explain the general relationship between risk and potential return.
2. Identify why higher potential returns generally come with higher risk.
3. Recognize this is a general tendency, not a guarantee.

## Core Content
*VERIFIED FACT:* In general, investments offering higher potential returns tend to carry higher risk of loss, and lower-risk investments tend to offer more modest potential returns — this is a foundational relationship in investing, though not an absolute rule for every individual investment.

*ASSUMPTION:* An investor's appropriate risk level depends on factors like time horizon, financial goals, and personal risk tolerance — there's no single "correct" risk level for everyone.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Malia compares two options: a savings account paying modest guaranteed interest, and individual growth shares with much higher historical average returns but significant year-to-year volatility, including potential losses. She's saving for retirement 30 years away, giving her time to ride out volatility — a very different situation than someone needing that money in one year.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is the general relationship between risk and potential return?
2. True/False: Every high-risk investment guarantees a high return.
3. Name a factor that affects appropriate risk level for an investor.
4. Why might a long time horizon change risk tolerance?

## Answer Key
1. Higher potential returns generally come with higher risk   2. False   3. Time horizon, financial goals, risk tolerance (among others)   4. More time allows riding out short-term volatility

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the foundational risk-return relationship in investing.
2. Higher risk means higher potential — and higher potential downside — not a guarantee.
3. These are core factors shaping an individual's appropriate risk level.
4. Longer horizons provide more time to recover from downturns before the money is needed.

## Key Takeaways
- Higher potential return generally correlates with higher risk.
- This is a general tendency, not a guarantee for any specific investment.
- Appropriate risk level depends on time horizon, goals, and personal tolerance.
