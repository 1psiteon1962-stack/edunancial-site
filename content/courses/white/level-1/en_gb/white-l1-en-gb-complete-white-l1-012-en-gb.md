# WHITE-L1-012: Asset Allocation Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Define asset allocation.
2. Explain how allocation differs from diversification.
3. Identify factors that typically influence allocation choices.

## Core Content
Asset allocation is how an investor divides their portfolio among broad asset categories — commonly shares, bonds, and cash — based on goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance. *ASSUMPTION:* While diversification spreads risk within a category (many different shares), allocation is the higher-level decision about how much to put in each category in the first place.

*ASSUMPTION:* A commonly cited general tendency is that longer time horizons can support higher share allocations, while shorter horizons often favour more bonds/cash — but this is a general guideline, not a rule applicable to every individual situation.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two investors both diversify well within their share holdings (many different companies). Investor A, 25 years from retirement, allocates 90% shares / 10% bonds. Investor B, 2 years from retirement, allocates 40% shares / 60% bonds. Same diversification principle applied within each category, but very different allocation between categories based on time horizon.

## Key Takeaways
- Asset allocation is the split among broad categories like shares, bonds, cash.
- It's distinct from diversification, which spreads risk within a category.
- Time horizon and risk tolerance commonly influence allocation choices.
