# WHITE-L1-013: Stock Market Indexes (S&P 500 and Others)

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a stock market index.
2. Explain what an index like the S&P 500 measures.
3. Identify why indexes are used as benchmarks.

## Core Content
A stock market index tracks the performance of a specific group of shares, used as a benchmark for a segment of the market. *VERIFIED FACT:* The S&P 500, for example, tracks 500 large U.S. companies and is commonly used as a proxy for overall U.S. large-cap stock market performance.

*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Other major indexes track different markets or segments (e.g., different countries or company sizes) — which index is most relevant depends on what market segment an investor cares about.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A financial news report says "the market was up 1.5% today," typically referring to a major index like the S&P 500 rising that amount. An investor holding an S&P 500 index fund would see their fund's value move similarly, since the fund is designed to track that index's performance closely.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a stock market index?
2. What does the S&P 500 track?
3. True/False: All indexes track the same group of companies.
4. Why are indexes used as benchmarks?

## Answer Key
1. A tool tracking the performance of a specific group of shares   2. 500 large U.S. companies   3. False   4. They provide a reference point for a segment of the market

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core function of a market index.
2. This is the specific composition of the S&P 500.
3. Different indexes track different markets, sizes, or regions.
4. Benchmarks let investors compare performance against a relevant market segment.

## Key Takeaways
- A market index tracks a defined group of shares as a performance benchmark.
- The S&P 500 tracks 500 large U.S. companies specifically.
- Different indexes serve as benchmarks for different market segments.
