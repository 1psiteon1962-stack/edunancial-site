# WHITE-L1-014: Bull Markets vs. Bear Markets

## Learning Objectives
1. Define bull market and bear market.
2. Identify the commonly cited percentage threshold for a bear market.
3. Explain why these cycles are a normal part of investing.

## Core Content
A **bull market** describes a period of generally rising prices and investor optimism; a **bear market** describes a period of generally falling prices and pessimism. *ESTIMATE:* A commonly cited (though not universally standardized) threshold defines a bear market as roughly a 20% decline from recent highs.

*ASSUMPTION:* Market cycles between bull and bear periods have historically been a normal, recurring feature of investing — though the exact timing and duration of each cycle can't be reliably predicted in advance.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor watches the market rise steadily for three years (a bull market), then decline 25% over several months (crossing into bear market territory by the common threshold). Long-term investors who stayed invested through similar historical cycles have generally recovered over time, though past patterns don't guarantee future results.

1. This defines the general bull market condition.
2. This defines the general bear market condition.
3. This is a commonly cited, though not perfectly standardized, threshold.

## Key Takeaways
- Bull markets: rising prices and optimism. Bear markets: falling prices and pessimism.
- A roughly 20% decline is a commonly cited bear market threshold.
- These cycles are a normal, recurring, but unpredictable feature of investing.
