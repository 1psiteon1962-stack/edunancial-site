# WHITE-L1-015: Compound Interest and Growth

## Learning Objectives
1. Define compound interest/growth.
2. Distinguish compounding from simple interest.
3. Explain why time is a critical factor in compounding.

## Core Content
Compound growth means earning returns not just on the original investment, but also on previously accumulated returns. *VERIFIED FACT:* This differs from simple interest, which only calculates returns on the original principal, never on prior gains.

*ASSUMPTION:* Because compounding builds on itself, starting earlier generally has an outsized long-term impact compared to contributing more later — though actual outcomes depend on assumed rates of return, which are never guaranteed.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* $10,000 invested at an assumed 7% average annual return grows to roughly $19,700 after 10 years and roughly $38,700 after 20 years — not double, but nearly quadruple, because the second decade's growth compounds on a much larger base than the first decade's did. This illustrates why the growth curve accelerates over time rather than staying linear.

## Practice Quiz

3. True/False: Starting to invest later has no disadvantage compared to starting earlier, assuming the same total contributions.
4. Why does the compounding growth curve accelerate over time?

## Answer Key
1. Earning returns on both original investment and prior accumulated returns   2. Simple interest only calculates on the original principal   3. False   4. Later growth compounds on an increasingly larger base

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of compound growth.
2. This is the key distinction from simple interest calculations.
3. Time in the market matters significantly due to compounding — starting earlier generally helps.
4. Each period's growth builds on a larger accumulated base than the prior period.

## Key Takeaways
- Compound growth earns returns on both principal and prior gains.
- This differs from simple interest, calculated only on the original principal.
- Time is a critical compounding factor — starting earlier generally has outsized long-term impact.
