# WHITE-L1-016: Dollar-Cost Averaging

## Learning Objectives
1. Define dollar-cost averaging (DCA).
2. Explain how DCA can reduce the impact of market timing.
3. Recognize DCA's trade-offs compared to investing a lump sum.

## Core Content
Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than investing a lump sum all at once. *VERIFIED FACT:* This approach buys more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, averaging the purchase price over time and reducing the impact of any single poorly-timed purchase.

*ASSUMPTION:* DCA doesn't guarantee better results than a lump sum investment — historically, lump sum investing has often outperformed DCA in rising markets, since money is invested (and growing) sooner. DCA's main benefit is reducing emotional and timing risk, not maximizing returns in every scenario.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Instead of investing $12,000 all at once, Yara invests $1,000 every month for a year. Some months she buys shares at higher prices, some months at lower prices — her average cost per share ends up smoother than if she'd happened to invest the full amount at a single bad-timing peak.
