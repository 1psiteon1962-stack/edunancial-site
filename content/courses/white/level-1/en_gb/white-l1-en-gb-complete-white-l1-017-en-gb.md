# WHITE-L1-017: Individual Shares vs. Index Funds

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish picking individual shares from investing in index funds.
2. Explain the trade-offs between the two approaches.
3. Identify why index funds are often recommended for beginners.

## Core Content
Picking **individual shares** means selecting specific companies to invest in, requiring research and carrying concentrated risk. Investing in an **index fund** means buying a fund that tracks a broad market index, spreading risk across many companies automatically.

*OPINION:* Many financial educators commonly suggest index funds as a reasonable starting point for beginners, given their built-in diversification and lower research burden — though this is general guidance, not personalized advice, and individual share picking remains a valid strategy for informed investors.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two friends start investing. Diego spends hours researching and picks 5 individual shares, concentrating his risk in a small number of companies. Fatima buys a single S&P 500 index fund, instantly gaining exposure to 500 companies with no company-specific research required. Diego's approach could outperform or underperform significantly based on his specific picks; Fatima's approach tracks the broader market's average performance.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does picking individual shares require?
2. What does an index fund provide automatically?
3. True/False: Index funds guarantee better returns than individual share picking.
4. Why are index funds often suggested for beginners?

## Answer Key
1. Research and company-specific analysis   2. Diversification across many companies   3. False   4. Built-in diversification and lower research burden

## Answers and Explanations
1. Share picking requires evaluating individual companies' prospects.
2. Index funds automatically spread exposure across their tracked index's holdings.
3. Neither approach guarantees outperformance — outcomes vary.
4. This reflects common educational guidance, not a universal rule for every investor.

## Key Takeaways
- Individual share picking requires research and carries concentrated risk.
- Index funds provide instant diversification with less research burden.
- Index funds are commonly suggested for beginners, though both approaches are valid.
