# WHITE-L1-018: Understanding Expense Ratios

## Learning Objectives
1. Define an expense ratio.
2. Calculate the dollar cost of a fund's expense ratio.
3. Explain why small differences in expense ratios matter over time.

## Core Content
An expense ratio is the annual fee a fund charges, expressed as a percentage of assets invested, covering management and operating costs. *VERIFIED FACT:* This fee is deducted automatically from fund returns — investors don't pay it as a separate bill, but it reduces overall performance.

*ASSUMPTION:* Because expense ratios compound over long holding periods, even small differences (like 0.05% vs. 0.5%) can meaningfully affect long-term returns — the exact impact depends on the investment's growth rate and holding period.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor holds $50,000 in a fund with a 0.5% expense ratio, costing roughly $250/year. A similar fund with a 0.05% expense ratio would cost roughly $25/year — a $225 annual difference that, compounded over decades, can add up to a substantial amount, even though the annual dollar difference looks small at first glance.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is an expense ratio?
2. How is the expense ratio fee typically paid?
3. On $50,000 invested, what does a 0.5% expense ratio cost annually?
4. True/False: Small expense ratio differences have no meaningful long-term impact.

## Answer Key
1. The annual fee a fund charges as a percentage of assets   2. Deducted automatically from fund returns   3. $250   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a fund's expense ratio.
2. Investors don't receive a separate bill — the fee reduces returns directly.
3. $50,000 × 0.005 = $250.
4. Compounded over long periods, even small fee differences can meaningfully affect outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Expense ratios are annual fund fees, expressed as a percentage of assets.
- These are deducted automatically, reducing overall returns.
- Even small expense ratio differences can compound into meaningful long-term impact.
