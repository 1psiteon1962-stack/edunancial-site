# WHITE-L1-019: What Is a 401(k)?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a 401(k) in general terms.
2. Explain the tax-advantaged nature of the account.
3. Recognize this is a U.S.-specific account type.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* A 401(k) is a U.S. employer-sponsored retirement account; other countries have different equivalent structures, and this lesson describes the U.S. concept specifically.

*VERIFIED FACT:* A 401(k) allows employees to contribute a portion of their paycheck to a retirement investment account, often with pre-tax contributions (reducing current taxable income) and tax-deferred growth until withdrawal in retirement.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Layla earns $60,000/year and contributes $6,000 to her 401(k). Her taxable income for the year is reduced to $54,000, lowering her current tax bill. That $6,000 grows tax-deferred inside the account — she won't owe tax on the growth until she eventually withdraws funds, typically in retirement.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a 401(k)?
2. How can pre-tax contributions affect current taxable income?
3. True/False: A 401(k) is available to employees worldwide under that exact name.
4. When does tax on a traditional 401(k)'s growth typically become due?

## Answer Key
1. A U.S. employer-sponsored retirement account   2. Contributions reduce current taxable income   3. False   4. Upon withdrawal, typically in retirement

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a 401(k).
2. Pre-tax contributions lower reported taxable income for that year.
3. This specific account type and name is U.S.-specific.
4. Tax is generally deferred until withdrawal, not owed during the growth phase.

## Key Takeaways
- A 401(k) is a U.S. employer-sponsored retirement account.
- Pre-tax contributions can reduce current taxable income.
- Growth is tax-deferred until withdrawal, typically in retirement.
