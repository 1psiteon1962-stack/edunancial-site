# WHITE-L1-020: What Is an IRA?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define an IRA (Individual Retirement Account).
2. Distinguish an IRA from a 401(k).
3. Recognize this is a U.S.-specific account type.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* An IRA is a U.S. tax-advantaged retirement account; this lesson describes the U.S. concept specifically.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Unlike a 401(k), which is employer-sponsored, an IRA is opened independently by an individual through a brokerage or financial institution, offering similar tax-advantaged retirement savings but with different contribution limits and rules.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Omar's employer doesn't offer a 401(k), so he opens an IRA directly through a brokerage firm. He contributes independently each year, choosing his own investments within the account — a path available to him regardless of what retirement benefits, if any, his employer offers.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is an IRA?
2. How does an IRA differ from a 401(k) in terms of who opens it?
3. True/False: An IRA requires employer sponsorship to open.
4. What is a key similarity between IRAs and 401(k)s?

## Answer Key
1. An individually-opened, tax-advantaged retirement account   2. An individual opens an IRA directly; a 401(k) is employer-sponsored   3. False   4. Both offer tax-advantaged retirement savings

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of an IRA.
2. This is the key structural distinction from a 401(k).
3. IRAs can be opened independently, without needing an employer plan.
4. Both account types share the general goal of tax-advantaged retirement savings.

## Key Takeaways
- An IRA is an individually-opened, U.S. tax-advantaged retirement account.
- Unlike a 401(k), it doesn't require employer sponsorship.
- Contribution limits and specific rules differ between IRAs and 401(k)s.
