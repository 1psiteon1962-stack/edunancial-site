# WHITE-L1-021: Roth vs. Traditional Retirement Accounts

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish Roth from Traditional tax treatment.
2. Explain the timing difference in when tax is paid.
3. Recognize this is a U.S.-specific concept.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Roth and Traditional account structures are U.S.-specific tax concepts, applicable to both 401(k)s and IRAs.

*VERIFIED FACT:* A **Traditional** account uses pre-tax contributions (reducing taxable income now) with withdrawals taxed in retirement. A **Roth** account uses after-tax contributions (no current tax break) but qualified withdrawals in retirement are generally tax-free, including growth.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Priya contributes $6,000 to a Traditional account, reducing her taxable income this year. Her colleague contributes $6,000 to a Roth account instead, paying tax on that income now but expecting tax-free withdrawals decades later, including all the growth accumulated in between — a bet that her tax rate now is lower than it will be in retirement.

## Practice Quiz
1. When are Traditional account contributions taxed?
2. When are qualified Roth withdrawals typically taxed?
3. True/False: Roth contributions provide an immediate tax deduction.
4. What is the general difference in timing between Roth and Traditional tax treatment?

## Answer Key
1. Withdrawals are taxed in retirement   2. Generally tax-free if qualified   3. False   4. Traditional taxes later; Roth taxes now

## Answers and Explanations
1. Traditional contributions defer tax until withdrawal.
2. Roth withdrawals are generally tax-free when qualified, since tax was paid upfront.
3. Roth contributions are after-tax, with no current-year deduction.
4. This timing difference is the core distinction between the two account types.

## Key Takeaways
- Traditional: pre-tax now, taxed on withdrawal.
- Roth: taxed now, generally tax-free qualified withdrawals later.
- Choosing between them often depends on expected future vs. current tax rates.
