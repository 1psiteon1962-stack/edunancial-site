# WHITE-L1-022: Employer Matching Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define employer matching in a retirement plan context.
2. Explain why unmatched contributions are often called "leaving money on the table."
3. Calculate a simple employer match scenario.

## Core Content
Employer matching means an employer contributes additional money to an employee's retirement account based on the employee's own contributions, up to a certain limit. *VERIFIED FACT:* A common match structure is matching a percentage of employee contributions up to a specified percentage of salary, though exact formulas vary significantly by employer.

*OPINION:* Because employer match is effectively free additional compensation tied to participation, many financial educators commonly suggest contributing at least enough to capture the full match before prioritizing other savings goals — though individual financial circumstances vary.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Devon's employer matches 50% of contributions up to 6% of his $50,000 salary. If he contributes 6% ($3,000), his employer adds an additional $1,500 (50% of $3,000) — free money added to his account simply for contributing enough to capture the full match. If he contributed only 2%, he'd receive a smaller match and effectively forfeit part of that available benefit.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is employer matching?
2. On a $3,000 contribution with a 50% match, how much does the employer add?
3. True/False: Employer match is typically unlimited regardless of contribution amount.
4. Why is failing to capture the full match commonly described as "leaving money on the table"?

## Answer Key
1. Additional employer contributions based on employee contributions, up to a limit   2. $1,500   3. False   4. It represents available free compensation the employee didn't claim

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of employer matching.
2. 50% × $3,000 = $1,500.
3. Matches are typically capped at a specified percentage of salary or contribution.
4. Match funds not captured represent unclaimed available compensation.

## Key Takeaways
- Employer matching adds employer funds based on employee contributions, up to a cap.
- Match structures and limits vary significantly by employer.
- Commonly cited guidance suggests contributing enough to capture the full available match.
