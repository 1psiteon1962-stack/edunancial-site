# WHITE-L1-024: Market Capitalization Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define market capitalization.
2. Calculate a simple market cap.
3. Identify common market cap categories.

## Core Content
Market capitalization (market cap) is the total value of a company's outstanding shares. *VERIFIED FACT:* Market Cap = Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding.

*ESTIMATE:* Common (though not perfectly standardized) categories include large-cap (roughly $10 billion+), mid-cap (roughly $2-10 billion), and small-cap (roughly $300 million-$2 billion) — exact thresholds vary by source.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A company trades at $50/share with 100 million shares outstanding. Market Cap = $50 × 100,000,000 = $5 billion, placing it in the mid-cap category by common thresholds. This figure represents the market's current collective valuation of the entire company, not just one share's price.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is the formula for market capitalization?
2. If a share trades at $20 with 50 million shares outstanding, what is the market cap?
3. True/False: Market cap categories have one single universally agreed definition.
4. What does market cap represent?

## Answer Key
1. Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding   2. $1 billion   3. False   4. The market's total valuation of the entire company

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the standard market cap calculation.
2. $20 × 50,000,000 = $1,000,000,000 = $1 billion.
3. Category thresholds vary somewhat by source, though general ranges are widely used.
4. Market cap reflects the company's total equity value at current market price.

## Key Takeaways
- Market Cap = Share Price × Shares Outstanding.
- It represents the total market valuation of a company, not per-share price alone.
- Common categories (large, mid, small-cap) have general, not perfectly standardized, thresholds.
