# WHITE-L1-025: Growth Shares vs. Value Shares

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish growth shares from value shares.
2. Identify general characteristics of each category.
3. Recognize neither category is universally superior.

## Core Content
**Growth shares** are companies expected to grow earnings faster than average, often reinvesting profits rather than paying dividends, and often trading at higher valuations relative to current earnings. **Value shares** are companies perceived as trading below their intrinsic worth, often more established, sometimes paying dividends, with lower valuations relative to earnings.

*ASSUMPTION:* Neither category consistently outperforms the other across all time periods — market leadership has historically rotated between growth and value at different times, and predicting which will lead going forward isn't reliable.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two investors build different portfolios. One favours growth shares — younger, fast-expanding companies reinvesting heavily, with no current dividends. The other favours value shares — established, stable companies paying steady dividends but with slower growth. Both are valid investing philosophies; which performs better in any given period isn't reliably predictable in advance.

## Practice Quiz
1. What generally characterizes growth shares?
2. What generally characterizes value shares?
3. True/False: Growth shares always outperform value shares.
4. What has historically happened with growth and value leadership over time?

## Answer Key
1. Faster expected earnings growth, often reinvesting profits   2. Perceived undervaluation, often more established, sometimes dividend-paying   3. False   4. Leadership has historically rotated between the two categories

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general growth share profile.
2. This is the general value share profile.
3. Neither category consistently outperforms across all periods.
4. Historical rotation between leadership styles is a commonly observed market pattern.

## Key Takeaways
- Growth shares emphasize expansion and reinvestment; value shares emphasize perceived undervaluation.
- Neither category is universally superior across all time periods.
- Market leadership has historically rotated between growth and value styles.
