# WHITE-L1-026: Blue-Chip Shares

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a blue-chip share.
2. Identify general characteristics of blue-chip companies.
3. Recognize that "blue-chip" doesn't guarantee safety from loss.

## Core Content
Blue-chip shares generally refer to large, well-established, financially stable companies with a long history of reliable performance. *ASSUMPTION:* While often perceived as relatively lower-risk compared to smaller or newer companies, "blue-chip" is a general reputational category, not a formal financial classification with a precise legal definition — and blue-chip shares can still decline significantly in value.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor building a "core" portfolio position includes several long-established, large companies with decades of consistent operating history — commonly described as blue-chip holdings. This doesn't mean these shares can't lose value; even well-established companies can decline substantially during broad market downturns or company-specific troubles.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does "blue-chip" generally describe?
2. True/False: Blue-chip is a precise, formally defined financial category.
3. Can blue-chip shares lose value?
4. What perception do blue-chip shares often carry, according to the lesson?

## Answer Key
1. Large, well-established, financially stable companies   2. False   3. Yes   4. Perceived relative stability compared to smaller/newer companies

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general description of blue-chip companies.
2. It's a widely used but informal reputational term, not a strict classification.
3. No share category is immune to potential loss.
4. This perception exists but isn't a guarantee against decline.

## Key Takeaways
- Blue-chip describes large, established, historically stable companies.
- This is an informal, widely used term, not a precise financial classification.
- Blue-chip status doesn't guarantee protection from loss.
