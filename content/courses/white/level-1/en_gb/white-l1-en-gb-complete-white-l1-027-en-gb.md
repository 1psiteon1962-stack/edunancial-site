# WHITE-L1-027: Penny Shares and Their Risks

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a penny share.
2. Identify why penny shares carry elevated risk.
3. Recognize common warning signs associated with penny share schemes.

## Core Content
*ESTIMATE:* Penny shares are commonly (though not perfectly uniformly) defined as shares trading below $5, often of small, thinly-traded companies with limited public information available.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Penny shares generally carry elevated risk due to low liquidity (harder to buy/sell without moving the price), limited public information, and greater susceptibility to manipulation schemes like "pump and dump," where promoters artificially inflate a price before selling their own shares to unsuspecting buyers.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A penny share is heavily promoted on social media with claims of guaranteed massive returns. Its price spikes rapidly as new buyers rush in. Shortly after, the original promoters sell their shares, causing the price to collapse — the classic pattern of a pump-and-dump scheme, leaving later buyers with significant losses.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a commonly cited price threshold for a penny share?
2. Name two reasons penny shares carry elevated risk.
3. What is a "pump and dump" scheme?
4. True/False: Penny shares always have abundant public information available.

## Answer Key
1. Below $5   2. Low liquidity, limited public information (among others)   3. A scheme where promoters inflate a price then sell before it collapses   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is a commonly cited, though not perfectly standardized, threshold.
2. These are among the primary risk factors for penny shares.
3. This is the classic manipulation pattern associated with penny shares.
4. Penny shares often have limited public information, unlike larger, well-covered companies.

## Key Takeaways
- Penny shares are commonly defined as low-priced shares of small, thinly-traded companies.
- They carry elevated risk from low liquidity, limited information, and manipulation potential.
- Pump-and-dump schemes are a recognised risk pattern specific to this category.
