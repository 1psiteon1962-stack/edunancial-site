# WHITE-L1-029: The Role of a Financial Advisor

## Learning Objectives
1. Define the general role of a financial advisor.
2. Distinguish fee-only from commission-based advisors.
3. Explain why understanding compensation structure matters.

## Core Content
A financial advisor provides guidance on financial planning, investing, retirement, and related decisions. *REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Licensing, regulation, and fiduciary standards for advisors vary significantly by jurisdiction.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Advisors are commonly compensated in different ways: **fee-only** (paid directly by the client, no product commissions), **commission-based** (earning money from products sold), or a hybrid of both — the compensation structure can influence what an advisor is incentivized to recommend.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Two advisors recommend different investment products to the same client. One is fee-only, charging a flat rate regardless of what's recommended. The other earns a commission on the specific product sold. Understanding this difference helps the client evaluate whether a recommendation is purely need-based or potentially influenced by the advisor's compensation structure.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does a financial advisor generally provide?

3. What is a "commission-based" advisor?
4. Why does compensation structure matter when evaluating advice?

1. This is the general scope of a financial advisor's role.
2. This defines the fee-only compensation model.
3. This defines the commission-based compensation model.

## Key Takeaways
- Financial advisors provide guidance across planning, investing, and related decisions.
- Compensation structures (fee-only, commission-based, hybrid) vary and can influence recommendations.
- Licensing and regulatory standards vary significantly by jurisdiction.
