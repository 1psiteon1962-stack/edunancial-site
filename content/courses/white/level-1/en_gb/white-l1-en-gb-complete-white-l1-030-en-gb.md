# WHITE-L1-030: Robo-Advisors Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a robo-advisor.
2. Explain how robo-advisors typically build portfolios.
3. Identify a common trade-off compared to human advisors.

## Core Content
A robo-advisor is an automated platform that builds and manages an investment portfolio based on an investor's stated goals and risk tolerance, typically using algorithms rather than a human advisor for day-to-day decisions. *ESTIMATE:* Robo-advisors commonly charge lower fees than traditional human financial advisors, often using low-cost index funds/ETFs as underlying investments.

*ASSUMPTION:* The trade-off is generally less personalized guidance for complex or unique financial situations compared to a human advisor, though this varies by platform and some offer hybrid human-plus-algorithm services.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Fatima answers a questionnaire about her goals, timeline, and risk tolerance on a robo-advisor platform. The algorithm automatically builds a diversified portfolio matching her profile and rebalances it periodically — all without her needing to speak to a human advisor or make individual investment selections herself.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a robo-advisor.
2. Lower cost is a commonly cited robo-advisor advantage.
3. Some platforms offer hybrid human-plus-algorithm services.
4. Reduced personalization is a general trade-off for the lower cost and automation.

## Key Takeaways
- Robo-advisors automate portfolio building and management based on stated goals.
- They commonly offer lower fees than traditional human advisors.
- The trade-off is generally less personalized guidance, though hybrid models exist.
