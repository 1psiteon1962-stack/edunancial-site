# WHITE-L1-033: What Is a Share Split?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a share split.
2. Explain why a split doesn't change total investment value.
3. Distinguish a forward split from a reverse split.

## Core Content
A share split increases (or decreases) the number of shares outstanding while proportionally adjusting the price, so the total value of a holding stays the same immediately after the split. *VERIFIED FACT:* In a **forward split** (e.g., 2-for-1), shareholders receive more shares at a proportionally lower price per share. In a **reverse split** (e.g., 1-for-10), shareholders receive fewer shares at a proportionally higher price per share.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor holds 100 shares worth $200 each ($20,000 total) before a 2-for-1 split. After the split, they hold 200 shares worth $100 each — still $20,000 total. Nothing about their actual ownership stake or company value changed; only the number of shares and price per share were adjusted proportionally.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is a share split?
2. In a 2-for-1 forward split, what happens to share count and price?
3. True/False: A share split changes the total value of an existing holding.
4. What is a reverse split?

## Answer Key
1. An adjustment to shares outstanding and price that keeps total value constant   2. Share count doubles; price per share roughly halves   3. False   4. A split that reduces share count while increasing price per share proportionally

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a share split.
2. This describes the standard forward split mechanic.
3. Total holding value remains the same immediately after a split — only the share count and price change.
4. Reverse splits work oppositely to forward splits.

## Key Takeaways
- A share split adjusts share count and price while keeping total value the same.
- Forward splits increase shares and lower price per share; reverse splits do the opposite.
- Splits don't change the underlying value of an investment.
