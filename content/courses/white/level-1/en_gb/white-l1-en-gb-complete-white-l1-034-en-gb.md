# WHITE-L1-034: Preferred Shares vs. Common Shares

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish preferred share from common share.
2. Explain the dividend and liquidation priority differences.
3. Identify a common trade-off of preferred share.

## Core Content
**Common share** is the standard form of ownership, typically including voting rights, with dividends (if any) paid at the company's discretion. **Preferred share** generally has a higher claim than common share on dividends and company assets in liquidation, but usually lacks voting rights and has less potential for dramatic price appreciation.

*VERIFIED FACT:* Preferred dividends are often fixed and typically must be paid before any common share dividends — giving preferred shareholders more income predictability, at the cost of the greater growth potential common share can offer.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A company facing financial trouble suspends its common share dividend. Preferred shareholders, holding a higher-priority claim, may still receive their fixed dividend if the company can pay it at all, since preferred obligations are generally satisfied before common dividends. In a liquidation scenario, preferred shareholders would also be paid before common shareholders, though after bondholders.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does common share typically include that preferred share usually lacks?
2. What claim priority does preferred share generally have over common share?
3. True/False: Preferred dividends are always guaranteed regardless of company performance.
4. What trade-off does preferred share typically involve compared to common share?

## Answer Key
1. Voting rights   2. Higher priority on dividends and liquidation assets   3. False   4. More income predictability but less growth potential

## Answers and Explanations
1. Common share typically carries voting rights that preferred share usually doesn't.
2. Preferred generally has priority, though not an absolute guarantee.
3. Preferred dividends can still be suspended in severe circumstances, though they have priority.
4. This is the general trade-off between the two share types.

## Key Takeaways
- Common share typically includes voting rights; preferred generally doesn't.
- Preferred share generally has priority on dividends and liquidation assets.
- Preferred trades growth potential for greater income predictability.
