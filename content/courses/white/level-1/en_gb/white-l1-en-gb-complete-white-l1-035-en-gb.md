# WHITE-L1-035: Bond Ratings Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a bond rating.
2. Explain the general distinction between investment-grade and high-yield bonds.
3. Identify why ratings can change over time.

## Core Content
Bond ratings are assessments by credit rating agencies of an issuer's ability to repay debt, expressed through letter grades (like AAA down to lower grades). *VERIFIED FACT:* Bonds rated in the higher tiers are generally called "investment-grade," while lower-rated bonds are often called "high-yield" or "junk" bonds, typically offering higher interest rates to compensate for higher perceived default risk.

Ratings can change over time as an issuer's financial health improves or deteriorates — a downgrade generally signals increased perceived risk.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor compares two bonds: one investment-grade at 4% interest, one high-yield (junk) at 9% interest. The higher rate on the junk bond compensates for its higher perceived risk of default — the investor must weigh whether that extra yield adequately compensates for the added risk of not being repaid in full.

## Practice Quiz
1. What do bond ratings assess?
2. What is generally true about high-yield bonds compared to investment-grade bonds?
3. True/False: Bond ratings never change once assigned.
4. What does a rating downgrade generally signal?

## Answer Key
1. An issuer's ability to repay debt   2. They typically offer higher interest rates due to higher perceived risk   3. False   4. Increased perceived risk

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core function of bond ratings.
2. Higher risk is generally compensated with higher yield, a fundamental risk-return relationship.
3. Ratings can and do change as issuer financial health changes.
4. Downgrades reflect the rating agency's reassessment of increased risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Bond ratings assess an issuer's ability to repay debt.
- Investment-grade bonds carry lower risk/yield; high-yield (junk) bonds carry higher risk/yield.
- Ratings can change over time as issuer financial health shifts.
