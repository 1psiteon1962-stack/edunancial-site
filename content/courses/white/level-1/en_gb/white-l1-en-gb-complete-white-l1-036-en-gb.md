# WHITE-L1-036: Treasury Bonds vs. Corporate Bonds

## Learning Objectives
1. Distinguish Treasury bonds from corporate bonds.
2. Explain the general risk difference between the two.
3. Recognize this is a U.S.-centric distinction, with equivalents elsewhere.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* "Treasury bonds" specifically refers to U.S. government debt; other countries issue equivalent sovereign government bonds under different names.

**Treasury bonds** are issued by the U.S. federal government and are generally considered very low default risk, given the government's taxing and money-printing authority. **Corporate bonds** are issued by companies and carry varying risk levels depending on the issuing company's financial health, generally offering higher yields than comparable Treasury bonds to compensate for that added risk.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor compares a 10-year Treasury bond yielding 4% to a 10-year corporate bond from a stable company yielding 5.5%. The extra 1.5% on the corporate bond compensates for the additional (though still relatively modest, for a financially healthy company) default risk compared to the government-backed Treasury.

## Practice Quiz
1. Who issues Treasury bonds?
2. Who issues corporate bonds?
3. True/False: Corporate bonds always carry the same risk level as Treasury bonds.
4. Why do corporate bonds generally offer higher yields than comparable Treasuries?

## Answer Key
1. The U.S. federal government   2. Companies   3. False   4. To compensate for higher perceived default risk compared to government debt

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the defining feature of Treasury bonds.
2. This is the defining feature of corporate bonds.
3. Corporate bond risk varies significantly by issuer, generally exceeding Treasury risk.
4. Higher yield compensates investors for taking on relatively higher risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Treasury bonds are U.S. government debt, generally very low default risk.
- Corporate bonds are issued by companies, with risk varying by issuer's financial health.
- Corporate bonds generally offer higher yields to compensate for added risk.
