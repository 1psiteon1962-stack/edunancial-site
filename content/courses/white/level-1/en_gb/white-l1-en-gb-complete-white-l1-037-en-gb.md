# WHITE-L1-037: Understanding Yield

## Learning Objectives
1. Define yield in general investing terms.
2. Distinguish yield from total return.
3. Calculate a simple yield example.

## Core Content
Yield generally measures income generated by an investment (like dividends or interest) relative to its price, expressed as a percentage. *VERIFIED FACT:* Yield differs from total return, which also includes price appreciation or depreciation — yield captures only the income component, not overall gain or loss.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A bond purchased at $1,000 pays $50 annual interest — a 5% yield. If that same bond's market price later rises to $1,050 while the investor still holds it, their yield calculation (based on original cost) stays conceptually tied to income, while their total return would now also reflect that price appreciation if they were to sell.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does yield generally measure?
2. How does yield differ from total return?
3. If a $500 investment pays $25 annual income, what is the yield?
4. True/False: Yield accounts for price appreciation.

## Answer Key
1. Income generated relative to price   2. Total return also includes price appreciation/depreciation   3. 5%   4. False

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of yield.
2. This is the key distinction between yield and total return.
3. $25 ÷ $500 = 0.05 = 5%.
4. Yield captures income only, not price movement.

## Key Takeaways
- Yield measures income relative to price, as a percentage.
- Yield is distinct from total return, which includes price changes too.
- Understanding this distinction avoids confusing income with overall investment performance.
