# WHITE-L1-038: Inflation and Its Effect on Investments

## Learning Objectives
1. Define inflation in general terms.
2. Explain why inflation erodes purchasing power.
3. Identify why "beating inflation" matters for long-term investing.

## Core Content
Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, which reduces the purchasing power of a given amount of money. *VERIFIED FACT:* If an investment's return doesn't exceed the inflation rate, the investor's real (inflation-adjusted) purchasing power can decline even if their account balance is nominally growing.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investment earns 3% annually while inflation runs at 4% that year. The account balance grows nominally, but its real purchasing power actually declines by roughly 1%, since prices rose faster than the investment gained value. This illustrates why "beating inflation" — not just earning a positive return — matters for genuinely growing wealth over time.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is inflation?
2. What happens to purchasing power if inflation exceeds investment returns?
3. True/False: A nominally growing account balance always means growing real purchasing power.
4. If an investment returns 5% and inflation is 3%, what is the approximate real return?

## Answer Key
1. The general rise in prices over time   2. Purchasing power declines despite nominal account growth   3. False   4. Roughly 2%

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of inflation.
2. This illustrates the real-versus-nominal distinction central to this lesson.
3. Nominal growth can mask real purchasing power decline if inflation outpaces it.
4. 5% − 3% = roughly 2% real return (a simplified approximation).

## Key Takeaways
- Inflation is the general rise in prices, eroding purchasing power over time.
- Nominal returns must exceed inflation for real purchasing power to actually grow.
- "Beating inflation" is a key long-term investing benchmark, not just earning any positive return.
