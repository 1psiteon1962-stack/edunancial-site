# WHITE-L1-039: The Time Value of Money

## Learning Objectives
1. Define the time value of money concept.
2. Explain why a dollar today is generally worth more than a dollar later.
3. Recognize this concept underlies compounding and investment decisions.

## Core Content
The time value of money is the principle that a given amount of money is generally worth more today than the same amount in the future, because today's money can be invested and grow over time. *VERIFIED FACT:* This concept underlies most investment and financial planning calculations, including compound growth (Lesson 015) and retirement planning.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Offered a choice between $1,000 today or $1,000 in five years, a rational investor would generally prefer $1,000 today — because that money, invested even modestly, could grow to more than $1,000 by the time five years pass. The delayed $1,000 has effectively less value today than the immediate $1,000, due to lost growth opportunity.

## Practice Quiz

3. True/False: This concept has no connection to compound growth.
4. Between $1,000 today and $1,000 in five years, which would a rational investor generally prefer?

## Answer Key
1. Money today is generally worth more than the same amount in the future   2. It can be invested and grow over that time   3. False   4. $1,000 today

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core principle of the time value of money.
2. Growth opportunity is the fundamental reason for this preference.
3. This concept is foundational to understanding compound growth.
4. Immediate money has growth potential the delayed amount lacks.

## Key Takeaways
- Money today is generally worth more than the same amount in the future.
- This is because today's money has the opportunity to be invested and grow.
- This principle underlies most investment and financial planning calculations.
