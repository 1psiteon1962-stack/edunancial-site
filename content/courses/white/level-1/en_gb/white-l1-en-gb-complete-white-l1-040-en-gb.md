# WHITE-L1-040: Rebalancing a Portfolio

## Learning Objectives
1. Define portfolio rebalancing.
2. Explain why allocations drift over time without rebalancing.
3. Identify common rebalancing approaches.

## Core Content
Rebalancing means periodically adjusting a portfolio back to its target asset allocation, since different assets grow at different rates and cause the original allocation to drift over time. *ASSUMPTION:* Common rebalancing approaches include calendar-based (e.g., annually) or threshold-based (rebalancing when an allocation drifts a certain percentage from target) — neither approach is universally superior.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor starts with a 70% shares / 30% bonds target allocation. After a strong year for shares, the portfolio drifts to 80% shares / 20% bonds — now carrying more risk than originally intended. Rebalancing means selling some shares and buying bonds to return to the original 70/30 target, realigning the portfolio's risk level with the investor's original plan.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is portfolio rebalancing?
2. Why do allocations drift over time?
3. Name two common rebalancing approaches.
4. In the worked example, what happened to the portfolio's risk level before rebalancing?

## Answer Key
1. Adjusting a portfolio back to its target allocation   2. Different assets grow at different rates   3. Calendar-based, threshold-based   4. Risk increased as share allocation grew beyond target

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of rebalancing.
2. Differential growth rates cause allocations to shift away from original targets.
3. These are the two commonly cited general rebalancing approaches.
4. The drift toward higher share allocation increased the portfolio's risk profile beyond the original plan.

## Key Takeaways
- Rebalancing restores a portfolio to its target allocation as it drifts over time.
- Drift happens because different assets grow at different rates.
- Common approaches include calendar-based and threshold-based rebalancing.
