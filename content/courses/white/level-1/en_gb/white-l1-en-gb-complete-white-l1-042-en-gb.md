# WHITE-L1-042: Behavioral Investing Mistakes to Avoid

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify common psychological biases affecting investing decisions.
2. Explain panic selling and its typical consequences.
3. Recognize the value of having a plan before emotions run high.

## Core Content
*OPINION:* Behavioral finance research commonly identifies several recurring investor mistakes, including: **panic selling** (selling during downturns out of fear, often locking in losses that might have recovered), **chasing performance** (buying whatever recently performed well, often after most of the gain has already happened), and **overconfidence** (underestimating risk based on recent success).

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* During a market decline, an investor without a predetermined plan panics and sells everything near the bottom, locking in significant losses. Months later, the market recovers — but having sold, the investor missed the recovery entirely. An investor with a predetermined plan (and the discipline to follow it) might have held through the decline, or even continued regular contributions at lower prices.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is panic selling?
2. What is "chasing performance"?
3. True/False: Emotional decision-making generally improves investment outcomes.
4. What can help an investor avoid emotional decisions during a downturn?

## Answer Key
1. Selling during downturns out of fear, often locking in losses   2. Buying recently well-performing investments, often after most gains have occurred   3. False   4. Having a predetermined plan before emotions run high

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is a commonly cited behavioral investing mistake.
2. This describes chasing recent performance rather than a disciplined strategy.
3. Emotional, reactive decisions are commonly associated with worse outcomes.
4. Predetermined plans provide a framework to follow rather than reacting emotionally in the moment.

## Key Takeaways
- Panic selling and performance chasing are commonly cited behavioral investing mistakes.
- Emotional, reactive decisions can lock in losses or miss recoveries.
- Having a predetermined plan can help investors avoid costly emotional decisions.
