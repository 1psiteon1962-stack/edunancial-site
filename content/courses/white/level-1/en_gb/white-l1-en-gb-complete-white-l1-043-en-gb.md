# WHITE-L1-043: Reading a Company's Annual Report — The Basics

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify the general purpose of an annual report.
2. Name common sections found in an annual report.
3. Explain why annual reports matter to individual investors.

## Core Content
*REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Annual report requirements and formats (such as the U.S. 10-K filing) vary by country and regulatory jurisdiction.

An annual report generally provides shareholders with a company's financial performance and business overview for the year. Common sections include: a letter from leadership, financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement), management's discussion of results, and risk disclosures.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor considering a company reads its annual report's risk disclosure section and discovers the company faces significant pending litigation not widely covered in casual news coverage. This detail, found in the formal report rather than headlines, informs the investor's decision in a way surface-level research might have missed.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does an annual report generally provide to shareholders?
2. Name two common sections of an annual report.
3. True/False: Annual report formats are identical worldwide.
4. Why might reading the full report reveal information casual news coverage misses?

## Answer Key
1. Financial performance and business overview for the year   2. Financial statements, risk disclosures (among others)   3. False   4. Formal disclosures often include details not prominently covered in general news

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the general purpose of an annual report.
2. These are among the standard sections found in most annual reports.
3. Formats and requirements vary by country and regulatory jurisdiction.
4. Detailed formal disclosures can surface risks or details underreported elsewhere.

## Key Takeaways
- Annual reports provide shareholders a formal overview of yearly financial performance.
- Common sections include financial statements, management discussion, and risk disclosures.
- Reading the full report can surface details not covered in general news.
