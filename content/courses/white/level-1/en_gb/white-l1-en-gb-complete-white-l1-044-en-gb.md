# WHITE-L1-044: What Is an IPO?

## Learning Objectives
1. Define an IPO (Initial Public Offering).
2. Explain why companies pursue an IPO.
3. Identify a common risk of investing in newly public companies.

## Core Content
An IPO is the process through which a private company first sells shares to the public, becoming a publicly traded company. *VERIFIED FACT:* Companies commonly pursue IPOs to raise capital for growth, provide liquidity for early investors/founders, and increase public profile.

*ASSUMPTION:* Newly public companies can carry elevated risk and volatility compared to established public companies, partly due to limited public trading history and sometimes high initial investor enthusiasm that may not be sustained.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A private technology company goes public via IPO, and its share price rises sharply on the first trading day amid strong investor demand. Months later, after the initial enthusiasm fades and the company reports its first public earnings, the share declines significantly — illustrating the elevated volatility risk often associated with newly public companies lacking an established public trading history.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is an IPO?
2. Name a common reason companies pursue an IPO.
3. True/False: Newly public companies typically carry the same risk profile as long-established public companies.
4. What can happen after initial IPO enthusiasm fades?

## Answer Key
1. The process of a private company first selling shares to the public   2. Raising capital for growth (among others)   3. False   4. The share price can decline significantly

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of an IPO.
2. Capital raising is a primary driver of the IPO decision.
3. Newly public companies often carry elevated risk compared to established ones.
4. Enthusiasm fading and subsequent price decline is a commonly observed pattern, though not universal.

## Key Takeaways
- An IPO is when a private company first sells shares to the public.
- Companies pursue IPOs primarily to raise capital and provide investor liquidity.
- Newly public companies can carry elevated risk and volatility compared to established ones.
