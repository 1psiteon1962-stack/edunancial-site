# WHITE-L1-045: Short Selling Explained

## Learning Objectives
1. Define short selling.
2. Explain how a short seller profits (or loses).
3. Identify why short selling carries theoretically unlimited risk.

## Core Content
Short selling means borrowing shares to sell them immediately, betting the price will fall, then buying them back later at (hopefully) a lower price to return to the lender — profiting from the difference. *VERIFIED FACT:* Unlike a standard "long" position where maximum loss is limited to the amount invested, short selling carries theoretically unlimited risk, since a share's price can rise indefinitely, and the short seller must eventually buy back shares at whatever price they've reached.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor borrows and sells 100 shares at $50 each ($5,000), betting the price will fall. If the price drops to $30, they buy back 100 shares for $3,000, return the borrowed shares, and pocket a $2,000 profit. But if the price instead rises to $100, they'd need to spend $10,000 to buy back the shares — a $5,000 loss, illustrating how losses can exceed the original amount involved if the price keeps climbing.

## Practice Quiz
1. What is short selling?
2. How does a short seller profit?
3. True/False: Short selling has the same limited risk profile as a standard long position.
4. What happens to a short seller's potential loss if the share price keeps rising?

## Answer Key
1. Borrowing and selling shares, betting the price will fall   2. Buying back at a lower price than sold, pocketing the difference   3. False   4. The potential loss keeps growing, theoretically without limit

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core mechanic of short selling.
2. Profit comes from the price decline between selling and repurchasing.
3. Short selling carries theoretically unlimited risk, unlike a standard long position.
4. Since there's no ceiling on how high a price can rise, losses can grow without a defined limit.

## Key Takeaways
- Short selling bets on a price decline by borrowing and selling, then repurchasing later.
- Profit comes from the spread between the sale price and repurchase price.
- Short selling carries theoretically unlimited risk, unlike standard long positions.
