# WHITE-L1-046: Options Basics — Calls and Puts

## Learning Objectives
1. Define an option contract in general terms.
2. Distinguish a call option from a put option.
3. Recognize options as generally higher-risk, complex instruments.

## Core Content
An option is a contract giving the holder the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell a security at a specified price within a specified timeframe. A **call option** gives the right to buy at a set price; a **put option** gives the right to sell at a set price.

*OPINION:* Options are generally considered more complex and higher-risk than basic shares or funds, and many financial educators commonly recommend building a solid foundational understanding of basic investing before using options — this lesson provides only a surface-level introduction, not sufficient guidance to trade options independently.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor buys a call option giving them the right to buy a share at $50 within the next month, paying a premium for that right. If the share rises to $60, they could exercise the option to buy at $50 and immediately have a position worth more — profiting from the difference minus the premium paid. If the share stays below $50, the option may expire worthless, and the investor loses only the premium paid.

- Calls involve the right to buy; puts involve the right to sell.
- Options are generally more complex and higher-risk — build foundational knowledge first.
