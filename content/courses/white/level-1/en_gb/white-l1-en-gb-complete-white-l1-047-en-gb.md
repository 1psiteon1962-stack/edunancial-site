# WHITE-L1-047: REITs — Real Estate Without Owning Property

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust).
2. Explain how REITs let investors access real estate through paper assets.
3. Identify a common REIT distribution requirement.

## Core Content
A REIT is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate, with shares that trade like a share — allowing investors to gain real estate exposure without directly buying, managing, or financing physical property themselves. *REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:* Specific legal requirements for REIT status (such as required distribution percentages) vary by country; in the U.S., REITs are commonly required to distribute a high percentage of taxable income to shareholders as dividends.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Instead of directly buying a rental property — requiring a large down payment, mortgage, and hands-on management — an investor buys shares of a REIT that owns a diversified portfolio of apartment buildings across many cities. They gain real estate income exposure and potential appreciation through a simple share purchase, with professional management handling all property-level operations.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This is the core definition of a REIT.
2. This is the primary appeal of REITs for individual investors.
3. Specific legal requirements vary by country.
4. REIT shares can be bought like any share, without hands-on property responsibilities.

## Key Takeaways
- REITs let investors gain real estate exposure through tradable shares.
- This avoids the direct ownership, financing, and management burdens of physical property.
- Distribution requirements and legal structures vary by country.
