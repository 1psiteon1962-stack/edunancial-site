# WHITE-L1-048: Socially Responsible and ESG Investing

## Learning Objectives
1. Define ESG investing.
2. Explain the general goal behind this investing approach.
3. Recognize criteria and standards can vary between funds/providers.

## Core Content
ESG investing considers Environmental, Social, and Governance factors alongside traditional financial analysis when selecting investments. *ASSUMPTION:* Investors pursuing this approach commonly aim to align investments with personal values or specific criteria, though what counts as meeting ESG standards can vary significantly between different funds and rating providers — there's no single universal ESG standard.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* An investor interested in environmental sustainability considers two funds both labeled "ESG." Upon closer research, she discovers they use different criteria — one excludes fossil fuel companies entirely, while the other simply favours companies with better environmental practices within their industry, including some energy companies. Because "ESG" isn't a single standardized definition, she needs to review each fund's specific methodology rather than assuming the label alone tells her what she needs to know.

## Practice Quiz
1. What does ESG investing consider alongside traditional financial analysis?
2. What is a general goal of ESG investing?
3. True/False: All funds labeled "ESG" use identical selection criteria.
4. Why should an investor research a specific ESG fund's methodology?

## Answer Key
1. Environmental, Social, and Governance factors   2. Aligning investments with personal values or specific criteria   3. False   4. Because criteria and standards vary significantly between providers

## Answers and Explanations
1. This defines the core focus areas of ESG investing.
2. This is the general motivation behind pursuing this approach.
3. ESG criteria vary meaningfully across different funds and providers.
4. The "ESG" label alone doesn't guarantee specific criteria match an investor's expectations.

## Key Takeaways
- ESG investing incorporates Environmental, Social, and Governance factors into investment selection.
- The general goal is aligning investments with personal values or specific criteria.
- ESG criteria vary significantly between funds — research specific methodology rather than assuming.
