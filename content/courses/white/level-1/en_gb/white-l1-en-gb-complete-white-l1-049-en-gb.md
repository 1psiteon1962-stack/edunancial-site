# WHITE-L1-049: Building a Long-Term Investment Plan

## Learning Objectives
1. Identify core components of a basic investment plan.
2. Explain why goals and timeline should precede specific investment selection.
3. Recognize a plan should be reviewed periodically, not set permanently.

## Core Content
*ASSUMPTION:* A basic long-term investment plan commonly starts with defining goals (what the money is for), timeline (when it's needed), and risk tolerance — before selecting specific investments, since these factors should generally drive investment choices, not the reverse.

*ASSUMPTION:* Plans generally benefit from periodic review, since life circumstances, goals, and timelines can change — a plan built once and never revisited may no longer fit an investor's current situation.

## Worked Example
*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* Instead of picking investments first and figuring out a purpose later, an investor starts by identifying her goal (retirement in 30 years), timeline (long, supporting higher risk tolerance), and comfort with volatility. Only after establishing these does she select an asset allocation and specific investments matching that profile — and she plans to revisit this plan every few years as her circumstances evolve.

3. True/False: A good investment plan never needs to be revisited once created.
4. What core components does a basic investment plan commonly start with?

## Answer Key
1. Goals, timeline, and risk tolerance   2. It affects appropriate risk tolerance and asset allocation   3. False   4. Goals, timeline, risk tolerance

## Answers and Explanations
1. These foundational factors should generally precede specific investment selection.
2. Longer timelines generally support different risk approaches than shorter ones.
3. Plans generally benefit from periodic review as circumstances change.
4. This is the commonly cited starting point for basic investment planning.

## Key Takeaways
- Define goals, timeline, and risk tolerance before selecting specific investments.
- These factors should generally drive investment choices, not the reverse.
- Review and adjust the plan periodically as life circumstances change.
