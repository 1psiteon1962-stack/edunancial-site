## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain what time decay is and why it affects options specifically
- Explain why time decay accelerates as expiration approaches
- Explain why time decay works against option buyers and in favor of option sellers

## Core Content

An option's value comes from two components: intrinsic value (how favorable the strike price already is compared to the current market price) and time value (the additional value reflecting the chance the option could become more profitable before expiration). As expiration approaches, that time value component shrinks — a process called **time decay**.

Here's the "why" this happens: less remaining time means less opportunity for the underlying asset's price to move favorably before the option expires. An option with six months until expiration has much more time to potentially become profitable than an identical option with one week left — so it's worth more, all else being equal, purely because of the additional time.

[VERIFIED FACT] Time decay isn't linear — it typically accelerates as expiration gets closer, meaning an option can lose a large portion of its remaining time value in its final weeks or days, even if the underlying asset's price hasn't moved at all. This is why option buyers are working against a constantly shrinking clock, while option sellers (covered in the next lesson) generally benefit from that same passage of time.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor buys a call option with three months until expiration. The underlying stock price stays exactly flat for the first two months. Despite no price movement at all, the option's value has still declined meaningfully over those two months, purely due to time decay — there's simply less time left for the stock to make the move that would make the option profitable. [ASSUMPTION] In the final month, as expiration approaches, that time-value erosion accelerates further, so even a small further delay in the stock's move can meaningfully hurt the option's remaining value.

## Practice Quiz

1. What are the two components of an option's value?
2. Why does an option's time value shrink as expiration approaches, even if the underlying price doesn't move?
3. Why does time decay accelerate rather than decline at a steady, constant pace?

## Answer Key

1. Intrinsic value (how favorable the strike price is compared to the current market price) and time value (reflecting the remaining opportunity for favorable movement before expiration).
2. Because less remaining time means less opportunity for the underlying price to move favorably, reducing the option's value even without any price change.
3. Because as expiration nears, there's proportionally much less time left for a favorable move to occur, causing the remaining time value to erode faster in the final stretch than earlier in the option's life.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This establishes the two-part value structure the rest of the lesson depends on.
2. This is the central mechanism of time decay.
3. This adds the important nuance about acceleration, setting up the seller-favorable dynamic discussed in the next lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- An option's value combines intrinsic value and time value; time value shrinks as expiration approaches.
- This "time decay" happens even without any movement in the underlying asset's price.
- Time decay accelerates closer to expiration, working against option buyers and in favor of option sellers.
