By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

## Core Content

Lesson 9 introduced margin's amplifying effect on both gains and losses. This lesson covers the specific mechanism that turns a losing margin position into a forced, and often poorly timed, sale: the **margin call**.

Brokerages require a minimum level of equity to be maintained in a margin account (the maintenance margin requirement). If a position's value falls enough that the account's equity drops below that minimum, the brokerage issues a margin call — a demand to deposit more cash or securities, or to sell part of the position, to bring the account back into compliance.

Here's the "why" this is especially damaging: a margin call is triggered by falling prices, which means it typically happens exactly when the investor is already experiencing losses — and if they can't or don't add more funds quickly, the brokerage can sell the investor's holdings automatically, often without further consultation, to restore the required equity level. [VERIFIED FACT] This can force a sale at a depressed price, locking in losses at precisely the moment when, similar to Lesson 1's discussion, a "buy low" opportunity might otherwise be forming — leverage removes the choice to simply wait it out.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor uses margin to buy a larger stock position than her cash alone would allow. The stock drops sharply, and her account's equity falls below the brokerage's maintenance requirement, triggering a margin call. [ASSUMPTION] Unable to deposit additional funds quickly enough, the brokerage sells a portion of her position automatically to bring the account back into compliance — locking in her losses at the depressed price, even though the stock later recovers. An investor holding the same stock without margin would have had the choice to simply hold through the downturn, since no forced sale requirement applied to her unleveraged position.

## Practice Quiz

1. What triggers a margin call?
2. Why is a margin call considered especially poorly timed?
3. What choice does a margin investor lose that an unleveraged investor still has during a downturn?

## Answer Key

1. The account's equity falling below the brokerage's required maintenance margin level, usually due to a decline in the value of the leveraged position.
2. Because it's triggered by falling prices, meaning it forces action exactly when the investor is already experiencing losses, potentially locking those losses in at a depressed price.
3. The choice to simply hold through a downturn and wait for a potential recovery — margin can force a sale regardless of the investor's own preference to wait.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the mechanical trigger the rest of the lesson depends on.
2. This is the central "why" — the timing mismatch between when a margin call happens and when an investor would ideally want to sell.
3. This connects directly back to Lesson 1's theme about the difficulty of buying low and holding through downturns.

## Key Takeaways
- A margin call is triggered when an account's equity falls below the brokerage's required maintenance level.
- Because it's triggered by falling prices, it can force a sale at a depressed price, locking in losses at a poorly timed moment.
- Margin removes an investor's choice to simply hold through a downturn, a choice available to unleveraged investors.
