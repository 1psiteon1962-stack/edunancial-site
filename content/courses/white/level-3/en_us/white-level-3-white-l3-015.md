## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain what sequence of returns risk means
- Explain why the same average return can produce very different outcomes depending on order
- Explain why this risk is much smaller during the accumulation phase than during retirement withdrawals

## Core Content

Most discussions of investment return focus on the average annual return over a period. **Sequence of returns risk** highlights something the average alone hides: the *order* in which those returns occur can significantly change the final outcome, especially when regular withdrawals are involved.

Here's the "why" order matters so much during withdrawals: if a portfolio experiences poor returns early in a withdrawal period, the investor is forced to sell more shares (or a larger percentage of the portfolio) at depressed prices to fund each withdrawal, permanently reducing the remaining asset base available to recover when the market eventually improves. The same poor returns occurring later, after some withdrawal years, do less damage — there's simply less time remaining for early damage to compound negatively.

[VERIFIED FACT] During the accumulation phase (when an investor is adding money, not withdrawing), sequence of returns matters much less, since there's no forced selling into a downturn — the investor can simply continue contributing and wait for a recovery without being forced to lock in losses through withdrawals.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two retirees each start with the same portfolio value and take the same annual withdrawal amount, and both experience the exact same average annual return over 20 years — but in a different order. Retiree A experiences poor returns in the first few years of retirement, forcing larger withdrawals (as a percentage of a shrinking portfolio) during that downturn. Retiree B experiences the same poor years, but later in the 20-year period, after the portfolio has already grown from earlier good years. [ASSUMPTION] Despite identical average returns, Retiree A's portfolio may run out of money well before Retiree B's, purely because of when the poor returns happened relative to the withdrawal schedule.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does sequence of returns risk describe?
2. Why can poor returns early in a withdrawal period cause much more damage than the same poor returns occurring later?
3. Why does sequence of returns risk matter much less during the accumulation phase?

## Answer Key

1. The risk that the order in which investment returns occur — not just their average — can significantly affect a portfolio's outcome, especially with regular withdrawals involved.
2. Because early poor returns force larger withdrawals as a percentage of an already-shrinking portfolio, permanently reducing the asset base available to benefit from a later recovery.
3. Because without forced withdrawals, an investor can simply continue contributing and wait out a downturn without needing to sell into it, so the order of returns doesn't compound the same way.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition for the lesson.
2. This is the central mechanism explaining why timing, not just average return, matters so much in retirement.
3. This contrasts the accumulation and withdrawal phases directly, clarifying when this risk is most relevant.

## Key Takeaways
- Sequence of returns risk means the order of investment returns can matter as much as their average, especially with withdrawals involved.
- Poor returns early in a withdrawal period do disproportionate damage, since they force selling more of a shrinking portfolio.
- This risk is much smaller during accumulation, since there's no forced selling to lock in early losses.
