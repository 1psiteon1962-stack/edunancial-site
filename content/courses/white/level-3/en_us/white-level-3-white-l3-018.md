## Core Content

**Duration** is a measure of a bond's sensitivity to changes in interest rates, expressed in a way that resembles years but represents something more specific: roughly, how much a bond's price is expected to change for a given change in interest rates.

Here's the "why" longer duration means more sensitivity: a bond with a longer time until its cash flows are received is more affected by a change in prevailing rates, because more of its value depends on cash flows further out in time, which are more heavily affected by a shift in the rate used to value them today (a concept related to the interest-rate-and-bond-price relationship covered in the next lesson).

[VERIFIED FACT] Duration isn't identical to a bond's maturity — a bond's coupon rate (the interest payments made along the way) also affects duration; a bond making larger regular payments returns more of its value sooner, generally giving it a somewhat shorter duration than a bond with the same maturity date but smaller or no regular payments (a zero-coupon bond, for example, has a duration equal to its full maturity, since all its value is returned at the very end).

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two bonds both mature in 10 years. Bond A pays regular, meaningful interest payments along the way, returning some of its value to the investor before maturity — giving it a duration somewhat shorter than 10 years. Bond B is a zero-coupon bond, paying nothing until it matures, returning its entire value only at the 10-year mark — giving it a duration equal to its full 10-year maturity. [ASSUMPTION] If interest rates rise by the same amount, Bond B's price would typically be expected to fall more than Bond A's, purely due to its longer duration, even though both bonds mature at the same time.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does bond duration measure?
2. Why does a longer duration generally mean greater sensitivity to interest rate changes?
3. Why can two bonds with the same maturity date have different durations?

## Answer Key

1. A bond's sensitivity to changes in interest rates — roughly, how much its price is expected to change for a given change in rates.
2. Because more of the bond's value depends on cash flows further out in time, which are more heavily affected by a change in the rate used to value them today.
3. Because duration also depends on the size and timing of a bond's regular interest payments — a bond returning more value sooner (larger coupon payments) has a shorter duration than one returning most or all of its value only at maturity.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition needed for the rest of the lesson.
2. This is the central mechanism explaining duration's relationship to interest rate sensitivity.
3. This adds an important nuance distinguishing duration from simple maturity length.

## Key Takeaways
- Duration measures a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes, distinct from its maturity date.
- Longer-duration bonds are generally more sensitive to interest rate changes than shorter-duration bonds.
- A bond's coupon payments affect its duration — larger, more frequent payments generally shorten duration relative to maturity.
