## Core Content

An **index fund** is designed to track a specific market index (such as a broad stock market index) by holding the same securities, in roughly the same proportions, as that index — rather than having a manager actively selecting which securities to buy or sell in an attempt to outperform the index.

Here's the "why" this represents a fundamentally different bet: an index investor isn't betting on any individual company, or on any particular fund manager's stock-picking skill — they're betting on the overall growth of the broader market (or market segment) that the index represents, over the long term. This removes an entire layer of risk that active investing carries: the risk that your chosen stocks, or your chosen manager's picks, underperform the market as a whole.

[VERIFIED FACT] This doesn't mean index investing eliminates risk altogether — index funds still carry the risk of the broader market itself declining, since they're specifically designed to move with that market, not to protect against its downturns.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor choosing an actively managed fund is implicitly betting that the fund's manager can select individual holdings that, combined, outperform the broader market after fees — a bet on that specific manager's skill and decisions. An investor choosing a broad market index fund instead is betting that the overall market will grow over time, without needing to identify which specific companies or which specific manager will do the picking. [ASSUMPTION] If the broader market rises over the investor's holding period, the index investor participates in that growth regardless of which individual companies drove it — she doesn't need to have correctly identified the specific winners in advance.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does an index fund attempt to do?
2. What specific bet is an index investor making, compared to an active fund investor?
3. Does index investing eliminate investment risk entirely? Why or why not?

## Answer Key

1. Track a specific market index by holding the same securities in roughly the same proportions, rather than having a manager actively select holdings.
2. A bet on the overall growth of the broader market or market segment, rather than a bet on any specific manager's or investor's stock-picking skill.
3. No — index funds still carry the risk of the broader market declining, since they're designed to track that market's movement, not to protect against its downturns.

## Key Takeaways
- Index funds track a market index rather than relying on active manager stock-picking.
- Index investing bets on the growth of the broader market as a whole, not on any individual manager's skill or stock selection.
- Index funds still carry market risk — they move with the market, including during declines.
