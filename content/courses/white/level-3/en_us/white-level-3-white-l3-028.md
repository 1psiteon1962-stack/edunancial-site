## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define recency bias
- Explain why recent market events tend to feel more predictive of the future than they actually are
- Explain how recency bias can push investors toward poorly timed decisions in both rising and falling markets

## Core Content

**Recency bias** describes the tendency to give recent events more weight when forming expectations about the future, compared to how much weight a fuller historical picture would actually justify.

Here's the "why" this distorts investment decisions: after an extended period of strong market gains, recency bias can make continued strong returns feel like the expected, normal state of affairs — leading investors to take on more risk or invest more aggressively right as conditions may be shifting. After an extended downturn, the same bias can make continued declines feel inevitable, leading investors to sell or avoid investing right as historical patterns (and Lesson 1's discussion of hindsight-obvious "low" points) might suggest a recovery could eventually follow.

[VERIFIED FACT] This bias is a well-documented pattern in behavioral research, distinct from a rational updating of expectations based on genuinely new information — recency bias specifically overweights the *most recent* pattern rather than considering the fuller range of historical outcomes and their varying likelihoods.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] After several years of strong, steady market gains, an investor comes to feel that this pace of growth is the normal expectation going forward, leading him to take on more investment risk than he originally intended. After a subsequent sharp downturn, that same feeling flips — the recent decline now feels like it will simply continue, leading him to sell near the bottom and avoid reinvesting even as conditions begin to stabilize. [ASSUMPTION] In both cases, his expectations were shaped disproportionately by the most recent period rather than a fuller historical perspective on how markets have moved through both up and down cycles over time.

## Key Takeaways
- Recency bias gives disproportionate weight to recent events when forming expectations about the future.
- After strong gains, it can lead to excessive risk-taking; after declines, it can lead to selling at poorly timed moments.
- This bias overweights the most recent pattern rather than considering the fuller range of historical market behavior.
