## Core Content

A **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate, and whose shares can be bought and sold on a stock exchange, similar to any other publicly traded stock — bridging the RED (Real Estate) and WHITE (Paper Assets) tracks of this curriculum.

Here's the "why" REITs offer a different experience than buying property directly, as covered throughout the RED track: a REIT investor buys shares representing a stake in a diversified portfolio of properties (often many properties across various markets), without directly managing tenants, handling maintenance, or arranging individual property financing — those responsibilities sit with the REIT's own management team. In exchange, the investor typically gives up the direct control and the specific tax treatments (like depreciation, covered in the RED track) available to a direct property owner.

[VERIFIED FACT] REITs are generally required to distribute a large majority of their taxable income to shareholders as dividends, which is part of why REITs are often known for relatively high dividend yields compared to many other stock categories — though, as covered in Lesson 17, a dividend still represents a distribution of value, not a bonus on top of it.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor wants exposure to commercial real estate but doesn't have the capital, time, or desire to manage a leveraged rental property directly, as described throughout this curriculum's RED track. Instead, she buys shares of a REIT that owns a large portfolio of office and retail properties. She gains exposure to real estate's income and potential appreciation, receives regular dividend distributions from the REIT's rental income, and can buy or sell her shares on the stock exchange at any time during market hours — a very different experience of "real estate investing" than directly financing and managing an individual rental property.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is a REIT?
2. What does a REIT investor gain, and what do they give up, compared to direct property ownership?
3. Why are REITs often known for relatively high dividend yields?

## Answer Key

1. A company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate, with shares that trade on a stock exchange like other publicly traded stocks.
2. They gain diversified real estate exposure without direct management responsibilities or property-level financing decisions, but give up direct control and certain tax treatments (like depreciation) available to a direct property owner.
3. Because REITs are generally required to distribute a large majority of their taxable income to shareholders as dividends.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition connecting RED and WHITE track concepts.
2. This is the central trade-off the lesson highlights between REIT investing and direct property ownership.
3. This connects to a structural requirement specific to REITs, while also linking back to Lesson 17's caution about dividends.

## Key Takeaways
- REITs let investors gain real estate exposure through publicly traded shares, without direct property ownership responsibilities.
- This trades away direct control and certain tax benefits for liquidity and diversification.
- REITs' required high income distribution is why they're often known for relatively high dividend yields.
