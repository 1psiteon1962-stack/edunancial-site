## Core Content

Lesson 3 established that correlation, not just the number of holdings, determines real diversification. **Sector concentration** is a specific, common way that correlation risk shows up: a portfolio holding many different companies that all operate in the same industry or sector.

Here's the "why" this matters: companies within the same sector often respond to many of the same economic and regulatory forces — technology companies affected by the same shifts in tech spending, energy companies affected by the same commodity price movements, healthcare companies affected by the same regulatory changes. [VERIFIED FACT] This shared sensitivity means a portfolio can hold a large number of individual stocks and still behave, in aggregate, much like a concentrated bet on that single sector's fortunes — the correlation between the holdings undermines the diversification benefit the raw holding count would suggest.

This is why evaluating a portfolio's diversification should include a look at sector exposure specifically, not just a simple count of how many different companies are held.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holds 25 different stocks, feeling well-diversified based on the number alone. On closer inspection, 20 of those 25 are technology companies. If a broad downturn specifically affects the technology sector — a shift in interest rates that particularly hurts high-growth tech valuations, for example — the vast majority of her portfolio is exposed to that same pressure at once, despite the seemingly diversified holding count. [ASSUMPTION] A portfolio with the same 25 total holdings, but spread more evenly across several different sectors, would likely experience a smaller overall impact from that same sector-specific downturn.

## Key Takeaways
- Sector concentration means a portfolio's holdings are clustered in the same industry, even if the total number of holdings looks diversified.
- Companies within the same sector tend to be highly correlated, undermining the diversification benefit that holding count alone would suggest.
- Portfolio diversification should be evaluated by sector exposure, not just by counting the number of different companies held.
