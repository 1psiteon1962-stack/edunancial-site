## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define asset allocation
- Explain why research has generally found asset allocation to be a larger driver of long-term portfolio outcomes than individual security selection
- Explain why this doesn't mean individual investment choices don't matter at all

## Core Content

**Asset allocation** refers to how a portfolio is divided among broad categories of investments — stocks, bonds, real estate, cash, and others — rather than which specific securities are chosen within each category.

Here's the "why" allocation tends to matter more than individual picks for most long-term investors: [VERIFIED FACT] a range of academic and industry research on portfolio returns has generally found that broad asset allocation decisions account for a substantial portion of the variation in long-term portfolio performance across different investors, more so than the specific securities chosen within each asset class — though findings and methodologies vary across different studies, and individual security selection can still meaningfully affect results within a given allocation.

This connects to concepts covered throughout this track: since diversification within an asset class (Lessons 2 and 3) reduces much of the company-specific risk anyway, the larger remaining driver of a portfolio's overall risk and return character becomes the broad mix between asset classes with genuinely different risk and return characteristics — stocks generally offering higher long-term growth potential with more volatility, bonds generally offering more stability with lower long-term growth potential, and so on.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two investors both hold well-diversified portfolios within their chosen asset classes, but Investor A allocates 80% to stocks and 20% to bonds, while Investor B allocates 30% to stocks and 70% to bonds. [ASSUMPTION] Over a long period, the difference in their overall portfolio performance and volatility is likely to be driven substantially by this broad allocation difference, rather than by the specific individual stocks or bonds each investor selected within their respective allocations — even if both made reasonably sound individual selections.

2. That asset allocation decisions tend to account for a substantial portion of the variation in long-term portfolio performance, more so than the specific securities chosen within each asset class, though findings vary across studies.
3. No — individual security selection can still meaningfully affect results within a given allocation; the lesson emphasizes relative importance, not that individual choices are irrelevant.

## Key Takeaways
- Asset allocation refers to how a portfolio is divided among broad investment categories like stocks, bonds, and cash.
- Research has generally found allocation decisions to be a larger driver of long-term portfolio outcomes than individual security selection, though both matter.
- This connects to the diversification concepts from earlier lessons — once company-specific risk is reduced through diversification, the broad asset mix becomes a larger remaining driver of results.
