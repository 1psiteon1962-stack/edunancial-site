## Core Content

A **glide path** describes a predetermined plan for gradually shifting a portfolio's asset allocation (Lesson 34) from a more aggressive, growth-oriented mix toward a more conservative, stability-oriented mix as an investor approaches a specific target date — most commonly retirement. Target-date funds are a common example of an investment product built around this concept.

Here's the "why" this timing matters, connecting directly to Lesson 15's sequence of returns risk: a portfolio with many years remaining before it needs to be accessed has more time to recover from a downturn, so it can typically afford to hold more stocks for their higher long-term growth potential. As the target date approaches and the time to recover from a potential downturn shrinks, gradually shifting toward more stable assets (like bonds) reduces the risk of experiencing a poorly timed, large decline right before the money is needed — precisely the risk described in Lesson 15.

[VERIFIED FACT] Different glide path designs vary in how aggressively or conservatively they shift over time, and in whether the shift continues even after the target date is reached — there's no single universal glide path, and the appropriate pace of adjustment depends on individual circumstances and risk tolerance.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A target-date fund designed for someone retiring in roughly 30 years might start with a portfolio heavily weighted toward stocks, taking advantage of the long time horizon to potentially recover from any downturns along the way. As the target retirement date approaches over those decades, the fund gradually increases its allocation to bonds and reduces its stock exposure, aiming to reduce the portfolio's vulnerability to a poorly timed downturn right as the investor might need to begin withdrawing funds.

1. What does a "glide path" describe?
2. Why does a portfolio typically shift from more aggressive to more conservative as a target date approaches?
3. How does this concept connect directly to Lesson 15's sequence of returns risk?

## Answer Key

1. A predetermined plan for gradually shifting a portfolio's asset allocation from more aggressive to more conservative as a target date, like retirement, approaches.
2. Because there's less remaining time to recover from a potential downturn as the target date nears, so reducing exposure to more volatile assets helps protect against a poorly timed decline right before the money is needed.
3. Sequence of returns risk showed that poor returns right before or during withdrawals can cause outsized damage — a glide path is specifically designed to reduce vulnerability to that exact risk by lowering volatility exposure as the target date approaches.
