## Core Content

**Position sizing** refers to how much of a portfolio is allocated to any single investment. This is a distinct decision from *which* investments to choose — even an excellent investment idea can create outsized risk for a portfolio if too large a share of total capital is committed to it.

Here's the "why" position sizing functions as its own form of risk control, separate from investment selection quality: no single investment thesis, however well-researched, is guaranteed to work out — unexpected events, new information, or simple bad luck can affect even a carefully chosen position. If that position represents a small portion of a portfolio, a disappointing outcome is a manageable setback. If it represents a very large portion, the same disappointing outcome can meaningfully damage the entire portfolio, regardless of how sound the original reasoning was.

[VERIFIED FACT] This connects to a broader principle used elsewhere in this curriculum around structuring risk so that losses are real and felt, teaching genuine lessons, but never catastrophic to the overall financial picture — position sizing is one of the most direct, controllable tools an investor has for keeping any single position's potential downside within a tolerable range.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor identifies what she believes is a strong investment opportunity after careful research. Putting 5% of her portfolio into that idea means that if it turns out poorly — even a complete loss on that position — the damage to her overall portfolio is limited and recoverable. [ASSUMPTION] Putting 50% of her portfolio into the same idea, based on the same research and conviction, means that same poor outcome could meaningfully set back her entire financial position — the quality of the underlying idea didn't change, but the position size dramatically changed how much that specific outcome could hurt her.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does position sizing refer to?
2. Why can even a well-researched investment idea still create outsized portfolio risk?
3. How does position sizing function as a risk-control tool, separate from investment selection?

## Answer Key

1. How much of a portfolio is allocated to any single investment.
2. Because no investment thesis is guaranteed to work out, and if a poorly performing position represents too large a share of the portfolio, the resulting damage can be significant regardless of how sound the original reasoning was.
3. By limiting how much any single position's outcome — good or bad — can affect the overall portfolio, position sizing directly controls potential downside independent of the quality of the investment choice itself.

## Key Takeaways
- Position sizing determines how much of a portfolio is allocated to any single investment.
- Even a well-researched idea can create outsized portfolio damage if sized too large, regardless of the reasoning behind it.
- Position sizing is a direct, controllable risk-management tool, separate from the quality of investment selection itself.
