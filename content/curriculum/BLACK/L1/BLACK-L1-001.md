# Leadership vs. Management: Two Different Skills

## Learning Objectives
- Define leadership and management as distinct but related skills
- Explain why an organization needs both
- Identify whether a given scenario calls for leadership or management

## Core Content

People use "leader" and "manager" like they're the same thing. They're not. Management is about organizing resources — people, time, money — to get a known job done efficiently. Leadership is about setting direction and inspiring people to move toward a goal, especially when the path isn't clear yet.

A manager asks, "How do we do this well?" A leader asks, "Why are we doing this, and where are we headed?" Both questions matter. A team with great leadership but poor management has an inspiring vision and no follow-through — deadlines slip, resources get wasted. A team with great management but no leadership runs efficiently but has no clear direction — everyone's busy, but busy doing what, exactly?

The best people in charge of something learn to do both: set a clear "why" and direction (leadership), and also build the systems that make the "how" actually happen (management). Neither skill replaces the other.

## Worked Example

Someone wants to start a public access TV program. The leadership move is deciding what the show is about, why it matters, and convincing guests and crew to believe in the idea enough to show up. The management move is booking the studio time, coordinating the camera crew's schedule, and confirming logistics.

If that person had only leadership (a great vision, an exciting pitch) but no management (nobody confirmed, no schedule locked down), the show never happens. If they had only management (a perfectly booked studio and crew) but no leadership (no compelling reason for guests to actually show up and believe in it), the show also never happens. Both were needed.

## Practice Quiz

1. What question does a manager typically ask?
2. What question does a leader typically ask?
3. Can a team succeed with only one of the two skills? Why or why not?
4. In the worked example, what was the leadership task, and what was the management task?

## Answer Key
1. "How do we do this well?"
2. "Why are we doing this, and where are we headed?"
3. Generally no — without direction, management runs efficiently toward nothing; without execution systems, leadership's vision never becomes reality.
4. Leadership: deciding the show's purpose and convincing people to believe in it. Management: booking the studio, crew, and logistics.

## Answers and Explanations
1. Management focuses on efficient execution of a known task.
2. Leadership focuses on direction and motivation, especially under uncertainty.
3. The lesson argues both are necessary — one without the other leaves a critical gap.
4. This example shows the two skills operating side by side on the same project.

## Key Takeaways
- Management organizes resources to execute a known task efficiently.
- Leadership sets direction and inspires people to move toward a goal.
- Strong organizations, and strong individuals, need both skills working together.
