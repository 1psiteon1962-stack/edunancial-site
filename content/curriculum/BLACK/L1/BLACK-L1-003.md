# Vision-Setting Basics

## Learning Objectives
- Define vision in a leadership context
- Explain why vague visions fail to motivate people
- Draft a basic vision statement for a simple project or business

## Core Content

A vision is a clear picture of where you're trying to go and why it matters. It's different from a goal, which is a specific, measurable target. A goal might be "sell 500 units this quarter." A vision is the bigger reason behind the goal — "we exist to make quality tools affordable for people who've been priced out."

A weak vision is vague enough to mean nothing: "be the best in the industry." Nobody can act on that. A strong vision is specific enough that someone hearing it understands exactly what future you're building toward and why it's worth their effort.

Vision matters because people don't stay motivated by tasks alone — they stay motivated by believing the tasks add up to something meaningful. A leader's job includes repeating and reinforcing that vision often, because day-to-day work can make people lose sight of the bigger picture.

## Worked Example

Someone launching a public access TV show could describe their vision vaguely: "I want to make a good show." Nobody gets excited by that. A sharper vision: "I want to give people in this community a real platform to be heard on issues nobody else covers." That's specific enough that potential guests and crew members can see themselves as part of something meaningful, not just extras in someone's personal project.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is the difference between a vision and a goal?
2. Why does a vague vision fail to motivate people?
3. Why is it a leader's job to repeat the vision often?
4. Write a one-sentence vision for a business that tutors kids in financial literacy.

## Answer Key
1. A goal is a specific, measurable target; a vision is the bigger reason behind the goal.
2. It gives people nothing concrete to act on or believe in.
3. Because day-to-day tasks can cause people to lose sight of the bigger purpose.
4. Answers will vary (example: "We exist to make sure no kid grows up without understanding how money actually works.")

## Answers and Explanations
1. This distinction clarifies why vision and goals serve different functions in leadership.
2. Vagueness prevents people from connecting emotionally or practically to the vision.
3. Repetition combats the natural drift toward losing sight of purpose during routine work.
4. A correct answer is specific enough to paint a clear picture of the intended impact.

## Key Takeaways
- Vision is the bigger "why" behind specific goals.
- Vague visions fail to motivate because they give people nothing concrete to hold onto.
- Leaders need to repeat the vision often, since daily tasks can obscure the bigger picture.
