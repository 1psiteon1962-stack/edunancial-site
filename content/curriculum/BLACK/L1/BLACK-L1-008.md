# Leading by Example

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why a leader's actions carry more weight than their words
- Identify common gaps between what leaders say and what they do
- Practice aligning stated expectations with personal behavior

## Core Content

A team watches what a leader does far more closely than they listen to what a leader says. If a leader talks about punctuality but consistently shows up late, punctuality stops being a real expectation — it becomes a suggestion nobody actually has to follow. This gap between stated values and actual behavior is one of the fastest ways to lose credibility.

Leading by example doesn't mean a leader has to personally do every task their team does. It means the standards a leader holds others to are standards they hold themselves to as well — in effort, honesty, and follow-through, even if the specific tasks look different.

This matters especially during hard moments. Anyone can lead by example when things are going well. The real test is whether a leader keeps their own standards — staying calm, being honest, following through — when a project is failing, money is tight, or the pressure is high.

## Worked Example

A leader tells the team that mistakes should be reported immediately, not hidden. Then the leader makes a costly scheduling error and doesn't mention it until someone else discovers it. The team notices the contradiction immediately, and the "report mistakes immediately" policy quietly stops being followed by anyone, because the leader didn't follow it either.

Compare that to a leader who makes the same mistake and immediately tells the team: "I made an error on the schedule — here's what happened and how I'm fixing it." That single action reinforces the stated value far more effectively than any speech about honesty could.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why do a leader's actions carry more weight than their words?
2. Does leading by example mean doing every task the team does personally?
3. What is "the real test" of leading by example, according to this lesson?
4. In the worked example, what undermined the "report mistakes immediately" policy?

## Answer Key
1. Because a team learns what's actually expected by watching behavior, not just hearing statements.
2. No — it means holding yourself to the same standards you hold others to, even if the tasks differ.
3. Whether a leader keeps their own standards during hard moments, not just when things are going well.
4. The leader failing to report their own mistake immediately, contradicting the stated policy.

## Answers and Explanations
1. Behavior is observable proof of what's really valued; words alone are easy to say and easy to ignore.
2. The lesson clarifies that leading by example is about consistent standards, not identical tasks.
3. This distinguishes performative leadership (easy when things go well) from real leadership (tested under pressure).
4. The gap between the stated policy and the leader's own behavior is what broke the team's trust in the policy.

## Key Takeaways
- Actions communicate expectations far more powerfully than words do.
- Leading by example means holding yourself to the same standards you set for others.
- The real test of this principle comes during hard moments, not easy ones.
