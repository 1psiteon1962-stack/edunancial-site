# Hiring Fundamentals for a Small Team

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why hiring mistakes cost more in a small team than a large one
- Identify the core things to evaluate beyond technical skill
- Practice writing a simple, honest job description

## Core Content

In a large company, one bad hire is diluted across hundreds of employees. In a small team, one bad hire can drag down morale, burn through limited cash, and consume a disproportionate amount of a leader's time and attention. This means small-team hiring decisions deserve more care per decision, not less, even though small businesses often have fewer resources to spend on the hiring process itself.

Technical skill matters, but it's rarely the whole picture. Reliability, honesty about their own limitations, and fit with how the team actually works day-to-day often predict long-term success better than a resume alone. A brilliant but unreliable hire can cost more than a competent, dependable one.

A job description should be honest about both the opportunity and the reality — including the parts that are genuinely hard, like limited resources or a fast pace — rather than oversold. Overselling a role to get someone in the door usually just delays the mismatch becoming obvious, at a higher cost later.

## Worked Example

A small business needs a bookkeeper and is tempted to hire the most credentialed applicant, who interviews well but has a history of missing deadlines at previous jobs (a reference check reveals this). A less credentialed but consistently reliable candidate is available instead. In a small business with no backup coverage, reliability matters more than an extra certification — the small team can't absorb the cost of chronic missed deadlines the way a large company with redundancy could.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why do hiring mistakes cost more in a small team than a large one?
2. Name two things worth evaluating beyond technical skill.
3. What should a job description honestly include?
4. In the worked example, why was reliability weighted more heavily than credentials?

## Answer Key
1. There's no redundancy to absorb the cost — one bad hire has an outsized impact on a small team.
2. Any two of: reliability, honesty about limitations, fit with how the team actually works.
3. Both the opportunity and the genuine challenges of the role, not an oversold version.
4. Because a small team has no backup coverage, so consistent reliability directly protects the business's ability to function.

## Answers and Explanations
1. Small teams lack the redundancy that absorbs individual hiring mistakes in larger organizations.
2. These qualities often predict long-term success more reliably than technical skill alone.
3. Honest job descriptions reduce mismatches that become expensive to fix after the fact.
4. This illustrates how small-team constraints change what actually matters most in a hire.

## Key Takeaways
- Small teams have less capacity to absorb hiring mistakes, making each hiring decision higher stakes.
- Reliability and fit often matter as much as, or more than, raw technical skill.
- Honest job descriptions prevent costly mismatches that oversold roles tend to create.
