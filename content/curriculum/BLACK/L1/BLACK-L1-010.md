# Delegation vs. Doing It All Yourself

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why doing everything yourself limits growth
- Identify the difference between delegating and abandoning responsibility
- Practice identifying a task that should be delegated

## Core Content

Many new leaders, especially founders, hold onto tasks personally long after they should have handed them off, often out of a belief that "nobody will do it as well as I would." This instinct feels responsible, but it usually caps growth — a leader who's personally doing every task has no capacity left to lead, and the business's growth becomes limited by exactly one person's available hours.

Delegation doesn't mean abandoning responsibility for the outcome. It means transferring the doing of the task while retaining accountability for the result — checking in appropriately, providing the context and resources needed, and correcting course if something goes off track, without micromanaging every step.

A useful filter for deciding what to delegate: does this task require your specific judgment and expertise, or could someone else, with reasonable training, do it about as well? Tasks that fall into the second category are strong delegation candidates, freeing up time for the things that genuinely require the leader's unique judgment.

## Worked Example

A founder personally handles every customer email, believing no one else can match their tone. This consumes hours daily that could go toward strategic decisions only the founder can make. After documenting common questions and response patterns, the founder trains a team member to handle routine emails, checking a sample regularly at first. Response quality stays high, and the founder now has hours back each week for higher-leverage work only they can do.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why does doing everything yourself limit a leader's growth?
2. Does delegation mean giving up responsibility for the outcome?
3. What filter helps decide what to delegate?
4. In the worked example, what freed up the founder's time without sacrificing quality?

## Answer Key
1. Because the business becomes limited by one person's available hours instead of scaling beyond them.
2. No — it means transferring the doing while retaining accountability for the result.
3. Whether the task requires your specific judgment, or could reasonably be done by someone else with training.
4. Documenting patterns and training a team member, with quality checks along the way.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This addresses the growth ceiling created by a founder holding onto too many tasks personally.
2. The lesson clarifies that delegation is not abdication — accountability for results remains with the leader.
3. This filter provides a practical decision-making tool for identifying delegation candidates.
4. Documentation and gradual training allowed responsibility to transfer without a quality drop.

## Key Takeaways
- Doing everything yourself caps growth at the limit of one person's available time.
- Delegation transfers the doing of a task while keeping accountability for the outcome.
- A useful filter: delegate tasks that don't require your unique judgment or expertise.
