# Trust as the Currency of a Team

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why trust functions like a currency within a team
- Identify actions that build or deplete team trust
- Recognize the compounding effect of trust over time

## Core Content

Trust within a team works something like currency: it's earned, it can be spent, and once depleted, everything becomes more expensive. A high-trust team can move fast, because people don't waste time second-guessing each other's motives or double-checking every commitment. A low-trust team moves slowly, because every interaction requires extra verification, extra explanation, and extra caution.

Trust builds through consistent, small actions over time: following through on commitments, communicating honestly even when it's uncomfortable, and treating mistakes as learning opportunities rather than occasions for blame. Trust depletes through the opposite: broken promises, hidden information, and punishing honesty about problems.

The compounding effect matters here. A team with high trust reserves can absorb an occasional mistake without major damage. A team with already-depleted trust reserves can be pushed into serious dysfunction by the same mistake, because there's no reserve left to absorb it.

## Worked Example

Two teams each experience the same setback — a missed deadline caused by an honest miscommunication. Team A has built up trust over years of consistent follow-through; they treat the miss as a process problem to fix together, and morale barely dips. Team B has a history of blame-shifting and broken promises; the same miss triggers finger-pointing, defensiveness, and a further erosion of already-thin trust. The event was identical — the outcome depended entirely on the trust reserve each team had built beforehand.

## Practice Quiz

1. In what sense does trust function like a currency?
2. Name two actions that build trust and two that deplete it.
3. Why can a high-trust team absorb mistakes more easily than a low-trust team?
4. In the worked example, what determined the different outcomes despite an identical setback?

## Answer Key
1. It's earned through consistent behavior, can be spent down, and everything becomes harder once it's depleted.
2. Builds: following through on commitments, honest communication. Depletes: broken promises, hidden information.
3. Because a reserve of trust absorbs the shock of a single mistake without triggering broader dysfunction.
4. The pre-existing trust reserve each team had built (or failed to build) before the setback occurred.

## Answers and Explanations
1. The currency metaphor captures how trust is accumulated, spent, and depleted through team behavior.
2. These specific actions define the practical building blocks of team trust.
3. Trust reserves function as a buffer that absorbs occasional setbacks without major damage.
4. This example isolates trust reserve as the deciding variable, holding the triggering event constant.

## Key Takeaways
- Trust functions like a currency: earned slowly, spent quickly, and costly once depleted.
- Consistent follow-through and honest communication build trust; broken promises and hidden information deplete it.
- Teams with strong trust reserves absorb setbacks far more gracefully than teams without them.
