# Communicating Expectations Clearly

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why vague expectations lead to predictable failures
- Identify the components of a clearly communicated expectation
- Practice rewriting a vague instruction as a clear one

## Core Content

A huge share of workplace frustration traces back to a simple root cause: expectations that were never actually made clear. A leader who says "get this done soon" and a team member who interprets "soon" completely differently than the leader intended are both technically communicating — but not clearly enough to prevent a predictable failure.

A clearly communicated expectation typically includes: what specifically needs to happen, by when specifically, to what standard of quality, and who's responsible if multiple people are involved. Vague language ("soon," "good enough," "handle it") leaves too much room for differing interpretation, and the gap between those interpretations becomes the leader's problem later.

This matters even more under pressure or during a crisis, when the temptation is to communicate quickly and assume people will "just get it." Under pressure is exactly when clarity matters most, because there's less time available to catch and correct a misunderstanding before it causes real damage.

## Worked Example

Vague: "Let's get the report done soon." Clear: "I need the draft report by end of day Thursday, covering Q2 revenue and expenses, and I'll review it Friday morning before it goes to the board." The second version leaves almost no room for a mismatched interpretation, because it specifies the deliverable, the deadline, the scope, and what happens next.

## Practice Quiz

1. What's the root cause of much workplace frustration, according to this lesson?
2. Name the four components a clearly communicated expectation should include.
3. Why does vague language become "the leader's problem later"?
4. Rewrite this vague instruction clearly: "Make sure the event goes well."

## Answer Key
1. Expectations that were never actually made clear, despite technically being communicated.
2. What specifically needs to happen, by when, to what standard, and who's responsible.
3. Because the gap between different interpretations eventually surfaces as a missed expectation the leader has to address.
4. Answers will vary but should specify concrete deliverables, deadline, and standard (example: "Confirm the venue, catering, and speaker schedule are locked in by Friday, and send me a final run-of-show by Monday.")

## Answers and Explanations
1. This identifies the specific communication gap the lesson addresses.
2. These four elements form the checklist for eliminating interpretive ambiguity.
3. Interpretation gaps don't disappear — they resurface later as missed or mismatched deliverables.
4. A correct rewrite adds specificity across the four components identified in the lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- Vague expectations predictably lead to mismatched interpretations and later frustration.
- Clear expectations specify what, by when, to what standard, and who's responsible.
- Clarity matters most under pressure, exactly when there's the least time to catch a misunderstanding.
