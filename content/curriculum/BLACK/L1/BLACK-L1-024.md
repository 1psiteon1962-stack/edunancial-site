# Crisis-Mode Decision-Making

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how crisis conditions change the appropriate approach to decision-making
- Identify the tradeoffs between speed and thoroughness under acute pressure
- Practice applying a simplified decision framework under time constraints

## Core Content

Most of the decision-making frameworks covered so far assume there's at least some time to think, gather input, and weigh options. A genuine crisis — where a decision has to be made quickly, with real consequences either way — calls for a different, more compressed approach.

Under acute time pressure, a useful simplification: identify the two or three most plausible options quickly, rule out any option that would be catastrophic and irreversible if wrong, and choose the best available option from what remains, rather than searching for a perfect option that doesn't exist in the time available. Speed itself often reduces risk in a crisis, because delay while searching for a perfect answer can be more costly than a good-enough answer acted on quickly.

After the crisis passes, a leader should return to it with a calmer, more thorough review — not to second-guess the pressured decision unfairly, but to extract any real lessons for handling the next crisis better.

## Worked Example

A sudden equipment failure threatens to shut down a small production run with a client deadline in hours. A leader in crisis mode doesn't have time for the extensive risk analysis process used for the earlier lease decision. Instead: rule out any option that risks permanent equipment damage (catastrophic and irreversible), quickly identify two viable temporary fixes, and choose the one with the higher chance of holding through the deadline, rather than spending an hour comparing every possible option. After the crisis passes, the leader reviews what happened calmly to improve equipment maintenance going forward.

## Practice Quiz

1. How does a genuine crisis change the appropriate decision-making approach?
2. What's the simplified crisis framework this lesson describes?
3. Why can speed itself reduce risk in a crisis?
4. What should a leader do after a crisis passes?

## Answer Key
1. It requires a faster, more compressed process than the thorough frameworks used for non-urgent decisions.
2. Identify a few plausible options quickly, rule out catastrophic/irreversible ones, choose the best available rather than a perfect option.
3. Because delay while searching for a perfect answer can itself be more costly than acting on a good-enough answer quickly.
4. Return to it with a calmer review to extract real lessons for handling the next crisis better.

## Answers and Explanations
1. Crisis conditions compress the available time for the deeper deliberation used in non-urgent decisions.
2. This framework provides a practical, fast decision process specifically suited to acute time pressure.
3. Analysis paralysis during a crisis can cause more harm than an imperfect but timely decision.
4. Post-crisis review extracts learning without unfairly second-guessing decisions made under legitimate time pressure.

## Key Takeaways
- Genuine crises call for a faster, more compressed decision-making approach than normal circumstances.
- Quickly rule out catastrophic, irreversible options, then choose the best available option rather than seeking perfection.
- Review crisis decisions calmly afterward to extract lessons, without unfairly second-guessing pressured judgment calls.
