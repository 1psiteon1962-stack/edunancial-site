# Roles vs. People: Why the Seat Matters More Than Who's In It

## Learning Objectives
- Distinguish between a role and the specific person currently filling it
- Explain why structuring around people instead of roles creates fragility
- Practice defining a role independent of a specific individual

## Core Content

It's natural, especially in a small business, to build structure around specific people — "Maria handles all the client relationships" rather than "there's a client relationship role, which Maria currently fills." This distinction seems subtle, but it matters enormously for organizational resilience. If Maria leaves, gets sick, or wants to move to a different role, a business structured around "Maria" specifically has a much harder time adapting than one structured around a clearly defined role that Maria happens to currently occupy.

Defining roles independent of the person filling them means documenting what the role actually requires — responsibilities, key relationships, necessary skills — separate from that specific individual's particular way of doing it. This makes the business more resilient to turnover and clearer about what actually needs to be true for someone to succeed in that seat, rather than depending entirely on Maria's specific personality or habits.

This doesn't mean treating people as interchangeable or ignoring individual strengths. It means the business's core functions shouldn't be so tightly coupled to one specific person that losing them creates a structural crisis rather than a manageable transition.

## Worked Example

A small business has always relied on one employee who "just knows" how vendor relationships work, with nothing documented. When that employee unexpectedly leaves, the business loses not just a person but the entire function, because the role was never actually defined — it existed only in that person's head. A business that had documented the vendor relationship role (key contacts, negotiation patterns, renewal timelines) independent of that specific employee could bring in a replacement far more smoothly.

## Practice Quiz

1. What's the difference between a role and the person currently filling it?
2. Why does structuring around specific people create fragility?
3. Does defining roles independently mean treating people as interchangeable?
4. In the worked example, what specifically caused the business to struggle after the employee left?

## Answer Key
1. A role is a defined set of responsibilities and requirements; the person is whoever currently occupies that role.
2. Because losing that specific person creates a structural crisis rather than a manageable transition.
3. No — it means the business's core functions shouldn't depend entirely on one person's undocumented knowledge.
4. The vendor relationship function existed only in that employee's head, with nothing documented independent of them.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This distinction is the core concept the lesson introduces for organizational resilience.
2. Undocumented, person-dependent functions create single points of failure for the business.
3. The lesson explicitly separates role definition from disregarding individual value or strengths.
4. This illustrates the practical cost of never separating the role from the specific individual filling it.

## Key Takeaways
- Distinguish the role (defined responsibilities) from the person currently filling it.
- Structuring around specific people rather than defined roles creates fragility and single points of failure.
- Documenting roles independently of individuals makes a business more resilient to turnover and transitions.
