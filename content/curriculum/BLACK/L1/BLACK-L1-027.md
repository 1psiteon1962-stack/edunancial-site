# Setting KPIs a Non-MBA Can Use

## Learning Objectives
- Define a KPI in plain, practical terms
- Identify the qualities of a useful KPI
- Practice setting a basic KPI for a simple business goal

## Core Content

KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator — a specific, trackable number that tells you whether something important is going well or not. It sounds like corporate jargon, but the underlying idea is simple: pick a number that actually reflects progress toward a goal, and check it regularly so you know whether you're on track without having to guess.

A useful KPI is specific, measurable, and tied directly to something that matters. "Grow the business" isn't a KPI — it's a vague wish. "New customers per month" or "conversion rate from proposal to close" are KPIs, because they're specific numbers that can be tracked and compared over time.

The mistake to avoid is picking too many KPIs at once, or picking ones that are easy to measure but don't actually reflect what matters (a "vanity metric," like social media followers, when the real goal is revenue). A small number of KPIs that genuinely reflect the business's real health are more useful than a dashboard cluttered with numbers that don't actually connect to anything important.

## Worked Example

A small consulting business wants to grow. Instead of a vague goal like "get more clients," the owner picks three specific KPIs: number of new proposals sent per month, proposal-to-close conversion rate, and average revenue per client. These three numbers, tracked monthly, tell the owner exactly where to focus — if proposals sent is high but conversion is low, the problem is in the sales process, not lead generation. This is far more useful than a vague sense of "business feels slow this month."

## Practice Quiz

1. Define KPI in plain terms.
2. What makes a KPI useful, according to this lesson?
3. What is a "vanity metric," and why is it a mistake to rely on one?
4. Set a basic KPI for a bakery trying to reduce food waste.

## Answer Key
1. A specific, trackable number that tells you whether something important is going well or not.
2. Being specific, measurable, and tied directly to something that actually matters.
3. A metric that's easy to measure but doesn't reflect what actually matters to the business's real goals, like followers instead of revenue.
4. Answers will vary (example: "percentage of prepared food discarded at end of day, tracked weekly.")

## Answers and Explanations
1. This defines KPI in accessible, non-jargon terms.
2. Specificity, measurability, and relevance are the three qualities that make a KPI genuinely useful.
3. Vanity metrics look good without necessarily reflecting real business health, which can mislead decision-making.
4. A correct answer picks a specific, trackable number directly tied to the stated goal (reducing waste).

## Key Takeaways
- A KPI is simply a specific, trackable number that reflects progress toward something that matters.
- Useful KPIs are specific, measurable, and directly tied to real business goals, not vanity metrics.
- A small number of meaningful KPIs beats a cluttered dashboard of numbers that don't connect to what actually matters.
