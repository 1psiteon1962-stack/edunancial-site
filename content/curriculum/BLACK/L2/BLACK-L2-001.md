## BLACK-L2-001
### From Doer to Leader: The First Interaction Between Pieces

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain how the transition from individual contributor to leader touches multiple pieces at once, echoing every prior color's opening lesson.
- Identify at least three areas this transition commonly affects.
- Apply Level 2 "what else does this move affect" thinking to a leadership transition scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Level 1 introduced individual leadership concepts — management, authority, teams — as separate pieces. Level 2 begins, as it has in every color, by watching what happens to the rest of the board when someone transitions from doing the work personally to leading others who do it.

Consider a top-performing individual contributor promoted into a leadership role for the first time. This single transition touches several pieces at once: their daily activities shift from personal task execution toward direction, coaching, and decision-making on behalf of others (developed further in Lesson 2's management-versus-leadership distinction); their success metric shifts from personal output to team output, a genuinely different measure requiring a different mindset; and their relationships with former peers now reporting to them shift in ways that require deliberate, thoughtful navigation, not an assumption that prior peer relationships simply continue unchanged.

This is the same "what else does this move affect" discipline built throughout every prior color's Level 2, now applied directly to the leadership transition. BLACK-L2 examines the skills required to lead people and organizations — a distinct competency from the technical or functional skills that often earn someone the opportunity to lead in the first place.

**3. Worked Example**
Promoted from top salesperson to sales team leader, a new manager initially continues spending most of her time personally closing deals, the activity that earned her the promotion, rather than shifting her time toward coaching her new team members (echoing Lesson 2's distinction). She soon recognizes this transition requires more than a title change: her daily activities, her success metric, and her relationships with former peers all need deliberate attention, not an assumption that what made her successful as an individual contributor will automatically make her successful as a leader.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. Which of the following is NOT one of the pieces the doer-to-leader transition commonly touches, per this lesson?
   a) Daily activities and success metrics
   b) Relationships with former peers
   c) Favorite lunch spot
   d) Team output versus personal output
2. True or False: The skills that make someone an excellent individual contributor automatically and fully transfer to making them an excellent leader.
3. Why did the new sales manager's initial approach fall short?
   a) She immediately shifted all her time toward coaching her team
   b) She continued personal task execution rather than shifting toward the direction and coaching her new role required
   c) Sales leadership requires no different skills than sales execution
   d) She had no former peers now reporting to her
4. What is the core Level 2 skill this lesson builds, echoing every prior color's opening lesson?
   a) Memorizing a single leadership formula
   b) Asking "what else does this move affect?" before acting
   c) Avoiding all leadership transitions entirely
   d) Ignoring team output as a success metric

**5. Answer Key**
1. c  2. False  3. b  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Favorite lunch spot has no relationship to this transition; the other three are directly affected pieces.
2. Individual contributor skills do not automatically transfer to leadership; the transition requires distinct skills developed throughout this level.
3. The new manager's continued personal task execution reflected a failure to shift toward the direction and coaching her leadership role actually required.
4. This lesson reinforces the same multi-piece thinking habit built throughout every prior color's Level 2.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The doer-to-leader transition touches daily activities, success metrics, and peer relationships simultaneously.
- Excellent individual contributor performance does not automatically transfer to excellent leadership performance.
- BLACK-L2 examines the distinct skills required to lead people and organizations, not the technical skills that often earn the opportunity to lead.
- This lesson reinforces the same multi-piece thinking habit built throughout every prior color's Level 2.
