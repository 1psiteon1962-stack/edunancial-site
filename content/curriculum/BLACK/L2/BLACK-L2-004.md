## BLACK-L2-004
### Delegation Revisited: The Leadership Skill Behind It (BLACK Meets BLUE)

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce BLUE-L2-026's delegation discussion from the leadership skill perspective.
- Identify the specific leadership skills delegation requires.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical delegation scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Revisiting BLUE-L2-026's delegation discussion, this lesson examines the specific leadership skills underlying effective delegation: clear communication (developed further in Lesson 10) of expectations and desired outcomes, genuine trust-building (Lesson 9) that allows tolerating a different approach than the leader's own, and the emotional discipline to resist the urge to take back a delegated task at the first sign of a different, though not necessarily worse, approach.

This lesson reframes delegation as fundamentally a leadership skill, not merely a management technique or time-management tool: the psychological barriers BLUE-L2-026 identified — believing "no one can do it as well as I can," discomfort with a different style — are genuinely leadership challenges requiring the trust-building, communication, and emotional discipline this level addresses throughout, not simply a checklist of steps to follow.

**3. Worked Example**
Struggling to delegate a specific responsibility despite having a capable team member available, a leader recognizes her difficulty is not primarily about the task's technical complexity, but about her own emotional discomfort with a different approach than her own and her underdeveloped trust in her team member's capability. Recognizing delegation as fundamentally a leadership skill requiring genuine trust-building and emotional discipline, not merely a management technique, she works specifically on these underlying leadership capacities rather than simply repeating delegation attempts without addressing the actual underlying barrier.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Clear communication, genuine trust-building, and emotional discipline are identified as leadership skills underlying effective delegation.
2. This lesson reframes delegation as fundamentally a leadership skill, not merely a management technique or time-management tool.
3. Delegation difficulties often stem from a leader's own emotional discomfort or underdeveloped trust, not merely a task's technical complexity.
4. The leader's recognition of her underlying leadership skill gaps appropriately redirected her development effort toward trust-building and emotional discipline.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- This lesson revisits BLUE-L2-026's delegation discussion, reframing it as fundamentally a leadership skill.
- Effective delegation requires clear communication, genuine trust-building, and emotional discipline to tolerate different approaches.
- Delegation difficulties often stem from underdeveloped trust or emotional discomfort, not merely technical task complexity.
- Addressing the underlying leadership skill gap, not just repeating delegation attempts, resolves persistent delegation difficulty.
