## BLACK-L2-006
### Leadership Styles: An Overview and Comparison

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify several commonly referenced leadership styles.
- Compare general characteristics of each style.
- Recognize that no single style is universally superior.

**2. Core Content**
Several commonly referenced leadership styles include: directive (the leader makes decisions and provides clear, specific instruction, suiting situations requiring quick, decisive action or less experienced teams), participative (the leader involves team members in decision-making, suiting situations benefiting from diverse input and buy-in), delegative (the leader grants significant autonomy to capable team members, suiting highly skilled, self-directed teams), and transformational (the leader inspires and motivates through vision and personal example, often emphasized for driving significant organizational change).

No single style is universally superior; each carries genuine tradeoffs and fits different circumstances, team compositions, and organizational needs — a directive style might be entirely appropriate during a genuine crisis requiring rapid, decisive action, while the same style applied consistently to a highly skilled, self-directed team might produce frustration and disengagement that a more delegative approach would avoid, setting up Lesson 7's situational leadership discussion.

**3. Worked Example**
Reflecting on her own default leadership style, a leader recognizes she tends to rely heavily on a directive approach across every situation, regardless of her team's specific skill level or the situation's specific demands. Recognizing this as only one of several available styles, each with genuine tradeoffs, she begins considering when a more participative or delegative approach might better fit her more experienced team members and more routine, non-crisis situations, rather than applying a single default style uniformly regardless of context.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What generally characterizes a directive leadership style?
   a) Granting significant autonomy to capable team members
   b) The leader makes decisions and provides clear, specific instruction
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Inspiring through vision alone, with no specific instruction
2. What generally characterizes a delegative leadership style?
   a) The leader makes every decision with no team input
   b) The leader grants significant autonomy to capable, self-directed team members
   c) A style suited only to inexperienced teams
   d) A concept identical to a directive style
3. True or False: One single leadership style is universally superior and should be applied identically across every situation and team.
4. Why did the leader reconsider her consistent reliance on a directive style?
   a) A directive style is always the single best approach regardless of context
   b) She recognized different styles carry different tradeoffs fitting different team skill levels and situational demands
   c) Leadership style has no relationship to team composition or situation
   d) She had no team members with varying skill levels

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A directive style generally involves the leader making decisions and providing clear, specific instruction.
2. A delegative style generally involves granting significant autonomy to capable, self-directed team members.
3. No single leadership style is universally superior; the right fit depends on team composition and situational demands.
4. The leader's reconsideration appropriately recognized that different styles fit different team skill levels and situations, setting up Lesson 7.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Commonly referenced leadership styles include directive, participative, delegative, and transformational.
- Each style carries genuine tradeoffs, fitting different team compositions and situational demands.
- No single style is universally superior across every context.
- This sets up Lesson 7's situational leadership discussion, matching style to specific circumstances.
