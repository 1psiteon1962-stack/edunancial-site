## BLACK-L2-007
### Situational Leadership: Matching Style to Circumstance

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general premise of situational leadership.
- Identify factors that inform which style fits a specific circumstance.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical team scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 6, situational leadership is the general premise that effective leaders deliberately adapt their style based on specific circumstances — particularly a given team member's or team's specific competence and confidence level for a specific task — rather than applying a single default style uniformly across every person and situation. A less experienced or less confident team member on a specific task might benefit from a more directive approach, while a highly skilled, confident team member on that same type of task might respond better to a delegative approach that would feel unnecessarily controlling if applied to them.

This means the same leader might reasonably apply different styles simultaneously across different team members, or even across different tasks for the same team member, depending on that specific person's competence and confidence for that specific situation — situational leadership is fundamentally about this kind of deliberate, individualized calibration, not a fixed, one-size-fits-all leadership formula.

**3. Worked Example**
Leading a team with varying experience levels, a manager applies a more directive approach with a newer team member still building competence and confidence in a specific task area, while applying a more delegative approach with an experienced team member handling a similar task type, recognizing their different competence and confidence levels call for different leadership styles even for comparable work. She also recognizes that as her newer team member's competence grows over time, her own style with that specific person should gradually shift toward more delegation as well.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general premise of situational leadership?
   a) Applying a single default style uniformly across every person and situation
   b) Deliberately adapting leadership style based on a specific team member's or team's competence and confidence for a specific circumstance
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A concept unrelated to team member skill level
2. Why might the same leader apply different styles to different team members simultaneously?
   a) This is never appropriate; leaders should apply one identical style to everyone
   b) Different team members' competence and confidence levels for specific tasks call for different, individually calibrated styles
   c) Leadership style has no relationship to individual team member differences
   d) Situational leadership requires ignoring individual differences entirely
3. True or False: A leader's style with a specific team member should remain fixed forever, regardless of that person's growing competence over time.
4. Why did the manager apply different styles to her newer and more experienced team members?
   a) She applied an identical style to both, regardless of their differing competence and confidence
   b) Their different competence and confidence levels for the specific task called for individually calibrated styles
   c) Leadership style should never vary across team members
   d) She had no team members with varying experience levels

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Situational leadership involves deliberately adapting style based on a specific team member's or team's competence and confidence for a given circumstance.
2. Different team members' competence and confidence levels for specific tasks call for different, individually calibrated leadership styles.
3. A leader's style with a specific person should evolve as that person's competence grows, not remain fixed indefinitely.
4. The manager's individually calibrated approach appropriately matched each team member's specific competence and confidence level.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Situational leadership deliberately adapts style based on a specific person's or team's competence and confidence for a given circumstance.
- The same leader might reasonably apply different styles across different team members or tasks simultaneously.
- A leader's style with a specific person should evolve as that person's competence and confidence grow over time.
- This builds directly on Lesson 6's style overview, adding the calibration discipline that makes style selection deliberate rather than default.
