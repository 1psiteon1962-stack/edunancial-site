## BLACK-L2-012
### Giving Feedback: Specific, Timely, and Actionable

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify the general characteristics of effective feedback.
- Explain why vague or delayed feedback undermines its own purpose.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical feedback scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Effective feedback is generally specific (addressing a particular, identifiable behavior or outcome, not a vague general impression), timely (delivered close enough to the actual event that it remains relevant and actionable, rather than delayed until a much later formal review), and actionable (providing clear guidance on what the recipient could specifically do differently or continue doing, rather than only identifying a problem without offering constructive direction).

Vague feedback ("you need to communicate better") generally fails to provide the specific, actionable guidance a recipient needs to actually improve, while delayed feedback (addressing a months-old incident only during an annual review, echoing Lesson 24's ongoing-versus-annual performance management discussion) loses much of its relevance and practical value, since the recipient may no longer clearly recall the specific circumstances or have had any opportunity to adjust in the intervening time.

**3. Worked Example**
Providing feedback to a team member about a recent client presentation, a leader avoids a vague statement like "your presentations need improvement," instead specifically noting that the presentation's data section lacked clear visual organization, offering a specific example of an alternative approach the team member could use next time, and delivering this feedback within a day of the presentation rather than waiting for a scheduled review months later. This specific, timely, actionable approach gives the team member genuinely useful information they can apply to their very next presentation.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does "specific" feedback generally involve, per this lesson?
   a) A vague general impression with no particular identifiable behavior
   b) Addressing a particular, identifiable behavior or outcome
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Feedback delayed until a much later formal review
2. Why does delayed feedback lose much of its relevance and practical value?
   a) Delayed feedback is always more valuable than timely feedback
   b) The recipient may no longer clearly recall specific circumstances or have had opportunity to adjust in the intervening time
   c) Timing has no relationship to feedback's usefulness
   d) Feedback delivered months later is always equally actionable as immediate feedback
3. True or False: Vague feedback like "you need to communicate better" generally provides sufficient specific, actionable guidance for genuine improvement.
4. Why was the leader's presentation feedback more useful than a vague statement would have been?
   a) It was equally vague and non-specific
   b) It specifically identified the issue, offered a concrete alternative, and was delivered promptly, making it genuinely actionable
   c) It was delayed until a much later scheduled review
   d) It provided no concrete example or specific guidance

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Specific feedback addresses a particular, identifiable behavior or outcome, not a vague general impression.
2. Delayed feedback loses relevance since the recipient may no longer clearly recall circumstances or have had opportunity to adjust.
3. Vague feedback generally fails to provide the specific, actionable guidance needed for genuine improvement.
4. The leader's specific, timely, actionable feedback gave the team member genuinely useful information applicable to their next presentation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Effective feedback is specific, timely, and actionable, not vague or delayed.
- Vague feedback fails to provide the specific guidance needed for genuine improvement.
- Delayed feedback loses relevance and practical value as circumstances and recall fade over time.
- This sets up Lesson 24's discussion of ongoing, rather than only annual, performance management.
