## BLACK-L2-013
### Receiving Feedback: A Leader's Own Growth Discipline

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain why a leader's own receptiveness to feedback matters for organizational culture.
- Identify common defensive responses that undermine genuine feedback reception.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical scenario involving upward feedback.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 12, this lesson addresses the reverse direction: a leader's own discipline in genuinely receiving feedback, including from team members without formal authority over the leader (echoing Lesson 42's managing-up discussion). A leader who models genuine openness to feedback — including feedback about their own leadership approach — sets a powerful example that shapes the broader organizational culture (developed further in Lesson 28) around feedback and psychological safety (Lesson 30).

Common defensive responses that undermine genuine feedback reception include immediately explaining or justifying rather than first genuinely considering the feedback, dismissing feedback based on the messenger's seniority or perceived standing rather than its actual substance, and failing to visibly act on feedback in ways that would demonstrate it was genuinely heard, all of which discourage team members from offering honest feedback in the future, echoing Lesson 9's trust-building discussion.

**4. Worked Example**
Recognizing his own tendency to immediately explain his reasoning whenever a team member offered critical feedback about his leadership approach, a leader deliberately works to pause and genuinely consider such feedback before responding, rather than reflexively justifying his prior decision. When a team member candidly points out that his recent communication style felt overly abrupt during a stressful period, he resists the urge to immediately explain the stress he was under, instead genuinely acknowledging the feedback and visibly adjusting his approach afterward — modeling the kind of feedback receptiveness he hopes to see throughout his broader team culture.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. Why does a leader's own receptiveness to feedback matter, per this lesson?
   a) It has no relationship to organizational culture
   b) It sets a powerful example shaping the broader organizational culture around feedback and psychological safety
   c) Leaders should never receive feedback from team members without formal authority over them
   d) Feedback reception only matters in the downward direction, never upward
2. What is a common defensive response that undermines genuine feedback reception?
   a) Genuinely considering the feedback before responding
   b) Immediately explaining or justifying rather than first genuinely considering the feedback
   c) Visibly acting on feedback to demonstrate it was heard
   d) A response with no relationship to feedback reception
3. True or False: Dismissing feedback based on the messenger's seniority, rather than its actual substance, is described as an effective feedback reception practice.
4. Why did the leader resist his urge to immediately explain his stress-related communication style?
   a) He wanted to reflexively justify his behavior rather than genuinely consider the feedback
   b) He recognized modeling genuine feedback receptiveness would shape his broader team culture positively
   c) Feedback reception has no relationship to team culture
   d) He had no interest in adjusting his communication approach

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A leader's genuine openness to feedback sets a powerful example shaping the broader organizational culture around feedback and psychological safety.
2. Immediately explaining or justifying rather than genuinely considering feedback first is a common defensive response undermining reception.
3. Dismissing feedback based on messenger seniority, rather than substance, undermines genuine feedback reception and discourages future honesty.
4. The leader's resistance to reflexive justification appropriately modeled the genuine feedback receptiveness he hoped to cultivate in his broader team.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A leader's own receptiveness to feedback shapes the broader organizational culture around feedback and psychological safety.
- Common defensive responses include reflexive justification, dismissing based on messenger standing, and failing to visibly act on feedback.
- Genuine feedback reception, especially from those without formal authority, models the culture a leader hopes to build.
- This connects directly to Lesson 9's trust-building and previews Lesson 42's managing-up discussion.
