## BLACK-L2-016
### Mediating Conflict Between Team Members

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce PURPLE-L2-029's mediation discussion from the leadership perspective.
- Identify general steps for a leader mediating team conflict.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical mediation scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Revisiting PURPLE-L2-029's mediation discussion — a neutral third party facilitating resolution rather than imposing a decision — this lesson applies the same underlying principle to a leader mediating conflict between team members. General steps include: meeting with each party individually first to understand their genuine perspective (echoing Lesson 15's underlying-source discussion), then facilitating a joint conversation focused on the actual underlying issue rather than accusations, and helping the parties themselves generate a mutually acceptable resolution rather than the leader unilaterally imposing one.

This mirrors PURPLE-L2-029's core distinction between mediation and arbitration: a leader who simply imposes a decision (functioning more like an arbitrator) may resolve the immediate dispute but often fails to address the underlying relationship dynamic, while genuine mediation — helping the parties themselves reach understanding and resolution — tends to produce more durable outcomes and can actually improve the working relationship going forward, rather than merely suppressing the visible conflict.

**3. Worked Example**
Mediating between two team members in ongoing conflict, a leader first meets with each individually to understand their genuine perspectives, echoing PURPLE-L2-029's mediation approach, then facilitates a joint conversation focused on their underlying, previously unclear role division (echoing Lesson 15's worked example) rather than relitigating specific past incidents. Rather than simply imposing her own decision about how responsibilities should be divided, she guides the two team members toward generating their own mutually acceptable resolution, resulting in an outcome both genuinely support rather than one merely imposed upon them.
