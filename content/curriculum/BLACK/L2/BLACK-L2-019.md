## BLACK-L2-019
### Building a Decision-Making Framework for a Team

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of a team decision-making framework.
- Identify general components such a framework might include.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical team scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lessons 17–18, this lesson addresses formalizing decision-making practices at the team level, not just for an individual leader's own decisions. A team decision-making framework generally clarifies which types of decisions require the leader's direct involvement versus which can be made independently by team members (echoing BLUE-L2-026's delegation discussion), what information or input should generally be gathered before a decision within each category, and how decisions and their reasoning should be communicated once made (echoing Lesson 10's clarity discussion).

This formalization matters because without a clear framework, teams often default to escalating every decision upward regardless of its actual significance (undermining the delegation and scaling benefits discussed throughout BLUE-L2), or conversely, making significant decisions without appropriate input or awareness, creating genuine risk — a clear framework helps calibrate the appropriate level of involvement and process to each decision's actual significance and reversibility.

**3. Worked Example**
Recognizing her team had been escalating even minor, low-stakes decisions to her unnecessarily, a leader establishes a clear decision-making framework categorizing decisions by significance and reversibility: low-stakes, easily reversible decisions can be made independently by team members without her involvement; moderate decisions require informing her but not necessarily awaiting her approval; and significant, difficult-to-reverse decisions require her direct involvement and approval before proceeding. This framework reduces unnecessary escalation while still ensuring appropriate involvement for genuinely significant decisions.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a team decision-making framework generally clarify, per this lesson?
   a) Nothing useful for team decision-making
   b) Which decisions require the leader's direct involvement versus which can be made independently, and how decisions should be communicated
   c) Only decisions with no relationship to significance or reversibility
   d) A framework requiring every decision to be escalated regardless of significance
2. What problem can occur without a clear decision-making framework, per this lesson?
   a) Teams always make perfectly calibrated decisions without any framework
   b) Teams may default to escalating every decision regardless of significance, or make significant decisions without appropriate input
   c) Decision-making frameworks have no relationship to team scaling or delegation
   d) No problems occur in the absence of a formal framework
3. True or False: An effective decision-making framework should require every decision, regardless of significance, to be escalated to the leader for approval.
4. Why did the leader establish a framework categorizing decisions by significance and reversibility?
   a) She wanted every decision, regardless of stakes, to require her direct involvement
   b) This framework reduced unnecessary escalation while ensuring appropriate involvement for genuinely significant decisions
   c) Significance and reversibility have no relationship to appropriate decision-making processes
   d) She had no interest in delegating any decision-making authority

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A team decision-making framework clarifies which decisions require leader involvement versus independent decision-making, and how decisions should be communicated.
2. Without a clear framework, teams may over-escalate low-stakes decisions or make significant decisions without appropriate input.
3. An effective framework calibrates involvement to significance and reversibility, not requiring every decision to be escalated regardless of stakes.
4. The leader's categorized framework appropriately reduced unnecessary escalation while preserving appropriate involvement for significant decisions.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A team decision-making framework clarifies appropriate involvement levels and communication for different decision types.
- This builds on Lessons 17–18's decision-making discussion, now formalized at the team level.
- Without a clear framework, teams may over-escalate minor decisions or under-involve leadership in significant ones.
- Calibrating involvement to a decision's significance and reversibility supports both efficiency and appropriate oversight.
