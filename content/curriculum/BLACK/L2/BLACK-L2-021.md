## BLACK-L2-021
### Hiring for Fit: Skills vs. Values Alignment

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish skills fit from values fit in hiring.
- Explain why both dimensions matter, and why values misalignment is often harder to correct later.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical hiring scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on BLUE-L2-021's hiring discussion, this lesson examines a specific dimension leaders should weigh: skills fit (whether a candidate has the technical or functional capabilities the role requires) versus values fit (whether a candidate's work style, ethics, and general approach align with the organization's culture, previewed here and developed further in Lesson 28). Both dimensions matter, but they carry different practical implications: skill gaps can often be addressed through training and development over time, while values misalignment — fundamental differences in work ethic, honesty, or collaborative approach — is generally much harder to correct after hiring.

This does not mean skills are unimportant, but it does mean a leader weighing a strong-skills, weak-values-fit candidate against a moderate-skills, strong-values-fit candidate should recognize this as a genuinely different kind of tradeoff than choosing between two candidates who differ primarily in skill level alone, since one gap is generally more trainable than the other.

**3. Worked Example**
Comparing two final candidates for an open position, a leader recognizes one candidate has stronger immediate technical skills but has shown some concerning signals regarding collaborative work style and honesty during the interview process, while the other candidate has slightly less immediate technical skill but demonstrates strong alignment with the team's collaborative values and work ethic. Recognizing that skill gaps are generally more trainable than values misalignment, she selects the values-aligned candidate, planning to invest in closing the more addressable skills gap through training.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does skills fit generally refer to, in this hiring context?
   a) A candidate's work style, ethics, and general cultural approach
   b) Whether a candidate has the technical or functional capabilities the role requires
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A concept identical to values fit with no distinction
2. Why is values misalignment generally harder to correct than a skills gap?
   a) Skills gaps are impossible to address through training
   b) Fundamental differences in work ethic, honesty, or collaborative approach are generally much harder to correct after hiring than technical skill gaps
   c) Values and skills present identical correction challenges
   d) Values misalignment always resolves itself naturally over time
3. True or False: Skills fit and values fit present identical, equally trainable challenges when a candidate falls short in either area.
4. Why did the leader select the values-aligned candidate over the candidate with stronger immediate technical skills?
   a) Skills gaps are generally harder to correct than values misalignment
   b) She recognized values misalignment is generally harder to correct, while the skills gap was more addressable through training
   c) She had no concerns about either candidate's values
   d) Technical skill has no relevance to hiring decisions

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Skills fit generally refers to whether a candidate has the technical or functional capabilities the role requires.
2. Fundamental differences in work ethic, honesty, or collaborative approach are generally much harder to correct after hiring than technical skill gaps.
3. Skills and values present genuinely different correction challenges, with values misalignment generally harder to address after hiring.
4. The leader's selection appropriately weighed the generally harder-to-correct values misalignment against the more trainable skills gap.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Skills fit addresses technical or functional capability; values fit addresses work style, ethics, and cultural alignment.
- Skill gaps are often more trainable than values misalignment, which is generally much harder to correct after hiring.
- This does not make skills unimportant, but does change the calculus when weighing tradeoffs between candidates.
- This builds directly on BLUE-L2-021's hiring discussion and previews Lesson 28's culture discussion.
