## BLACK-L2-022
### Onboarding: The First 90 Days and Why They Matter

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain why the initial onboarding period carries outsized importance.
- Identify general components of effective onboarding.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical new hire scenario.

**2. Core Content**
The initial period after a new hire joins — commonly referenced as the first 90 days — carries outsized importance for the employment relationship's long-term trajectory: this period generally shapes a new team member's understanding of expectations (previewing Lesson 23), their sense of belonging and cultural fit (previewing Lesson 28), and their early impressions of the organization's competence and genuine care for their success, all of which can significantly influence their engagement, performance, and even likelihood of remaining with the organization long-term.

Effective onboarding generally includes clear communication of role expectations and success criteria from the outset (echoing Lesson 23), proactive introduction to key relationships and resources the new hire will need, and regular, structured early check-ins rather than leaving a new hire to independently discover how to succeed — a poorly managed onboarding period can leave even a well-qualified hire struggling unnecessarily, sometimes leading to preventable early departures that a more deliberate onboarding process could have avoided.

**3. Worked Example**
Bringing on a new team member, a leader deliberately structures a comprehensive onboarding process rather than leaving the new hire to independently figure out expectations and relationships: she clearly communicates specific success criteria for the role from day one, proactively introduces the new hire to key colleagues and resources, and schedules regular structured check-ins throughout the first 90 days rather than only an ad hoc, occasional check-in. This deliberate approach helps the new hire build confidence and genuine understanding more quickly than an unstructured onboarding period would have allowed.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. Why does the initial onboarding period carry outsized importance, per this lesson?
   a) It has no relationship to a new hire's long-term engagement or performance
   b) It significantly shapes understanding of expectations, cultural fit, and early impressions, influencing long-term engagement and retention
   c) Onboarding only matters for the very first day, with no lasting effect
   d) The initial period has no relationship to preventable early departures
2. What is a general component of effective onboarding, per this lesson?
   a) Leaving a new hire to independently discover expectations with no structured support
   b) Clear communication of role expectations, proactive relationship introductions, and regular structured check-ins
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Avoiding any communication of success criteria
3. True or False: A poorly managed onboarding period has no relationship to a well-qualified hire's likelihood of struggling or leaving early.
4. Why did the leader schedule regular structured check-ins throughout the new hire's first 90 days?
   a) She wanted to leave the new hire to independently figure out expectations
   b) Regular structured check-ins support a new hire's understanding and confidence-building more effectively than an unstructured approach
   c) Check-ins have no relationship to onboarding effectiveness
   d) She had no interest in the new hire's early success

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The initial onboarding period significantly shapes a new hire's understanding of expectations, cultural fit, and early impressions, influencing long-term engagement and retention.
2. Clear expectation communication, proactive relationship introductions, and regular structured check-ins are general components of effective onboarding.
3. A poorly managed onboarding period can leave even a well-qualified hire struggling unnecessarily, sometimes leading to preventable early departures.
4. The leader's structured check-ins appropriately supported the new hire's confidence and understanding more effectively than an unstructured approach.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The initial onboarding period carries outsized importance for long-term engagement, performance, and retention.
- Effective onboarding includes clear expectation communication, proactive relationship introductions, and regular structured check-ins.
- A poorly managed onboarding period can lead to preventable early departures, even for well-qualified hires.
- This previews Lesson 23's expectation-setting discussion and Lesson 28's culture discussion.
