## BLACK-L2-023
### Setting Expectations: The Root of Most Performance Problems

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain why unclear expectations underlie many apparent performance problems.
- Identify general characteristics of well-set expectations.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical performance scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 22, this lesson addresses a commonly overlooked root cause behind many apparent performance problems: expectations that were never clearly communicated or mutually understood in the first place. A team member who appears to be underperforming may, in some cases, actually be performing reasonably against their own genuine understanding of what was expected — an understanding that differs from the leader's actual, unstated expectations, echoing Lesson 10's communication clarity discussion.

Well-set expectations are generally specific (clearly defining what success looks like, not a vague general sense), mutually confirmed (the leader has verified the team member genuinely understands, not merely assumed clear delivery guarantees clear reception, echoing Lesson 10), and appropriately documented where useful for future reference — investing this effort upfront generally prevents a significant portion of the performance problems that might otherwise require more difficult intervention later (Lesson 26).

**3. Worked Example**
Investigating an apparent performance shortfall, a leader discovers, upon closer examination, that the team member had never actually received a clear, specific statement of what success in the role looked like — the leader had assumed this was understood, but had never explicitly and specifically communicated it, nor confirmed genuine understanding. Recognizing this expectation-setting failure as the actual root cause, rather than a genuine performance or effort problem, the leader addresses the gap directly by clearly communicating and confirming specific expectations going forward, resolving much of the apparent performance issue without needing the more difficult underperformance intervention discussed in Lesson 26.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What commonly overlooked root cause does this lesson identify behind many apparent performance problems?
   a) Excessively clear, well-communicated expectations
   b) Expectations that were never clearly communicated or mutually understood in the first place
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A cause entirely unrelated to communication
2. What generally characterizes well-set expectations, per this lesson?
   a) Vague, unconfirmed, and undocumented
   b) Specific, mutually confirmed, and appropriately documented where useful
   c) A concept identical to poorly set expectations
   d) Expectations that are never explicitly communicated
3. True or False: A team member's apparent underperformance is always genuinely about their effort or capability, never about unclear expectations.
4. Why did the leader's investigation reveal an expectation-setting failure rather than a genuine performance problem?
   a) The team member had received a clear, specific, confirmed statement of expectations
   b) The leader had assumed understanding without ever explicitly communicating or confirming specific expectations
   c) Expectation-setting has no relationship to performance outcomes
   d) The team member had no role responsibilities at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Expectations that were never clearly communicated or mutually understood are identified as a commonly overlooked root cause of apparent performance problems.
2. Well-set expectations are specific, mutually confirmed, and appropriately documented where useful.
3. Apparent underperformance can sometimes actually reflect unclear expectations rather than genuine effort or capability problems.
4. The leader's investigation revealed she had assumed understanding without explicit communication or confirmation, the actual root cause.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Unclear or unconfirmed expectations underlie many apparent performance problems, distinct from genuine effort or capability issues.
- Well-set expectations are specific, mutually confirmed, and appropriately documented.
- Investing effort in clear expectation-setting upfront prevents many performance problems that would otherwise require more difficult intervention.
- This builds directly on Lesson 10's communication clarity discussion and previews Lesson 26's underperformance discussion.
