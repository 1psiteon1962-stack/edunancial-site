## BLACK-L2-024
### Performance Management: Ongoing, Not Just Annual

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain why ongoing performance management outperforms an annual-review-only approach.
- Connect this concept directly to Lesson 12's timely feedback discussion.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical performance management scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 12's timely feedback discussion, this lesson addresses performance management as an ongoing leadership practice, not an activity confined to a single annual review. An annual-review-only approach generally suffers from the same timeliness problem Lesson 12 identified: feedback about events from many months earlier has lost much of its relevance and actionability, and a team member receiving their first substantive performance discussion of the year during a single formal review may be understandably surprised or blindsided by concerns that could have been addressed much earlier.

Ongoing performance management generally involves regular, less formal check-ins throughout the year addressing specific, recent performance observations (echoing Lesson 12), with the annual or periodic formal review serving as a summary and forward-looking planning conversation rather than the sole occasion for substantive performance feedback — this approach generally produces fewer surprises, more timely course-correction opportunities, and a stronger ongoing relationship than a purely annual approach.

**3. Worked Example**
Restructuring her performance management approach, a leader shifts from relying solely on a single annual review toward regular, brief monthly check-ins addressing specific, recent performance observations throughout the year, echoing Lesson 12's timely feedback principle. When her formal annual review eventually occurs, it serves primarily as a summary of themes already discussed throughout the year and a forward-looking planning conversation, rather than surprising her team member with newly raised concerns about events from many months earlier that could have been addressed much sooner.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What problem does an annual-review-only approach generally suffer from, per this lesson?
   a) No problems whatsoever
   b) The same timeliness problem Lesson 12 identified — feedback about months-old events loses relevance and actionability
   c) Annual reviews are always more timely and effective than ongoing check-ins
   d) Annual reviews have no relationship to feedback timeliness
2. What role does the formal annual review generally serve, in an ongoing performance management approach?
   a) The sole occasion for any substantive performance feedback throughout the year
   b) A summary and forward-looking planning conversation, building on themes already discussed through regular check-ins
   c) A concept unrelated to ongoing performance management
   d) An opportunity to surprise the team member with entirely new concerns
3. True or False: Ongoing performance management throughout the year makes the formal annual review entirely unnecessary and should replace it completely.
4. Why did the leader's team member experience fewer surprises during the formal annual review after this restructuring?
   a) The annual review remained the sole occasion for any performance discussion
   b) Regular monthly check-ins throughout the year had already addressed specific, recent observations, making the annual review a summary rather than a surprise
   c) Ongoing check-ins have no relationship to reducing surprises during formal reviews
   d) The leader avoided any performance discussion throughout the year

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An annual-review-only approach suffers from the same timeliness problem Lesson 12 identified, since months-old feedback loses relevance and actionability.
2. In an ongoing approach, the formal annual review serves as a summary and forward-looking planning conversation, building on regular check-ins.
3. Ongoing performance management complements, rather than entirely replaces, a periodic formal review's summary and planning function.
4. The leader's regular monthly check-ins throughout the year appropriately reduced surprises during the eventual formal annual review.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Ongoing performance management throughout the year outperforms relying solely on an annual review.
- This builds directly on Lesson 12's timely feedback discussion, applied to the broader performance management structure.
- The formal annual review functions best as a summary and planning conversation, not the sole occasion for substantive feedback.
- This ongoing approach produces fewer surprises, more timely course-correction, and a stronger relationship.
