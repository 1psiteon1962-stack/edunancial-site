## BLACK-L2-025
### Recognizing and Rewarding Performance

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of recognizing and rewarding strong performance.
- Distinguish genuine, specific recognition from generic, infrequent praise.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical recognition scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 12's feedback principles, recognizing and rewarding strong performance generally follows the same specificity and timeliness standards effective constructive feedback requires — genuine, specific recognition (acknowledging a particular achievement or behavior, not a vague general compliment) delivered promptly tends to be more meaningful and more likely to reinforce the recognized behavior than generic, infrequent praise disconnected from specific accomplishments.

This connects directly to Lesson 46's later compensation discussion: formal compensation and rewards matter genuinely, but research and common experience also suggest that genuine, specific recognition — a leader authentically acknowledging a team member's particular contribution — carries meaningful motivational value independent of, and complementary to, formal compensation structures, meaning a leader relying entirely on compensation while neglecting genuine day-to-day recognition may be missing a significant, low-cost motivational tool.

**3. Worked Example**
Rather than offering only occasional, generic praise like "good job team" during broader meetings, a leader makes a deliberate practice of specifically and promptly recognizing individual contributions — acknowledging a particular team member's specific, effective handling of a challenging client situation immediately after it occurred, rather than folding it into a vague, delayed, group-level acknowledgment. This specific, timely recognition, distinct from her formal compensation structure, provides a meaningful, low-cost motivational tool that reinforces the specific behavior she wants to see continue.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Genuine recognition acknowledges a particular achievement or behavior specifically, distinct from a vague general compliment.
2. Genuine, specific recognition carries meaningful motivational value independent of, and complementary to, formal compensation structures.
3. A leader relying entirely on compensation while neglecting genuine recognition may be missing a significant, low-cost motivational tool.
4. The leader's specific, timely recognition appropriately reinforced the desired behavior, echoing Lesson 12's feedback principles.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Genuine, specific recognition, delivered promptly, tends to be more meaningful than generic, infrequent praise.
- This applies the same specificity and timeliness standards from Lesson 12's feedback discussion to positive recognition.
- Recognition carries motivational value independent of, and complementary to, formal compensation, previewed here for Lesson 46.
- Neglecting genuine day-to-day recognition may leave a significant, low-cost motivational tool unused.
