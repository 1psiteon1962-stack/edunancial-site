## BLACK-L2-026
### Addressing Underperformance: Structure Over Improvisation

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of a structured underperformance process.
- Identify general components such a process might include.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical underperformance scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lessons 14 (difficult conversations) and 23 (expectation-setting), this lesson addresses addressing genuine underperformance — distinct from the expectation-setting failures Lesson 23 identified as a common root cause — through a structured process rather than ad hoc improvisation. A structured approach generally includes: first confirming expectations were genuinely clear and understood (ruling out Lesson 23's more common root cause), then clearly identifying the specific performance gap with concrete examples (echoing Lesson 12's specificity standard), developing a specific improvement plan with clear, measurable milestones, and establishing a defined timeline for reassessment.

This structured approach protects both the organization and the team member: it provides the team member a genuine, clearly defined opportunity to improve with specific guidance rather than vague criticism, while also providing the leader and organization a documented, fair process should the underperformance ultimately require the more difficult decision discussed in Lesson 27, rather than an improvised, inconsistent approach that could feel arbitrary or unfair to the team member involved.

**3. Worked Example**
Addressing a genuine underperformance situation after first confirming (echoing Lesson 23) that expectations had indeed been clearly communicated and understood, a leader develops a structured improvement plan with the team member: specifically identifying the performance gap with concrete examples, establishing clear, measurable milestones for improvement, and setting a defined timeline for reassessment. This structured approach, rather than an improvised or purely informal conversation, gives the team member genuine clarity and opportunity while also establishing a fair, documented process should further action eventually become necessary.

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Confirming whether expectations were genuinely clear and understood should occur first, ruling out Lesson 23's more common root cause.
2. Clearly identifying the specific performance gap with concrete examples and a specific improvement plan with measurable milestones are general components.
3. A structured process provides genuine benefit over improvisation, offering clarity for the team member and a fair, documented process for the organization.
4. The leader's confirmation step appropriately ruled out the more common expectation-setting root cause before proceeding with genuine underperformance intervention.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Addressing underperformance should first rule out Lesson 23's more common root cause of unclear expectations.
- A structured process includes specific gap identification, a clear improvement plan with measurable milestones, and a defined reassessment timeline.
- This structured approach protects both the team member (genuine opportunity to improve) and the organization (a fair, documented process).
- This builds directly on Lessons 14 and 23's difficult conversation and expectation-setting discussions.
