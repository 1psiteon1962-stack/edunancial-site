## BLACK-L2-027
### When to Let Someone Go: A Difficult Leadership Decision

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general considerations informing a termination decision.
- Reinforce the BLUE-L2-020 employer responsibility theme from this specific, difficult context.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical termination scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 26's structured underperformance process, this lesson addresses the difficult decision of termination when a structured improvement process has not produced sufficient improvement, or when a more severe issue (a serious policy violation or ethical breach) warrants more immediate action. This decision should generally follow the structured, documented process Lesson 26 described where applicable, both for fairness to the individual and for the organization's legitimate interests, and should be made with the genuine stewardship consideration Lesson 8 and BLUE-L2-020 emphasized, recognizing the significant impact on the affected individual's livelihood.

This does not mean termination decisions should be indefinitely delayed out of discomfort — echoing Lesson 14's difficult-conversation-avoidance discussion, delaying a genuinely necessary termination often serves neither the individual (who may benefit from a clean transition to a role better suited to them) nor the team (who may be affected by continued underperformance or a problematic behavior pattern) nor the organization, meaning this remains a decision requiring both genuine care and appropriate timeliness, not indefinite avoidance.

**3. Worked Example**
After a structured improvement process (Lesson 26) has not produced sufficient improvement despite genuine support and clear expectations, a leader makes the difficult decision to terminate a team member's employment, having documented the process fairly and thoroughly throughout. She approaches the actual termination conversation with genuine care and respect for the individual's dignity, echoing this level's stewardship theme, while also recognizing that continuing to delay this necessary decision, out of her own discomfort, would not genuinely serve the individual, her team, or the organization.

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The structured, documented underperformance process from Lesson 26 should generally precede a termination decision where applicable.
2. Delaying a genuinely necessary termination often serves neither the individual, the team, nor the organization well.
3. A termination decision should generally follow a structured, documented process, not proceed immediately without any process where applicable.
4. The leader's genuine care and respect during the termination conversation appropriately reflected this level's stewardship theme.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Termination decisions should generally follow the structured, documented process from Lesson 26 where applicable.
- This decision should be made with genuine stewardship consideration, recognizing significant impact on the individual's livelihood.
- Indefinite delay out of discomfort often serves neither the individual, the team, nor the organization.
- This requires both genuine care and appropriate timeliness, echoing this level's consistent themes from Lessons 8 and 14.
