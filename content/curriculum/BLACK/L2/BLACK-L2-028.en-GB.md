## BLACK-L2-028
### Organisational Culture: What It Is and Who Actually Builds It

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define organisational culture at a conceptual level.
- Explain why culture is built through consistent behaviour, not stated values alone.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical culture-building scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Organisational culture generally refers to the shared values, norms, and behavioural patterns that actually characterise how people within an organisation genuinely act and interact — distinct from a company's stated or aspirational values, which may or may not reflect the actual, lived culture. Culture is built primarily through consistent, observed behaviour over time, particularly leadership's own behaviour (echoing Lesson 9's trust-building discussion): what leaders actually do, reward, and tolerate shapes culture far more powerfully than what an organisation's mission statement or values poster states.

This means a leader claiming to value collaboration while consistently rewarding individual competition, or claiming to value honesty while punishing those who deliver unwelcome but truthful feedback (echoing Lesson 13's feedback reception discussion), is actually building a culture inconsistent with their stated values, regardless of the stated aspiration — culture reflects what is actually consistently modelled and reinforced, not what is merely written down or announced.

**3. Worked Example**
Despite her organisation's stated values prominently emphasising collaboration, a leader recognises that her actual promotion and recognition practices have consistently rewarded individual achievement over collaborative behaviour, effectively building a culture of internal competition inconsistent with the stated collaborative values. Recognising that culture reflects what is actually modelled and rewarded, not what is merely stated, she begins deliberately adjusting her own behaviour and recognition practices to genuinely reinforce the collaborative culture she wants to build, rather than assuming the stated values alone would produce that culture.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Organisational culture refers to the shared values, norms, and behavioural patterns that actually characterise how people genuinely act and interact.
2. Culture is built primarily through consistent, observed behaviour over time, particularly leadership's own behaviour.
3. Stated values do not automatically reflect actual culture, which depends on what is consistently modelled and reinforced.
4. The leader's recognition of her inconsistent recognition practices revealed a genuine gap between stated values and actual, lived culture.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Organisational culture is the shared values, norms, and behaviours that actually characterise an organisation, distinct from stated aspirations.
- Culture is built primarily through consistent behaviour over time, particularly leadership's own modelled behaviour.
- What leaders actually reward and tolerate shapes culture far more than a mission statement or values poster.
- This sets up Lesson 29's culture-versus-perks distinction and Lesson 30's psychological safety discussion.
