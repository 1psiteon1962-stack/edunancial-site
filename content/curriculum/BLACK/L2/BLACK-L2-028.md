## BLACK-L2-028
### Organizational Culture: What It Is and Who Actually Builds It

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define organizational culture at a conceptual level.
- Explain why culture is built through consistent behavior, not stated values alone.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical culture-building scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Organizational culture generally refers to the shared values, norms, and behavioral patterns that actually characterize how people within an organization genuinely act and interact — distinct from a company's stated or aspirational values, which may or may not reflect the actual, lived culture. Culture is built primarily through consistent, observed behavior over time, particularly leadership's own behavior (echoing Lesson 9's trust-building discussion): what leaders actually do, reward, and tolerate shapes culture far more powerfully than what an organization's mission statement or values poster states.

This means a leader claiming to value collaboration while consistently rewarding individual competition, or claiming to value honesty while punishing those who deliver unwelcome but truthful feedback (echoing Lesson 13's feedback reception discussion), is actually building a culture inconsistent with their stated values, regardless of the stated aspiration — culture reflects what is actually consistently modeled and reinforced, not what is merely written down or announced.

**3. Worked Example**
Despite her organization's stated values prominently emphasizing collaboration, a leader recognizes that her actual promotion and recognition practices have consistently rewarded individual achievement over collaborative behavior, effectively building a culture of internal competition inconsistent with the stated collaborative values. Recognizing that culture reflects what is actually modeled and rewarded, not what is merely stated, she begins deliberately adjusting her own behavior and recognition practices to genuinely reinforce the collaborative culture she wants to build, rather than assuming the stated values alone would produce that culture.

   d) She had no promotion or recognition practices to examine

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Organizational culture refers to the shared values, norms, and behavioral patterns that actually characterize how people genuinely act and interact.
2. Culture is built primarily through consistent, observed behavior over time, particularly leadership's own behavior.
3. Stated values do not automatically reflect actual culture, which depends on what is consistently modeled and reinforced.
4. The leader's recognition of her inconsistent recognition practices revealed a genuine gap between stated values and actual, lived culture.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Organizational culture is the shared values, norms, and behaviors that actually characterize an organization, distinct from stated aspirations.
- Culture is built primarily through consistent behavior over time, particularly leadership's own modeled behavior.
- What leaders actually reward and tolerate shapes culture far more than a mission statement or values poster.
- This sets up Lesson 29's culture-versus-perks distinction and Lesson 30's psychological safety discussion.
