## BLACK-L2-032
### Building and Leading Teams: From Individuals to a Unit

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general distinction between a group of individuals and a genuine team.
- Identify factors that help transform individuals into a cohesive unit.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical team-building scenario.

**2. Core Content**
A group of individuals working in proximity is not automatically a genuine team; a team generally requires shared purpose (previewing Lesson 37's vision discussion), interdependent collaboration (team members genuinely relying on and coordinating with each other, not merely working independently in parallel), and the trust (Lesson 9) and psychological safety (Lesson 30) this level has emphasized throughout, allowing genuine collaboration rather than merely coexisting individual effort.

Transforming individuals into a genuine team generally requires deliberate leadership effort: clearly articulating shared purpose and how individual contributions connect to it, creating structured opportunities for genuine collaboration rather than assuming it will emerge spontaneously, and consistently modeling and reinforcing the trust and safety that allow team members to genuinely rely on and support one another, rather than treating team-building as a one-time event (an occasional retreat or activity) disconnected from ongoing daily leadership practice.

**3. Worked Example**
Recognizing her team members had been working largely independently in parallel, with limited genuine collaboration or shared sense of purpose, a leader deliberately works to transform this group into a genuine team: clearly articulating how each individual's work connects to a shared team purpose, creating structured collaborative opportunities where team members must genuinely coordinate rather than work in isolation, and consistently modeling the trust and psychological safety that allow genuine mutual reliance. She recognizes this transformation requires sustained, ongoing effort, not a single team-building event disconnected from her daily leadership practice.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What generally distinguishes a genuine team from a group of individuals working in proximity, per this lesson?
   a) Nothing distinguishes the two; they are identical concepts
   b) Shared purpose, interdependent collaboration, and the trust and psychological safety allowing genuine collaboration
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Physical proximity alone, with no further requirement
2. What does transforming individuals into a genuine team generally require, per this lesson?
   a) A single team-building event disconnected from ongoing daily practice
   b) Deliberate leadership effort including clear shared purpose, structured collaboration opportunities, and consistent trust-building
   c) Assuming genuine collaboration will emerge spontaneously with no leadership effort
   d) A concept unrelated to leadership practice
3. True or False: A group of individuals working in proximity automatically constitutes a genuine, cohesive team with no further leadership effort required.
4. Why did the leader recognize her group needed deliberate transformation into a genuine team?
   a) Her team members already demonstrated strong interdependent collaboration and shared purpose
   b) Her team members had been working largely independently in parallel, lacking genuine collaboration and shared purpose
   c) Team transformation has no relationship to leadership effort
   d) She had no interest in her team's collaborative dynamics

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A genuine team requires shared purpose, interdependent collaboration, and the trust and psychological safety allowing genuine collaboration.
2. Transforming individuals into a genuine team requires deliberate leadership effort, not a one-time event or spontaneous emergence.
3. Physical proximity alone does not automatically constitute a genuine, cohesive team without deliberate leadership effort.
4. The leader's recognition of her group's parallel, uncoordinated work appropriately prompted deliberate team-transformation effort.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A genuine team requires shared purpose, interdependent collaboration, and trust/psychological safety, distinct from mere proximity.
- Transforming individuals into a team requires deliberate, ongoing leadership effort, not a single event.
- This previews Lesson 37's vision discussion and builds on Lessons 9 and 30's trust and safety discussions.
- Genuine team cohesion requires sustained daily leadership practice, not an occasional disconnected activity.
