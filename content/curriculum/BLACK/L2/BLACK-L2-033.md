## BLACK-L2-033
### Team Dynamics: Stages of Team Development

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify commonly referenced stages of team development.
- Explain why teams commonly experience some friction before reaching full cohesion.
- Apply this framework to a hypothetical new team scenario.

**2. Core Content**
A commonly referenced team development model describes general stages: forming (team members initially getting acquainted, often with polite, cautious interaction), storming (genuine differences in style, opinion, or approach surface, sometimes producing friction, echoing Lesson 15's conflict discussion), norming (the team develops shared norms and ways of working together effectively), and performing (the team operates with genuine cohesion and effectiveness, having worked through earlier friction).

Recognizing this general progression helps a leader respond appropriately to each stage rather than becoming unduly alarmed by the storming stage's friction, which is a commonly normal part of team development rather than necessarily a sign of team failure — the leadership skill lies in helping a team navigate through storming constructively (using the conflict resolution principles from Lessons 15–16) toward norming and performing, rather than either avoiding necessary friction entirely or allowing it to derail the team's development.

**3. Worked Example**
Observing her newly formed team beginning to experience some friction and disagreement after an initial polite, cautious period, a leader recognizes this as the commonly normal storming stage of team development, rather than a sign her team-building efforts have failed. Applying the conflict resolution principles from Lessons 15–16, she helps her team navigate this friction constructively, gradually observing the team develop shared working norms (norming) and eventually operate with genuine cohesion and effectiveness (performing), rather than becoming alarmed by the storming stage's friction or attempting to suppress it entirely.

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The storming stage involves genuine differences in style, opinion, or approach surfacing, sometimes producing friction.
2. Recognizing this progression helps a leader respond to storming as a commonly normal developmental stage, not necessarily team failure.
3. Storming is a commonly normal part of team development, not necessarily an indication of failure requiring structural abandonment.
4. The leader's application of conflict resolution principles appropriately helped her team navigate storming toward norming and performing.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Commonly referenced team development stages include forming, storming, norming, and performing.
- Storming's friction is commonly a normal part of team development, not necessarily a sign of failure.
- The leadership skill lies in helping a team navigate storming constructively, using conflict resolution principles from Lessons 15–16.
- Recognizing this progression helps a leader respond appropriately rather than becoming unduly alarmed by normal friction.
