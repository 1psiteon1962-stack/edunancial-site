## BLACK-L2-035
### Change Management: Leading Through Organizational Change

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain general principles for leading a team through organizational change.
- Connect this concept directly to Lesson 9's trust-building discussion.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical change scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Leading through organizational change — a restructuring, a new system implementation, a strategic shift — requires specific leadership attention beyond routine operational leadership. General principles include clear, honest communication about the change's rationale and expected impact (echoing Lesson 10's clarity discussion, particularly important given the uncertainty change naturally introduces), genuine acknowledgment of legitimate concerns rather than dismissing them (echoing Lesson 11's active listening discussion), and consistent follow-through demonstrating the change is being implemented thoughtfully, not chaotically.

This connects directly to Lesson 9's trust-building discussion: a leader who has built genuine trust over time generally finds their team more willing to extend good faith during the inherent uncertainty and discomfort of organizational change, while a leader without that trust foundation may find change efforts meeting significantly more resistance and skepticism, echoing Lesson 36's resistance discussion, regardless of the change's actual merits.

**3. Worked Example**
Leading her team through a significant organizational restructuring, a leader communicates honestly and clearly about the rationale and expected impact, genuinely acknowledges her team's legitimate concerns rather than dismissing them, and follows through consistently on her commitments throughout the implementation process. She recognizes that her team's relatively constructive response to this significant change reflects, in part, the genuine trust she had built over time (echoing Lesson 9), which allowed her team to extend good faith during this inherently uncertain period.

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Clear, honest communication about rationale and impact, and genuine acknowledgment of legitimate concerns, are general change leadership principles.
2. A leader with genuine trust built over time generally finds their team more willing to extend good faith during change.
3. A leader's trust foundation significantly affects how their team responds to organizational change, whether constructively or with resistance.
4. The leader's genuine, previously built trust appropriately supported her team's relatively constructive response to the significant change.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Leading through organizational change requires clear communication, genuine concern acknowledgment, and consistent follow-through.
- This connects directly to Lesson 9's trust-building discussion, since prior trust affects a team's willingness to extend good faith during change.
- Change efforts led without a trust foundation may meet significantly more resistance, previewing Lesson 36's discussion.
- These principles apply this level's consistent themes to the specific, high-stakes context of organizational change.
