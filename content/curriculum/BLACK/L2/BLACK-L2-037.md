## BLACK-L2-037
### Vision and Mission: Giving a Team Something to Align Around

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish vision from mission at a conceptual level.
- Explain why a clear vision and mission support team alignment and motivation.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical team scenario.

**2. Core Content**
A mission generally describes an organization's or team's core purpose — why it exists and what it fundamentally does. A vision generally describes an aspirational future state — what the organization or team is working toward over the longer term. Together, these provide the shared purpose Lesson 32 identified as essential for transforming individuals into a genuine team, giving team members something larger than their individual daily tasks to align around and find meaning in.

A clear vision and mission support motivation and alignment particularly during difficult periods (echoing Lesson 35's change management discussion) or when individual tasks feel disconnected from larger meaning — a team member who understands how their specific daily work connects to a larger, meaningful purpose generally shows greater engagement and resilience than one who sees their work as merely a disconnected series of individual tasks with no larger context or meaning.

**3. Worked Example**
Recognizing her team members sometimes struggled to see how their individual daily tasks connected to anything larger, a leader works to clearly and consistently articulate the team's mission (its core purpose) and vision (its aspirational future direction), explicitly connecting individual contributions to this larger context in regular communications. She observes that team members who previously described their work as routine and disconnected begin expressing greater engagement and sense of meaning once this larger connection became clear and consistently reinforced.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a mission generally describe?
   a) An aspirational future state the organization is working toward
   b) An organization's or team's core purpose — why it exists and what it fundamentally does
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A concept identical to vision with no distinction
2. What does a vision generally describe, as distinguished from mission?
   a) The organization's core, present-day purpose
   b) An aspirational future state the organization or team is working toward over the longer term
   c) A concept unrelated to any future direction
   d) Only individual daily tasks
3. True or False: A clear vision and mission have no relationship to team member engagement or motivation.
4. Why did the leader's team members express greater engagement after she connected their work to a larger mission and vision?
   a) Vision and mission have no relationship to individual engagement
   b) Understanding how daily work connects to a larger, meaningful purpose generally increases engagement and resilience
   c) Individual tasks are always more motivating when disconnected from any larger context
   d) She had no interest in her team's sense of meaning or purpose

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A mission describes an organization's or team's core purpose — why it exists and what it fundamentally does.
2. A vision describes an aspirational future state the organization or team is working toward over the longer term.
3. A clear vision and mission generally support greater team member engagement and motivation, particularly during difficult periods.
4. Understanding how their work connected to a larger, meaningful purpose appropriately increased the team members' engagement and resilience.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A mission describes an organization's core purpose; a vision describes its aspirational future direction.
- Together, these provide the shared purpose Lesson 32 identified as essential for genuine team cohesion.
- A clear vision and mission support motivation and resilience, particularly during difficult periods like organizational change.
- Connecting individual daily work to this larger context generally increases engagement compared to work seen as disconnected tasks.
