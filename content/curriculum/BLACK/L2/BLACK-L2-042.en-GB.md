## BLACK-L2-042
### Managing Up: Leading Even Without Formal Authority

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define "managing up" at a conceptual level.
- Explain how Lesson 5's authority-versus-influence distinction applies to this specific context.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical managing-up scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 5's authority-versus-influence distinction, "managing up" generally refers to proactively and constructively managing one's relationship with a supervisor or higher-level leader — anticipating their needs and communication preferences, providing well-organised, relevant information proactively, and constructively raising concerns or disagreements through appropriate channels — a form of leadership and influence exercised without formal authority over the person being "managed."

This directly applies Lesson 5's core insight: a person with no formal authority over their supervisor can still exercise genuine influence through the same mechanisms this level has emphasised throughout — trust-building (Lesson 9), clear communication (Lesson 10), and understanding the other person's specific needs and perspective (echoing Lesson 11's active listening discussion) — illustrating that leadership skills apply usefully in every organisational direction, not merely downward toward those a person has formal authority over.

**3. Worked Example**
Recognising his supervisor consistently prefers concise, data-focused updates rather than lengthy narrative explanations, a team member adapts his own communication approach accordingly, proactively providing well-organised, relevant information in his supervisor's preferred format rather than expecting his supervisor to adapt to his own default communication style. He also learns to raise disagreements constructively through appropriate timing and framing, recognising this as a genuine application of the same influence-building principles this level has emphasised throughout, applied in the upward organisational direction despite having no formal authority over his supervisor.
