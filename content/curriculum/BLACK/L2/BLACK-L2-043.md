## BLACK-L2-043
### Cross-Functional Leadership: Leading Beyond Your Own Team

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general challenges of leading across functional boundaries.
- Connect this concept directly to Lesson 5's influence discussion.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical cross-functional scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 5's authority-versus-influence discussion and Lesson 42's managing-up discussion, cross-functional leadership addresses a related challenge: leading or coordinating with people from other teams or departments who do not report to the leader and may have different priorities, incentives, or working norms than the leader's own team. This requires relying almost entirely on influence rather than formal authority, since cross-functional leadership generally lacks the direct authority a leader has over their own reporting team.

Effective cross-functional leadership generally involves genuinely understanding the other team's specific priorities and constraints (echoing Lesson 11's active listening discussion), framing requests or proposals in terms of genuine mutual benefit rather than assuming the other team should simply prioritize the requesting leader's needs, and building the same kind of trust (Lesson 9) across functional boundaries that this level has emphasized throughout within a single team.

**3. Worked Example**
Needing cooperation from another department with different priorities and no reporting relationship to her, a leader takes time to genuinely understand that department's specific constraints and priorities, framing her request in terms of genuine mutual benefit rather than assuming they should simply prioritize her team's needs above their own legitimate priorities. This approach, relying on influence rather than any formal authority she does not have over that department, echoes Lesson 5's core insight applied specifically to this cross-functional context.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Cross-functional leadership involves leading or coordinating with people from other teams who do not report to the leader and may have different priorities.
2. Effective cross-functional leadership requires genuinely understanding other teams' priorities and framing requests in terms of mutual benefit.
3. Cross-functional leadership generally relies on influence rather than formal authority, since the leader lacks direct reporting relationships across functional boundaries.
4. The leader's mutual-benefit framing appropriately reflected reliance on influence rather than formal authority she did not have.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Cross-functional leadership involves leading or coordinating with people from other teams lacking formal reporting relationships.
- This relies almost entirely on influence rather than formal authority, echoing Lesson 5's core distinction.
- Effective cross-functional leadership requires genuinely understanding other teams' priorities and framing requests as mutual benefit.
- This builds trust across functional boundaries, applying this level's team-level principles more broadly across an organization.
