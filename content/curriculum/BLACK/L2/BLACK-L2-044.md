## BLACK-L2-044
### Leadership and Fiduciary Duty Revisited (BLACK Meets PURPLE)

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce PURPLE-L2-043's fiduciary duty discussion from the leadership perspective.
- Explain how fiduciary duty applies to leaders beyond formal corporate directors.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical leadership decision.

**2. Core Content**
Revisiting PURPLE-L2-043's fiduciary duty discussion — a heightened legal obligation requiring a party to act in the best interests of another party — this lesson examines how this concept, and the broader ethical principle behind it, applies to leadership more broadly, beyond the formal corporate directors PURPLE-L2-043 specifically addressed. While not every leadership position carries a formal, legally enforceable fiduciary duty, the underlying ethical principle — prioritizing the interests of those one leads and represents over one's own narrow self-interest — echoes directly through this level's stewardship theme (Lesson 8) and trust-building discussion (Lesson 9).

A leader making decisions that benefit themselves personally at their team's or organization's genuine expense — taking credit for team members' work, making decisions primarily to advance personal standing rather than genuine organizational or team benefit — violates this broader ethical principle even in contexts without a formal, legally enforceable fiduciary duty, echoing this level's consistent theme that leadership carries genuine responsibility, not merely formal legal obligation where technically applicable.

**3. Worked Example**
Considering whether to take personal credit for a project his team collectively developed, a leader recognizes this would violate the broader ethical principle underlying fiduciary duty (echoing PURPLE-L2-043), even though his specific position may not carry a formally, legally enforceable fiduciary obligation. Recognizing this connects directly to this level's stewardship theme (Lesson 8) and trust-building discussion (Lesson 9), he ensures his team receives genuine, visible credit for their collective work, prioritizing their genuine interests over his own narrow self-interest in personal recognition.
