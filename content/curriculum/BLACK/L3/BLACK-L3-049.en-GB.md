## Core Content

This track has covered delegation, incentive design, hiring, termination timing, crisis leadership, ego, risk appetite, and short-term-versus-long-term balance — genuinely different topics. This closing synthesis revisits the single question running underneath nearly all of it: what am I actually optimising for with this decision, and what is that choice costing, in a category I might not be explicitly naming?

A leader optimising for short-term results may be costing long-term organisational health (Lesson 46). A leader optimising for personal comfort by avoiding a difficult conversation may be costing team morale and eventual, larger cost (Lessons 5, 12). A leader optimising for the appearance of consensus may be costing the genuine pushback that catches errors early (Lessons 10, 11, 19). A leader optimising for their own sense of having been right may be costing the organisation's actual strategic health (Lessons 17, 39). None of these leaders were making an obviously bad decision in isolation — each was optimising for something real, just without naming the cost honestly.

Take your current top leadership priority and apply this question fully: what are you actually optimising for, and naming it honestly, what is that costing — not the obvious, visible cost, but the quieter one this track has trained you to look for?

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A leader evaluating whether to continue a demanding personal pace applies this track's full framework to the decision: what am I optimising for (proving my own indispensability, perhaps, echoing Lessons 31 and 33) and what is that costing (burnout risk and the continuity risk it creates, per Lesson 38; the key person concentration Lesson 2 warned against; the modelling of an unsustainable pace that Lesson 22 suggests will shape the whole organisation's expectations)? Naming the actual optimisation target honestly, rather than the more flattering "I'm just working hard for the mission," reveals costs this leader hadn't previously connected to a single, coherent decision.
