# Leadership vs Management Analysis: Why Doing the Right Things Differs From Doing Things Right

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once. As a widespread disruption forces businesses to rapidly rethink both what they should be doing and how efficiently they are doing it, many discover that these are genuinely two separate questions, requiring two genuinely different kinds of organizational attention.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the classic distinction between leadership (setting direction, doing the right things) and management (executing efficiently, doing things right).
- Explain why an organization genuinely needs both functions, and why strength in one does not substitute for a deficiency in the other.
- Explain why the same individual can, and often must, exercise both leadership and management functions, even though the two require different mental modes.
- Explain why an organization excelling at management but lacking genuine leadership can become highly efficient at pursuing the wrong direction.

## Core Content
VERIFIED FACT: a long-standing distinction in organizational theory separates leadership (the function of setting direction, establishing vision, and determining what an organization should actually be doing) from management (the function of executing that direction efficiently, coordinating resources, and ensuring things actually get done as planned) — this distinction is commonly summarized as leadership being about doing the right things, while management is about doing things right, two genuinely different questions that require genuinely different kinds of organizational attention.

Here is the "why" an organization genuinely needs both functions, and why strength in one does not substitute for a deficiency in the other: an organization with excellent management but no genuine leadership can become highly efficient at executing a plan, while lacking any clear, well-considered sense of whether that plan is actually the right one to pursue in the first place, while an organization with visionary leadership but poor management can have an inspiring, well-considered direction that simply never gets executed effectively enough to actually achieve anything, meaning genuine organizational success generally requires both functions operating together, rather than either one alone being sufficient.

This is why the same individual can, and often must, exercise both leadership and management functions, even though the two require different mental modes: a single manager or executive is frequently called upon to both set direction for their own specific area of responsibility (a leadership function) and to ensure that direction is actually executed efficiently (a management function), meaning an individual in this kind of role must genuinely develop both sets of skills and consciously shift between these two different modes of thinking, rather than assuming that being skilled at one function automatically confers skill at the other. This is why an organization excelling at management but lacking genuine leadership can become highly efficient at pursuing the wrong direction: OPINION and a widely observed organizational risk, an organization with strong management systems and processes can continue efficiently executing an established plan long after the underlying assumptions behind that plan have become outdated or mistaken, precisely because strong management systems are specifically designed to maintain consistent, efficient execution rather than to periodically question the underlying direction itself, meaning genuine leadership attention to whether the organization is still pursuing the right direction remains necessary even, and perhaps especially, within an organization whose management systems are otherwise operating quite smoothly and efficiently.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A business with highly efficient internal processes continues executing its established business plan with excellent operational discipline, even as a widespread disruption fundamentally changes customer needs in ways the original plan never anticipated. The business's strong management ensures every existing process continues running smoothly, but no one within the organization has taken on the genuine leadership function of asking whether the underlying direction itself still makes sense given the changed circumstances, illustrating precisely why excellent management without genuine leadership attention can produce an organization that is highly efficient at pursuing an increasingly outdated direction.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** This distinction between setting direction (leadership) and executing efficiently (management) is precisely the classic framework this lesson describes.

**Q2.** This recognition that each function addresses a genuinely different organizational need is precisely why both are necessary together, rather than either alone being sufficient.

**Q3.** This frequent dual requirement within a single role is precisely why individuals must develop and consciously shift between both leadership and management modes of thinking.

**Q4.** This specific design purpose of management systems — efficient execution rather than direction-questioning — is precisely why genuine leadership attention remains necessary regardless of how well management itself is functioning.

**Q5.** This direct illustration of efficient execution without genuine leadership direction-questioning exemplifies precisely why this lesson distinguishes leadership from management as two genuinely separate, both-necessary organizational functions.

## Key Takeaways
- Leadership sets direction (doing the right things), while management executes efficiently (doing things right) — two genuinely distinct organizational functions.
- An organization needs both functions together, since strength in either one alone does not substitute for a deficiency in the other.
- The same individual often must exercise both functions within a single role, requiring genuine development of both skill sets.
- Strong management without genuine leadership attention can produce an organization highly efficient at pursuing an increasingly outdated or mistaken direction.
