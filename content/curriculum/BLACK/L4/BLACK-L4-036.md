# Team Charter and Norms Analysis: Why Explicit Norms Outperform Assumed, Unstated Expectations

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once. As a widespread disruption brings together team members with genuinely different backgrounds and prior working experiences, teams that had explicitly articulated their own shared norms and expectations function considerably more smoothly than teams that had simply assumed everyone shared the same unstated expectations regarding how the team would actually operate.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why leaving a team's working norms and expectations unstated, assumed to be shared, risks genuine misalignment once team members' actual individual assumptions turn out to differ.
- Explain why explicitly articulating a team's shared norms — regarding communication expectations, decision-making processes, and other working practices — reduces this genuine misalignment risk.
- Explain why a team charter or explicit norms document should be developed collaboratively with genuine team input, rather than being unilaterally dictated by the leader alone.
- Explain why explicit norms should be periodically revisited and updated, rather than treated as a permanently fixed document established once and never subsequently reconsidered.

## Core Content
VERIFIED FACT: leaving a team's working norms and expectations unstated, simply assumed to be shared among all team members, risks genuine misalignment once individual team members' own actual, unstated assumptions turn out to differ from one another, since different individuals frequently bring genuinely different prior experiences and genuinely different implicit assumptions regarding matters such as expected response time to a message, how decisions should actually be made, or what constitutes acceptable versus unacceptable working behavior, meaning a team that has never explicitly discussed and aligned on these specific expectations frequently discovers, often only after a specific instance of friction or misunderstanding has already occurred, that individual team members had actually been operating under genuinely different, unstated assumptions all along.

Here is the "why" explicitly articulating a team's shared norms reduces this genuine misalignment risk: a team that deliberately, explicitly discusses and documents its actual shared expectations regarding communication practices, decision-making processes, and other genuinely important working norms establishes a genuine, shared, explicit reference point that team members can actually consult and rely upon, rather than each team member operating according to their own individually assumed, unstated expectations that may or may not actually align with other team members' own different assumptions, meaning explicit norm articulation specifically prevents the kind of after-the-fact discovery of misaligned assumptions that frequently produces genuine team friction and misunderstanding.

This is why a team charter or explicit norms document should be developed collaboratively with genuine team input, rather than being unilaterally dictated by the leader alone: echoing this level's Lesson 9 change management discussion regarding genuine buy-in mattering more than a purely top-down mandate, a set of team norms genuinely developed with meaningful input from the actual team members who will need to follow them tends to produce considerably stronger genuine buy-in and actual adherence than a comparable set of norms simply dictated unilaterally by the team's leader with no meaningful team input into their actual development, meaning sound team charter development generally involves genuine, collaborative discussion among team members, rather than the leader alone determining and then simply announcing the team's specific norms. This is why explicit norms should be periodically revisited and updated, rather than treated as a permanently fixed document: since a team's own composition, its specific working context, and its genuine collective experience with its initially established norms all naturally continue evolving over time, a team's explicit norms document established once and never subsequently reconsidered risks becoming genuinely outdated or poorly fitted to the team's own current, actual circumstances, meaning sound team charter practice generally involves periodically revisiting and, where genuinely warranted, updating the team's explicit norms, rather than treating the document as a permanently fixed artifact established once at the team's original formation and never subsequently reconsidered as circumstances genuinely evolve.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A newly formed team, bringing together members with genuinely different prior working experiences during a widespread disruption, deliberately holds an explicit discussion establishing shared norms regarding expected communication response times, how decisions will actually be made, and other genuinely important working practices, developing this charter collaboratively with meaningful input from every team member rather than the leader simply dictating these norms unilaterally. The team also establishes a specific point for periodically revisiting these norms as the team's own circumstances evolve. This explicit, collaboratively developed, periodically revisited charter helps the team avoid the kind of friction that frequently arises when team members operate under genuinely different, unstated assumptions regarding these same important working practices.

## Key Takeaways
- Leaving a team's working norms unstated and assumed to be shared risks genuine misalignment once individual team members' actual assumptions turn out to differ.
- Explicitly articulating shared norms establishes a genuine, shared reference point, preventing the after-the-fact discovery of misaligned individual assumptions.
- A team charter should be developed collaboratively with genuine team input, echoing the broader principle that genuine buy-in produces stronger adherence than a top-down mandate.
- Explicit norms should be periodically revisited and updated, since a team's composition and context naturally continue evolving over time.
