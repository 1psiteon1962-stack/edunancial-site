# Selling the Company: Orchestrating Valuation, Tax, Legal Structure, and Team Futures

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): An owner of a profitable digital marketing agency receives interest from two buyers: a large competitor offering a higher price paid partly over time, and a private investor offering a lower price paid in cash at closing.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the main drivers of business value that buyers evaluate.
- Explain how deal structure — cash, payments over time, and earnouts — changes the real value of an offer.
- Explain why tax and legal terms can matter as much as the headline price.
- Apply an orchestrated evaluation that includes the futures of employees and customers.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: The price is not the deal. Two offers with different headline numbers can be worth very different amounts once structure, tax, risk, and obligations are considered.

Buyers value businesses on reliable, transferable earnings. Value is usually higher when revenue is recurring, customers are diversified, systems are documented, and the business does not depend on the owner personally. Preparing for a sale often means spending one to three years strengthening these drivers.

Deal structure changes real value. Cash at closing is certain. Payments over time depend on the buyer's ability and willingness to pay. Earnouts tie part of the price to future performance, which the seller may no longer control after the sale. A smaller certain payment can be worth more than a larger uncertain one.

Tax consequences, covered in the GREEN track, depend on how the deal is structured and on the seller's jurisdiction; what the seller keeps after tax is what matters. Legal terms, covered in PURPLE, include representations and warranties, indemnities, non-compete agreements, and how long the seller must stay involved. These can create obligations long after closing.

People matter too. Employees, customers, and the owner's reputation are affected by who buys the business. Many sellers care about whether the team will be treated well and whether customers will continue to be served. These preferences are legitimate and can be negotiated, such as through retention bonuses or commitments about the team.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The agency owner asks advisors to compare both offers after tax and after risk. The competitor's higher price includes a large earnout based on client retention that the owner would no longer control, plus a three-year non-compete and a two-year employment requirement. The investor's lower cash offer, after tax, produces almost the same certain amount and keeps the team intact under its current managers. The owner negotiates a modest retention bonus pool for staff and accepts the investor's offer. The decision was based on certain after-tax value and the team's future, not on the largest headline number.

## Practice Quiz

A) The owner's personal relationships only
B) Reliable, transferable earnings that do not depend on the owner personally
C) The age of the company
D) The size of the office

**Q2. Why can a smaller cash offer be worth more than a larger offer?**

**Q3. What is an earnout?**
A) A tax on the sale price
B) A fee paid to advisors
C) A loan from the buyer to the seller
D) A portion of the price tied to the business's future performance after the sale

**Q4. Why do legal terms matter after closing?**
A) They expire immediately at closing
B) They only affect the buyer
C) Warranties, indemnities, and non-competes can create obligations long after the sale
D) They only matter in very large sales

**Q5. In the example, why did the owner choose the investor's offer?**
A) It had the highest headline price
B) It produced similar certain after-tax value and kept the team intact
C) It required the longest non-compete
D) It included the largest earnout

## Answer Key
Q1: B
Q2: A
Q3: D
Q4: C
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Earnings that continue after the owner leaves are what a buyer is really purchasing.

**Q2.** Uncertain or conditional payments are worth less than their face value.

**Q3.** An earnout makes part of the price depend on future results.

**Q4.** Post-closing obligations can limit the seller's future and create financial exposure.

**Q5.** Certain after-tax value and the team's future outweighed a larger but riskier headline price.

## Key Takeaways
- The headline price is not the deal; compare offers after structure, tax, and risk.
- Recurring revenue, diversified customers, and owner independence increase value.
- Earnouts and payments over time carry risk the seller may not control.
- Legal terms and the team's future deserve as much attention as price.
