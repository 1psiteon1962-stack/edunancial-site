# Debt and Leverage Decisions: Orchestrating Growth, Risk, Covenants, and Personal Guarantees

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A trucking company can expand its fleet by borrowing heavily at a moderate interest rate. Demand is strong now, but the owner remembers previous years when fuel prices rose and shipping volumes fell.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how leverage can increase both returns and risk.
- Identify common loan conditions, including covenants and guarantees.
- Explain why downside planning is essential before borrowing.
- Apply an orchestrated borrowing decision.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Debt magnifies results. When investments earn more than the cost of borrowing, leverage increases returns. When results disappoint, fixed payments remain, increasing the risk of financial distress.

Leaders should assess repayment capacity under realistic and difficult conditions. A loan that is affordable in a strong year may become dangerous in a weak one. Stress testing — modeling lower revenue, higher costs, or rising interest rates — reveals how much debt the business can safely carry.

Loan agreements often include covenants: requirements such as maintaining certain financial ratios or limiting additional borrowing. Breaking covenants can allow lenders to demand changes or repayment. Personal guarantees (PURPLE and GREEN) place the owner's personal assets at risk.

The purpose of borrowing matters. Debt used for productive assets that generate cash is generally safer than debt used to cover ongoing losses.

Leaders can reduce risk by borrowing in stages, matching repayment schedules to asset life, maintaining cash reserves, and negotiating flexible terms.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The trucking owner models a scenario with twenty percent lower volume and higher fuel costs. Under that scenario, borrowing for the full expansion would leave little room for error. The owner instead finances half the trucks now, keeps a cash reserve, negotiates covenant terms with more flexibility, and limits the personal guarantee. After one year of strong performance, the company finances additional trucks from a stronger position.

B) It guarantees approval

D) It is only needed after default

**Q4. Which use of debt is generally safer?**
A) Covering ongoing losses

A) Borrowed for the full expansion immediately

Q3: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Fixed obligations amplify both gains and losses.

**Q2.** Covenants are conditions the borrower must meet.

**Q3.** Testing difficult scenarios reveals hidden risk.

**Q4.** Assets that produce cash support repayment.

**Q5.** Staged borrowing and reserves preserved flexibility.

## Key Takeaways
- Debt magnifies both success and failure.
- Stress test repayment under difficult conditions.
- Understand covenants and personal guarantees before signing.
- Borrow in stages and keep reserves to reduce risk.
