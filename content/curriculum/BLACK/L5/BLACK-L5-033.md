# Measurement and Rewards: Orchestrating What Leaders Track With What They Recognize

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A call center measures employees mainly by how quickly they end calls. Average call time improves, but customer complaints and repeat calls rise.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why measurement shapes behavior.
- Identify characteristics of useful performance indicators.
- Explain common measurement traps.
- Apply an orchestrated approach connecting metrics, strategy, and rewards.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: What leaders measure and reward tends to become what people focus on. Good measures guide behavior toward strategy; poor measures can produce unintended consequences.

Useful indicators are connected to goals, understandable, timely, and difficult to manipulate. Most organizations need a balanced set: financial results, customer outcomes, operational quality, and people measures.

Common traps include measuring only activity rather than outcomes, focusing on one number while ignoring others, and changing measures too often.

Recognition and rewards must align with the measures. If leaders talk about customer service but reward only speed, employees will follow the reward.

Dashboards help leaders track trends and compare results across teams, countries, or products. They should support conversations and decisions, not replace judgment.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The call center replaces its single speed measure with a balanced set: first-call resolution, customer satisfaction, call time, and quality review scores. Team recognition now highlights employees who solve problems completely. Repeat calls decrease and customer satisfaction improves, while call time remains acceptable.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. Why does measurement shape behavior?**
A) Measures are always ignored
B) Employees never see metrics
C) People tend to focus on what is measured and rewarded
D) Measurement has no effect

**Q2. What makes an indicator useful?**
A) It changes every week
B) It is connected to goals and difficult to manipulate
C) It measures only activity
D) It is impossible to understand

**Q3. What is a common measurement trap?**
A) Focusing on one number while ignoring others
B) Using a balanced set of measures
C) Linking metrics to goals
D) Reviewing trends

**Q4. Why should rewards align with measures?**
A) Rewards never affect behavior
B) Metrics should be secret
C) Rewards replace strategy
D) Misalignment sends conflicting signals

**Q5. What change improved the call center?**
A) Measuring only speed
B) Removing all measures
C) Using balanced measures including resolution and satisfaction
D) Increasing call speed requirements only

## Answer Key
Q1: C
Q2: B
Q3: A
Q4: D
Q5: C

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Measurement signals priorities.

**Q2.** Good measures reflect real goals.

**Q3.** Narrow focus creates distortions.

**Q4.** Rewards must reinforce the same priorities.

**Q5.** Balanced metrics encouraged better outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Measurement shapes behavior.
- Use balanced, goal-connected indicators.
- Avoid single-metric traps.
- Align recognition and rewards with strategy.
