# Strategic Pivots: Orchestrating Evidence, Sunk Costs, Team Belief, and Timing

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A startup spent two years building a product for large corporations, but customers instead show strong interest in a simpler version for small businesses.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain when a strategic pivot may be necessary.
- Explain the sunk cost trap.
- Identify evidence that supports or challenges a pivot.
- Apply an orchestrated approach to leading change.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Strategies are hypotheses about how an organization will succeed. When evidence shows a different path is more promising, leaders may need to change direction.

The sunk cost trap occurs when leaders continue a strategy because of past investment rather than future potential.

Evidence should guide decisions: customer behavior, sales results, profitability, market trends, and competitive position.

Pivots affect people deeply. Leaders must explain why the change is needed, what remains the same, and how the team's experience still matters.

Timing matters. Pivoting too early wastes opportunities; pivoting too late can exhaust resources.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The startup tests a small-business version with a limited group of customers. Sales and retention exceed the original product. Leadership explains the evidence to employees and shifts development toward the new market while preserving key technology. Revenue grows faster than before.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What is a strategic pivot?**
A) A significant change in direction based on evidence
B) A temporary office move
C) A price increase only
D) A hiring freeze

**Q2. What is the sunk cost trap?**
A) Investing too little
B) Ignoring customers
C) Paying taxes late
D) Continuing because of past investment rather than future potential

**Q3. What should guide pivot decisions?**
A) Emotion alone
B) Past investment only
C) Evidence such as customer behavior and profitability
D) Competitors' opinions only

**Q4. Why must leaders communicate carefully during a pivot?**
A) Communication is unnecessary
B) Changes affect employees' work and confidence
C) Employees never notice changes
D) Pivots are always easy

**Q5. In the example, what supported the pivot?**
A) Strong sales and retention from the new version
B) Personal preference only
C) A competitor's advice
D) Random selection

## Answer Key
Q1: A
Q2: D
Q3: C
Q4: B
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** A pivot changes strategy based on evidence.

**Q2.** Past costs should not determine future decisions.

**Q3.** Evidence reduces bias.

**Q4.** Clear communication supports trust.

**Q5.** Market results justified the change.

## Key Takeaways
- Treat strategy as a testable hypothesis.
- Avoid the sunk cost trap.
- Use evidence to guide pivots.
- Lead people through change with clarity and respect.
