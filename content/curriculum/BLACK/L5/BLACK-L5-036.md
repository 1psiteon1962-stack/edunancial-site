# Mission and Profit: Orchestrating Social Impact With Financial Sustainability

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A financial education company wants to offer free courses to every low-income family in its region. The founder believes deeply in the mission, but the company is not yet profitable and has six months of cash remaining.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why financial sustainability is a requirement for long-term impact.
- Identify ways organizations connect mission and revenue.
- Explain how to evaluate decisions that trade impact against profit.
- Apply an orchestrated approach that protects both mission and survival.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: A mission that bankrupts the organization ends the mission. Sustainable impact requires a financial model that can fund the work year after year.

Many organizations connect mission and revenue through tiered pricing (customers who can pay support those who cannot), sponsorships and partnerships with employers or institutions, grants for specific programs, or a free basic offering combined with paid advanced services. Each model has tradeoffs in stability, independence, and administrative effort.

When a decision trades impact for profit, leaders can ask: How many people does this help, and how deeply? What does it cost, and who pays? Does it strengthen or weaken our ability to help more people later? Is there a way to achieve most of the impact at a sustainable cost?

Transparency builds trust. Customers, partners, and employees respect organizations that explain how they balance mission and money.

Measuring impact matters as much as measuring profit. Organizations that track who they serve and what changes for those people can make better decisions and attract mission-aligned support.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The education company offers its first level of lessons free to everyone and asks employers and community organizations to sponsor full access for low-income families. Paid memberships for individuals fund course development. Within a year, sponsorships cover thousands of free learners, paid memberships cover operating costs, and the company publishes a simple impact report showing completion rates. The mission grows because the model is sustainable.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. Why is financial sustainability essential for a mission-driven organization?**
A) Profit is the only goal
B) Missions do not require money
C) Sustainability prevents impact
D) A mission that bankrupts the organization ends the mission

**Q2. What is tiered pricing in this context?**
A) Charging everyone the same high price
B) Offering nothing for free
C) Customers who can pay support access for those who cannot
D) Changing prices daily

**Q3. Which question helps evaluate an impact-versus-profit decision?**
A) How can we hide costs?
B) Does this strengthen or weaken our ability to help more people later?
C) Which customers can we ignore?
D) How can we avoid measuring results?

**Q4. Why should organizations measure impact as well as profit?**
A) It supports better decisions and attracts mission-aligned support
B) Impact cannot be measured
C) Profit is irrelevant
D) Measurement is legally prohibited

**Q5. In the example, who funded access for low-income families?**
A) The founder personally
B) Government taxes only
C) No one
D) Employer and community sponsors

## Answer Key
Q1: D
Q2: C
Q3: B
Q4: A
Q5: D

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Without a sustainable model, the organization cannot continue its work.

**Q2.** Paying customers help subsidize those who cannot pay.

**Q3.** Long-term capacity to help matters as much as immediate impact.

**Q4.** Impact data improves decisions and builds credibility.

**Q5.** Sponsors funded access while paid memberships covered operations.

## Key Takeaways
- Sustainable finances keep a mission alive.
- Connect mission and revenue through tiered pricing, sponsorships, grants, or free-and-paid models.
- Evaluate decisions by their effect on long-term capacity to help.
- Measure and communicate impact transparently.
