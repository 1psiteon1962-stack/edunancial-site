# Inflation and Currency Shocks: Orchestrating Pricing, Costs, Contracts, and Reserves

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A Caribbean importer buys products priced in US dollars and sells them locally. The local currency weakens sharply, increasing costs almost overnight.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how inflation and currency changes affect organizations.
- Identify ways to manage rising costs.
- Explain how contracts and pricing policies reduce risk.
- Apply an orchestrated response to economic shocks.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Inflation and currency changes affect costs, pricing, customer demand, and cash flow simultaneously.

Leaders can reduce risk by reviewing supplier contracts, diversifying suppliers, adjusting pricing policies, managing inventory carefully, and maintaining reserves.

Currency risk affects organizations that buy or sell across borders. Matching revenue and costs in the same currency, negotiating price adjustment clauses, and using financial tools where appropriate can reduce exposure (WHITE).

Communication with customers matters when prices must change. Transparent explanations maintain trust.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The importer negotiates price adjustment clauses with major customers, finds a regional supplier for some products, and reduces excess inventory. Prices are updated monthly rather than daily, with clear explanations. Margins stabilize despite continued currency volatility.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Economic shocks influence many areas at once.

**Q2.** Matching currencies reduces exposure.

**Q3.** Adjustment clauses share risk fairly.

**Q4.** Transparency builds trust.

**Q5.** Multiple coordinated actions reduced risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Inflation and currency changes affect the whole organization.
- Reduce exposure through contracts, suppliers, and currency matching.
- Manage inventory and maintain reserves.
- Communicate price changes clearly.
