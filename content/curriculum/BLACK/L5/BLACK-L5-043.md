# Leading With Data and Judgment: Orchestrating Analysis With Experience and Intuition

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Sales data suggests closing a store location, but the manager believes a new community development nearby will soon increase customer traffic.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the strengths and limitations of data.
- Explain the role of experience and judgment.
- Identify common decision biases.
- Apply an orchestrated decision process.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Data improves decisions, but it rarely tells the whole story.

Data reveals patterns, trends, and performance. However, it may be incomplete, outdated, or unable to capture future changes.

Experience and judgment help interpret data and recognize factors not yet visible in numbers.

Common biases include confirmation bias (favoring information that supports existing beliefs), overconfidence, and recency bias.

Leaders improve decisions by combining data with diverse perspectives, testing assumptions, and reviewing results afterward.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Leadership delays closing the store for six months while tracking construction progress and customer traffic. Data from the new development shows growing demand, and the location becomes profitable.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Data reflects the past and present.

**Q2.** Judgment interprets context.

**Q3.** Bias distorts decisions.

**Q4.** Multiple inputs reduce errors.

**Q5.** New evidence informed the decision.

## Key Takeaways
- Data is powerful but incomplete.
- Judgment interprets context and change.
- Watch for common biases.
- Combine analysis, perspectives, and review.
