# Decisive Commitment: Orchestrating Preparation, Timing, and Full Commitment When the Moment Arrives

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A regional company learns that a major competitor is exiting the market. Customers immediately begin searching for new suppliers.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why preparation enables decisive action.
- Explain the importance of timing.
- Explain why partial commitment can fail.
- Apply an orchestrated commitment strategy.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Opportunities often appear suddenly. Organizations that have prepared resources, leadership, and plans can respond quickly.

Preparation includes financial reserves, scalable operations, trained teams, and clear decision rights.

Timing matters. Acting too slowly allows competitors to capture opportunities.

Partial commitment can be costly. Moving forward without enough resources may produce weak execution.

Decisive leadership combines speed with disciplined preparation.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The company activates an expansion plan prepared months earlier, hires additional staff, contacts the competitor's former customers, and expands production. Within one year, it becomes the market leader.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Prepared organizations can act faster.

**Q2.** Opportunities are often time-sensitive.

**Q3.** Commitment requires resources.

**Q4.** Decisiveness should be disciplined.

**Q5.** Advance planning enabled rapid action.

## Key Takeaways
- Preparation enables decisive action.
- Timing often determines success.
- Commit enough resources to execute well.
- Combine speed with discipline.
