# Reassessment and Exit: Orchestrating the Decision to Stop, Retreat, or Change Course

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A company expanded into a new country three years ago, but losses continue despite repeated investment.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why reassessment is part of good leadership.
- Explain the value of predefined exit criteria.
- Identify signals that a change may be needed.
- Apply an orchestrated exit decision.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Not every initiative should continue forever. Leaders must recognize when resources would be better used elsewhere.

Predefined exit criteria help reduce emotional decision-making.

Signals include sustained losses, weak customer demand, strategic misalignment, or better alternatives.

Exiting responsibly includes protecting customers, employees, partners, and reputation.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The company reviews its original targets, concludes the market is unlikely to become profitable, transfers customers to a reliable partner, and redeploys resources to stronger markets. Overall profitability improves.

## Answer Key
Q1: A
Q2: D
Q3: C
Q4: B
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Resources should support the best opportunities.

**Q2.** Clear criteria improve objectivity.

**Q3.** Persistent losses are a warning sign.

**Q4.** Responsible exits protect trust.

**Q5.** Resources moved to stronger opportunities.

## Key Takeaways
- Reassessment is part of strong leadership.
- Use predefined criteria.
- Watch for warning signals.
- Exit responsibly when necessary.
