# Courage and Consequence Ownership: Orchestrating Calculated Risk With Accountability

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): A CEO approves a new product launch after careful analysis, but it underperforms expectations.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain calculated risk.
- Explain why leaders must own consequences.
- Explain how organizations learn from outcomes.
- Apply an orchestrated accountability approach.

## Core Content
PRINCIPLE: Leadership requires taking risks. Calculated risk means understanding possible outcomes, preparing for them, and acting deliberately.

Leaders must own the consequences of their decisions rather than blaming others.

Organizations improve by reviewing both successful and unsuccessful decisions.

A culture of accountability encourages thoughtful risk-taking and continuous learning.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The CEO publicly takes responsibility, leads a review of the launch, identifies lessons, and uses them to improve the next product. Employees remain willing to propose new ideas.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What is calculated risk?**
A) Taking random chances
B) Avoiding all risk
C) Ignoring consequences
D) Acting deliberately after understanding possible outcomes

**Q2. Why should leaders own consequences?**
A) It eliminates mistakes
B) It prevents decisions
C) It builds trust and accountability
D) It shifts blame

**Q3. Why review unsuccessful decisions?**
A) To assign blame
B) To learn and improve
C) To avoid future risks entirely
D) To hide results

**Q4. What does a culture of accountability encourage?**
A) Thoughtful risk-taking and learning
B) Fear of mistakes
C) Avoidance of new ideas
D) Blame

**Q5. How did the CEO respond in the example?**
A) Blamed employees
B) Ignored the results
C) Cancelled all future projects
D) Took responsibility and led a learning review

## Answer Key
Q1: D
Q2: C
Q3: B
Q4: A
Q5: D

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Calculated risk combines preparation and action.

**Q2.** Ownership creates credibility.

**Q3.** Learning improves future decisions.

**Q4.** Accountability supports innovation.

**Q5.** Responsibility and learning preserved trust.

## Key Takeaways
- Leadership requires calculated risk.
- Own the consequences of decisions.
- Learn from success and failure.
- Build accountable, learning cultures.
