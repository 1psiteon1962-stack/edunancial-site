# Every Business Sells One of Two Things: Product, Service, or Both

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define product and service in business terms.
- Classify a real business as product-based, service-based, or blended.
- Apply this lens to a business you're personally familiar with.

## Core Content

### Two Questions to Ask About Any Business

When you look at a business, ask: what does the customer walk away
with — something they can hold onto, or something someone did for them?
A **product** is a thing handed over. A **service** is an action, effort,
or expertise performed for the customer.

### Why Most Businesses Are a Blend

Think about a restaurant. Is it selling food, or selling the experience
of being served? Both — the food is the product, being seated and
attended to is the service. Very few real businesses land purely on one
side.

### Applying This to Financial Education

Consider a platform that teaches financial literacy. What's the
product? What's the service? The service is the teaching itself —
guiding understanding, building competency. The product is the tangible
deliverable — the lessons, the assessments, the structured exercises.
Naming these separately, instead of just saying "we teach people," makes
it much clearer what's actually being built.

## Worked Example

Picture a furniture maker who sells chairs — that's the product. Now
picture the same maker offering custom furniture repair — that's a
service layered onto a product business. Ask yourself: if that repair
service grew large enough, would the business still be "a furniture
maker," or would it have quietly become something else?

## Practice Quiz

1. A product is best defined as:
   A. An action performed for a customer
   B. A tangible or digital thing a customer receives and keeps
   C. A company's marketing plan
   D. A type of business license

2. True or False: Most real businesses are purely product-based or
   purely service-based, with little blending.

3. In the financial education example, what counts as the "service"?
   A. The lessons and exercises themselves
   B. The teaching and guidance itself
   C. The company's logo
   D. The pricing structure

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A product is a tangible or digital thing handed over
to the customer.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Most real businesses blend both to some degree.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. The teaching/guidance is the service; the lessons
themselves are the product.

## Key Takeaways

- A product is handed over; a service is done for someone.
- Most real businesses blend both, not purely one or the other.
- Naming the product and service parts of any business clarifies what's
  actually being delivered.
