# What Is a Sole Proprietorship?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a sole proprietorship in plain terms.
- Explain how it's formed.
- Explain the biggest legal risk unique to this structure.

## Core Content

### The Simplest Structure There Is

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and run by one person, with no
legal separation between the owner and the business. No separate legal
entity exists — the business simply is the person, in the eyes of the
law.

### How It's Formed

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: laws vary by country.)* In many places, no
formal paperwork is required — if someone starts doing business under
their own name with no other structure, they're automatically a sole
proprietor. A local license or a "doing business as" filing may still
apply.

### The Real Risk: Ask Yourself This

If this business gets sued, or can't pay a debt, whose money is on the
line? Because there's no legal separation between the person and the
business, the answer is: the owner's personal bank account, car,
potentially their home. That's the single biggest trade-off of this
structure — and exactly why later lessons introduce LLCs and
corporations, built specifically to change that answer.

## Worked Example

A freelance photographer books clients under her own name — no LLC, no
paperwork beyond a basic license. She's a sole proprietor by default.
Now imagine her equipment fails and damages a client's property. Ask
yourself: whose assets could a lawsuit reach? Because there's no
separation, it's hers personally — not just "the business's."

## Practice Quiz

1. A sole proprietorship is best defined as:
   A. A business with multiple legal owners
   B. A business with no legal separation between owner and business
   C. A business that must file incorporation paperwork
   D. A business that protects personal assets from lawsuits

2. True or False: In many places, no formal paperwork is required to
   begin operating as a sole proprietorship.

3. What is the biggest legal risk of a sole proprietorship?
   A. High taxes
   B. Unlimited personal liability
   C. Limited operating hours
   D. Required government approval for every sale

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. No legal separation between owner and business is the
defining feature.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Many places require no formal paperwork for the
structure itself.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Unlimited personal liability is the structure's
central risk.

## Key Takeaways

- A sole proprietorship has no legal separation between owner and
  business.
- It typically requires little to no paperwork to begin.
- Its biggest risk is unlimited personal liability.
