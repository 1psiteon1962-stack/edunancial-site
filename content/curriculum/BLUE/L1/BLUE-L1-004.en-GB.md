# What Is an LLC?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define an LLC in plain terms.
- Explain what "limited liability" actually limits.
- Explain why this changes the risk picture from a sole proprietorship.

## Core Content

### What LLC Stands For, and What It Does

LLC stands for Limited Liability Company. Ask yourself: what's the one
problem from the last lesson this structure exists to solve? Unlimited
personal liability. An LLC creates a legal separation between the owner
(called a "member") and the business — the business becomes its own
legal entity.

### What "Limited" Actually Limits

If an LLC is sued or can't pay a debt, the member's personal assets are
generally protected — the business's own assets are what's at risk, not
the owner's house or personal savings, *provided* the owner follows the
state's required formalities (keeping business and personal finances
separate, proper filings, and so on).

### The Catch Worth Knowing Now

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: formation and formality requirements vary by
state/country.)* That protection isn't automatic or unconditional — it
depends on actually treating the LLC as a separate entity in practice,
not just on paper. Later lessons in this track cover what those
formalities look like.

## Worked Example

Compare the freelance photographer from the last lesson to a version of
herself who's formed an LLC. Same equipment failure, same lawsuit. Ask
yourself: what's different this time? If she's followed the LLC's
formalities, the claim generally reaches the business's assets — not her
personal ones. Same event, different legal exposure, purely because of
structure.

## Practice Quiz

1. LLC stands for:
   A. Limited Liability Corporation
   B. Limited Liability Company
   C. Legal License Certificate
   D. Local Licensed Contractor

2. True or False: An LLC's liability protection is automatic and applies
   no matter what the owner does.

3. What does an LLC primarily protect, compared to a sole
   proprietorship?
   A. The business's tax rate
   B. The owner's personal assets from business debts and lawsuits
   C. The business's marketing budget
   D. The owner's employees

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. LLC stands for Limited Liability Company.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Protection depends on following required
formalities, not automatic just from forming the LLC.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Personal asset protection is the core benefit over a
sole proprietorship.

## Key Takeaways

- An LLC creates legal separation between owner and business.
- This generally protects personal assets from business debts and
  lawsuits.
- That protection depends on following the required formalities, not
  just filing paperwork once.
