# What Is a Corporation?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a corporation in plain terms.
- Explain what it means to say a corporation is its own legal "person."
- Explain one practical implication of that separate legal status.

## Core Content

### A Legal Person of Its Own

A corporation is a business structure that creates a legal entity
entirely separate from its owners (shareholders) — more formally
separate, in many ways, than an LLC. The law treats a corporation almost
like its own "person": it can own property, enter contracts, sue and be
sued, independent of the individuals who own it.

### Why "Its Own Person" Matters

Ask yourself: if a corporation's owner dies, does the business
automatically end? Generally not — because the corporation exists as its
own legal entity, it can continue independent of any single owner's
life, unlike some simpler structures.

### A Practical Implication: Ownership Through Shares

Corporations are owned through shares, introduced back in the WHITE
track — this is the direct connection between "owning stock" and this
business structure. A corporation can have one shareholder or millions,
and shares can be bought and sold without necessarily disrupting the
underlying business.

## Worked Example

Picture a family-owned corporation whose founder passes away. Ask
yourself: does the business legally cease to exist? Because the
corporation is its own legal entity, ownership of the shares simply
transfers (through inheritance, sale, or otherwise) — the corporation
itself keeps existing, separate from any one person's life.

## Practice Quiz

1. A corporation is best described as:
   A. The same legal entity as its owners
   B. A legal entity separate from its owners
   C. Only available to large companies
   D. A type of sole proprietorship

2. True or False: A corporation generally ceases to exist if a single
   owner dies.

3. How is ownership of a corporation typically structured?
   A. Through a single, non-transferable title
   B. Through shares, which can be owned by one or many shareholders
   C. Corporations cannot be owned by anyone
   D. Ownership is determined annually by the government

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A corporation is legally separate from its owners.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The corporation generally continues independent
of any single owner's life.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Ownership is structured through shares.

## Key Takeaways

- A corporation is its own legal entity, separate from its owners.
- It can continue to exist independent of any single owner.
- Ownership is structured through shares, connecting back to the WHITE
  track.
