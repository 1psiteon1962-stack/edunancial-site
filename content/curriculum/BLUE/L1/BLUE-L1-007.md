## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Define customer acquisition cost (CAC) and customer lifetime value (LTV)
- Calculate CAC and LTV from basic business data
- Evaluate whether a business's growth economics are sustainable
- Identify the primary customer acquisition channels and their cost profiles
- Apply the LTV:CAC framework to marketing investment decisions

## Core Content

### Why Marketing Is a Financial Decision

Marketing is commonly treated as a creative function — branding, messaging, design. These matter. But the underlying question is financial: How much does it cost to acquire a customer, and how much does that customer generate over their lifetime?

A business that spends $500 to acquire a customer who pays $200 once and never returns is not growing — it is declining with increasing speed. A business that spends $500 to acquire a customer who pays $300 per month for three years has found a growth engine.

Understanding customer acquisition economics converts marketing from a cost into an investment with a measurable return.

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

**CAC = Total sales and marketing expenses ÷ Number of new customers acquired**

Include in total sales and marketing expenses:
- Advertising spend (online and offline)
- Salaries of sales and marketing employees
- Agency or contractor fees
- Software tools used for marketing and sales
- Events, trade shows, and sponsorships

Example: A business spends $30,000 per month on sales and marketing and acquires 60 new customers in that month.

CAC = $30,000 ÷ 60 = $500 per customer

CAC should be calculated separately for each acquisition channel to identify which channels are most efficient.

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

**LTV = Average revenue per customer × Average customer lifespan**

Or more precisely:

**LTV = (Average transaction value × Purchase frequency) × Average customer lifespan**

Example: A customer spends $150 per visit, visits 6 times per year, and remains a customer for an average of 3 years.

LTV = ($150 × 6) × 3 = $900 × 3 = $2,700

If the CAC for this customer is $500, then the business earns $2,200 over the customer's lifetime after recovering acquisition cost. The LTV:CAC ratio is 5.4:1.

### The LTV:CAC Ratio

The LTV:CAC ratio is the single most important metric for evaluating growth sustainability.

| Ratio | Interpretation |
|-------|----------------|
| Below 1:1 | The business loses money on every customer acquired |
| 1:1 to 2:1 | The business barely recovers acquisition costs — unsustainable |
| 3:1 | Industry benchmark for sustainable growth |
| 5:1 or higher | Strong growth economics — consider investing more in acquisition |
| Very high (10:1+) | May indicate underinvestment in marketing — growth potential not captured |

A 3:1 ratio means the business earns three dollars in lifetime value for every dollar spent acquiring a customer. After accounting for operating costs and margin, this typically supports profitable growth.

### Primary Customer Acquisition Channels

**1. Organic Search (SEO)**
Publishing content that ranks in search engines. Long-term, compounding, low marginal cost per acquisition. High initial investment in content creation; CAC decreases over time as content accumulates authority.

**2. Paid Search and Social Advertising**
Paying for placement in search results or social media feeds. Immediate results, predictable volume, but stops producing when spending stops. CAC is directly controllable through budget and targeting.

**3. Referral and Word of Mouth**
Existing customers refer new customers. The highest-quality and lowest-CAC channel. Requires delivering an experience worth sharing. Often the first acquisition channel for service businesses.

**4. Direct Outreach (Sales)**
Contacting prospects directly through calls, emails, or meetings. High labor cost per acquisition but allows precise targeting. Best for high-LTV customers in B2B markets.

**5. Partnerships and Distribution**
Reaching customers through another business's existing relationships — co-marketing, reseller agreements, platform distribution. Can rapidly expand reach with low direct cost, but requires sharing margin or revenue.

### Reducing CAC Over Time

Sustainable businesses systematically reduce their CAC:

- Build brand awareness that lowers conversion friction
- Invest in referral programs that turn customers into acquisition channels
- Improve conversion rates so the same spend generates more customers
- Identify and double down on the most efficient acquisition channels
- Build content and SEO assets that compound over time

A business with decreasing CAC and stable or increasing LTV has improving unit economics — each dollar of growth investment produces a better return over time.
