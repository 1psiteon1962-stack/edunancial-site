# Subchapter C vs. Subchapter S: What Actually Changes?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what "C corp" and "S corp" refer to.
- Explain the core tax difference between them.
- Recognize that S corp status comes with real eligibility limits.

## Core Content

### Same Legal Structure, Different Tax Treatment

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this describes U.S. federal tax law.)* Both
terms refer to the same underlying corporation structure from the last
lesson — the difference is which section of the federal tax code
applies to how the corporation is taxed.

### The Core Difference: Who Pays, and When

A C corporation generally pays corporate-level income tax on its
profits; if it then distributes dividends to shareholders, those
shareholders generally pay tax again on that income — sometimes called
"double taxation." An S corporation generally passes profit directly
through to shareholders' personal tax returns, avoiding that
corporate-level tax layer.

### Ask Yourself This

If S corp status avoids that double taxation, why doesn't every
corporation just elect it? Because S corp status comes with real
eligibility limits — restrictions on number and type of shareholders,
among other requirements — that not every corporation qualifies for or
wants.

## Worked Example

Picture two identical corporations with the same profit. One elects C
corp treatment; the other elects S corp treatment. Ask yourself: if both
distribute all their profit to shareholders, where does the tax get
paid differently? In the C corp, tax is generally paid at the corporate
level and again at the shareholder level. In the S corp, it's generally
only paid once, passed directly to the shareholders' personal returns.

## Practice Quiz

1. What do "C corp" and "S corp" refer to?
   A. Two entirely different legal business structures
   B. The same corporation structure, taxed under different sections of
      the tax code
   C. Only the size of the company
   D. A distinction that only matters for nonprofits

2. True or False: A C corporation avoids double taxation, while an S
   corporation does not.

3. Why doesn't every corporation simply elect S corp status?
   A. S corp status is illegal in most states
   B. S corp status has real eligibility limits not every corporation
      meets
   C. S corps are always taxed more
   D. There is no such thing as S corp status

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Both refer to the same structure, taxed differently
under the tax code.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. It's the reverse — C corps generally face
potential double taxation; S corps generally avoid it.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Real eligibility requirements limit who can elect S
corp status.

## Key Takeaways

- C corp and S corp refer to the same structure, taxed differently.
- C corps generally face potential double taxation; S corps generally
  pass profit through once.
- S corp status comes with real eligibility limits, not available to
  every corporation.
