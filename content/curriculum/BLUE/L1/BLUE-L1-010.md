## Core Content

### The Eight Foundations of BLUE Level 1

Over the past nine lessons you have built a foundational understanding of business as a financial discipline:

**Lesson 1 — Business as an Asset Class**
A business is an asset only when it generates cash flow that exceeds costs without requiring the owner's constant direct labor. The goal is to build a system, not a job.

**Lesson 2 — Business Models**
The model determines how value is created and how revenue is captured. Understanding your model is prerequisite to optimizing it.

**Lesson 3 — Cash Flow**
Profit and cash are not the same. Managing cash timing — accelerating inflows and managing outflows — is a survival skill.

**Lesson 4 — Profit Margins**
Gross margin, operating margin, and net margin reveal where value is created and where it is destroyed. Margin is managed, not accepted.

**Lesson 5 — Key Performance Indicators**
A business measured is a business that can be improved. A KPI dashboard converts activity into evidence and evidence into decisions.

**Lesson 6 — Pricing Strategy**
Price is the most powerful lever in business finance. A 10% price increase at 30% gross margin increases profit by 33%. Pricing decisions compound over time.

**Lesson 7 — Customer Acquisition**
CAC and LTV determine whether growth is profitable. A 3:1 or better LTV:CAC ratio is the benchmark for sustainable growth.

**Lesson 8 — Operations and Systems**
Documented systems are financial assets. Operational leverage — the ability to grow revenue without proportionate cost increases — is what separates scalable businesses from jobs.

**Lesson 9 — Business Risk**
Financial resilience requires a cash reserve, diversified revenue, appropriate insurance, and systematic risk review. Resilience is built before it is needed.

### Where to Focus First

Most business operators have uneven competency across these dimensions. They may have strong intuitions about pricing but no KPI tracking. Or they may have detailed operational documentation but no cash reserve.

The highest-impact improvement is almost always the one that addresses the area of greatest financial risk. A business with no cash reserve and unpredictable cash flow should address those first — no amount of improved KPI tracking will matter if the business runs out of money next month.

Use the assessment above to identify your lowest-rated dimension. That is where to start.

### Your 90-Day Business Competency Action Plan

The BLUE track is designed to convert knowledge into action. Each lesson ends with an application exercise because competency requires practice.

Your 90-day action plan should include:

**Days 1–30: Measure**
- Complete or update your KPI dashboard with current data
- Calculate your actual gross margin, operating margin, and net margin for the last 90 days
- Calculate your CAC and LTV
- Assess your current cash reserve against the 3-month minimum

**Days 31–60: Improve**
- Identify the single highest-impact pricing adjustment and implement it
- Document the most critical business process that is currently undocumented
- Begin tracking at least one new leading indicator KPI weekly
- Fund the cash reserve to at least 1 month of fixed expenses if below minimum

**Days 61–90: Review and Advance**
- Review all KPIs against targets set in Days 1–30
- Quantify the financial impact of at least one improvement made
- Identify the most pressing topic from BLUE Level 2 based on current business needs
- Set the 90-day targets for the next period

## Application

The application for this lesson is your 90-day action plan.

1. Complete the Business Competency Assessment above. Write your scores honestly.
2. Identify your single lowest-rated dimension. Write the one concrete action you will take in the next 30 days to improve it.
3. Set one measurable target for each of the eight dimensions. These targets will serve as your benchmark when you return to assess progress after completing BLUE Level 2.

Competency is measured by what changes in your business and in your financial decisions — not by how many lessons you complete. Apply what you have learned, return to reassess, and continue building.
