# Choosing a Business Structure: What Questions Should You Actually Ask First?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- List key questions that narrow down an appropriate business structure.
- Apply those questions to a basic hypothetical scenario.
- Recognize that this is a starting framework, not personalized advice.

## Core Content

### The Questions Worth Asking First

Before diving into legal specifics, ask: How many owners are there? How
much personal liability protection is actually needed given the
business's risk level? Is this a licensed profession with its own
structural norms? Is minimizing double taxation a priority? These
questions don't give a final answer by themselves, but they narrow the
field significantly.

### Working Through It

A solo owner with real liability concerns and no interest in raising
outside investors is often pointed toward an LLC. Multiple licensed
professionals might consider an LLP. A business planning to raise
significant outside capital, or interested in issuing many shares, is
often pointed toward a corporation — a connection to material covered
more in Level 3.

### An Important Limit

*IMPORTANT:* This lesson provides a general framework for
understanding, not personalized legal or tax advice. An actual decision
should involve a qualified professional, reviewing someone's specific
situation — the "doctor analogy" covered later in this level explains
exactly why that step is worth it.

## Worked Example

A single person starting a landscaping business, worried about liability
if equipment damages someone's property, works through the framework:
one owner, real liability concern, no immediate need to raise outside
capital. Ask yourself: which structure from this level's earlier lessons
does that combination point toward? An LLC fits that profile well.

## Practice Quiz

1. Which question is a useful starting point when choosing a business
   structure?
   A. What color should the logo be?
   B. How many owners are there, and how much liability protection is
      needed?
   C. What time of year should the business open?
   D. How many social media followers does the owner have?

2. True or False: This lesson's framework is a substitute for
   professional legal or tax advice.

3. A single owner with real liability concerns and no plans to raise
   outside capital is often pointed toward which structure?
   A. A general partnership
   B. An LLC
   C. An LLP
   D. A nonprofit

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Ownership count and liability needs are core starting
questions.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. This is a general framework, not a substitute for
professional advice.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. That profile commonly points toward an LLC.

## Key Takeaways

- Key starting questions: ownership count, liability needs, profession
  type, and capital-raising plans.
- These questions narrow the field but don't replace professional
  advice.
- This framework connects directly to the structures covered earlier in
  this level.
