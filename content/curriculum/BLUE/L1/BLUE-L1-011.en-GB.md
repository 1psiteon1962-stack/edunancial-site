# What Is Revenue vs. Profit? (The Most Confused Pair of Terms in Business)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define revenue and profit separately.
- Calculate basic profit given revenue and expenses.
- Explain why strong revenue doesn't guarantee profit.

## Core Content

### Two Numbers People Mix Up Constantly

Ask yourself: if a business "made $500,000 last year," does that mean
they kept $500,000? Almost certainly not — and this exact confusion is
why revenue and profit get mixed up so often.

**Revenue** is the total money a business brings in from sales, before
any expenses are subtracted. **Profit** is what's left after subtracting
all the expenses it took to generate that revenue.

### The Formula

Profit = Revenue − Expenses. A business with $500,000 in revenue and
$480,000 in expenses has only $20,000 in profit — a very different
picture than the headline revenue number alone suggests.

### Why Strong Revenue Doesn't Guarantee Profit

A business can have impressive, growing revenue and still be losing
money, if its expenses grow just as fast or faster. Revenue measures
activity; profit measures whether that activity actually left the
business better off.

## Worked Example

A new business proudly announces $200,000 in its first year of revenue.
Ask yourself: is that good news by itself? Only if you also know the
expenses. If expenses were $210,000, the business actually lost $10,000
that year — a loss hiding behind a headline number that sounds like
success.

## Practice Quiz

1. Revenue is:
   A. What's left after subtracting all expenses
   B. Total money brought in from sales, before expenses
   C. A business's total debt
   D. A type of tax

2. True or False: Strong revenue always means a business is profitable.

3. A business has $300,000 in revenue and $275,000 in expenses. What is
   its profit?
   A. $575,000
   B. $25,000
   C. $300,000
   D. $0

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Revenue is total sales income, before expenses.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. High revenue with even higher expenses can still
mean a loss.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $300,000 − $275,000 = $25,000.

## Key Takeaways

- Revenue is total sales income, before expenses.
- Profit = Revenue − Expenses — what's actually left over.
- Strong revenue does not guarantee profit.
