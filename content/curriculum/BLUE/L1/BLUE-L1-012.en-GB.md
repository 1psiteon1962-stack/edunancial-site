# What Is a Profit Margin?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- State the profit margin formula.
- Calculate a basic profit margin.
- Explain why margin matters for comparing businesses of different
  sizes.

## Core Content

### The Formula

Profit Margin = Profit ÷ Revenue, usually expressed as a percentage. It
takes the profit concept from the last lesson and expresses it relative
to revenue, not as a raw dollar figure.

### Why a Percentage, Not Just a Dollar Amount

Ask yourself: is $50,000 in profit impressive? It depends entirely on
how much revenue it took to generate that profit. $50,000 profit on
$100,000 revenue (a 50% margin) is a very different business than
$50,000 profit on $2,000,000 revenue (a 2.5% margin) — the percentage
tells a story the raw number alone hides.

### What It's Useful For

Profit margin lets you compare businesses of very different sizes on
equal footing, and it's a useful way to track whether a single business
is becoming more or less efficient over time, independent of whether its
revenue is growing.

## Worked Example

Two businesses each report $40,000 in profit. Business A had $100,000
in revenue; Business B had $800,000 in revenue. Ask yourself: are these
equally strong results? Business A's margin is 40%; Business B's is 5%
— very different levels of efficiency hiding behind the identical profit
number.

## Practice Quiz

1. Profit margin is calculated as:
   A. Revenue ÷ Profit
   B. Profit ÷ Revenue
   C. Expenses ÷ Revenue
   D. Revenue − Expenses

2. True or False: A higher raw profit dollar amount always means a more
   efficient business.

3. A business has $60,000 in profit and $300,000 in revenue. What is its
   profit margin?
   A. 5%
   B. 20%
   C. 50%
   D. 60%

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Profit Margin = Profit ÷ Revenue.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A large business can have a lower margin than a
much smaller one.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $60,000 ÷ $300,000 = 20%.

## Key Takeaways

- Profit Margin = Profit ÷ Revenue, expressed as a percentage.
- It allows fair comparison between businesses of different sizes.
- It's a useful measure of efficiency, separate from raw profit dollars.
