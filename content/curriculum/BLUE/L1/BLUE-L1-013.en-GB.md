# What Is Overhead?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define overhead in plain terms.
- Distinguish overhead from costs directly tied to making a sale.
- Explain why overhead matters even during slow periods.

## Core Content

### What Overhead Is

Overhead refers to the ongoing costs of running a business that aren't
directly tied to producing a specific product or making a specific
sale — things like rent, insurance, and administrative salaries.

### Overhead vs. Direct Costs

Ask yourself: if a business made zero sales this month, would rent still
be due? Yes — that's the defining trait of overhead. Costs that scale
directly with each sale (like the raw materials for one specific
product) are different, and covered separately in later lessons.

### Why It Matters Even When Business Is Slow

Overhead keeps accruing whether or not revenue is coming in, which is
why understanding it matters for planning — a business with high fixed
overhead needs a certain baseline of sales just to break even, a concept
covered in the next lesson.

## Worked Example

A small retail shop pays $2,000/month in rent regardless of how many
items it sells that month. Ask yourself: does that $2,000 change if
sales are unusually slow one month? No — that's exactly what makes it
overhead, distinct from the cost of the specific inventory sold.

## Practice Quiz

1. Overhead refers to:
   A. Costs directly tied to producing one specific unit sold
   B. Ongoing costs of running the business, not directly tied to one
      sale
   C. A type of business license
   D. A company's total revenue

2. True or False: Overhead costs disappear during a month with no sales.

3. Which of these is a typical overhead cost?
   A. Raw materials for one specific product
   B. Monthly rent for the business location
   C. A one-time equipment purchase
   D. Shipping cost for one specific order

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Overhead is ongoing, not tied to a specific sale.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Overhead generally continues regardless of sales
activity.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Rent is a classic overhead cost, unrelated to any one
sale.

## Key Takeaways

- Overhead is ongoing cost, not tied to any one specific sale.
- It continues whether or not revenue is coming in.
- It sets a baseline a business needs to cover through sales, covered
  further in the next lesson.
