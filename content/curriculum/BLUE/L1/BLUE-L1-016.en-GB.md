# What Is Cash Flow in a Business? (Why Profitable Businesses Still Run Out of Money)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define cash flow in a business context.
- Explain why profit and cash flow are not the same thing.
- Explain how timing gaps can create real cash problems.

## Core Content

### What Cash Flow Means

Cash flow refers to the actual movement of cash into and out of a
business — not the accounting profit figure, but the real money
actually available at any given moment.

### Ask Yourself This

Can a business be profitable on paper and still not have enough cash to
pay its bills this week? Yes — and this is one of the most
counterintuitive, important lessons in running a business. Profit is
calculated over a period of time; cash flow is about timing, moment to
moment.

### How Timing Gaps Create Real Problems

If a business sells a large order but the customer doesn't pay for 60
days, while the business's own suppliers and rent are due in 30 days,
the business can be profitable on paper (the sale counts as revenue) and
still run short on actual cash to cover its own bills in the meantime.

## Worked Example

A business lands a great new contract worth $50,000 — clearly a
profitable deal. But the client pays 60 days after delivery, while the
business had to pay for materials and labor upfront. Ask yourself: does
being profitable on that contract guarantee the business has cash to
make payroll next week? Not necessarily — the timing gap is the real
danger, separate from whether the deal itself was a good one.

## Practice Quiz

1. Cash flow refers to:
   A. The same thing as accounting profit
   B. The actual movement of cash into and out of a business
   C. A business's total revenue for the year
   D. A type of business license

2. True or False: A profitable business can never run short on cash.

3. What can create a real cash flow problem even for a profitable deal?
   A. Nothing — profit guarantees available cash
   B. A timing gap between when expenses are paid and when revenue is
      actually collected
   C. Having too much overhead
   D. Having too few customers

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Cash flow is the actual movement of cash, distinct
from accounting profit.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Timing gaps between expenses and collections can
create real cash shortages even in profitable businesses.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Timing gaps between paying expenses and collecting
revenue are the core danger.

## Key Takeaways

- Cash flow is the actual movement of cash, distinct from accounting
  profit.
- A profitable business can still run short on cash due to timing.
- Timing gaps between expenses and collections are a real, common risk.
