## Main Lesson

### Topic 1: What an EIN Is
[REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a unique number issued by the US federal government to identify a business for tax purposes — functioning similarly to how a Social Security Number identifies an individual.

### Topic 2: The Misleading Name
Despite the name "Employer" Identification Number, [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] many businesses with zero employees still obtain one — for opening a business bank account, filing certain tax forms, or simply keeping business identification separate from the owner's personal Social Security Number.

### Topic 3: Why Separation Matters
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A sole proprietor who uses an EIN instead of their personal Social Security Number for business banking and vendor forms reduces how often their personal identifying number circulates, which can be a meaningful privacy and identity-protection consideration.

### Topic 4: Who Typically Needs One
[REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] Businesses with employees generally need an EIN. Many LLCs, corporations, and partnerships need one regardless of employee count. Some sole proprietors with no employees can sometimes operate using their Social Security Number instead, though many choose an EIN anyway for the separation benefits above.

---

## Key Definitions
- **EIN (Employer Identification Number):** A unique number issued by the US federal government to identify a business for tax purposes, regardless of whether it employs anyone.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A single-member LLC with no employees obtains an EIN specifically so the business bank account isn't tied directly to the owner's personal Social Security Number.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming "Employer" in the name means only businesses with employees need one
- Using a personal Social Security Number for business purposes when an EIN would offer more separation
- Assuming this concept applies outside the US without checking local equivalents

## FAQ
**Q: Does obtaining an EIN cost money?**
A: [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, VERIFIED FACT] As of general knowledge, obtaining an EIN directly through the US federal government is typically free; costs sometimes appear when using third-party services to file on someone's behalf, which is unnecessary for most applicants.

**Q: Do sole proprietors with no employees ever need one?**
A: [REGION-SPECIFIC RULE] Not always required, but often obtained anyway for the identity-separation and banking benefits described in Topic 3.

## Key Takeaways
- An EIN identifies a US business for tax purposes, similar to a personal Social Security Number
- Businesses with zero employees often still obtain one, despite the name
- Using an EIN instead of a personal number offers identity-separation benefits
- Requirements vary by business structure and employee status
