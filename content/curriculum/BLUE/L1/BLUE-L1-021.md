# What Is an EIN (Employer Identification Number)?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define an EIN in plain terms.
- Explain what it's commonly used for.
- Explain why a business with no employees might still need one.

## Core Content

### What an EIN Is

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this is a U.S. tax identifier.)* An EIN
(Employer Identification Number) is a unique number issued by the IRS to
identify a business for tax purposes — functioning similarly to a Social
Security number, but for a business entity.

### What It's Commonly Used For

An EIN is commonly required to open a business bank account, file
certain business tax forms, and hire employees — it's the identifier
that keeps a business's financial identity separate from the owner's
personal one.

### Ask Yourself This

If a business has no employees at all, does "Employer" Identification
Number still make sense to need? Often yes — many banks require an EIN
to open a business account regardless of employee count, and it's often
a practical requirement for keeping business and personal finances
cleanly separated, which matters directly for the liability protections
covered earlier in this level.

## Worked Example

A solo consultant with no employees forms an LLC and wants to open a
business bank account to keep finances separate — a formality that
supports the liability protection covered earlier. Ask yourself: does
she need an EIN even though she'll never hire anyone? In most cases,
yes — the bank will likely require one regardless of employee count.

## Practice Quiz

1. An EIN is:
   A. A personal Social Security number
   B. A unique number identifying a business for tax purposes
   C. A type of business license
   D. A marketing identifier

2. True or False: Only businesses with employees ever need an EIN.

3. Why might a business with no employees still need an EIN?
   A. It's never actually needed without employees
   B. Banks often require one to open a business account, regardless of
      employee count
   C. It replaces the need for a business license
   D. It's only required for corporations

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An EIN identifies a business for tax purposes.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Many businesses with no employees still need one,
often for banking purposes.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Banking requirements are a common reason, regardless
of employee count.

## Key Takeaways

- An EIN identifies a business for tax purposes, similar to a personal
  SSN.
- It's commonly required for banking, tax filing, and hiring.
- Even businesses with no employees often need one for banking purposes.
