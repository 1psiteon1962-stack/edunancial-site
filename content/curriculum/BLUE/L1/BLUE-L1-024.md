# What Is a Profit and Loss Statement (P&L)?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a P&L statement in plain terms.
- Identify its core components.
- Explain how it ties together earlier concepts from this level.

## Core Content

### What a P&L Statement Is

A Profit and Loss statement (also called an income statement) summarizes
a business's revenue, expenses, and resulting profit or loss over a
specific period of time — a month, a quarter, or a year.

### Its Core Components

At its simplest, a P&L lists total revenue at the top, subtracts
expenses (including overhead, covered earlier), and arrives at profit or
loss at the bottom — the same Profit = Revenue − Expenses formula from
earlier in this level, organized into a formal document.

### Ask Yourself This

If bookkeeping (the last lesson) is the raw data, what is a P&L? It's
one of the finished reports built from that data — a concrete
illustration of why consistent bookkeeping matters: without it, there's
no reliable P&L to actually look at.

## Worked Example

A business owner pulls together a P&L for the past quarter and sees
$120,000 in revenue, $95,000 in expenses, and $25,000 in profit. Ask
yourself: is that number alone enough to judge performance? Not
entirely — comparing it to last quarter's P&L, or calculating the profit
margin (covered earlier), gives much more useful context than the
number in isolation.

## Practice Quiz

1. A P&L statement summarizes:
   A. Only a business's tax filings
   B. Revenue, expenses, and resulting profit or loss over a period
   C. A business's employee roster
   D. A marketing plan

2. True or False: A P&L statement has no connection to a business's
   bookkeeping records.

3. What formula does a P&L statement's bottom line reflect?
   A. Revenue + Expenses
   B. Revenue − Expenses
   C. Overhead ÷ Revenue
   D. Assets − Liabilities

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A P&L summarizes revenue, expenses, and profit/loss
over a period.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A P&L is built directly from a business's
bookkeeping records.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Profit = Revenue − Expenses is the underlying
formula.

## Key Takeaways

- A P&L statement summarizes revenue, expenses, and profit/loss over a
  period.
- It's built directly from a business's bookkeeping records.
- It applies the Profit = Revenue − Expenses formula in a formal report.
