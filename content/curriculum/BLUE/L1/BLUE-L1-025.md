# What Is a Balance Sheet, in Plain Terms?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a balance sheet in plain terms.
- State the basic balance sheet equation.
- Explain how it differs from a P&L statement.

## Core Content

### What a Balance Sheet Shows

A balance sheet shows what a business owns (assets), what it owes
(liabilities), and the resulting owner's equity — but at one specific
moment in time, not over a period.

### The Basic Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity. This is why it's called a
"balance" sheet — both sides of the equation always balance, by
definition.

### How It Differs From a P&L

Ask yourself: does a P&L tell you what a business owns right now, or
what happened over the last quarter? A P&L covers a period of activity
(revenue and expenses over time). A balance sheet is a snapshot — what
the business's financial position looks like at one specific date, like
a photograph rather than a video.

## Worked Example

A business's P&L shows $25,000 in profit for the quarter. Its balance
sheet, taken on the last day of that quarter, shows $80,000 in assets
and $30,000 in liabilities, meaning $50,000 in owner's equity. Ask
yourself: are these two documents showing the same thing? No — one shows
what happened over time; the other shows a specific moment's position.

## Practice Quiz

1. A balance sheet shows:
   A. Revenue and expenses over a period of time
   B. What a business owns and owes at one specific moment
   C. A business's marketing budget
   D. A list of employees

2. True or False: A balance sheet and a P&L statement show the exact
   same information.

3. What is the basic balance sheet equation?
   A. Revenue − Expenses = Profit
   B. Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity
   C. Profit ÷ Revenue = Margin
   D. Assets − Revenue = Liabilities

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A balance sheet is a snapshot of assets, liabilities,
and equity at one moment.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A P&L covers a period; a balance sheet is a
single-moment snapshot.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity.

## Key Takeaways

- A balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and equity at one specific
  moment.
- The basic equation is Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity.
- It differs from a P&L, which covers activity over a period of time.
