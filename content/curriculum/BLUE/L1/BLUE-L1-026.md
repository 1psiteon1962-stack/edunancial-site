# What Is Inventory, and What Does It Cost You to Hold It?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define inventory in plain terms.
- Name costs associated with holding inventory, beyond purchase price.
- Explain why excess inventory can hurt cash flow.

## Core Content

### What Inventory Is

Inventory is the goods a business holds for the purpose of selling
them — raw materials, work in progress, or finished products sitting in
storage, waiting for a buyer.

### Ask Yourself This

If a business buys $50,000 of product upfront and it takes six months to
sell it all, is that $50,000 just sitting there doing nothing? In a real
sense, yes — that money is tied up, unavailable for other uses, while
also potentially incurring storage costs, insurance, and risk of the
product becoming outdated or damaged before it sells.

### Why Excess Inventory Hurts Cash Flow

This connects directly back to the cash flow lesson earlier in this
level: cash spent on inventory that hasn't sold yet is cash the business
doesn't have available to cover other expenses — a real, common cause of
cash flow strain, even for a business that will eventually sell
everything at a profit.

## Worked Example

A retailer buys a large batch of seasonal inventory, expecting strong
sales. Ask yourself: what happens to their cash position if sales come in
slower than expected? The cash spent on unsold inventory isn't available
for rent, payroll, or other obligations in the meantime — a real risk,
independent of whether the inventory eventually sells.

## Practice Quiz

1. Inventory refers to:
   A. A business's employees
   B. Goods a business holds for the purpose of selling
   C. A type of business license
   D. A marketing plan

2. True or False: Money spent on unsold inventory is still fully
   available for other business expenses.

3. How can excess inventory affect cash flow?
   A. It has no effect on cash flow
   B. Cash spent on unsold inventory isn't available for other expenses
   C. It always improves cash flow
   D. It only affects profit, never cash flow

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Inventory is goods held for the purpose of selling.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Cash spent on unsold inventory is tied up, not
available elsewhere.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Tied-up cash in unsold inventory strains available
cash for other needs.

## Key Takeaways

- Inventory is goods held for the purpose of selling.
- Holding inventory ties up cash and carries real additional costs.
- Excess inventory is a real, common cause of cash flow strain.
