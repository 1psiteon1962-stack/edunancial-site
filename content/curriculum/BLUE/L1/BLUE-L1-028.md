## Executive Summary

A genuinely good business idea can still be blocked entirely by a capital wall that has nothing to do with the idea's quality — the minimum order quantity. This lesson explains what an MOQ is and why it matters so much for new, low-capital businesses.

## Core Content

### Topic 1: What an MOQ Is
A minimum order quantity is the smallest amount of product a supplier or manufacturer will agree to produce or sell in a single order, regardless of how much a buyer actually wants.

### Topic 2: Why Suppliers Set MOQs
[ESTIMATE] Suppliers typically set MOQs because smaller orders often aren't cost-effective for them to produce — production setup costs, minimum material batch sizes, and administrative overhead per order all make very small runs disproportionately expensive relative to the price they could charge.

### Topic 3: How This Can Block a Good Idea
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An entrepreneur wants to launch a private-label pet food line but discovers the manufacturer's MOQ is 5,000 units — requiring tens of thousands of dollars upfront, far beyond available capital, even though the product idea itself, market research, and business plan are all genuinely solid.

### Topic 4: This Isn't a Failure of the Idea
[OPINION] Hitting an MOQ wall is a capital and supply-chain barrier, not evidence the idea itself was flawed. Recognizing this distinction matters — walking away from an MOQ-blocked idea, or pivoting the approach, is often a sound decision rather than a sign of failure.

## Key Definitions
- **Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ):** The smallest amount of product a supplier or manufacturer will agree to produce or sell in a single order.
- **Capital Wall:** A barrier to launching or scaling a business caused by needing more upfront money than is available, independent of the idea's underlying merit.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two entrepreneurs have equally strong product ideas. One finds a supplier with a low MOQ and launches with modest capital. The other's chosen manufacturer requires a 5,000-unit MOQ, creating a capital wall that blocks the same quality of idea from launching the same way.

## Common Mistakes
- Assuming a blocked idea means the idea itself was bad
- Not researching MOQs before committing significant time to a business plan
- Assuming all suppliers or manufacturers have similarly high MOQs

## FAQ
**Q: Do all suppliers have high MOQs?**
A: [ESTIMATE] No — MOQs vary significantly by industry, product type, and specific supplier; some are quite low or even negotiable, especially for less complex products.

**Q: What can be done if an MOQ blocks a business idea?**
A: [ESTIMATE] Options can include finding a different supplier with a lower MOQ, adjusting the business model (such as a lower-capital alternative), or reconsidering the approach entirely — later lessons and higher levels cover these pivots in more depth.

## Key Takeaways
- An MOQ is the smallest order a supplier will accept, regardless of buyer demand
- Suppliers set MOQs largely due to their own production cost efficiency
- A high MOQ can block a genuinely good idea purely due to capital requirements
- Being blocked by an MOQ isn't evidence the underlying idea was flawed

## Practical Exercise
Explain in your own words why hitting an MOQ wall is different from an idea simply "not being good enough."
