# What Is Drop Shipping?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define drop shipping in plain terms.
- Explain how it avoids the inventory and MOQ problems from earlier
  lessons.
- Identify a real trade-off of the model.

## Core Content

### What Drop Shipping Is

Drop shipping is a business model where a business sells products
without holding any inventory itself — when a customer orders, the
business's supplier ships the product directly to the customer, and the
business never physically handles the goods.

### Ask Yourself This

Given the last two lessons — the real cost of holding inventory, and the
real wall an MOQ can create — what problem does drop shipping directly
solve? Both. There's no large upfront inventory purchase, and no MOQ
wall to hit, because the business isn't buying in bulk at all — it's
only ordering exactly what's already been sold.

### A Real Trade-Off

In exchange for that lower capital requirement and reduced risk, drop
shipping generally means lower profit margins per sale (since the
supplier keeps more of the markup), less control over shipping speed and
quality, and heavier reliance on the supplier's own reliability — the
supplier-reliability concern from two lessons ago becomes even more
central in this model.

## Worked Example

An entrepreneur who couldn't meet a manufacturer's MOQ for a private
-label product instead builds a website selling similar products through
drop shipping — no large upfront inventory purchase, no MOQ wall, but
also a smaller margin on each sale and real dependence on the
drop-shipping supplier's own reliability and speed.

## Practice Quiz

1. Drop shipping means:
   A. Buying large amounts of inventory upfront
   B. Selling products without holding inventory; the supplier ships
      directly to the customer
   C. A type of business loan
   D. A marketing strategy for physical stores only

2. True or False: Drop shipping generally requires meeting a large
   manufacturer's minimum order quantity.

3. What is a real trade-off of the drop shipping model?
   A. No trade-off exists
   B. Generally lower profit margins and heavy reliance on supplier
      reliability
   C. It always guarantees higher profit than holding inventory
   D. It eliminates all business risk

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Drop shipping avoids holding inventory; the supplier
ships directly.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Drop shipping specifically avoids large upfront
orders and MOQ walls.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Lower margins and supplier dependence are real
trade-offs.

## Key Takeaways

- Drop shipping avoids holding inventory — the supplier ships directly
  to the customer.
- It sidesteps both the inventory cost and MOQ problems from earlier
  lessons.
- The trade-off is generally lower margins and heavier reliance on
  supplier reliability.
