# What Is a Business Model? (Not the Same as a Business Plan)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define business model in plain terms.
- Distinguish it from a business plan.
- Apply the concept to a basic example.

## Core Content

### What a Business Model Specifically Describes

A business model describes, specifically, how a business creates,
delivers, and captures value — in plain terms, how it actually makes
money. It's a narrower, more specific concept than a "business plan,"
covered earlier in this level.

### How It Differs From a Business Plan

Ask yourself: is "we sell software subscriptions" a business plan or a
business model? It's the business model — a specific description of the
value-and-money mechanism. A full business plan would include that
model, plus market research, financial projections, staffing plans, and
much more, as covered in the earlier lesson.

### Applying the Concept

Drop shipping (covered two lessons ago) and traditional retail with held
inventory are two different business models for potentially the exact
same product — the underlying mechanism of how money is made and value
delivered differs, even if the product being sold is identical.

## Worked Example

Two businesses sell the exact same category of product. One holds
inventory and ships directly; the other drop ships with no inventory at
all. Ask yourself: are these the same business model? No — despite
selling similar products, they operate on fundamentally different
mechanisms for creating and capturing value.

## Practice Quiz

1. A business model specifically describes:
   A. A company's marketing budget
   B. How a business creates, delivers, and captures value
   C. A business's employee handbook
   D. A government tax filing

2. True or False: A business model and a business plan mean exactly the
   same thing.

3. Can two businesses selling the same product have different business
   models?
   A. No, identical products always mean identical models
   B. Yes, the underlying mechanism for creating and capturing value can
      differ
   C. Business model only applies to service businesses
   D. Business model is decided by the government

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A business model describes how value is created,
delivered, and captured.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A business model is a narrower concept within the
broader business plan.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Different mechanisms (like drop shipping vs. held
inventory) mean different models, even for the same product.

## Key Takeaways

- A business model describes how a business creates, delivers, and
  captures value.
- It's narrower and more specific than a full business plan.
- Different businesses can sell the same product under entirely
  different models.
