## Core Content

### Topic 1: What a Business Model Specifically Describes
A business model describes, specifically, how a business creates, delivers, and captures value — in plainer terms, how it actually makes money from what it offers.

### Topic 2: How This Differs From a Business Plan
Recall from BLUE-L1-014 that a business plan is a broader written document covering the whole business — market, operations, financials, and more. A business model is narrower: it's specifically the mechanism by which money flows in, one component that a business plan would describe in more detail.

### Topic 3: Business Models Seen So Far in This Level
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Drop shipping (BLUE-L1-029) is a business model — a way of capturing value without holding inventory. A traditional retailer buying and reselling inventory is a different business model, even if both sell similar products.

### Topic 4: Why the Distinction Matters
[OPINION] Confusing the two can lead an entrepreneur to write a lengthy business plan without ever clearly articulating the actual mechanism by which the business will make money — a business plan can be well-written and still built around a poorly defined or unclear business model.

---

## Key Definitions
- **Business Model:** The specific mechanism by which a business creates, delivers, and captures value — in short, how it makes money.
- **Business Plan (reference):** A broader written document describing what a business does, how it makes money, who its customers are, and how it operates and grows — of which the business model is one component.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Two coffee shops could have identical business plans (same market research, same financial projections) but different business models — one sells coffee purely at a physical counter, the other primarily through a subscription box mailed monthly. Same plan structure, fundamentally different mechanism for capturing value.

## Common Mistakes
- Using "business model" and "business plan" interchangeably
- Writing a detailed business plan without a clearly defined underlying business model
- Assuming a business model can't change even as a business evolves

## FAQ
**Q: Can a business change its business model over time?**
A: Yes — [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] a company might start selling products directly, then later shift toward a subscription model, changing its business model while still serving similar customers.

**Q: Is one business model always better than another?**
A: [OPINION] No — the right business model depends heavily on the specific product, market, and resources available; there's no universally superior model.

## Key Takeaways
- A business model describes specifically how a business creates, delivers, and captures value
- A business plan is a broader document, of which the business model is one component
- Different business models (like drop shipping vs. traditional retail) can serve similar markets
- Being unclear about the underlying business model can undermine even a well-written business plan

## Practical Exercise
Pick two businesses you're familiar with that sell similar products but use clearly different business models. Describe each model in one sentence.
