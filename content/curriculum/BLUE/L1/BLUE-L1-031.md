## Core Content

### Topic 1: What a Target Market Is
A target market is the specific group of people or businesses a company designs its product, messaging, and pricing to serve — defined by shared characteristics like needs, demographics, location, or behavior.

### Topic 2: Why "Everyone" Isn't a Real Answer
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A product marketed to "everyone" often ends up appealing strongly to no one, because different groups have different needs, price sensitivities, and priorities — a message trying to speak to all of them tends to speak clearly to none of them.

### Topic 3: Basic Characteristics Used to Define a Target Market
[ESTIMATE] Common ways to define a target market include demographics (age, income, location), needs or problems the product solves, behavior (how and where they shop), and sometimes psychographics (values, interests, lifestyle) — specific combinations vary by business.

### Topic 4: Narrowing Doesn't Mean Excluding Everyone Else
[OPINION] Defining a specific target market doesn't mean refusing sales from anyone outside it — it means designing the core offer and messaging around the group most likely to buy and be satisfied, even if others occasionally purchase too.

## Key Definitions
- **Target Market:** The specific group of people or businesses a company designs its product, messaging, and pricing to serve.
- **Demographics:** Measurable population characteristics such as age, income, location, or occupation, often used to define a target market.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A high-end ergonomic office chair company targets remote professionals with disposable income who value comfort during long work hours — not "everyone who sits in a chair," which would include people with entirely different needs and budgets.

## Common Mistakes
- Describing a target market as "everyone" or "anyone who might want this"
- Defining a target market too vaguely to actually inform marketing or product decisions
- Assuming a defined target market excludes all other potential customers

## FAQ
**Q: Isn't a narrower target market limiting potential sales?**
A: [OPINION] Not necessarily — a clearly defined target market often allows more effective, resonant marketing, which can outperform a vague, broad approach even with a smaller initial audience.

**Q: Can a target market change over time?**
A: Yes — as a business grows or its product evolves, its target market can shift or expand deliberately, rather than being fixed forever.

## Key Takeaways
- A target market is the specific group a business designs its product and messaging around
- "Everyone" fails as an answer because different groups have different needs and priorities
- Target markets are typically defined by demographics, needs, behavior, or psychographics
- Narrowing a target market doesn't exclude other buyers — it focuses the core design and messaging

## Practical Exercise
Pick a product or service you use. Describe its likely target market in one or two sentences, using at least one specific characteristic.

`BLUE-L1-031-SOCRATIC` (Level 1 — "What")

3. **Follow-up:** What kinds of characteristics can define one?
4. **Concept 2 — Why "Everyone" Fails:** Why does trying to appeal to everyone often mean appealing strongly to no one?
5. **Follow-up:** Can you think of a product or ad that felt like it was speaking directly to you? What made it feel that way?
6. **Concept 3 — Defining Characteristics:** Name two ways a target market can be defined.
7. **Follow-up:** Which of these feels most relevant to a business you're familiar with?
8. **Concept 4 — Narrowing vs. Excluding:** Does defining a target market mean turning away other customers?
9. **Synthesis:** How is "focus" different from "exclusion" in this context?
10. **Reflection:** If you were starting a business, how would you begin narrowing down your own target market?
