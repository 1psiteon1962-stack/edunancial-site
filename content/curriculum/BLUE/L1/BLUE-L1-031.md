# What Is a Target Market?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define target market in plain terms.
- Explain why "everyone" is not a useful target market.
- Apply the concept to a basic example.

## Core Content

### What a Target Market Is

A target market is the specific group of people or businesses a company
focuses on serving — defined by shared characteristics like needs,
location, age, income, or other relevant traits.

### Ask Yourself This

If a new business says its target market is "everyone," what has that
actually told you about who to reach, what to say, or where to sell? Not
much. "Everyone" isn't a target — it's an absence of one, and it usually
means marketing dollars and effort get spread too thin to be effective
anywhere.

### Why Specificity Actually Helps

A narrower, specific target market makes marketing decisions
(covered in the next few lessons), product choices, and messaging
dramatically clearer — precisely because there's a real answer to "who is
this actually for."

## Worked Example

A new tutoring business initially describes its target market as
"students." Ask yourself: does that description help decide where to
advertise or what to say? Narrowing it to "parents of middle school
students struggling with math" gives the business a real, specific group
to design its marketing and offering around.

## Practice Quiz

1. A target market is:
   A. Every possible customer everywhere
   B. A specific group defined by shared relevant characteristics
   C. A type of business license
   D. A financial statement

2. True or False: "Everyone" is a strong, useful target market
   definition.

3. Why does a narrower target market help a business?
   A. It doesn't help at all
   B. It makes marketing and product decisions clearer and more focused
   C. It guarantees more total customers
   D. It eliminates the need for any marketing

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A target market is a specific group defined by
shared characteristics.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. "Everyone" provides no real focus for decisions.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Specificity clarifies marketing and product
decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- A target market is a specific group defined by shared characteristics.
- "Everyone" is effectively no target market at all.
- Specificity makes marketing and product decisions clearer.
