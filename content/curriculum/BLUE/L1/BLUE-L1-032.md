# What Is a Marketing Plan, at Its Most Basic?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Name the core questions a basic marketing plan answers.
- Explain why the target market lesson comes before this one.
- Apply the framework to a basic example.

## Core Content

### The Core Questions

At its most basic, a marketing plan answers: who is the target market
(covered in the last lesson)? What message will reach them? Where will
that message actually reach them? And what budget and timeline support
that effort?

### Why Target Market Comes First

Ask yourself: can you decide where to advertise before knowing who
you're trying to reach? Not effectively — the target market lesson is a
genuine prerequisite here, because the answer to "where" and "what
message" depends entirely on who the audience actually is.

### Keeping It Basic on Purpose

A marketing plan doesn't need to be complicated to be useful. Even a
simple, clear answer to those four core questions gives a new business
far more direction than jumping straight into random advertising without
a plan behind it.

## Worked Example

The tutoring business from the last lesson, now with a clear target
market (parents of struggling middle schoolers), can build a real
marketing plan: message focused on confidence and results, reaching
parents through school-adjacent community groups and local social media,
on a modest starting budget. Ask yourself: would this plan have made
sense before the target market was defined? No — it depends entirely on
that earlier decision.

## Practice Quiz

1. A basic marketing plan answers questions about:
   A. Only the business's tax obligations
   B. Who the audience is, what message to use, where to reach them, and
      budget/timeline
   C. Only the company's logo design
   D. Employee benefits

2. True or False: A marketing plan can be effectively built before
   defining a target market.

3. Why might a simple marketing plan still be valuable for a new
   business?
   A. Simplicity has no value
   B. It gives clear direction, even without complexity
   C. Only complex plans are ever useful
   D. Marketing plans are optional and rarely help

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. These four questions form the core of a basic
marketing plan.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Target market definition is a genuine
prerequisite for an effective plan.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Even simple clarity provides real direction over no
plan at all.

## Key Takeaways

- A basic marketing plan answers who, what message, where, and
  budget/timeline.
- Defining the target market is a genuine prerequisite for this step.
- Simplicity doesn't reduce a marketing plan's usefulness.
