# What Is Customer Acquisition Cost?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- State the customer acquisition cost formula.
- Calculate a basic example.
- Explain why this number matters for pricing and marketing decisions.

## Core Content

### The Formula

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) = Total Marketing/Sales Spend ÷ Number
of New Customers Acquired. It measures, on average, how much it costs to
gain one new customer.

### Ask Yourself This

If it costs $50 to acquire a customer, and that customer only ever
spends $30 with the business total, is that a sustainable situation? No
— acquiring customers at a loss, without a plan to make it back over
time, is a real, common business trap.

### Why This Number Matters

Knowing CAC helps a business judge whether its marketing spend is
actually working, and helps set realistic pricing and retention goals —
connecting forward to the next lesson on customer retention, which
becomes especially important when CAC is high.

## Worked Example

A business spends $2,000 on marketing in a month and gains 40 new
customers. CAC = $2,000 ÷ 40 = $50 per customer. Ask yourself: is that
number good or bad by itself? It depends entirely on how much value
each customer brings over time — a number this lesson doesn't answer
alone, but sets up for later exploration.

## Practice Quiz

1. Customer Acquisition Cost is calculated as:
   A. Total revenue ÷ number of customers
   B. Total marketing/sales spend ÷ number of new customers acquired
   C. Total profit ÷ total expenses
   D. Number of customers ÷ total revenue

2. True or False: A high CAC is automatically fine as long as new
   customers keep coming in.

3. A business spends $3,000 on marketing and gains 60 new customers.
   What is the CAC?
   A. $20
   B. $50
   C. $180,000
   D. $3,000

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. CAC = Marketing/Sales Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A high CAC without enough customer value in
return is a real risk.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $3,000 ÷ 60 = $50.

## Key Takeaways

- CAC = Marketing/Sales Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired.
- CAC alone doesn't tell the full story — it must be weighed against
  customer value over time.
- Understanding CAC helps judge whether marketing spend is actually
  working.
