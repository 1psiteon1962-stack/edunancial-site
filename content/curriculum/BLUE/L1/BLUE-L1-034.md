## Core Content

### Topic 1: What Customer Acquisition Cost Measures
Customer acquisition cost (CAC) measures how much money, on average, a business spends to gain one new customer — including marketing spend, advertising, and related costs, divided by the number of new customers gained.

### Topic 2: A Basic Calculation Example
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE, ESTIMATE] If a business spends $1,000 on marketing in a month and gains 20 new customers from that spending, its CAC for that period is $50 per customer ($1,000 ÷ 20).

### Topic 3: Why This Number Matters for Pricing
If a product sells for $40 but costs $50 in CAC to acquire each customer, the business is losing money on every new customer before even accounting for the cost of the product itself — a critical number to know before setting prices or marketing budgets.

### Topic 4: CAC in Relation to Customer Value
[ESTIMATE] CAC becomes especially meaningful when compared against how much a customer is worth over time (their total future value to the business) — a topic covered more fully alongside customer retention in the next lesson.

## Key Definitions
- **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** The average cost of gaining one new customer, calculated as total acquisition-related spending divided by the number of new customers gained.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A subscription box company spends $5,000 on ads in a month and gains 100 new subscribers. CAC is $50 per subscriber. If the subscription costs $30/month, the company needs that customer to stay subscribed for at least two months just to recover the acquisition cost.

## Common Mistakes
- Setting marketing budgets without tracking how many actual customers result from that spending
- Pricing a product without considering CAC relative to price
- Assuming a low sticker price for a product automatically means a low-cost customer

## FAQ
**Q: Is a high CAC always bad?**
A: [OPINION] Not necessarily — it depends on how much that customer is worth to the business over time; a higher CAC can be sustainable if customer value is high enough, a relationship explored further in the next lesson.

**Q: How can a business calculate its own CAC?**
A: [ESTIMATE] At a basic level: total acquisition-related spending over a period, divided by the number of new customers gained during that same period.

## Key Takeaways
- CAC measures the average cost of gaining one new customer
- It's calculated as acquisition spending divided by number of new customers
- A product priced below its CAC is losing money on every new customer
- CAC becomes especially meaningful when compared to customer value over time

## Practical Exercise
A business spends $2,000 on marketing and gains 40 new customers. Calculate the CAC, and explain what that number tells the business about its pricing.
