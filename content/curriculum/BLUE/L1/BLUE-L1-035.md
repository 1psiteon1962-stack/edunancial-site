# What Is Customer Retention, and Why Is It Usually Cheaper Than New Sales?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define customer retention in plain terms.
- Explain why retaining customers is often cheaper than acquiring new
  ones.
- Connect this directly to the CAC lesson.

## Core Content

### What Customer Retention Means

Customer retention refers to a business's ability to keep existing
customers buying or engaging over time, rather than losing them after
one purchase.

### Ask Yourself This

Given the CAC lesson — the real cost of gaining a new customer — is it
generally cheaper to sell again to someone already familiar with the
business, or to spend that same acquisition cost finding someone brand
new? Selling to an existing customer is generally far cheaper, since the
trust and awareness that acquisition cost paid for is already in place.

### Why This Matters for Sustainability

A business with strong retention needs fewer new customers (each costing
real acquisition dollars) to maintain the same revenue, compared to a
business that constantly has to replace customers who don't come back —
a real, practical reason retention deserves as much attention as
acquisition.

## Worked Example

Two businesses both spend $50 to acquire a new customer. One customer
buys once and never returns; another becomes a repeat customer buying
regularly for years. Ask yourself: which $50 acquisition cost turned out
to be the better investment? The repeat customer, by a wide margin — the
same acquisition cost produced far more value.

## Practice Quiz

1. Customer retention refers to:
   A. Acquiring a customer for the first time
   B. Keeping existing customers buying or engaging over time
   C. A type of marketing budget
   D. A business's tax filing

2. True or False: Acquiring a brand new customer is generally cheaper
   than retaining an existing one.

3. Why does strong retention matter for a business's sustainability?
   A. It doesn't matter at all
   B. It reduces reliance on constantly replacing lost customers with
      costly new acquisition
   C. It eliminates the need for any marketing
   D. It only matters for large businesses

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Retention is about keeping existing customers
engaged over time.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Retaining an existing customer is generally
cheaper than acquiring a new one.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Strong retention reduces dependence on costly ongoing
acquisition.

## Key Takeaways

- Customer retention means keeping existing customers engaged over time.
- Retaining a customer is generally cheaper than acquiring a new one.
- Strong retention reduces a business's dependence on constant new
  acquisition.
