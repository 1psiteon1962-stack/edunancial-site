# What Is Market Research, and How Do You Do It Without a Big Budget?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define market research in plain terms.
- Name low-cost ways to conduct it.
- Explain why skipping it entirely is a real risk.

## Core Content

### What Market Research Actually Is

Market research is the process of gathering real information about a
target market (covered earlier), competitors, and demand — replacing
assumptions with actual evidence before committing significant time or
money.

### Ask Yourself This

Does market research require expensive consultants and formal studies to
be useful? Not necessarily — direct conversations with potential
customers, observing competitors, and testing a small-scale version of
an idea before fully committing are all real, low-cost forms of market
research available to nearly anyone.

### Why Skipping It Is a Real Risk

A business built entirely on assumptions about what customers want,
without ever checking those assumptions against reality, risks investing
significant time and money into something the actual target market
doesn't want strongly enough — a risk basic research can meaningfully
reduce, even if it can't eliminate it.

## Worked Example

Before committing to a full product line, an entrepreneur talks
directly to twenty potential customers about a specific problem she
wants to solve. Ask yourself: is this "real" market research, even
without a formal study or budget? Yes — direct, honest customer
conversations are a legitimate, low-cost research method available to
almost any new business.

## Practice Quiz

1. Market research is:
   A. Only possible with a large budget and formal studies
   B. The process of gathering real information to replace assumptions
      with evidence
   C. A type of business license
   D. A financial statement

2. True or False: Direct conversations with potential customers count as
   a legitimate form of market research.

3. What real risk does skipping market research create?
   A. No real risk exists
   B. Investing significant time and money into something the market
      doesn't actually want
   C. It guarantees business failure
   D. It only affects large corporations

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Market research replaces assumptions with real
evidence.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Direct customer conversations are a legitimate,
low-cost method.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Skipping research risks investing in something the
market doesn't actually want.

## Key Takeaways

- Market research replaces assumptions with real evidence.
- Low-cost methods like direct customer conversations are legitimate and
  accessible.
- Skipping it risks investing significant time and money based on
  untested assumptions.
