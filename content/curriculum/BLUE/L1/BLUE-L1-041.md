# What Is an Independent Contractor vs. an Employee?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define independent contractor and employee separately.
- Explain why the label alone doesn't determine legal classification.
- Explain why misclassification is a real business risk.

## Core Content

### The Basic Distinction

An employee generally works under the business's direction and control
— set hours, specific methods, ongoing supervision. An independent
contractor generally operates with more autonomy, controlling how and
when the work gets done, often for multiple clients.

### Ask Yourself This

If a business simply calls someone a "contractor" on paper, does that
settle their legal classification? No — in many places, classification
depends on the actual working relationship (control, autonomy, tools
used), not just the label used in a contract.

### Why Misclassification Is a Real Risk

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: specific classification tests vary by
jurisdiction.)* A business that treats someone like an employee (set
hours, close supervision) but classifies and pays them as a contractor
can face real legal and tax consequences if that classification is later
challenged — the label doesn't override the actual working relationship.

## Worked Example

A business hires someone, calls them a "contractor," but requires set
hours, provides all tools, and closely supervises every task, just like
an employee. Ask yourself: does calling them a contractor actually
protect the business if this classification is challenged? Not
necessarily — the real working relationship, not the label, is generally
what determines proper classification.

## Practice Quiz

1. An employee generally:
   A. Works with full autonomy for multiple clients
   B. Works under the business's direction and control
   C. Is never paid regularly
   D. Cannot be supervised at all

2. True or False: Simply labeling someone a "contractor" in a written
   agreement guarantees that classification is legally correct.

3. What is a real risk of misclassifying an employee as a contractor?
   A. No real risk exists
   B. Real legal and tax consequences if the classification is
      challenged
   C. It always benefits the business with no downside
   D. It only matters for very large companies

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Employees generally work under the business's
direction and control.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The actual working relationship, not the label,
generally determines classification.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Misclassification carries real legal and tax risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Employees generally work under the business's direction; contractors
  operate with more autonomy.
- The written label alone doesn't determine legal classification — the
  actual relationship does.
- Misclassification carries real legal and tax risk for a business.
