## Core Content

### Topic 1: What an SOP Is
A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a written, step-by-step document describing exactly how to perform a specific task or process, intended to produce consistent results regardless of who follows it.

### Topic 2: The Direct Connection to Systems
Recall from BLUE-L1-039 that a business system is a documented, repeatable process. An SOP is the concrete written form that system typically takes — the actual document someone can follow, rather than the abstract concept of "having a system."

### Topic 3: What Makes an SOP Actually Useful
[ESTIMATE] A genuinely useful SOP is specific enough that someone unfamiliar with the task could follow it and get a consistent result — vague instructions like "handle the customer politely" are far less useful than specific steps like "greet the customer by name, confirm their order, and offer the current promotion."

### Topic 4: Common SOP Pitfalls
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An SOP that's too vague fails to actually guide behavior; an SOP that's excessively long and rigid can frustrate people and become outdated quickly as the business evolves — the goal is specific and clear, not necessarily exhaustive.

## Key Definitions
- **Standard Operating Procedure (SOP):** A written, step-by-step document describing exactly how to perform a specific task or process, intended to produce consistent results.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A restaurant's SOP for opening the kitchen each morning might specify: check refrigerator temperatures, restock specific ingredients to specific par levels, turn on and calibrate equipment in a specific order, and complete a signed checklist — specific enough that any trained staff member could open correctly and consistently.

## Common Mistakes
- Writing SOPs so vague they don't actually guide consistent behavior
- Writing SOPs so rigid and lengthy that staff avoid using them
- Never updating SOPs as the business or process changes, letting them become outdated and unreliable

## FAQ
**Q: Does every task in a business need a formal SOP?**
A: [OPINION] Not necessarily every task — but tasks that are repeated often, are important to get right consistently, or will eventually be delegated to someone else are strong candidates for a documented SOP.

**Q: How detailed should an SOP be?**
A: [ESTIMATE] Detailed enough that someone reasonably capable but unfamiliar with the task could follow it and get a consistent result — more detail isn't always better if it makes the document harder to actually use.

## Key Takeaways
- An SOP is the concrete, written form a business system typically takes
- It should be specific enough to guide consistent behavior from someone unfamiliar with the task
- Overly vague SOPs fail to guide behavior; overly rigid ones become unused or outdated
- Not every task needs an SOP, but repeated, important, or delegated tasks are strong candidates

## Practical Exercise
Write a short, specific SOP (3-5 steps) for a simple recurring task you're familiar with.
