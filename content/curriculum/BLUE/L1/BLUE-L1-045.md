# What Is a Pivot? (Changing Direction Without Starting Over)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a pivot in plain terms.
- Explain what's typically kept versus changed during a pivot.
- Distinguish a pivot from simply giving up.

## Core Content

### What a Pivot Is

A pivot is a significant change in a business's direction — its product,
target market, or model — while generally keeping something valuable
from the original effort: knowledge, relationships, skills, or brand
recognition already built.

### Ask Yourself This

If a business changes direction completely, is that the same as
starting over from nothing? Not usually — a real pivot keeps something
of value from the original attempt. That's the actual difference between
a pivot and simply abandoning everything and beginning again with a
blank slate.

### Not the Same as Giving Up

A pivot is a strategic redirection based on real information — often
learned specifically because the original direction hit a genuine wall,
like the capital wall from the MOQ lesson earlier in this level — not a
quiet admission of total failure.

## Worked Example

Someone who developed real expertise trying to launch a private-label
product line, but hit a capital wall they couldn't clear, pivots toward
building a website-based service business instead — helping other new
businesses succeed. Ask yourself: was anything from the original effort
actually wasted? The research skills, the understanding of what small
businesses struggle with, the resilience built along the way — all of it
carried forward into the new direction. That's what makes it a pivot,
not simply starting from zero.

## Practice Quiz

1. A pivot in business generally means:
   A. Starting completely over with nothing carried forward
   B. A significant change in direction while keeping something
      valuable from the original effort
   C. A type of business license
   D. Closing the business permanently

2. True or False: A pivot and giving up completely mean the same thing.

3. What often triggers a legitimate pivot?
   A. Random chance with no real reason
   B. Learning, often the hard way, that the original direction hit a
      genuine wall
   C. A government requirement
   D. A change in the business's logo

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A pivot keeps something valuable while changing
direction significantly.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A pivot is a strategic redirection, not the same
as abandoning everything.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Hitting a genuine wall with real information often
triggers a legitimate pivot.

## Key Takeaways

- A pivot is a significant change in direction that keeps something
  valuable from the original effort.
- It's different from starting over with nothing carried forward.
- A pivot is a strategic redirection based on real information, not an
  admission of total failure.
