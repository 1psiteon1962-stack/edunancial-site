# What Is OPM (Other People's Money)? (Introductory Version — Full Method in Level 3)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define OPM in plain terms.
- Name the two broad categories OPM generally falls into.
- Recognize that OPM comes with real obligations, not free money.

## Core Content

### What OPM Means

OPM (Other People's Money) refers to using money from outside sources —
rather than only the owner's own savings or reinvested profit, covered
in the earlier startup capital lesson — to fund or grow a business.

### Two Broad Categories

OPM generally falls into two categories: **debt** (borrowed money that
must be repaid, usually with interest, regardless of how the business
performs) and **equity** (money exchanged for partial ownership, where
the provider shares in the business's success or failure, but doesn't
require fixed repayment the way debt does).

### Ask Yourself This

Is OPM free money with no strings attached? Never — debt requires
repayment regardless of outcome; equity requires giving up a real
portion of ownership and future profit. Understanding this distinction
now sets up Level 3's deeper look at specific OPM sources like
crowdfunding, angel investors, and venture capital.

## Worked Example

A business could grow using its own reinvested profit (covered earlier),
or it could take a loan (debt, requiring repayment regardless of
performance), or bring in an investor (equity, sharing ownership and
future profit). Ask yourself: are these three paths interchangeable,
just with different names? No — each carries a fundamentally different
obligation and trade-off, worth understanding clearly before choosing
one.

## Practice Quiz

1. OPM refers to:
   A. Only money from personal savings
   B. Using outside sources of money to fund or grow a business
   C. A type of business license
   D. A marketing budget

2. True or False: OPM is generally free money with no obligations
   attached.

3. What is the core difference between debt and equity as forms of OPM?
   A. There is no real difference
   B. Debt requires repayment regardless of performance; equity shares
      ownership and outcome instead
   C. Debt is always cheaper than equity in every situation
   D. Equity never requires giving up any ownership

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. OPM means using outside money sources, not personal
savings.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Both debt and equity come with real obligations.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Debt requires repayment regardless of outcome;
equity shares ownership and outcome.

## Key Takeaways

- OPM means using outside money sources to fund or grow a business.
- It generally falls into debt (repayment required) or equity (ownership
  shared).
- Neither form of OPM is free — both carry real obligations, explored
  further in Level 3.
