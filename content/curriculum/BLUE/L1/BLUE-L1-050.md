## Core Content

### Topic 1: Step One — Idea, Target Market, and Research
Start by identifying a business idea and, crucially, its target market (BLUE-L1-031) — not "everyone." Conduct low-cost market research (BLUE-L1-037): direct conversations, observing existing behavior, or a small pilot test to gauge real, not just polite, interest.

### Topic 2: Step Two — Structure and Foundational Decisions
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] Once there's early evidence of demand, walk through the structure decision framework from BLUE-L1-010: how many owners, how much liability risk, investment plans, licensed-profession considerations. Address foundational needs — business licensing (BLUE-L1-020), an EIN if applicable (BLUE-L1-021), and a separate business bank account (BLUE-L1-022) — before real money starts moving.

### Topic 3: Step Three — Pricing, Costs, and the Numbers That Matter
Understand revenue vs. profit (BLUE-L1-011), profit margin (BLUE-L1-012), overhead (BLUE-L1-013), and break-even (BLUE-L1-015) well enough to price sustainably. Know the actual customer acquisition cost (BLUE-L1-034) relative to that price, so the first sale isn't accidentally a loss.

### Topic 4: Step Four — Reaching the First Customer and Beyond
Build a basic marketing plan (BLUE-L1-032): who, what message, where, and how much. Make the first sale — and immediately think beyond it: what makes this customer likely to return (BLUE-L1-035), and what documented process (BLUE-L1-039, BLUE-L1-043) would let this first sale be repeatable by someone other than the owner personally, addressing the growth ceiling introduced in BLUE-L1-038.

## Worked Examples
[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An aspiring candle maker identifies a target market (eco-conscious gift buyers), tests demand through direct conversations and a small pilot sale to 10 people, confirms a sole proprietorship fits her low-risk, single-owner situation, checks local licensing requirements, sets a price that covers her overhead and direct costs with a healthy margin above her estimated CAC, builds a simple social-media-based marketing plan reaching local gift buyers, and makes her first sale — while already documenting her process so a future assistant could eventually replicate it.

## Common Mistakes
- Skipping market research (BLUE-L1-037) and jumping straight to structure or pricing decisions
- Setting a price without knowing overhead, direct costs, or CAC
- Making a first sale with no plan for retention or repeatability, treating it as a one-off rather than the start of a system

## FAQ
**Q: Does this Capstone require actually starting a real business?**
A: [OPINION] Not necessarily — the exercise can be worked through with a real idea the learner is genuinely considering, or a well-thought-out hypothetical; the value is in demonstrating the integrated sequence of reasoning, not necessarily in immediate real-world execution.

**Q: What happens after BLUE-L1?**
A: Per the Curriculum Constitution's production sequencing, BLUE-L2 (50 lessons) follows, going deeper into each of these areas — this Capstone marks the completion of Level 1 awareness/literacy, not the end of the BLUE track.

## Key Takeaways
- A genuine business idea moves from target market and research, through structure and foundational setup, through pricing grounded in real numbers, to a first sale built for repeatability
- Nearly every BLUE-L1 lesson maps to a specific step in this sequence
- Skipping steps (especially research and numbers) creates the kinds of risk covered throughout this level
- The first sale is the beginning of a repeatable system, not just an isolated event

## Practical Exercise
Using a real or hypothetical business idea, write a brief walkthrough (one or two sentences per step) covering: (1) target market and research, (2) structure and foundational setup, (3) pricing and key numbers, (4) marketing and the first sale, including a note on repeatability.
