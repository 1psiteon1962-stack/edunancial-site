## BLUE-L2-012
### Break-Even Analysis: Knowing Your Number

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define break-even point at a conceptual level.
- Calculate a simple break-even point given fixed costs, price, and variable cost per unit.
- Explain why knowing this number matters before and during operation.

**2. Core Content**
The break-even point is the level of sales at which a business's total revenue exactly equals its total costs — neither profit nor loss. Below this point, the business operates at a loss; above it, the business operates at a profit (all else being equal). Calculating a simple break-even point requires three inputs: fixed costs (expenses that do not change with sales volume, such as rent), price per unit, and variable cost per unit (costs that do change with each unit sold, such as materials).

Knowing this number matters both before launching (to realistically assess whether a business concept is financially viable, given its likely fixed costs and reasonably anticipated sales volume) and during ongoing operation (to understand how close the business is running to this threshold, and how sensitive it is to changes in price, cost, or volume).

**3. Worked Example**
Planning her cupcake business, Marisol calculates her monthly fixed costs (rent, insurance, base staffing) at $4,000. Her price per cupcake is $4, and her variable cost per cupcake (ingredients, packaging) is $1.50, leaving $2.50 in contribution toward fixed costs per cupcake sold. Dividing her $4,000 in fixed costs by her $2.50 contribution margin per cupcake, Marisol calculates she needs to sell 1,600 cupcakes per month just to break even.

This number helps Marisol realistically assess her local market's capacity before committing to the lease, and gives her a clear, concrete monthly target to track against as she begins operating.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the break-even point?
   a) The point of maximum possible profit
   b) The sales level at which total revenue exactly equals total costs
   c) A type of business loan
   d) A tax filing deadline
2. What three inputs are needed for a simple break-even calculation?
   a) Revenue, taxes, and insurance
   b) Fixed costs, price per unit, and variable cost per unit
   c) Only the business's name and location
   d) Profit margin alone
3. True or False: A business operating above its break-even point is operating at a loss.
4. What did Marisol's break-even calculation tell her?
   a) Nothing useful
   b) She needed to sell 1,600 cupcakes per month just to break even
   c) Her business could never be profitable
   d) Her fixed costs were irrelevant

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The break-even point is where total revenue exactly equals total costs, with neither profit nor loss.
2. Fixed costs, price per unit, and variable cost per unit are the standard inputs for a simple break-even calculation.
3. A business operating above its break-even point is operating at a profit, not a loss.
4. Marisol's calculation gave her a concrete, realistic monthly sales target needed to cover her costs.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The break-even point is the sales level where total revenue exactly equals total costs.
- Calculating it requires fixed costs, price per unit, and variable cost per unit.
- This number supports realistic pre-launch assessment and ongoing operational tracking.
- Knowing this number turns an abstract sense of "am I doing okay" into a concrete, measurable target.
