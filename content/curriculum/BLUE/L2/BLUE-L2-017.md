## BLUE-L2-017
### Business Loans and Lines of Credit: An Overview

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Compare a term loan and a line of credit at a conceptual level.
- Explain general use cases for each.
- Recognize the importance of matching financing type to specific business need.

**2. Core Content**
A business term loan generally provides a lump sum upfront, repaid over a set schedule with a fixed or variable interest rate — well suited to a specific, one-time capital need, such as purchasing equipment or funding an expansion. A business line of credit generally provides access to a set credit limit that can be drawn upon, repaid, and drawn upon again as needed, echoing the WHITE-track HELOC discussion (WHITE-L2, RED-L2-019) in its revolving structure — well suited to managing fluctuating short-term needs, such as bridging a cash flow gap (Lesson 11) between when expenses are paid and when customer payments arrive.

Matching financing type to specific need matters: using a term loan for a fluctuating, ongoing cash flow need can mean paying interest on borrowed funds not currently being used, while using a line of credit for a large, one-time capital purchase may not provide the most favorable long-term rate or repayment structure compared to a term loan designed for that purpose.

**3. Worked Example**
Needing to purchase a specific piece of equipment to expand her production capacity, Marisol (Lesson 12) takes out a term loan with a repayment schedule matched to the equipment's expected useful life. Separately, recognizing that her cash flow fluctuates seasonally (a topic explored further in Lesson 35), she also establishes a business line of credit specifically to bridge short-term gaps between slower and busier seasons, drawing on it only when needed and repaying it as cash flow allows — using each financing tool for the specific need it is best suited to address.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a business term loan generally provide?
   a) A revolving credit limit
   b) A lump sum upfront, repaid over a set schedule
   c) Free money with no repayment
   d) Equity ownership in the business
2. What does a business line of credit generally provide?
   a) A one-time lump sum only
   b) A revolving credit limit that can be drawn upon, repaid, and drawn upon again
   c) Guaranteed business ownership
   d) A tax refund
3. True or False: A term loan and a line of credit are functionally identical and interchangeable for any business need.
4. Why did Marisol use two different financing tools for two different needs?
   a) She had no specific reasoning
   b) Each tool was better suited to its specific need — a one-time equipment purchase versus fluctuating seasonal cash flow
   c) She was required to use both by her lender
   d) One tool was free and the other was not

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A term loan generally provides a lump sum upfront, repaid over a set schedule.
2. A line of credit generally provides a revolving credit limit that can be drawn and repaid repeatedly as needed.
3. Term loans and lines of credit serve different purposes and are not interchangeable for every need.
4. Marisol matched each financing tool to its specific, appropriate use case — a one-time purchase versus fluctuating ongoing needs.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A term loan provides a lump sum for a specific, one-time need, repaid on a set schedule.
- A line of credit provides revolving access to funds, well suited to fluctuating short-term needs.
- This structure echoes the WHITE and RED-track discussion of revolving versus lump-sum credit tools.
- Matching financing type to the specific business need supports more efficient, cost-effective borrowing.
