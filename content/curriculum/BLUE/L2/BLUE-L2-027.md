## BLUE-L2-027
### Standard Operating Procedures: Capturing What You Know

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) at a conceptual level.
- Explain the general process of creating an effective SOP.
- Connect SOPs to the systems and delegation concepts from Lessons 24 and 26.

**2. Core Content**
A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a written, step-by-step document describing how a specific task or process should be performed, providing the concrete substance behind the "systems" concept introduced in Lesson 24. An effective SOP is specific enough that someone unfamiliar with the task could follow it and achieve a consistent, acceptable result, without needing to rely entirely on the original expert's unwritten, personal knowledge.

Creating effective SOPs generally involves the business owner (or another expert) documenting their own process in detail — often more detailed than initially expected, since experienced individuals frequently perform steps automatically without consciously narrating them — then testing the SOP with someone unfamiliar with the task to identify gaps or unclear instructions before relying on it for actual delegation (Lesson 26). SOPs are living documents, meant to be updated as processes improve or circumstances change, not written once and left untouched indefinitely.

**3. Worked Example**
Writing her first cupcake recipe SOP, Marisol initially produces a document that assumes too much unstated knowledge — steps she performs automatically after years of practice but had never consciously written down. When she tests the SOP with a new employee unfamiliar with her process, several points of confusion emerge specifically because Marisol's document skipped details she considered "obvious." She revises the SOP based on this real feedback, adding the missing specifics, and confirms the revised version allows the new employee to achieve a consistent, acceptable result independently — validating the SOP as genuinely ready to support delegation.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)?
   a) An informal, unwritten understanding
   b) A written, step-by-step document describing how a specific task should be performed
   c) A type of business loan
   d) A tax filing form
2. Why is testing an SOP with someone unfamiliar with the task important?
   a) It is unnecessary
   b) It helps identify gaps or unclear instructions before relying on the SOP for actual delegation
   c) It guarantees the SOP will never need updating
   d) It has no relationship to delegation
3. True or False: An SOP should be written once and never updated, regardless of process improvements or changes.
4. What did testing her SOP with a new employee reveal for Marisol?
   a) Nothing — the SOP was perfect on the first attempt
   b) Points of confusion from unstated, "obvious" knowledge she had not written down
   c) That her recipe was completely wrong
   d) That SOPs are never useful

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An SOP is a written, step-by-step document describing how a specific task should be performed consistently.
2. Testing with someone unfamiliar with the task helps identify gaps before the SOP is relied upon for actual delegation.
3. SOPs are living documents meant to be updated as processes improve or circumstances change.
4. Testing revealed unstated, assumed knowledge that needed to be added for the SOP to genuinely support delegation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An SOP is a written, step-by-step document capturing how a task should be performed for consistent results.
- Effective SOPs are detailed enough for someone unfamiliar with the task to follow successfully.
- Testing an SOP with an unfamiliar person helps surface gaps before it is relied upon for delegation.
- SOPs are living documents, meant to be updated as processes and circumstances evolve.
