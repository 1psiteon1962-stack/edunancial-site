## BLUE-L2-037
### Scaling Production: Capacity Constraints and Solutions

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define production capacity constraint at a conceptual level.
- Identify general approaches to addressing a capacity constraint.
- Apply this concept to the MOQ discussion from Lesson 7.

**2. Core Content**
As a business grows (Lesson 36), it often encounters a production capacity constraint — a limit on how much the business can produce or deliver within a given timeframe, given its current equipment, staffing, space, or processes. This connects back to the MOQ discussion (Lesson 7) from the opposite direction: where MOQ constrains how small an order a business can place with a supplier, a capacity constraint limits how much a business itself can produce to fulfill growing customer demand.

General approaches to addressing a capacity constraint include investing in additional equipment or space (often requiring the financing tools discussed in Lesson 17), hiring and training additional staff (Lessons 21–22, 26–27), outsourcing certain production steps to a third party, or, in some cases, deliberately limiting growth temporarily until capacity can be responsibly expanded, echoing Lesson 36's caution against uncontrolled, unsupported growth.

**3. Worked Example**
As demand for her cupcakes grows beyond what her current kitchen space and staffing can reliably produce, Marisol evaluates her options: investing in a larger leased kitchen space and additional equipment (requiring a term loan, Lesson 17), hiring and training additional bakers using her established SOPs (Lesson 27), or temporarily capping her order volume while she expands capacity responsibly. She chooses a combination approach — gradually expanding both space and staff while temporarily managing order volume during the transition — rather than either overextending immediately or refusing to grow at all.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a production capacity constraint?
   a) A supplier's minimum order requirement
   b) A limit on how much a business can produce given its current equipment, staffing, space, or processes
   c) A type of business loan
   d) A government regulation
2. How does a capacity constraint relate to the MOQ discussion from Lesson 7?
   a) They are identical concepts
   b) MOQ constrains order size from a supplier; capacity constrains a business's own production ability
   c) They have no relationship to each other
   d) Capacity constraints only affect service businesses
3. True or False: The only solution to a capacity constraint is unlimited, immediate expansion regardless of cost or risk.
4. What approach did Marisol choose to address her capacity constraint?
   a) Immediate, unlimited expansion regardless of risk
   b) A combination of gradual expansion and temporarily managing order volume during the transition
   c) Refusing to grow at all
   d) Ignoring the constraint entirely

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A production capacity constraint limits how much a business can produce given its current resources and processes.
2. MOQ and capacity constraints are related but distinct: one limits supplier order size, the other limits the business's own production ability.
3. Multiple approaches exist for addressing capacity constraints, not only immediate, unlimited expansion.
4. Marisol's combined, gradual approach balanced growth with responsible capacity management during the transition.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A production capacity constraint limits how much a business can produce given its current resources.
- This relates to, but is distinct from, the MOQ constraint discussed in Lesson 7.
- Approaches include investing in capacity, hiring and training staff, outsourcing, or temporarily managing growth.
- A combined, gradual approach can balance growth opportunity against responsible capacity management.
