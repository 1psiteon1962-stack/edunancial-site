## BLUE-L2-038
### Franchising and Licensing as Growth Models

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define franchising and licensing at a conceptual level.
- Compare these growth models to organic, owner-operated expansion.
- Identify general benefits and risks of each model.

**2. Core Content**
Franchising involves allowing another party (a franchisee) to operate a business using an established brand, systems (Lesson 24), and support, typically in exchange for fees and ongoing royalties, allowing a business concept to expand geographically without the original owner personally opening and operating every new location. Licensing involves granting another party the right to use specific intellectual property (a brand name, a proprietary process, a product design) in exchange for fees or royalties, without necessarily replicating the entire business model as comprehensively as franchising does.

Both models can allow faster growth than organic, owner-funded and owner-operated expansion, since franchisees or licensees typically provide their own capital and often their own local management. But both also involve giving up some direct control over how the business or intellectual property is represented and operated by others, and both require the underlying systems (Lesson 24) and SOPs (Lesson 27) to be genuinely well-documented and transferable, since inconsistent execution across franchise or license locations can damage the overall brand.

**3. Worked Example**
Having built well-documented systems and SOPs (Lessons 24, 27) for her cupcake business, Marisol considers franchising as a way to expand into other cities without personally funding and operating each new location. She recognizes this would require an even more rigorous level of system documentation than she currently has, since franchisees she has never personally trained would need to replicate her quality and brand consistently, and she would need to accept less direct daily control over each individual location than she has over her own — a significant tradeoff she carefully weighs before deciding whether franchising fits her specific goals for the business.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does franchising generally involve?
   a) Selling the entire business outright
   b) Allowing another party to operate a business using an established brand and systems, for fees and royalties
   c) Eliminating the need for any systems or SOPs
   d) A type of business loan
2. What is a general tradeoff of franchising or licensing, compared to organic expansion?
   a) No tradeoffs exist
   b) Giving up some direct control over how the business or intellectual property is represented by others
   c) Guaranteed identical execution at every location
   d) Elimination of all brand risk
3. True or False: Franchising requires no meaningful systems documentation, since franchisees figure things out on their own.
4. What did Marisol recognize she would need before pursuing franchising?
   a) No additional preparation at all
   b) An even more rigorous level of system documentation than she currently had
   c) The elimination of her current systems entirely
   d) Less documentation than her current single-location operation

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Franchising allows another party to operate a business using an established brand and systems, typically for fees and royalties.
2. Franchising and licensing both involve giving up some direct control over representation and execution by others.
3. Franchising requires rigorous, well-documented systems to support consistent execution across independently operated locations.
4. Marisol recognized franchising would demand more rigorous documentation than her current systems provided.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Franchising allows business expansion through others operating under an established brand and systems, for fees and royalties.
- Licensing grants rights to specific intellectual property, without necessarily replicating the full business model.
- Both models can enable faster growth than organic expansion, using others' capital and local management.
- Both require rigorous systems and SOPs and involve accepting less direct control over execution by others.
