## BLUE-L2-044
### Reading Basic Financial Statements: Income Statement and Balance Sheet

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define the general purpose of an income statement and a balance sheet.
- Distinguish what each statement shows.
- Apply this understanding to a simplified example.

**2. Core Content**
An income statement (sometimes called a profit and loss statement) shows a business's revenue, expenses, and resulting profit or loss over a specific period of time — a month, a quarter, or a year — directly connecting to the profit concept discussed in Lesson 11. A balance sheet shows a business's assets (what it owns), liabilities (what it owes), and owner's equity (the residual value belonging to the owner) at a specific point in time, rather than over a period — a structural snapshot rather than a period-based flow.

Understanding the distinction matters: the income statement answers "how did the business perform during this period?" while the balance sheet answers "what does the business own and owe as of this specific moment?" Together, alongside the cash flow statement (Lesson 45), these three statements provide a comprehensive picture of a business's financial position and performance — a picture that even a business owner not planning to become an accountant benefits from being able to read and interpret at a basic level.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her business's income statement for the past quarter, Marisol sees her total revenue, her various expense categories, and her resulting profit for that specific three-month period. Separately, reviewing her balance sheet as of the last day of that same quarter, she sees a snapshot of her business's assets (cash, inventory, equipment), liabilities (any outstanding loan balances, unpaid vendor bills), and her resulting owner's equity — a different, complementary view of her business's financial position at that specific moment, rather than its performance over the preceding period.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does an income statement generally show?
   a) A snapshot of assets and liabilities at one moment
   b) Revenue, expenses, and resulting profit or loss over a specific period
   c) Only the business's cash balance
   d) A tax filing form
2. What does a balance sheet generally show?
   a) Performance over a period of time
   b) Assets, liabilities, and owner's equity at a specific point in time
   c) Only the business's marketing budget
   d) A type of insurance policy
3. True or False: An income statement and a balance sheet show exactly the same information in the same format.
4. What distinct information did Marisol's income statement and balance sheet each provide?
   a) Identical information with no distinction
   b) The income statement showed performance over the quarter; the balance sheet showed her financial position at that specific moment
   c) Neither statement provided any useful information
   d) Only the balance sheet was relevant to her business

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An income statement shows revenue, expenses, and resulting profit or loss over a specific period.
2. A balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and owner's equity at a specific point in time.
3. These statements serve different, complementary purposes — one covers a period, the other a specific moment.
4. Marisol's two statements provided distinct, complementary views: performance over time and position at a specific moment.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An income statement shows revenue, expenses, and profit or loss over a specific period of time.
- A balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and owner's equity at a specific point in time.
- These statements answer different questions: performance over time versus position at a moment.
- Basic financial statement literacy benefits any business owner, not just those pursuing formal accounting training.
