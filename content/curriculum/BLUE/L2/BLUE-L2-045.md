## BLUE-L2-045
### Reading Basic Financial Statements: Cash Flow Statement

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define the general purpose of a cash flow statement.
- Explain how it complements the income statement and balance sheet.
- Connect this concept directly back to the cash flow vs. profit discussion in Lesson 11.

**2. Core Content**
Completing the trio introduced across Lessons 44–45, a cash flow statement shows the actual movement of cash into and out of a business over a specific period, categorized generally into operating activities (cash from core business operations), investing activities (cash used for or generated from investments like equipment purchases), and financing activities (cash from or used for loans, owner contributions, or distributions). This statement directly addresses the profit-versus-cash-flow distinction from Lesson 11: a business can show a profit on its income statement while its cash flow statement reveals that actual cash decreased during the same period, due to timing differences discussed throughout this level.

Together, the income statement (performance over a period), balance sheet (position at a moment), and cash flow statement (actual cash movement over a period) provide the comprehensive financial picture referenced in Lesson 44 — a business owner who understands all three is far better equipped to recognize a cash flow problem developing before it becomes a genuine crisis, rather than being caught by surprise despite a profitable income statement.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her cash flow statement alongside her income statement for the same quarter, Marisol notices her income statement shows a healthy profit, but her cash flow statement reveals her actual cash balance decreased during the period, specifically due to a combination of unpaid customer invoices (operating activities) and a recent equipment purchase (investing activities). This combined view, rather than the income statement alone, gives Marisol the complete picture she needs to understand why her bank balance feels tighter than her "profitable" income statement alone might suggest, directly illustrating the Lesson 11 concept using her own actual financial statements.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a cash flow statement generally show?
   a) Only assets and liabilities at a moment in time
   b) The actual movement of cash into and out of a business over a specific period
   c) Only the business's tax obligations
   d) A type of insurance policy
2. What are the three general categories within a cash flow statement?
   a) Revenue, expenses, and profit
   b) Operating, investing, and financing activities
   c) Assets, liabilities, and equity
   d) Marketing, sales, and production
3. True or False: A business showing a profit on its income statement is guaranteed to show increased cash on its cash flow statement for the same period.
4. What did Marisol's combined review of her income statement and cash flow statement reveal?
   a) Nothing useful
   b) A profitable income statement alongside an actual cash decrease, due to unpaid invoices and an equipment purchase
   c) That her business had no cash flow at all
   d) That her income statement was inaccurate

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A cash flow statement shows the actual movement of cash into and out of a business over a specific period.
2. Operating, investing, and financing activities are the three general categories within a cash flow statement.
3. A profitable income statement does not guarantee increased cash, due to timing differences discussed throughout this level.
4. Marisol's combined statement review revealed the specific causes behind her profitable-but-cash-tight quarter.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A cash flow statement shows actual cash movement over a period, categorized into operating, investing, and financing activities.
- This statement directly addresses the profit-versus-cash-flow distinction introduced in Lesson 11.
- Together with the income statement and balance sheet, it completes the comprehensive financial picture.
- Understanding all three statements helps a business owner recognize cash flow problems before they become crises.
