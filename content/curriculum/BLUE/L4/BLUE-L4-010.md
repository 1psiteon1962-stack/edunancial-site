# Vesting Schedule Analysis: Why Equity Compensation Is Earned Over Time, Not Granted All at Once

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a populous nation's real estate and consumer credit markets, after years of rapid expansion, beginning to show signs of a sharp correction as defaults start rising. As a credit-fueled hiring boom unwinds and companies reassess their workforce, employees who joined recently discover that a large portion of the equity compensation they were promised has not actually been earned yet. This lesson explains the mechanism that determines exactly how much equity an employee has genuinely earned at any given point.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain what a vesting schedule is and why equity compensation is typically structured this way.
- Explain what a 'cliff' means in a standard vesting schedule.
- Calculate how much equity an employee has vested at a given point under a standard schedule.
- Explain why vesting schedules are designed to align incentives between the company and equity recipients.

## Core Content
A vesting schedule determines the rate at which an employee, founder, or other recipient of equity compensation actually earns full ownership rights to their granted shares or options over time, rather than receiving full ownership immediately upon the grant. VERIFIED FACT: a very common standard structure in many industries is a four-year vesting schedule with a one-year "cliff" — meaning the recipient earns no vested shares at all during the first year of their tenure, and then, upon reaching exactly one year, a significant portion (commonly 25%) vests all at once, with the remaining shares typically vesting monthly or quarterly over the following three years.

Here is the "why" equity compensation is structured to vest over time rather than being granted in full immediately: vesting is specifically designed to align the incentives of the equity recipient with the long-term interests and commitment the company is seeking, similar in underlying purpose to the earnout structures sometimes used in business acquisitions — if equity were granted in full immediately with no vesting requirement, a recipient could depart the company shortly after receiving the grant while still retaining the entire equity award, undermining the primary purpose of using equity compensation to encourage sustained, long-term contribution and commitment.

This is why the vesting cliff specifically protects the company against a very early departure, relevant to the workforce reassessment scenario described above: an employee who departs (or is let go) before reaching their one-year cliff typically forfeits their entire equity grant, having vested nothing at all, while an employee who departs shortly after their cliff has already earned a meaningful, though still partial, portion of their overall grant. This is why understanding one's own vesting schedule and current vested percentage is an important piece of financial literacy for any equity compensation recipient, since the actual, currently-owned value of an equity grant, at any given point before full vesting is complete, can be significantly less than the total headline grant value initially communicated.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: An employee is granted 40,000 stock options under a standard four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff. If the employee departs the company after 10 months, before reaching the one-year cliff, they have vested nothing and forfeit the entire 40,000-option grant. If the employee instead stays for exactly 18 months, they have passed the one-year cliff (vesting 25%, or 10,000 options, all at once at the 12-month mark) and vested an additional 6 months of monthly vesting on the remaining 30,000 options (roughly 3,750 more options, since the remaining shares vest over 36 months), giving them a total of roughly 13,750 vested options out of their original 40,000-option grant — meaningfully less than the full headline grant, illustrating why the vesting schedule, not just the total grant size, determines an employee's actual currently-owned equity value at any given point.

**Q4. What happens to an employee's equity grant if they depart before reaching the one-year cliff?**

**Q5. In the example, why did the employee who departed after 18 months have only about 13,750 vested options rather than the full 40,000-option grant?**
A) The employee had actually forfeited their entire grant by departing before four years
B) The vesting schedule meant only the cliff portion (25%) plus a proportional amount of monthly vesting on the remaining shares had been earned by the 18-month mark, well short of the full four-year vesting period
C) Vesting schedules do not affect the total number of options an employee has earned at any point
D) The employee received additional options beyond their original grant due to their early departure

## Answer Key
Q1: B
Q2: B
Q3: B
Q4: B
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Vesting specifically governs the timing and pace at which granted equity becomes genuinely, fully owned by the recipient.

**Q2.** The cliff specifically describes this initial no-vesting period followed by a lump-sum vesting event at a defined milestone, commonly the one-year mark.

**Q3.** The incentive-alignment purpose of vesting directly explains why gradual earning over time, rather than immediate full ownership, is the standard structure.

**Q4.** The cliff structure specifically means no equity has been earned at all if departure occurs before reaching that initial milestone.

**Q5.** Since full vesting requires the complete four-year schedule, departing at the 18-month mark meant only a fraction of the total grant had actually vested by that point.

## Key Takeaways
- A vesting schedule determines the rate at which equity compensation is actually earned over time, rather than granted immediately in full.
- A common structure includes a one-year cliff, after which a significant portion vests at once, followed by ongoing vesting over the following years.
- Vesting is designed to align recipient incentives with sustained, long-term commitment to the company.
- An equity grant's actual currently-owned value, before full vesting, can be significantly less than its total headline grant value.
