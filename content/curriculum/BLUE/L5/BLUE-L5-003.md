# Business Acquisition Financing: Orchestrating SBA Loans, Seller Financing, and Strategic Debt Framework

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation. As a central bank's sharply rising interest rates change the genuine tradeoffs among different acquisition financing structures, a prospective buyer discovers that orchestrating SBA loan terms, seller financing, and this curriculum's strategic-debt framework together is essential to structuring a genuinely sound acquisition.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the general structure of an SBA-backed acquisition loan, REGION-SPECIFIC RULE given this program's jurisdiction-specific nature, and why this financing typically requires a meaningful down payment alongside government-guaranteed lender financing.
- Explain seller financing as an alternative or supplementary structure, in which the seller themselves finances a portion of the purchase price, and why this can signal genuine seller confidence in the business's own continued performance.
- Connect business acquisition debt directly to this curriculum's strategic-versus-consumer debt distinction, explaining why debt used to acquire a genuinely income-producing business functions as strategic, productive debt.
- Explain why a buyer should orchestrate the combination of SBA financing, seller financing, and their own available capital together as a single, integrated capital stack, rather than evaluating each financing source in isolation.

## Core Content
REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, confirm current specific SBA or equivalent government-backed lending program requirements for your jurisdiction, since this is a technical, jurisdiction-specific program, but VERIFIED FACT, this kind of government-backed acquisition loan typically requires a meaningful down payment from the buyer alongside government-guaranteed lender financing, meaning this financing structure generally reduces, but does not eliminate, the buyer's own required capital contribution to a specific acquisition.

Here is the "why" seller financing can signal genuine seller confidence in the business's own continued performance: seller financing generally involves the seller themselves financing a portion of the purchase price, to be repaid by the buyer over time out of the business's own future earnings, meaning a seller's genuine willingness to accept this kind of deferred payment, with their own repayment depending on the business's own continued success under new ownership, can represent a meaningful, credible signal of that seller's own genuine confidence in the business's continued viability, since a seller who does not genuinely believe in the business's continued prospects would have less incentive to accept this kind of deferred, performance-dependent payment structure. This is why business acquisition debt connects directly to this curriculum's strategic-versus-consumer debt distinction: debt used specifically to acquire a genuinely income-producing business represents strategic, productive debt within this curriculum's earlier framework, since the acquired business's own ongoing earnings are specifically intended to service this debt, meaning a buyer should specifically orchestrate this strategic debt framework into their overall financing analysis, confirming the business's own genuine, realistic earnings capacity can actually service the combined debt obligations this financing structure would create. This is why a buyer should orchestrate the combination of financing sources together as a single, integrated capital stack: a genuine acquisition financing structure often combines SBA or equivalent lender financing, seller financing, and the buyer's own available capital together, meaning a buyer should specifically orchestrate this full combination as a single, integrated capital stack, confirming the combined debt service obligation across all these sources remains genuinely serviceable by the business's own realistic earnings, rather than evaluating any single financing source in isolation from the others.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A prospective buyer structures an acquisition combining a meaningful personal down payment, government-backed lender financing for a substantial portion of the purchase price, and a seller-financed note for the remainder, specifically orchestrating this full capital stack against the business's own realistic, honestly projected earnings capacity. This buyer also recognizes the seller's willingness to accept deferred, performance-dependent payment as a genuine, credible signal of the seller's own confidence in the business's continued prospects, rather than evaluating the SBA financing, seller financing, and personal capital contribution as three separate, unrelated decisions.
