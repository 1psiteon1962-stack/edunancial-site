## Core Content
Extending this curriculum's earlier RED-L5 Lesson 4 entity structure discussion to a general business ownership context, VERIFIED FACT, choosing among sole proprietorship, LLC, S-corporation, and C-corporation involves a genuine tradeoff among liability protection, tax treatment, and financing access, precisely paralleling that earlier discussion's finding regarding real estate holding entities, meaning a decision optimized around any single one of these three considerations in isolation frequently comes at a genuine cost to the other two.

Here is the "why" operating as a sole proprietorship provides no genuine liability protection: a sole proprietorship represents no genuine legal separation between the business and its owner, meaning the owner's personal assets remain directly, fully exposed to any business liability, unlike an LLC or corporation, which generally provides a genuine legal separation shielding the owner's personal assets from business liabilities, provided the entity's own required formalities are genuinely, consistently maintained, echoing the earlier discussion's finding regarding formalities being essential to genuine liability protection. This is why the general tax treatment differences among these structures matter, REGION-SPECIFIC RULE given this area's technical nature: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, confirm current specific entity tax treatment rules for your jurisdiction, since these vary and change, but as a general matter, a sole proprietorship, an LLC, and an S-corporation often provide pass-through taxation (business income taxed once, at the owner's individual level), while a C-corporation can involve potential double taxation (taxed once at the corporate level and again when profits are distributed to owners), meaning this genuine tax treatment difference should be orchestrated into the entity structure decision alongside liability protection. This is why a specific entity structure's effect on financing access should be orchestrated alongside liability protection and tax treatment: lenders and outside investors may have genuine preferences or requirements regarding entity structure, since some outside investors specifically prefer or require a corporate structure for their own investment, meaning a business owner's own entity structure choice should specifically orchestrate this genuine financing access consideration alongside liability protection and tax treatment, rather than optimizing for any single consideration without regard to how it affects the other two.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A new business owner orchestrates all three considerations together: recognizing that an LLC would provide genuine liability protection provided its formalities are actually maintained, that this structure would likely provide favorable pass-through tax treatment, and confirming this structure remains compatible with the specific financing sources this owner intends to pursue. Weighing all three factors together, rather than choosing an entity structure based on tax treatment alone, this owner selects a structure genuinely compatible with all three considerations, rather than one optimized for a single factor that would have created complications elsewhere.
