# Risk Tolerance and Time Horizon

## Learning Objectives
- Define risk tolerance and time horizon in an investing context
- Explain how the two concepts relate to each other
- Practice matching an appropriate risk level to a given time horizon

## Core Content

Risk tolerance is how much price fluctuation and potential loss an investor is willing and able to accept in pursuit of growth. Time horizon is how soon the invested money will actually be needed. These two concepts are closely connected: a longer time horizon generally allows for more risk tolerance, because there's more time for the investment to recover from a downturn before the money is actually needed. A shorter time horizon generally calls for lower risk tolerance, because there's less time to recover if the value drops right before the money is needed.

This connects back to the earlier Make/Keep/Grow/Pass On lesson — "Grow" money, meant for long-term goals, can typically absorb more short-term volatility than "Keep" money, meant for near-term needs. Mismatching risk tolerance and time horizon — taking on high risk with money needed soon, or being overly cautious with money that has decades to grow — usually produces a worse outcome than matching them appropriately.

## Worked Example

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A 15-year-old adding gold to a growing position, with the money intended for a goal ten or more years away, can reasonably tolerate more short-term price fluctuation, since there's substantial time for any downturn to recover before the money is needed. Compare that to someone who needs a specific amount of money in six months for a known, fixed expense — that money should generally sit in something far more stable, because a downturn right before it's needed would leave no time to recover.

- Risk tolerance and time horizon are closely connected — longer horizons generally allow for more risk.
- Money needed soon should generally carry less risk, since there's less time to recover from a downturn.
- Matching risk tolerance appropriately to time horizon, rather than mismatching them, tends to produce better outcomes.
