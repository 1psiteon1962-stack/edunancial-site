# Setting an Investment Budget You Can Afford

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why investing more than you can sustainably afford creates real risk
- Identify basic factors to consider when setting an investment budget
- Practice a simple approach to determining a sustainable investment amount

## Core Content

Before investing consistently, it's worth deliberately deciding how much can genuinely be invested on a sustainable, ongoing basis — not the maximum theoretically possible in a good month, but an amount that can reasonably continue even during a leaner period. Investing more than is truly sustainable creates real risk: it can lead to needing to sell investments at a bad time to cover an unrelated need, or to abandoning the habit entirely during a difficult stretch, both of which work against the consistency this track has emphasized as so valuable.

A simple approach: first ensure basic needs and, where possible, some emergency savings (the "Keep" function from earlier) are reasonably covered, then determine an investment amount from what remains that could realistically continue even if income or circumstances became somewhat tighter. This amount can always increase later as circumstances allow — starting conservatively and increasing over time tends to build a more durable habit than starting aggressively and having to cut back or stop entirely.

## Worked Example

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: In the case study, starting silver purchases at age 11 with a modest, genuinely sustainable amount meant the habit could continue consistently for years without strain, rather than starting with an amount that might have felt exciting initially but proved difficult to sustain. This modest, sustainable starting budget is part of what allowed years of consistent dollar-cost averaging to actually happen, building the foundation the later gold purchases at age 15 were added onto.
