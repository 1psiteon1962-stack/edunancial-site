# Precious Metals vs. Other Asset Classes

## Learning Objectives
- Compare precious metals to stocks, bonds, and cash at a basic level
- Identify the distinct role precious metals typically play in a broader financial plan
- Understand why this comparison isn't about ranking assets as "better" or "worse"

## Core Content

Every asset class serves a somewhat different purpose, and precious metals are no exception. Stocks represent ownership in a company and can generate returns both through price appreciation and dividends, generally carrying higher long-term growth potential alongside higher volatility. Bonds represent a loan to a government or company, generally offering more predictable income and lower volatility than stocks, but typically lower long-term growth potential. Cash is the most liquid and stable in nominal terms, but loses purchasing power over time to inflation, as covered earlier in this track.

Precious metals, as covered throughout this track, function primarily as a store of value and potential inflation hedge, without generating income the way stocks or bonds can, and with real practical costs (storage, premiums, the buy-sell spread) that don't apply to those other asset types. This means precious metals typically play a different role in a financial plan than stocks or bonds do — less about generating primary growth or income, and more about preserving value and diversifying against risks that might affect currency or other asset classes differently.

This comparison isn't about declaring any single asset class universally "better" — each serves a different function, and a well-considered financial plan (a topic covered more extensively in other tracks of this curriculum) often includes a thoughtful mix based on an individual's specific goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

## Worked Example

An investor building a long-term financial plan might hold stocks for primary long-term growth, bonds for more stability and some income, a portion of cash for near-term liquidity needs, and precious metals specifically as a store of value and inflation hedge — each asset class serving a distinct role within the overall plan, rather than competing to be the single "best" investment on its own.
