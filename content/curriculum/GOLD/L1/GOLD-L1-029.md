# Correlation With the Stock Market

## Learning Objectives
- Define correlation in the context of investing
- Explain the general historical tendency of precious metals relative to stock market movements
- Understand why this relationship supports the diversification concept

## Core Content

Correlation describes how closely two assets tend to move in relation to each other. Two highly correlated assets tend to rise and fall together; two assets with low or negative correlation tend to move more independently of each other, or even in opposite directions during certain periods.

Precious metals have historically tended to show relatively low, and at times negative, correlation with stocks — meaning that during some periods when stock markets have declined significantly, precious metals prices have held steady or even risen, though this relationship isn't perfectly consistent or guaranteed in every market period. This general tendency is part of why precious metals are often discussed specifically in the context of diversification, introduced briefly in an earlier lesson.

Diversification works precisely because not all assets move in perfect lockstep — holding a mix of assets with different correlation patterns can reduce a portfolio's overall volatility, since a decline in one asset type may be partially offset by stability or gains in another. This is a general, historically observed tendency rather than a guarantee for any specific future period, consistent with the cautious framing this track has applied throughout to historical patterns.

## Worked Example

During a period of significant stock market decline driven by broad economic uncertainty, precious metals have, at various points in history, held their value or even risen, as investors sought a perceived safer store of value during that uncertainty. This doesn't happen in every single market downturn, and it's not something to count on with certainty for any specific future event, but this general historical tendency is part of the reasoning some investors use for including precious metals as one piece of a diversified portfolio.
