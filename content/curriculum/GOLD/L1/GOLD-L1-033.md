# How to Start Buying: First Steps

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the practical steps involved in making a first precious metals purchase
- Apply concepts from earlier lessons in this track to a real first purchase decision
- Recognize that starting small and simple is a reasonable, common approach

## Core Content

Making a first precious metals purchase can feel intimidating given everything covered in this track so far, but the practical steps are fairly straightforward when broken down: decide on a starting budget (even a small one, consistent with the accessibility lessons earlier in this track), choose a metal (silver, given its lower entry price, is a common and reasonable starting point, as illustrated by the biographical case study), identify a reputable dealer (connecting to the authentication lesson), and choose a simple, widely recognized product (a standard coin, connecting to the coins-vs-bars lesson) for that first purchase.

There's no requirement to have every concept from this track fully mastered before making a first purchase. Starting with something modest and simple — the same approach illustrated throughout the biographical case study — allows an investor to build genuine, practical familiarity with the process itself, which is valuable in its own right, separate from the specific dollar amount involved.

This first purchase can also be an opportunity to begin the tracking habit covered earlier in this track, starting a simple record from the very first transaction rather than trying to reconstruct that history later.

## Worked Example

Someone ready to make a first purchase, applying this track's lessons, might decide on a modest budget, choose a single one-ounce silver coin from a well-known, reputable dealer, and record that first purchase in a simple tracking log — date, price paid, quantity — mirroring the exact starting approach illustrated by the biographical case study at age 11, just applied to their own specific circumstances and starting point.
