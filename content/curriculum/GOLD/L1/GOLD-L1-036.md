# Diversification: Where Metals Fit in a Broader Plan

## Learning Objectives
- Define diversification and explain its basic purpose
- Understand the general concept of precious metals as one modest piece of a broader portfolio
- Recognize why concentrating entirely in one asset class carries added risk

## Core Content

Diversification means spreading investments across different asset types so that a decline in any single one doesn't disproportionately damage an entire portfolio. This concept, introduced briefly in earlier lessons through the correlation discussion, applies directly to how precious metals typically fit into a broader financial plan: as one piece of a diversified mix, not as the entire strategy on its own.

Financial professionals and educational resources often discuss precious metals as occupying a modest portion of a well-diversified portfolio — commonly cited illustrative ranges in general financial education fall in the single digits to low double-digit percentages of total investable assets, though the right amount for any individual depends entirely on their specific goals, risk tolerance, and overall financial picture, and shouldn't be treated as a fixed, universal rule.

Concentrating entirely, or very heavily, in any single asset class — including precious metals — carries added risk precisely because it forgoes the risk-reduction benefit that diversification specifically provides. This doesn't mean precious metals can't play a meaningful, valuable role in a financial plan, as the biographical case study illustrates — it means that role is typically as one thoughtful piece of a broader picture, not the entire picture itself.

## Worked Example

Rather than putting all available investment capital into precious metals, a thoughtfully diversified approach — illustrated conceptually, not as specific individual advice — might include stocks for long-term growth, bonds for stability, and a modest, deliberate allocation to precious metals for the store-of-value and diversification benefits covered throughout this track, with each piece serving its own distinct role within an overall plan tailored to the individual's actual goals and circumstances.

- Precious metals typically occupy a modest portion of a well-diversified portfolio, not the entire strategy.
- There's no single universal allocation percentage — the right amount depends on individual goals, risk tolerance, and overall financial circumstances.
