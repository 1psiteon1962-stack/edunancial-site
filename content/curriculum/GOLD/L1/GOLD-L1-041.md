# Building a DCA Plan Step by Step

## Learning Objectives
- Apply the DCA concept into a concrete, step-by-step personal framework
- Practice calculating a simple DCA schedule based on a hypothetical budget
- Synthesize earlier lessons on DCA, tracking, and planning into one applied exercise

## Core Content

Building on the DCA concept introduced earlier in this track, a concrete step-by-step DCA plan involves: determining a total amount available for investing over a set period (say, a year), dividing that amount into equal, regular installments (monthly is common and manageable for most people), choosing a fixed purchase date each period (rather than trying to pick an "ideal" day, which reintroduces the market-timing problem DCA is meant to avoid), and committing to following that schedule regardless of the specific price on each purchase date.

This step-by-step structure removes ambiguity and decision fatigue from the process — rather than deciding fresh each time whether "now" is a good time to buy, the schedule itself makes that decision automatically, freeing the investor from a decision that, as covered earlier in this track, even professional investors can't reliably make well in the short term.

This structure also naturally supports the tracking habit covered earlier — a fixed schedule makes it easy to build a consistent, comparable tracking log, since each entry follows the same regular interval and format.

## Worked Example

An investor with $600 available for the year might build the following DCA plan: $50 invested in silver on the 1st of every month, for 12 months, from the same reputable dealer, tracked in the same simple log each time. Some months, that $50 buys slightly more silver (when prices are lower); other months, slightly less (when prices are higher) — but the schedule itself never changes based on the current price, consistent with the core DCA principle from earlier in this track.
