# Teaching the Next Generation: An Introduction to "Pass On"

## Learning Objectives
- Revisit the "Pass On" function from the framework introduced in lesson one
- Explain why teaching investing concepts to others is itself a form of "passing on" value
- Reflect on how knowledge, not just assets, can be part of a Pass On legacy

## Core Content

This track opened with the Make/Keep/Grow/Pass On framework, and has focused heavily on "Grow" throughout. This lesson previews the fourth function — Pass On — which later levels of this track will cover in more depth, particularly around formal tools like wills, trusts, and beneficiary planning.

At this foundational level, it's worth recognizing that "Pass On" isn't only about transferring assets after death — it also includes passing on knowledge, habits, and financial understanding to the next generation while still very much present to teach it directly. A young person who's learned to dollar-cost average, understand risk and time horizon, and build a consistent investing habit has received something valuable that will likely matter more over their lifetime than any single asset transferred later — the capability to build and manage wealth themselves, not just wealth handed to them.

## Worked Example

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: The entire case study running through this track — the son's accumulation of silver from age 11 and gold from age 15 — is itself a real-world example of Pass On in action, not through inheritance, but through direct teaching. The knowledge of dollar-cost averaging, risk tolerance, diversification, and consistent habit-building being passed on through this shared learning experience is a form of generational wealth transfer that doesn't require waiting for a formal estate transfer to matter.
