## GOLD-L2-004
### Income vs. Wealth: Why High Earners Aren't Always Wealthy

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish income from wealth directly.
- Explain why a high income does not automatically produce high net worth.
- Apply this distinction to a comparative scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 2's net worth discussion, this lesson addresses a common and consequential confusion: income (money earned over a period) and wealth (accumulated net worth) are related but distinct, and a high income does not automatically produce high wealth. A high earner who spends most or all of their income — echoing GREEN-L2-027's lifestyle inflation preview, developed further in Lesson 27 — may accumulate relatively little net worth over time, while a more modest earner who consistently saves and invests a meaningful portion of their income can accumulate substantial wealth over the same period.

This distinction is central to this level's entire framing: GOLD-L2 is not about maximizing income (a topic touched on elsewhere in this curriculum), but about the gap between income and expenses (developed in Lesson 5) and what happens to that gap — whether it is spent, saved, or invested — that actually determines long-term wealth accumulation.

**3. Worked Example**
Comparing two colleagues with significantly different incomes, a learner observes that the higher earner, who spends close to their entire income on an expansive lifestyle, has accumulated relatively little net worth over a decade, while the more modest earner, who has consistently saved and invested a meaningful portion of their income throughout that same decade, has accumulated substantially more net worth despite the lower income. This comparison illustrates directly why this level's focus rests on the gap between income and expenses, not income alone.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general relationship between income and wealth, per this lesson?
   a) They are identical concepts
   b) They are related but distinct — income measures money earned, wealth measures accumulated net worth
   c) High income always guarantees high wealth
   d) Income has no relationship to wealth whatsoever
2. Why might a high earner accumulate relatively little net worth over time?
   a) High earners always accumulate the most wealth automatically
   b) Spending most or all of their income leaves little gap available for saving or investing
   c) Income and spending have no relationship to net worth
   d) This never actually happens
3. True or False: This level's central focus is on maximizing income alone, rather than the gap between income and expenses.
4. What did the comparison between the two colleagues illustrate?
   a) Income alone determines net worth
   b) The gap between income and expenses, not income alone, drives long-term wealth accumulation
   c) The higher earner always accumulates more wealth
   d) Spending habits have no effect on net worth

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Income and wealth are related but distinct — income measures earnings over a period, wealth measures accumulated net worth.
2. A high earner spending most or all of their income leaves little gap available for saving or investing, limiting wealth accumulation.
3. This level's central focus is the gap between income and expenses, not income maximization alone.
4. The comparison illustrated that the income-expense gap, not income alone, drove the more modest earner's greater wealth accumulation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Income measures money earned over a period; wealth measures accumulated net worth.
- A high income does not automatically produce high wealth, particularly if spending consumes most or all of it.
- This level's central focus is the gap between income and expenses, developed further in Lesson 5.
- A more modest earner who consistently saves and invests can out-accumulate a higher earner who does not.
