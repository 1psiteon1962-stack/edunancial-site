## GOLD-L2-011
### Evaluating an Investment Opportunity: A Framework

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Assemble a general framework for evaluating any investment opportunity.
- Apply this framework across different asset classes.
- Explain why a consistent framework matters more than asset-class-specific knowledge alone.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson introduces a general evaluation framework applicable across the asset classes this level integrates: What is the expected return, and is it consistent with the risk involved (Lesson 7)? What is the liquidity profile — how easily can this investment be converted back to cash if needed (developed further in Lesson 14)? What is the time horizon required, and does it match the investor's own goals (echoing WHITE-L2-027)? What are the fees and costs involved (echoing WHITE-L2-015 and Lesson 33)? And who is presenting this opportunity, and what is their track record and incentive structure (echoing BLUE-L2-029's sponsor evaluation discussion)?

This framework's value lies in its consistency across asset classes: whether evaluating a real estate syndication (RED-L2-029), a business investment (BLUE-L2-039), or a paper asset opportunity (WHITE-L2), the same core questions apply, even though the specific answers and relevant details differ by asset class. This consistency is precisely what allows an investor to evaluate an unfamiliar opportunity type without needing to become an expert in every possible asset class first.

**3. Worked Example**
Presented with an unfamiliar investment opportunity in an asset class she has not previously considered, a learner applies this consistent framework rather than feeling unqualified to evaluate it simply because it falls outside her prior experience: she asks about expected return relative to risk, liquidity terms, required time horizon, all associated fees, and the sponsor's track record and incentive alignment. This structured approach allows her to ask informed, probing questions and reach a reasoned conclusion, even without prior specific expertise in this particular asset class.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Asking about expected return relative to risk is a core question in this lesson's evaluation framework.
2. This framework's consistency allows evaluation of unfamiliar opportunity types without requiring prior expertise in every asset class.
3. This framework applies across asset classes, including real estate, business, and paper asset opportunities.
4. The learner's application of the consistent framework allowed informed evaluation despite her lack of prior specific experience with that asset class.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A consistent evaluation framework addresses expected return/risk, liquidity, time horizon, fees, and sponsor track record.
- This framework applies across asset classes, from real estate to business to paper assets.
- Its consistency allows evaluating unfamiliar opportunity types without requiring prior expertise in every asset class.
- This framework sets up the specific red-flag and fraud-recognition discussion in Lessons 12–13.
