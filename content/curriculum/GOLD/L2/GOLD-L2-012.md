## GOLD-L2-012
### Red Flags in Investment Opportunities: Recognizing Warning Signs

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify common red flags in investment opportunities.
- Connect these red flags directly to Lesson 7's risk-return principle.
- Apply this recognition to a hypothetical suspicious opportunity.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 7's risk-return principle and Lesson 11's evaluation framework, this lesson identifies common warning signs worth specific attention. These include: claims of guaranteed or unusually high returns with little or no acknowledged risk (directly contradicting Lesson 7's foundational principle), pressure to invest quickly before "missing out" (undermining the careful evaluation process from Lesson 11), vague or evasive answers about how returns are actually generated, unregistered or unlicensed sellers where registration would normally be expected, and difficulty verifying the opportunity's or sponsor's actual track record independently.

None of these red flags alone definitively proves a problem, but the presence of multiple red flags together should significantly heighten scrutiny, echoing this level's broader theme that careful, deliberate evaluation (Lesson 11) protects against exactly the kind of pressure and evasiveness these warning signs represent — pressure and evasiveness are themselves信号 designed to short-circuit the careful evaluation process this level has built.

**3. Worked Example**
Presented with an investment opportunity promising unusually high, guaranteed returns, combined with pressure to decide within 24 hours and vague, evasive answers about exactly how the promised returns would be generated, a learner recognizes multiple red flags simultaneously: the guaranteed-high-return claim contradicts Lesson 7's risk-return principle, the time pressure undermines Lesson 11's careful evaluation process, and the evasive answers prevent genuine due diligence. Rather than proceeding despite her discomfort, she declines the opportunity, recognizing that the combination of these warning signs together, not any single one alone, provided sufficient reason for serious concern.

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Pressure to invest quickly before "missing out" is identified as a common red flag in this lesson.
2. Time pressure undermines the careful, deliberate evaluation process this level has built, functioning as a specific warning sign.
3. No single red flag alone definitively proves fraud, but multiple red flags together should significantly heighten scrutiny.
4. The learner's decision reflected the combined weight of multiple red flags, not any single warning sign in isolation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Common red flags include guaranteed high returns, time pressure, evasive answers, unregistered sellers, and unverifiable track records.
- These connect directly to Lesson 7's risk-return principle and Lesson 11's careful evaluation framework.
- No single red flag alone definitively proves a problem, but multiple red flags together warrant significant scrutiny.
- Pressure and evasiveness are themselves designed to short-circuit careful evaluation, making recognizing them essential.
