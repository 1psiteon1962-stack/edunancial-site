## GOLD-L2-015
### Time Horizon Revisited: Matching Assets to Goals

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce the WHITE-L2-027 time horizon discussion at the integrative, multi-asset-class level.
- Explain how different goals with different time horizons might warrant different asset classes, not just different allocations within one.
- Apply this concept to a multi-goal wealth-building scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on WHITE-L2-027's discussion of multiple time horizons within paper assets, this lesson extends the same principle across the broader range of asset classes this GOLD-track level integrates. Just as a retirement goal and a near-term home down payment goal might warrant different paper asset allocations, different goals might reasonably warrant entirely different asset classes altogether: a long-term wealth-building goal might comfortably accommodate the illiquidity of real estate (Lesson 14) or business equity, while a shorter-term goal requiring more certain, liquid access to funds would generally favor more liquid paper assets or cash equivalents instead.

This reinforces a core Level 2 skill applied at the widest scale this curriculum offers: rather than asking "what is the best investment" in the abstract, the more useful question is "what asset class and specific investment best fits this particular goal's specific time horizon and liquidity needs" — a question that can produce different, equally valid answers for different goals held by the exact same individual simultaneously.

**3. Worked Example**
Planning for both a long-term retirement goal and a shorter-term goal of purchasing a business within the next few years, a learner recognizes these two goals reasonably warrant different asset class choices: her retirement funds, with a multi-decade horizon, comfortably accommodate a diversified mix including less liquid real estate and business equity positions, while her business-purchase savings, needed with more certainty within a few years, are held in far more liquid, lower-volatility assets, despite potentially lower expected returns — a deliberate application of matching specific assets to each specific goal's time horizon, rather than applying one uniform strategy across all her wealth.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. This lesson extends WHITE-L2-027's time horizon principle to the broader, multi-asset-class level this GOLD-track integrates.
2. Different time horizons and liquidity needs can favor fundamentally different asset classes, not merely different allocations within one.
3. The best investment choice depends on a specific goal's time horizon and liquidity needs, not an abstract, universal determination.
4. The learner's two goals' different time horizons and liquidity needs appropriately warranted different, matched asset class choices.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- This lesson extends WHITE-L2-027's time horizon principle across the broader range of asset classes this level integrates.
- Different goals can warrant entirely different asset classes, not just different allocations within a single asset class.
- The useful question is not "what is the best investment" abstractly, but "what fits this specific goal's time horizon and liquidity needs."
- The same individual can reasonably apply different asset class strategies to different goals held simultaneously.
