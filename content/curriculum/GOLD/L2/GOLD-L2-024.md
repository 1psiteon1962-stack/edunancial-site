## GOLD-L2-024
### Net Worth Statements: Building Your Own

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the practical structure of a personal net worth statement.
- Apply Lessons 2–3's concepts to build an actual net worth statement.
- Recognize this as a practical tool, not merely an abstract calculation.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lessons 2–3, this lesson moves from the conceptual net worth discussion to the practical exercise of actually building a personal net worth statement: listing all assets (cash, retirement accounts, investment accounts, real estate equity, business equity, and other significant property) in one column and all liabilities (mortgage balances, loans, credit card debt, and other obligations) in another, then calculating the difference to arrive at an actual, personal net worth figure.

This practical exercise transforms the abstract concept from Lesson 2 into a genuinely useful tool: a completed net worth statement provides a concrete baseline against which future progress can be measured (developed further in Lesson 25), reveals concentration or diversification patterns across asset classes (echoing Lesson 9) that might not be obvious without seeing everything listed together, and can surface overlooked liabilities or underappreciated assets that a purely mental accounting might miss.

**3. Worked Example**
Building her first comprehensive net worth statement, a learner lists every asset she can identify — checking and savings balances, retirement account values, her home's estimated equity, and a small investment account — alongside every liability — her remaining mortgage balance, an auto loan, and a credit card balance — arriving at her actual net worth figure. In the process, she discovers she had been mentally overlooking a small forgotten savings account and underestimating her actual credit card balance, illustrating how the concrete, complete exercise revealed information her prior general sense of her finances had missed.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does building a personal net worth statement generally involve?
   a) Only estimating income for the year
   b) Listing all assets and liabilities, then calculating the difference
   c) Only listing liabilities, with no reference to assets
   d) A type of tax filing
2. What can a completed net worth statement reveal, beyond the final number itself?
   a) Nothing beyond the single final figure
   b) Concentration or diversification patterns, and overlooked liabilities or underappreciated assets
   c) Only the person's income level
   d) A guaranteed future net worth projection
3. True or False: A net worth statement is purely an abstract calculation with no practical use beyond the number itself.
4. What did the learner's completed net worth statement reveal that her prior general sense of her finances had missed?
   a) Nothing new was revealed
   b) A forgotten savings account and an underestimated credit card balance
   c) Her exact future income for the next decade
   d) A guaranteed investment return

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Building a net worth statement involves listing all assets and liabilities, then calculating the difference.
2. A completed statement can reveal concentration patterns, overlooked liabilities, or underappreciated assets beyond the single final number.
3. A net worth statement is a genuinely practical tool, not merely an abstract calculation, providing a baseline and revealing patterns.
4. The learner's completed statement surfaced a forgotten savings account and an underestimated credit card balance she had not previously accounted for.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Building a net worth statement involves listing all assets and liabilities, then calculating the difference.
- This practical exercise transforms Lesson 2's abstract concept into a genuinely useful, concrete tool.
- A completed statement can reveal concentration patterns and overlooked liabilities or assets.
- This provides a concrete baseline for measuring future progress, developed further in Lesson 25.
