## GOLD-L2-028
### Paying Yourself First: A Wealth-Building Habit

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the "pay yourself first" concept at a practical level.
- Contrast this approach with saving whatever happens to remain after spending.
- Connect this concept to Lesson 27's lifestyle inflation discussion.

**2. Core Content**
"Paying yourself first" means treating saving and investing as a priority obligation paid immediately upon receiving income, before discretionary spending occurs, rather than the more common but less effective approach of spending first and saving only whatever happens to remain afterward. This directly counters Lesson 27's lifestyle inflation risk: when saving happens only from leftover funds, rising expenses (even reasonable-seeming individual increases) can gradually crowd out saving entirely, since spending naturally tends to expand to fill whatever income is available if not deliberately directed elsewhere first.

Practically, this often means automatically directing a specific percentage or amount of each paycheck into savings or investment accounts immediately upon receipt (echoing Lesson 29's automation discussion), treating this allocation with the same non-negotiable priority as a required bill payment, rather than as a discretionary choice made only after other spending decisions have already been made.

**3. Worked Example**
Restructuring her financial habits, the learner from Lesson 27 sets up an automatic transfer directing a specific percentage of each paycheck directly into her investment account immediately upon deposit, before any of her regular spending occurs — treating this transfer with the same priority as her rent or mortgage payment, rather than as money she might get around to saving if anything remains at the end of the month. This restructuring directly prevents her previously observed lifestyle inflation pattern from silently consuming her entire income increase going forward.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does "paying yourself first" generally mean?
   a) Spending first, then saving whatever happens to remain
   b) Treating saving and investing as a priority obligation paid immediately upon receiving income, before discretionary spending
   c) Never saving any portion of income at all
   d) A type of tax filing status
2. How does this approach directly counter Lesson 27's lifestyle inflation risk?
   a) It has no relationship to lifestyle inflation
   b) Prioritizing saving before spending prevents rising expenses from gradually crowding out saving entirely
   c) It guarantees spending will never increase at all
   d) It eliminates the need for any budgeting whatsoever
3. True or False: Saving only whatever happens to remain after spending is generally described as equally effective as paying yourself first.
4. Why did the learner set up an automatic transfer immediately upon each paycheck deposit?
   a) She wanted to spend her entire paycheck before considering any saving
   b) Treating the saving allocation with the same priority as a required bill payment directly prevented her previous lifestyle inflation pattern
   c) Automatic transfers have no relationship to saving discipline
   d) She had no interest in countering her previous spending pattern

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. "Paying yourself first" means prioritizing saving and investing immediately upon receiving income, before discretionary spending occurs.
2. Prioritizing saving before spending prevents rising expenses from gradually crowding out saving entirely, directly countering lifestyle inflation.
3. Saving only leftover funds is generally described as less effective than proactively paying yourself first.
4. The learner's automatic, prioritized transfer directly prevented her previous pattern of lifestyle inflation silently consuming her income increases.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- "Paying yourself first" treats saving and investing as a priority obligation paid immediately, before discretionary spending.
- This contrasts with the less effective approach of saving only whatever happens to remain after spending.
- This directly counters Lesson 27's lifestyle inflation risk, since spending naturally expands to fill available income if not directed elsewhere first.
- This practice sets up the automation discussion developed further in Lesson 29.
