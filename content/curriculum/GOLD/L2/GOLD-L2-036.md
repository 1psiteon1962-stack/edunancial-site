## GOLD-L2-036
### Concentration Risk Revisited: A GOLD Perspective

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce the WHITE-L2-024 and RED-L2-038 concentration risk discussions at the integrative level.
- Identify concentration risk sources unique to a comprehensive wealth-building strategy.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical concentrated wealth scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on WHITE-L2-024's sector concentration discussion and RED-L2-038's property diversification discussion, and directly extending Lesson 9's cross-asset-class diversification concept, this lesson addresses concentration risk sources unique to a comprehensive wealth-building strategy: concentration in a single employer (holding significant company stock alongside dependence on that same employer for income, creating a compounded risk if that specific employer struggles), concentration in a single geographic area (property, business, and even employment all tied to one local economy, echoing RED-L2-038's local market risk discussion), and concentration in a single asset class overall (echoing Lesson 9).

This lesson specifically highlights the employer-concentration pattern as a particularly common, sometimes overlooked risk: an employee holding significant company stock, especially if that stock represents a large portion of their overall net worth, faces a compounded risk where both their income and a significant portion of their accumulated wealth depend on the same single employer's fortunes, a concentration this level's integrative framework helps identify and address.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing his overall wealth picture, an employee at a publicly traded company recognizes that a significant portion of his investment portfolio consists of company stock accumulated through employee stock programs, meaning both his income and a substantial portion of his net worth depend on his employer's continued success. Recognizing this as a specific, compounded concentration risk this lesson describes, he develops a plan to gradually diversify his company stock holdings into a broader mix of assets over time, reducing this specific dependency without necessarily eliminating his position entirely all at once.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What concentration risk source does this lesson specifically highlight as particularly common?
   a) Holding a perfectly diversified portfolio with no employer connection
   b) Holding significant company stock in one's own employer, compounding income and wealth dependence on the same source
   c) Concentration risk does not actually exist for any employee
   d) Only geographic concentration matters, never employer concentration
2. Why is employer stock concentration described as "compounded"?
   a) It has no relationship to income at all
   b) Both income and a significant portion of accumulated wealth depend on the same single employer's fortunes
   c) Compounding only applies to investment growth, never to risk
   d) Employer stock carries no risk whatsoever
3. True or False: Holding significant company stock in one's own employer carries no additional risk beyond a diversified investment in an unrelated company.
4. What plan did the employee develop after recognizing his compounded concentration risk?
   a) He decided to increase his company stock holdings further
   b) He developed a plan to gradually diversify his company stock holdings into a broader mix of assets over time
   c) He ignored the risk entirely
   d) He quit his job immediately

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Holding significant company stock in one's own employer, compounding income and wealth dependence, is highlighted as particularly common.
2. This concentration is "compounded" because both income and a significant portion of accumulated wealth depend on the same single employer.
3. Company stock concentration carries additional, compounded risk beyond a diversified investment in an unrelated company.
4. The employee's gradual diversification plan appropriately addressed his compounded concentration risk without an abrupt, all-at-once change.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- This lesson extends WHITE-L2-024 and RED-L2-038's concentration risk discussions to the integrative, whole-wealth level.
- Employer stock concentration is a particularly common, compounded risk, tying both income and wealth to a single source.
- Geographic and single-asset-class concentration also apply at this integrative level.
- Gradual diversification, rather than abrupt, all-at-once changes, can responsibly address identified concentration risks.
