## GOLD-L2-043
### The Wealth-Building Timeline: Accumulation, Growth, and Preservation Phases

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define the general phases of a wealth-building timeline.
- Explain how strategy generally shifts across these phases.
- Connect this concept to Lesson 38's active-versus-passive income discussion.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson formalizes a framework previewed throughout this level: a wealth-building journey generally moves through phases, commonly described as accumulation (early-career years, when active income, Lesson 38, typically dominates and the focus is building the income-expense gap, Lesson 5, and investing it consistently), growth (mid-career years, when accumulated capital itself begins generating meaningful returns and passive income, Lesson 38, becomes increasingly significant), and preservation (later years, when the focus shifts toward protecting accumulated wealth and managing the distribution-phase risks discussed in Lesson 35, rather than primarily continuing to accumulate).

This is a general framework, not a rigid, universal timeline — individual circumstances, goals, and risk tolerance (Lesson 8) affect exactly when and how these phases unfold for a specific person — but recognizing these general phases helps clarify why an appropriate wealth-building strategy reasonably evolves over time, rather than remaining static throughout an individual's entire life.

**3. Worked Example**
Reflecting on his own position within this general framework, a learner in his early career recognizes he is firmly in the accumulation phase, with active income currently dominating and his primary focus on building and consistently investing his income-expense gap. He recognizes that his strategy will reasonably evolve over time — shifting toward the growth phase as his accumulated capital itself begins generating more significant returns, and eventually toward the preservation phase as he approaches a stage where protecting accumulated wealth becomes more important than continued aggressive accumulation.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The accumulation phase generally features active income dominance and a focus on building and investing the income-expense gap.
2. The preservation phase generally focuses on protecting accumulated wealth and managing distribution-phase risks.
3. This is a general framework, not a rigid, universal timeline identical for every individual.
4. The learner's self-assessment appropriately placed him in the accumulation phase, given his current career stage and focus.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A wealth-building journey generally moves through accumulation, growth, and preservation phases.
- The accumulation phase emphasizes active income and building the income-expense gap; preservation emphasizes protection and distribution-risk management.
- This is a general framework, not a rigid, universal timeline, since individual circumstances affect how phases unfold.
- Recognizing these phases clarifies why an appropriate strategy reasonably evolves over an individual's lifetime.
