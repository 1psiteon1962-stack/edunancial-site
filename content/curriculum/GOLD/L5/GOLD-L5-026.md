# Benchmarking Alternative Asset Performance: Orchestrating Genuine Historical Evidence, Survivorship Bias, and Behavioral Discipline

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a mid-sized economy's currency losing a large share of its value against major reserve currencies over a few months, driven by capital fleeing the country and eroding investor confidence. As a mid-sized economy's currency instability causes genuine dispersion in how different alternative assets perform, some promoters cite historical performance claims to support specific recommendations, requiring genuine historical evidence, survivorship bias awareness, and behavioral discipline to be orchestrated together.

## Learning Objectives
- Extend this curriculum's earlier BLUE-L5 benchmarking and best-practice discussion, explaining why genuine historical evidence should still be orchestrated before accepting a specific alternative asset's promoted historical performance claim.
- Explain the general concept of survivorship bias, and why this specific bias can meaningfully distort historical performance claims for certain alternative asset categories, particularly collectibles or specific commodity sub-categories.
- Explain why a specific historical performance claim, even if genuinely accurate for the specific period and item cited, does not guarantee this same performance will actually repeat for a different specific item or future period.
- Explain why an investor should orchestrate honest skepticism regarding promoted historical performance claims alongside behavioral discipline against being drawn in purely by a compelling historical narrative.

## Core Content
Extending this curriculum's earlier BLUE-L5 benchmarking and best-practice discussion, VERIFIED FACT, genuine historical evidence should still be orchestrated before accepting a specific alternative asset's promoted historical performance claim, echoing that earlier discussion's core finding regarding a compelling narrative alone not substituting for genuine historical research, meaning an investor should specifically confirm actual, credible, comprehensive evidence supports a given historical performance claim before treating this claim as a reliable basis for a specific investment decision.

Here is the "why" survivorship bias can meaningfully distort historical performance claims for certain alternative asset categories: survivorship bias generally refers to the genuine tendency for historical performance data to disproportionately reflect only those specific items or categories that happened to survive and perform well, while items or categories that performed poorly or were entirely lost, damaged, or forgotten are often genuinely excluded from this same historical record, meaning a promoted historical performance claim citing specific surviving, successful examples may create a genuinely misleading impression of this broader category's actual, complete historical performance, particularly for collectibles or specific commodity sub-categories where this kind of selective survival is genuinely common. This is why a specific historical performance claim does not guarantee this same performance will actually repeat: even a genuinely accurate historical performance claim for a specific item and period does not actually guarantee an identical outcome for a different specific item or a different future period, echoing this curriculum's broader forecasting humility discussion regarding genuine uncertainty in any specific future prediction. This is why an investor should orchestrate honest skepticism alongside behavioral discipline against a compelling historical narrative: an investor should specifically orchestrate honest skepticism regarding promoted historical performance claims, specifically considering genuine survivorship bias and the non-guaranteed nature of any specific historical precedent, alongside genuine behavioral discipline against being drawn in purely by a compelling historical narrative, rather than accepting a promoted claim's surface appeal without this fuller, honestly skeptical orchestration.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: An investor presented with a promoted historical performance claim regarding a specific collectible category specifically confirms whether this claim's underlying data genuinely accounts for survivorship bias, discovering the cited examples represent only the specific items that happened to survive and appreciate, while many comparable items from the same original category were lost, damaged, or never appreciated at all. This investor specifically applies honest skepticism and behavioral discipline, declining to treat this promoted claim as a reliable basis for a significant investment decision, rather than being drawn in purely by this compelling but genuinely incomplete historical narrative.
