# Payroll Tax: Social Security and Medicare

## Learning Objectives

- Define payroll tax and how it differs from general income tax.
- Explain that payroll tax is often split between employer and employee.
- Identify common programs funded by payroll tax.

## Core Content

Payroll tax is a tax specifically tied to employment wages. It's related to income tax but distinct from it — while income tax funds general government spending, payroll tax is frequently earmarked for specific programs, most commonly retirement systems and health-related insurance programs.

In many systems, payroll tax is split: part is withheld from the employee's paycheck, and part is paid directly by the employer on top of the wage, meaning the true cost of an employee to a business is higher than just the paycheck amount. This split matters later when we look at self-employment tax, because a self-employed person often ends up responsible for both halves.

## Worked Example

**Question:** An employer pays a worker a wage, and separately sends an additional payroll tax payment to the government. Is the employer's payroll tax cost part of the worker's paycheck?

**Student:** I'd assume it's separate since the worker never sees that money.

**Guide:** Right, but is it still a cost of employing that worker?

**Student:** Yes — it's an extra cost on top of wages, even though it doesn't show up on the pay stub.

**Guide:** Exactly, which is why 'cost of an employee' is usually higher than the wage alone.

A) Exactly the paycheck amount

C) Lower than the paycheck

## Answer Key

1. B, 2. A, 3. C, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Payroll tax is distinct from general income tax because it's specifically linked to wages from employment.

**2.** Retirement systems and health-related insurance programs are commonly funded through dedicated payroll tax revenue.

**3.** Splitting the tax between employer and employee is a common structural choice in many systems.

**4.** Because employers pay a share on top of wages, the full cost of an employee exceeds their paycheck.

## Key Takeaways

- Payroll tax is tied specifically to employment wages, distinct from general income tax.
- It commonly funds retirement and health-related programs.
- Many systems split payroll tax between employer and employee.
- This split becomes especially relevant for self-employed people, covered in a later lesson.
