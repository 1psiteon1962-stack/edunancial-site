# Tax Bands Explained

## Learning Objectives

- Read and interpret a simple tax band table.
- Calculate tax owed across multiple bands for a sample income.
- Reinforce the marginal-rate concept with a numeric example.

## Core Content

Building on the previous lesson, let's walk through bands with actual numbers. Imagine an illustrative, simplified band table: income from £0–£10,000 is taxed at 10%; income from £10,001–£40,000 is taxed at 20%; income above £40,000 is taxed at 30%.

If someone earns £50,000, they do not pay 30% on the whole £50,000. Instead: the first £10,000 is taxed at 10% (£1,000), the next £30,000 (from £10,001 to £40,000) is taxed at 20% (£6,000), and the remaining £10,000 (from £40,001 to £50,000) is taxed at 30% (£3,000). Total tax owed: £10,000, which works out to an effective rate of 20% — well below the 30% top band.

This step-by-step, band-by-band calculation is exactly what marginal taxation means in practice. Real-world band numbers vary by country and change over time, so the specific figures here are illustrative only — the method of calculation is the durable, transferable skill.

> **Note:** The band percentages and thresholds in this lesson are illustrative teaching numbers, not figures for any specific country, including the UK. Verify the actual current bands for your jurisdiction, for example on GOV.UK.

## Worked Example

**Question:** Using the illustrative bands above, how much tax would someone earning exactly £40,000 owe?

**Student:** Let me work through it in order — first £10,000 at 10% is £1,000.

**Guide:** Good, keep going.

**Student:** The remaining £30,000, from £10,001 to £40,000, is taxed at 20%, which is £6,000.

**Guide:** And is any of it taxed at 30%?

**Student:** No — they didn't earn anything above £40,000, so the 30% band never applies. Total is £1,000 + £6,000 = £7,000.

**Guide:** Exactly right — and notice their effective rate is 17.5%, not 20% and not 30%.

## Practice Quiz

**Question 1.** Using the illustrative bands in this lesson, how much of a £50,000 income is taxed at 30%?

A) 30%

B) No — they're illustrative; real bands vary by country and change over time

## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Only the portion of income above £40,000 — which is £10,000 — falls into the 30% band.

**2.** Adding £1,000 + £6,000 + £3,000 across the three bands gives a total of £10,000.

**3.** Dividing total tax (£10,000) by total income (£50,000) gives an effective rate of 20%.

**4.** The figures are a teaching tool; the calculation method is what transfers to any real band table.

## Key Takeaways

- Bands apply step by step, with each rate only touching the income within that band.
- Walking through the maths concretely reinforces why the marginal rate concept matters in practice.
- Effective tax rate is always a blend, and always lower than the top band touched.
- The specific numbers used here are illustrative — always check your own jurisdiction's actual band table.
