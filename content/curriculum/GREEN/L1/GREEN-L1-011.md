# Gross Income vs. Net Income

## Learning Objectives

- Define gross income and net income.
- Explain what typically causes the gap between the two.
- Apply the distinction to a paycheck scenario.

## Core Content

Gross income is the total amount earned before anything is subtracted. Net income is what's left after subtractions — taxes, and often other deductions like retirement contributions or health insurance premiums — are taken out. The gap between gross and net is where most people get surprised the first time they see a real paycheck.

This distinction matters far beyond paychecks. Businesses talk about gross revenue versus net profit using the same underlying logic: gross is the top-line total, net is what remains after costs and obligations are subtracted. Whenever you hear a number described as 'gross,' the first question worth asking is: gross of what, exactly, and how much smaller will net be?

## Worked Example

**Question:** A job offer says the salary is $60,000. Is that the amount that will actually land in the bank account each year?

**Student:** I'd guess no, since taxes and other deductions come out first.

**Guide:** Right — what would you call the $60,000 figure, and what would you call the amount that actually lands in the account?

**Student:** The $60,000 is gross income, and the amount left after taxes and deductions is net income.

**Guide:** Exactly — always budget based on net, not gross.


## Practice Quiz


**Question 1.** Gross income is:

A) The amount after all deductions
B) The total amount earned before any deductions
C) Only investment income
D) Always equal to net income


**Question 2.** Net income is:

A) The amount before deductions
B) What remains after taxes and other deductions are subtracted
C) Unrelated to gross income
D) Always higher than gross income


**Question 3.** Why does the gap between gross and net matter for budgeting?

A) It doesn't matter
B) Budgeting on gross instead of net can lead to overestimating available money
C) Net is always higher than gross
D) Gross is irrelevant to businesses


**Question 4.** Businesses use a similar gross vs. net distinction for:

A) Revenue versus profit
B) Nothing — it's personal-finance-only
C) Only employee headcount
D) Only marketing spend


## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. A

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Gross is the top-line, pre-deduction total — the headline number.

**2.** Net is the actual amount left after taxes and other subtractions, and it's what actually reaches your account.

**3.** Planning around the gross figure risks assuming you have more spendable money than you actually will.

**4.** Businesses apply the identical logic to revenue (gross) versus profit (net).

## Key Takeaways

- Gross income is the total before deductions; net income is what's left after them.
- The gap is typically taxes plus other withholdings like retirement or insurance.
- Always budget based on net income, not gross.
- The same gross-versus-net logic applies to business revenue and profit.
