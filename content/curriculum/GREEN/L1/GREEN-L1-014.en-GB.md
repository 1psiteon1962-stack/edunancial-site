# Employed or Self-Employed? PAYE vs. Self Assessment

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the general difference between employee-type income reporting and self-employed income reporting.
- Connect this distinction to the self-employment vs. business ownership concept from earlier tracks.
- Recognise why this classification affects who handles deducting tax.

## Core Content

Most tax systems treat employees and independent workers differently. In the UK, for example, employees are taxed through PAYE, where the employer deducts tax and National Insurance throughout the year and gives the employee a year-end summary (a P60). Self-employed people, by contrast, usually register with HMRC, report their income through a Self Assessment tax return, and pay their own tax and National Insurance, often through payments on account. Other countries use different labels — in the US, for example, the W-2 and 1099 forms reflect a similar split — but the underlying distinction, employee versus independent contractor, shows up almost everywhere.

This maps directly onto the self-employment concept from earlier in your curriculum: an employee has taxes deducted automatically by someone else, while an independent contractor is functioning more like a self-employed person and must proactively set aside and pay their own taxes. Misunderstanding which category you fall into is one of the most common and costly tax mistakes — a contractor who spends their full payment without setting aside anything for taxes can face a painful surprise when the bill arrives. (Employment status can also have legal and tax consequences of its own, so check HMRC's employment status guidance if you're unsure.)

## Worked Example

**Question:** A young web designer works for several different clients, sets her own hours, and uses her own equipment. Is she more likely an employee or an independent contractor?

**Student:** Since she sets her own hours and works for multiple clients, she sounds more like an independent contractor.

**Guide:** If that's true, who is responsible for setting aside her tax payments during the year?

**Student:** She is — since no employer is deducting tax for her, she has to do that herself.

**Guide:** Exactly, which is why contractors need to actively plan for taxes instead of assuming it's handled automatically.

A) No taxes deducted
B) Taxes deducted throughout the year by the employer
C) Only business profit
D) Only investment income

**Question 2.** An independent contractor is generally responsible for:

A) Nothing — taxes are automatic
B) Setting aside and paying their own tax
C) Only VAT
D) Filing no paperwork ever

**Question 3.** This employee vs. contractor distinction connects most directly to which earlier concept?

A) Public goods
B) Self-employment vs. business ownership
C) Marginal tax bands
D) Property tax

**Question 4.** A common costly mistake for new contractors is:

A) Filing too early
B) Spending all income without setting aside money for taxes
C) Having too much tax deducted
D) Owning too much property

## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Employee-type reporting generally comes with automatic tax deductions handled by the employer.

**2.** Without an employer deducting tax on their behalf, contractors must proactively manage their own tax payments.

**3.** This is the tax-system expression of the self-employment concept covered earlier in the curriculum.

**4.** Spending 100% of contractor income without reserving a portion for taxes is one of the most common early mistakes.

## Key Takeaways

- Employee-type income generally has tax deducted automatically; self-employed income generally does not.
- This distinction mirrors the self-employment vs. employment concept from earlier lessons.
- Contractors must proactively set aside money for taxes throughout the year.
- Misclassifying yourself, or not planning for the difference, is a common and costly mistake.
