# Form W-2 vs Form 1099: Employee or Contractor?

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the general difference between an employee-type income document and a contractor-type income document.
- Connect this distinction to the self-employment vs. business ownership concept from earlier tracks.
- Recognize why this classification affects who handles withholding.

## Core Content

In some tax systems, particularly the U.S., there are distinct forms used to report different types of income: one for employees (commonly W-2), where the employer withholds tax throughout the year, and one for independent contractors (commonly 1099), where no tax is withheld and the worker is responsible for handling their own estimated payments. Other countries use different labels, but the underlying distinction — employee versus independent contractor — shows up almost everywhere.

This maps directly onto the self-employment concept from earlier in your curriculum: an employee has taxes withheld automatically by someone else, while an independent contractor is functioning more like a self-employed person and must proactively set aside and pay their own taxes. Misunderstanding which category you fall into is one of the most common and costly tax mistakes — a contractor who spends their full payment without setting aside anything for taxes can face a painful surprise at filing time.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A young web designer works for several different clients, sets her own hours, and uses her own equipment. Is she more likely an employee or an independent contractor?

**Student:** Since she sets her own hours and works for multiple clients, she sounds more like an independent contractor.

**Guide:** If that's true, who is responsible for setting aside her tax payments during the year?

**Student:** She is — since no employer is withholding tax for her, she has to do that herself.

**Guide:** Exactly, which is why contractors need to actively plan for taxes instead of assuming it's handled automatically.

**Question 2.** An independent contractor is generally responsible for:

C) Only sales tax

A) Public goods

A) Filing too early
B) Spending all income without setting aside money for taxes
C) Withholding too much tax

## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Employee-type reporting generally comes with automatic withholding handled by the employer.

**2.** Without an employer withholding on their behalf, contractors must proactively manage their own tax payments.

**3.** This is the tax-system expression of the self-employment concept covered earlier in the curriculum.

**4.** Spending 100% of contractor income without reserving a portion for taxes is one of the most common early mistakes.

## Key Takeaways

- Employee-type income generally has tax withheld automatically; contractor-type income generally does not.
- This distinction mirrors the self-employment vs. employment concept from earlier lessons.
- Contractors must proactively set aside money for taxes throughout the year.
- Misclassifying yourself, or not planning for the difference, is a common and costly mistake.
