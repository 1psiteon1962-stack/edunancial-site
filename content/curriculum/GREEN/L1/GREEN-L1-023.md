# Self-Employment Tax: What Freelancers and Business Owners Owe

## Learning Objectives

- Recall how payroll tax is split between employer and employee.
- Explain why a self-employed person often owes both halves.
- Recognize this as a key planning consideration for freelancers and small business owners.

## Core Content

Earlier, you learned that payroll tax is often split between employer and employee — part withheld from wages, part paid separately by the employer. When you're self-employed, you are, in effect, both the employer and the employee. Many systems account for this by requiring self-employed individuals to pay a version of payroll tax that covers both halves — sometimes called self-employment tax.

This is one of the most commonly underestimated costs of self-employment. A new freelancer comparing their invoice total to what an equivalent employee's paycheck shows can badly underestimate their true tax burden if they forget that, as a business of one, they're covering both sides of this tax. Understanding this in advance — and setting money aside accordingly — prevents an unpleasant surprise at filing time.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A freelancer compares her $80,000 in annual invoices to a friend's $80,000 salaried job and assumes their tax situations are basically the same. Are they?

**Student:** Probably not, since her friend's employer is covering half the payroll-style tax automatically.

**Guide:** What does that mean for the freelancer's true tax burden compared to her friend's?

**Student:** She likely owes more in total payroll-style tax, since she's responsible for both the employer and employee portions herself.

**Guide:** Exactly — same top-line income, meaningfully different tax reality.

## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Self-employment tax is essentially the mechanism for collecting both halves of payroll-style tax from one person.

**2.** Acting as both employer and employee means the self-employed person is on the hook for both shares.

**3.** Underestimating this cost is a common trap that leads to a larger-than-expected bill at filing time.

**4.** Proactive awareness and saving, rather than reacting after the fact, is the practical defense.

## Key Takeaways

- Self-employed individuals often owe both the employer and employee shares of payroll-style tax.
- This means self-employment income carries a meaningfully different tax burden than equivalent salaried income.
- This is one of the most commonly underestimated costs of self-employment.
- Planning ahead and setting money aside prevents an unpleasant surprise at filing time.
