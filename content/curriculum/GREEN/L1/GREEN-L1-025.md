# Common Deductible Business Expenses

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the general principle behind deductible business expenses.
- Identify common categories of deductible expenses.
- Recognize why personal expenses generally don't qualify.

## Core Content

Most tax systems allow businesses to deduct expenses that are 'ordinary and necessary' for running the business — meaning normal for that type of business and genuinely needed to operate, not lavish or purely personal. Common categories, described generally, include supplies and materials, rent for business space, business-related travel, marketing and advertising, professional services (like a lawyer or accountant), and equipment.

A useful test to hold onto: would this expense exist if the business didn't? A laptop bought specifically for client work passes that test more clearly than a vacation loosely justified as 'networking.' Mixing personal and business expenses without a clear line is one of the most common mistakes new business owners make, and it's exactly why the earlier lesson on record-keeping matters so much here — clean documentation makes the business-versus-personal line defensible.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A business owner buys a laptop used entirely for client design work. Later, they also expense a family vacation, calling it 'networking.' Are these treated the same way?

**Student:** I wouldn't think so — the laptop is clearly tied to doing the business's actual work.

**Guide:** What's the test you'd apply to the vacation?

**Student:** Would that expense exist if the business didn't? The laptop probably wouldn't have been bought without the business; the vacation likely would have happened anyway.

**Guide:** Exactly — that's the kind of reasoning that separates a legitimate business expense from a personal one dressed up as business.


## Practice Quiz


**Question 1.** A common general standard for deductible business expenses is:

A) Any expense at all, personal or not
B) Ordinary and necessary for the business
C) Only expenses over $10,000
D) Only expenses paid in cash


**Question 2.** Common categories of deductible business expenses include:

A) Supplies, rent, travel, marketing, professional services, equipment
B) Only groceries
C) Only vacations
D) Only personal clothing


**Question 3.** A useful test for whether an expense is business-related is:

A) Would this expense exist if the business didn't?
B) Is it expensive?
C) Was it paid in cash?
D) Did it happen on a weekday?


**Question 4.** Mixing personal and business expenses without a clear line:

A) Is encouraged
B) Is a common and risky mistake for new business owners
C) Has no consequence
D) Only matters for large corporations


## Answer Key

1. B, 2. A, 3. A, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Ordinary and necessary is a widely used general standard, though exact rules vary by jurisdiction.

**2.** These categories represent the typical operating costs of running most kinds of businesses.

**3.** This test helps separate expenses genuinely tied to the business from personal spending loosely relabeled.

**4.** Blurring the personal-business line without documentation is a common trap that record-keeping helps prevent.

## Key Takeaways

- Deductible business expenses are generally those that are ordinary and necessary for the business.
- Common categories include supplies, rent, travel, marketing, professional services, and equipment.
- A useful test: would the expense exist without the business?
- Mixing personal and business expenses without a clear line is a common, risky mistake.
