# Depreciation: Spreading a Cost Over Time

## Learning Objectives

- Define depreciation in plain terms.
- Explain why depreciation exists as a concept.
- Distinguish assets likely to be depreciated from ones typically expensed immediately.

## Core Content

When a business buys something that will be used for years — a vehicle, machinery, a building — it usually doesn't make sense to deduct the entire cost in the year of purchase. Instead, many tax systems require or allow that cost to be spread out, or depreciated, over the asset's useful life, matching the deduction to the years the asset actually helps generate income.

The logic mirrors basic accounting sense: if a piece of equipment will be used for ten years, treating its full cost as a single-year expense would understate profit in year one and overstate it in every following year. Depreciation smooths that out. By contrast, everyday consumable business expenses — office supplies, for example — are typically expensed immediately in full, since they don't provide value across multiple years the way durable equipment does.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A bakery buys a commercial oven expected to last ten years. Should the full cost be deducted immediately, or spread out?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, it sounds like it should be spread out, since the oven will be used for many years.

**Guide:** Why does spreading it out give a more accurate picture than deducting it all at once?

**Student:** Because the oven helps generate income across all ten years, not just the year it was bought, so matching the cost to those years is more accurate.

**Guide:** Exactly — that's the core logic of depreciation.


## Practice Quiz


**Question 1.** Depreciation is best described as:

A) Deducting an asset's full cost immediately
B) Spreading an asset's cost over its useful life
C) Ignoring the cost of business assets entirely
D) A type of sales tax


**Question 2.** Depreciation is typically applied to:

A) Long-lasting assets like equipment, vehicles, or buildings
B) Everyday office supplies
C) Sales tax payments
D) Payroll withholding


**Question 3.** Why not deduct a ten-year asset's full cost in year one?

A) It's illegal to buy equipment
B) It would misrepresent profit across the years the asset actually helps generate income
C) There's no reason, it's arbitrary
D) Depreciation doesn't apply to businesses


**Question 4.** Everyday consumable supplies are typically:

A) Depreciated over ten years
B) Expensed immediately in full
C) Never deductible
D) Treated the same as real estate


## Answer Key

1. B, 2. A, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Depreciation matches an asset's cost to the years it actually contributes value, rather than lumping it into one year.

**2.** Long-lasting assets are the typical candidates for depreciation, unlike short-lived consumables.

**3.** Spreading the cost avoids distorting the picture of profit in the purchase year versus later years.

**4.** Supplies get used up quickly, so immediate expensing better matches their short-term value.

## Key Takeaways

- Depreciation spreads an asset's cost over its useful life rather than deducting it all at once.
- It applies to long-lasting assets like equipment, vehicles, and buildings.
- This produces a more accurate year-by-year picture of business profit.
- Everyday consumable expenses are typically deducted immediately instead.
