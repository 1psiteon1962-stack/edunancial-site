# Traditional vs. Roth: Pay Taxes Now or Later

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the 'pay now vs. pay later' choice found in many retirement account systems.
- Identify the basic tradeoff between the two approaches.
- Recognize that the better choice can depend on expected future tax rates.

## Core Content

Many retirement account systems offer a choice between two structures, often generically described as 'traditional' and 'Roth' style, regardless of the exact local names. In a traditional-style account, contributions typically reduce taxable income now, but withdrawals in retirement are taxed. In a Roth-style account, contributions are made with already-taxed money now, but withdrawals in retirement are typically tax-free.

The core tradeoff is timing: pay tax now at your current rate, or pay tax later at your future rate. If you expect your tax rate to be lower in retirement than it is now, a traditional-style approach often comes out ahead. If you expect your rate to be similar or higher in retirement — common for younger people early in their careers with room to grow — a Roth-style approach can come out ahead. There's no universally correct answer; it depends on assumptions about the future, which is exactly why this is a common area to seek professional guidance rather than guess.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A 22-year-old just starting her career is deciding between a traditional-style and Roth-style retirement account. What's the general reasoning she should think through?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, she should think about whether her tax rate now is likely lower or higher than it will be later in her career.

**Guide:** Given that she's early in her career, what's a reasonable assumption about her future income and tax rate?

**Student:** Her income, and likely her tax rate, will probably rise over time — so paying tax now at a lower rate, through a Roth-style account, could make sense.

**Guide:** That's a reasonable line of thinking — though it's still an assumption about the future, not a guarantee, which is why this is worth discussing with a professional for a specific plan.


## Practice Quiz


**Question 1.** In a traditional-style account, contributions typically:

A) Are taxed twice
B) Reduce taxable income now, with withdrawals taxed later
C) Are never taxed at all
D) Have no effect on taxes


**Question 2.** In a Roth-style account, contributions are typically:

A) Made with already-taxed money, with tax-free withdrawals later
B) Deducted from current taxable income
C) Taxed twice
D) Illegal


**Question 3.** The core tradeoff between the two approaches is:

A) There is no tradeoff
B) Paying tax now versus paying tax later
C) One approach never involves taxes
D) Only businesses can choose


**Question 4.** Which approach tends to favor someone expecting a lower tax rate in retirement than today?

A) Roth-style
B) Traditional-style
C) Neither matters
D) Only sales-tax-based accounts


## Answer Key

1. B, 2. A, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** The traditional-style structure defers tax, offering a benefit today at the cost of tax owed on withdrawals later.

**2.** The Roth-style structure front-loads the tax, so qualifying withdrawals later are generally untaxed.

**3.** This is fundamentally a timing decision about when you'd rather pay tax, based on assumptions about future rates.

**4.** If today's rate is higher than the expected future rate, deferring tax (traditional-style) is often more advantageous.

## Key Takeaways

- Traditional-style accounts defer tax to withdrawal; Roth-style accounts tax contributions now, not withdrawals.
- The core tradeoff is paying tax now versus later.
- The better choice depends on assumptions about your current versus future tax rate.
- This is a common, legitimate area to seek professional guidance rather than guess.
