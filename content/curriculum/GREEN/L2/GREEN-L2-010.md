## GREEN-L2-010
### Self-Employment Tax: The Hidden Extra Cost of 1099 Income

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general concept and purpose of self-employment tax.
- Compare self-employment tax to the FICA withholding W-2 employees experience.
- Connect this concept to the BLUE-track entrepreneurship discussion.

**2. Core Content**
Self-employed individuals and independent contractors (echoing BLUE-L2-023's classification discussion) generally must pay self-employment tax, which covers both the employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare contributions (Lesson 11) that a traditional employer and W-2 employee would otherwise each pay half of separately. This means a self-employed person's effective FICA-equivalent tax rate is generally roughly double what a W-2 employee sees withheld from their own paycheck, since the self-employed individual is effectively paying both halves.

This is a commonly underestimated cost for new entrepreneurs and freelancers transitioning from W-2 employment, precisely the kind of piece BLUE-L2-006's capital planning and BLUE-L2-011's cash flow discussions warned against overlooking — a new freelancer who prices their services based only on replacing their prior W-2 salary, without accounting for this additional self-employment tax burden, may find their actual after-tax income falls meaningfully short of their prior employment income at an equivalent gross revenue level.

**3. Worked Example**
Transitioning from a W-2 job to full-time freelancing, Marcus initially sets his freelance rates to replace his prior gross salary, without accounting for the fact that he would now be responsible for the full self-employment tax burden, rather than splitting the FICA-equivalent cost with an employer as he had before. After learning about self-employment tax specifically, he recalculates his target rates upward to account for this additional cost, ensuring his actual after-tax income would genuinely replace his prior take-home pay, not merely his prior gross salary figure.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does self-employment tax generally cover?
   a) Only federal income tax
   b) Both the employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare contributions
   c) Only state sales tax
   d) A penalty for underpayment
2. Why is a self-employed person's effective FICA-equivalent rate generally about double a W-2 employee's withheld rate?
   a) Self-employed individuals pay no FICA-equivalent tax at all
   b) They are effectively paying both the employee and employer portions themselves
   c) W-2 employees pay double what self-employed individuals pay
   d) There is no difference between the two
3. True or False: Self-employment tax is not commonly overlooked by new entrepreneurs transitioning from W-2 employment.
4. Why did Marcus need to recalculate his freelance rates upward?
   a) His original rate calculation already fully accounted for self-employment tax
   b) His original rates only replaced his prior gross salary, without accounting for the added self-employment tax burden
   c) Self-employment tax does not actually exist
   d) He wanted to lower his rates further

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Self-employment tax covers both the employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare contributions.
2. Self-employed individuals effectively pay both halves themselves, roughly doubling their FICA-equivalent rate compared to a W-2 employee's withheld portion.
3. Self-employment tax is a commonly underestimated cost for new entrepreneurs and freelancers transitioning from W-2 employment.
4. Marcus's original calculation only replaced his gross salary, overlooking the additional self-employment tax burden he would now bear alone.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Self-employment tax covers both the employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare contributions.
- This roughly doubles the FICA-equivalent rate compared to what a W-2 employee sees withheld from their own paycheck.
- This is a commonly underestimated cost for new entrepreneurs and freelancers transitioning from W-2 employment.
- Pricing freelance or self-employment income to replace a prior W-2 salary requires accounting for this additional tax burden.
