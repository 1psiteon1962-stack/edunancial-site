## GREEN-L2-011
### FICA Taxes: Social Security and Medicare Explained

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define FICA and its two components.
- Explain the general purpose of Social Security and Medicare taxes.
- Connect this concept to Lesson 10's self-employment tax discussion.

**2. Core Content**
FICA (Federal Insurance Contributions Act) taxes fund two federal programs: Social Security, generally providing retirement, disability, and survivor benefits, and Medicare, generally providing health insurance coverage for eligible individuals, primarily those 65 and older. For W-2 employees, these taxes are split between employee and employer, with each generally paying a specific percentage of wages up to certain thresholds (Social Security taxation is generally subject to an annual wage base limit, above which no further Social Security tax is withheld for that year, though Medicare taxation generally continues without a similar wage limit).

As Lesson 10 established, self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer portions themselves through self-employment tax, since there is no separate employer to split the cost with. Understanding FICA's purpose — funding specific future benefits, not merely a generic tax — can help contextualize why this particular tax exists as a distinct line item from general income tax withholding, even though both come out of the same paycheck.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her pay stub, Amara (from Lesson 2) notices separate line items for Social Security and Medicare withholding, distinct from her federal and state income tax withholding. Researching further, she learns these FICA withholdings specifically fund her own future Social Security retirement benefits and current Medicare program funding, rather than being simply additional general income tax — a distinction that helps her understand why these specific taxes exist as their own category, separate from the income tax withholding covered in Lesson 9.

   d) FICA does not apply to self-employed individuals

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. FICA taxes fund Social Security and Medicare programs specifically.
2. Self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer portions themselves through self-employment tax.
3. FICA is a distinct category of withholding from general federal income tax, funding specific programs.
4. Amara's research clarified that FICA withholding serves a specific, distinct purpose from general income tax withholding.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- FICA taxes fund Social Security (retirement, disability, survivor benefits) and Medicare (health insurance for eligible individuals).
- W-2 employees split FICA taxes with their employer; self-employed individuals pay both portions themselves.
- Social Security taxation is generally subject to an annual wage base limit; Medicare generally is not.
- FICA is a distinct tax category from general income tax withholding, funding specific future and current benefits.
