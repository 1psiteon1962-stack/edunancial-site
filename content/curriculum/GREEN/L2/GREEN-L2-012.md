## GREEN-L2-012
### Quarterly Estimated Taxes: Who Must Pay and Why

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of quarterly estimated tax payments.
- Identify who generally must make these payments.
- Connect this concept to the self-employment tax discussion in Lesson 10.

**2. Core Content**
Because the U.S. tax system is generally a "pay-as-you-go" system, taxpayers who do not have sufficient tax withheld throughout the year — commonly self-employed individuals, freelancers, and those with significant investment or other income not subject to withholding — are generally required to make quarterly estimated tax payments directly to the relevant tax authorities, rather than waiting to pay their full tax liability at the annual filing deadline. This connects directly to Lesson 10: since self-employment income has no employer withholding anything automatically, the self-employed individual must proactively estimate and pay throughout the year instead.

Failing to pay sufficient estimated tax throughout the year can result in an underpayment penalty, even if the full remaining balance is eventually paid by the annual filing deadline — the penalty specifically addresses the "pay-as-you-go" timing expectation, not merely whether the total amount was eventually paid. Calculating the correct estimated payment amount involves projecting the year's expected income and tax liability, an area where a tax professional's guidance can help avoid both underpayment penalties and unnecessarily overpaying.

**3. Worked Example**
In his first year of full-time freelancing, Marcus (from Lesson 10) initially assumes he can simply pay his entire tax liability when he files his annual return, as he had somewhat informally done with any small balance due during his W-2 employment years. His tax professional explains that as a self-employed individual with no withholding, he is now expected to make quarterly estimated payments throughout the year, and that waiting until the annual filing deadline to pay his full liability could result in an underpayment penalty, regardless of his ability to pay the full amount by that deadline. Marcus adjusts his practice, working with his tax professional to calculate and submit quarterly estimated payments going forward.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does the "pay-as-you-go" tax system concept generally require?
   a) Waiting until the annual filing deadline to pay any tax at all
   b) Paying tax throughout the year as income is earned, not just at the annual filing deadline
   c) Paying tax only once every five years
   d) Eliminating the need for any tax payments
2. Who commonly must make quarterly estimated tax payments?
   a) Only W-2 employees with full withholding
   b) Self-employed individuals, freelancers, and those with significant income not subject to withholding
   c) No one — quarterly payments do not exist
   d) Only retirees
3. True or False: Paying the full tax liability by the annual filing deadline always avoids any underpayment penalty, regardless of payments made throughout the year.
4. Why did Marcus need to change his approach after becoming a full-time freelancer?
   a) His W-2 approach of paying at filing time remained fully appropriate
   b) As a self-employed individual with no withholding, he was now expected to make quarterly estimated payments throughout the year
   c) Freelancers are exempt from all tax payments
   d) He had no tax liability at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The pay-as-you-go system generally requires paying tax throughout the year as income is earned, not only at the annual deadline.
2. Self-employed individuals, freelancers, and those with significant unwithheld income commonly must make quarterly estimated payments.
3. An underpayment penalty can apply based on insufficient payments throughout the year, even if the full balance is eventually paid.
4. Marcus's shift to self-employment, with no automatic withholding, required him to proactively make quarterly estimated payments.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The U.S. tax system generally operates on a "pay-as-you-go" basis, requiring payment throughout the year, not just at filing.
- Self-employed individuals, freelancers, and those with significant unwithheld income commonly must make quarterly estimated payments.
- Underpayment penalties can apply for insufficient timely payments, even if the full balance is eventually paid.
- This connects directly to Lesson 10, since self-employment income has no automatic employer withholding.
