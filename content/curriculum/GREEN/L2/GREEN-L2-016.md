## GREEN-L2-016
### Emergency Funds: Sizing and Purpose

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of a personal emergency fund.
- Compare general sizing guidelines and the factors that adjust them.
- Connect this concept to the BLUE and RED-track reserves discussions.

**2. Core Content**
An emergency fund is cash set aside specifically to cover essential expenses during an unexpected disruption — job loss, a medical emergency, an urgent home or car repair — echoing the reserves concept introduced in RED-L2-009 and BLUE-L2-034, now applied at the personal, individual level. A commonly referenced general guideline suggests three to six months of essential living expenses, similar to the reserve sizing discussed in those earlier colors, though the right amount varies based on factors such as job stability, whether income comes from a single source or multiple sources (echoing the side-hustle and self-employment discussions in Lessons 10–14), and household size and obligations.

Someone with irregular self-employment income, for example, may reasonably want a larger emergency fund than someone with highly stable W-2 employment and a dual-income household, given the greater income unpredictability the self-employed person faces — directly paralleling the business reserve-sizing logic from BLUE-L2-034, now applied to personal finances specifically.

**3. Worked Example**
Building her emergency fund, Renata (from Lesson 15) considers her own specific circumstances: her income comes from a single, stable W-2 source, and she has no dependents, leading her toward a target closer to three months of essential expenses. Her friend, who is self-employed with more variable monthly income and two dependents, reasonably targets a larger emergency fund closer to six months of essential expenses, reflecting the same underlying principle from the RED and BLUE-track reserves discussions — someone facing greater income unpredictability or larger obligations benefits from a larger buffer.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general purpose of a personal emergency fund?
   a) To fund discretionary luxury spending
   b) To cover essential expenses during an unexpected disruption, such as job loss or a medical emergency
   c) To replace the need for any insurance
   d) To eliminate the need for a budget entirely
2. What is a commonly referenced general emergency fund sizing guideline?
   a) One week of expenses
   b) Three to six months of essential living expenses
   c) Twenty years of expenses
   d) No emergency fund is ever necessary
3. True or False: Every individual should target the exact identical emergency fund size, regardless of income stability or household circumstances.
4. Why did Renata's self-employed friend target a larger emergency fund than Renata?
   a) She had no specific reason for a larger fund
   b) Her more variable income and larger household obligations warranted a bigger buffer
   c) Self-employed individuals never need emergency funds
   d) Renata's fund should have been larger instead

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An emergency fund covers essential expenses during an unexpected disruption, such as job loss or a medical emergency.
2. Three to six months of essential expenses is a commonly referenced general sizing guideline.
3. The right emergency fund size varies based on income stability, income source variety, and household circumstances.
4. The friend's more variable income and greater household obligations reasonably warranted a larger emergency fund target.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An emergency fund covers essential expenses during unexpected disruptions like job loss or medical emergencies.
- Three to six months of essential expenses is a commonly referenced starting guideline, adjusted for individual circumstances.
- Income stability, income source variety, and household obligations all affect the appropriate target size.
- This directly parallels the RED and BLUE-track reserves discussions, now applied to personal finances.
