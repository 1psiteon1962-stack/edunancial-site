## GREEN-L2-017
### Good Debt vs. Bad Debt: An Introduction (Preview of Level 3)

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define the general distinction between productive and consumptive debt at an introductory level.
- Connect this concept to the RED-L2-050 and BLUE-L2-050 strategic debt previews.
- Recognize this as a Level 3 theme being introduced early for context.

**2. Core Content**
Echoing the strategic-versus-consumer debt distinction previewed in RED-L2-050 and BLUE-L2-050, this lesson introduces the same core concept from the GREEN, personal finance perspective: debt used to acquire an asset or capability that can generate future income or value (a mortgage on a rental property, a loan for education leading to increased earning potential) is sometimes called "productive" or "strategic" debt, while debt used to fund immediate consumption with no lasting asset or income-generating capability (most credit card debt for everyday spending, a loan for a rapidly depreciating consumer good) is sometimes called "consumptive" or "consumer" debt.

This distinction is introduced here at an introductory level specifically to preview a theme that will become central in Level 3 across multiple colors — RED-L3's mortgage leverage case study and BLUE-L3's seller financing case study will both build directly on this same foundational concept, now introduced from the personal, everyday finance perspective this GREEN-track lesson addresses.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing his own debts, a young professional distinguishes between his student loan, which funded education directly connected to his current, higher-earning career (an example of productive debt, echoing the RED and BLUE-track strategic debt previews) and his credit card balance carried over from covering everyday living expenses during a period of underemployment, with no corresponding asset or income-generating capability behind it (an example of consumptive debt). Recognizing this distinction helps him prioritize which debt to focus on paying down more aggressively, a strategy developed further in Lesson 18.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What generally characterizes "productive" or "strategic" debt?
   a) Debt used for immediate consumption with no lasting value
   b) Debt used to acquire an asset or capability that can generate future income or value
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Debt that never needs to be repaid
2. What generally characterizes "consumptive" or "consumer" debt?
   a) Debt backed by an income-producing asset
   b) Debt used to fund immediate consumption with no lasting asset or income-generating capability
   c) Debt used exclusively for real estate
   d) Debt that always earns the borrower money
3. True or False: This distinction is entirely new to this curriculum and unrelated to any other color's discussion.
4. Why did the young professional's student loan qualify as productive debt in his analysis?
   a) It funded consumption with no lasting value
   b) It funded education directly connected to his current, higher-earning career
   c) All debt is automatically productive
   d) He had already paid it off completely

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Productive or strategic debt funds an asset or capability capable of generating future income or value.
2. Consumptive or consumer debt funds immediate consumption with no lasting asset or income-generating capability.
3. This distinction directly echoes and previews the RED-L2-050 and BLUE-L2-050 strategic debt discussions.
4. The student loan's connection to his current, higher-earning career qualified it as an example of productive debt.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Productive/strategic debt funds an asset or capability that can generate future income or value.
- Consumptive/consumer debt funds immediate consumption with no lasting asset or income-generating capability.
- This distinction echoes and previews the RED-L2-050 and BLUE-L2-050 strategic debt discussions from the personal finance perspective.
- This concept will become central in Level 3 across multiple colors, introduced here at an introductory level.
