## GREEN-L2-018
### Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche: Comparing Payoff Strategies

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define the debt snowball and debt avalanche methods.
- Compare their mathematical and psychological tradeoffs.
- Recognize that the right choice depends on individual behavioral tendencies.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 17's debt categorization, this lesson addresses strategies for paying down multiple debts (particularly consumptive debts warranting prioritized payoff). The debt avalanche method prioritizes paying extra toward the debt with the highest interest rate first, while making minimum payments on all others — mathematically, this method generally minimizes total interest paid over time. The debt snowball method prioritizes paying extra toward the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate, while making minimum payments on all others — this method generally does not minimize total interest paid, but can provide earlier psychological wins as smaller debts are eliminated more quickly, potentially supporting better follow-through for some individuals.

Neither method is universally correct: the avalanche method is mathematically optimal, but the snowball method's behavioral, motivation-sustaining benefit can, for some individuals, result in better real-world follow-through and ultimately more total debt paid off, echoing WHITE-L2-020's broader discussion of behavioral factors sometimes outweighing purely mathematical optimization.

**3. Worked Example**
Facing several debts of varying balances and interest rates, Julio considers both methods. The avalanche method would save him more in total interest if he follows through consistently, but reflecting honestly on his own past experience with long-term financial follow-through, he recognizes he has historically struggled to stay motivated on long-term goals without visible, early progress. He chooses the snowball method instead, prioritizing his smallest balance first, explicitly trading some mathematical optimization for a structure better suited to his own behavioral tendencies — directly echoing WHITE-L2-020's lump-sum-versus-dollar-cost-averaging discussion of prioritizing genuine follow-through over pure math.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does the debt avalanche method prioritize?
   a) The smallest balance, regardless of interest rate
   b) The debt with the highest interest rate first
   c) Ignoring all debts equally
   d) Only debts with no interest at all
2. What does the debt snowball method prioritize?
   a) The debt with the highest interest rate first
   b) The smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate
   c) Ignoring all debts equally
   d) Only mortgage debt
3. True or False: The debt avalanche method always results in better real-world outcomes for every individual, since it is mathematically optimal.
4. Why did Julio choose the snowball method despite its lower mathematical optimization?
   a) He wanted to pay more total interest
   b) His own history with follow-through suggested the snowball method's early wins would better sustain his motivation
   c) The avalanche method does not exist
   d) He had only one debt to pay off

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. The debt avalanche method prioritizes the debt with the highest interest rate first, minimizing total interest mathematically.
2. The debt snowball method prioritizes the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate.
3. While mathematically optimal, the avalanche method may not produce the best real-world outcome for every individual's behavioral tendencies.
4. Julio's honest self-assessment of his own follow-through history led him to prioritize the snowball method's motivational benefit.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The debt avalanche method prioritizes the highest interest rate debt, minimizing total interest mathematically.
- The debt snowball method prioritizes the smallest balance, providing earlier psychological wins.
- Neither method is universally correct; the right choice depends on individual behavioral tendencies.
- This echoes WHITE-L2-020's broader theme of behavioral factors sometimes outweighing pure mathematical optimization.
