## GREEN-L2-019
### Credit Scores: What Actually Moves the Number

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify general factors that influence a credit score.
- Explain the relative weight commonly attributed to different factors.
- Connect this concept to earlier discussions of business and mortgage credit.

**2. Core Content**
Credit scores are generally influenced by several factors, commonly including payment history (whether payments have been made on time), amounts owed relative to available credit (developed further in Lesson 20), length of credit history, credit mix (variety of credit types used), and new credit inquiries. Payment history and amounts owed are commonly cited as carrying the greatest relative weight among these factors in many common scoring models, though exact weightings can vary by the specific scoring model used.

This echoes concepts introduced elsewhere in this curriculum: RED-L2-001 discussed how a mortgage payment history affects credit, and BLUE-L2-016 discussed building separate business credit through consistent, timely payments — the same core principle (consistent, on-time payment as the most heavily weighted factor) recurs across personal, real estate, and business credit contexts alike.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her credit report after a period of financial stress led to a few late payments, Amara notices her score has dropped more significantly than she expected from what seemed like a relatively minor lapse. Researching the general weighting of credit score factors, she learns that payment history carries substantial weight in common scoring models, explaining why even a few late payments had an outsized effect on her score compared to other factors. She commits to a consistent, on-time payment pattern going forward, recognizing this as the single most impactful factor she can control.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Payment history is commonly cited as a heavily weighted factor in common credit scoring models.
2. The consistent, on-time payment principle recurs across personal, real estate, and business credit contexts throughout this curriculum.
3. Common scoring models generally weight factors like payment history and amounts owed more heavily than some other factors.
4. Payment history's substantial weighting explains why even a few late payments had an outsized effect on Amara's score.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Credit scores are commonly influenced by payment history, amounts owed, credit history length, credit mix, and new inquiries.
- Payment history and amounts owed commonly carry substantial relative weight in many scoring models.
- This recurring principle connects to RED-L2-001's mortgage credit discussion and BLUE-L2-016's business credit discussion.
- Consistent, on-time payment is a controllable factor with an outsized effect on credit scores across contexts.
