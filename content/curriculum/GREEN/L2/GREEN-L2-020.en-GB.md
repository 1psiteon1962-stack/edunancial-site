## GREEN-L2-020
### Credit Utilisation and Its Outsized Impact

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define credit utilisation at a conceptual level.
- Explain why this factor carries significant weight in credit scoring.
- Calculate a simple credit utilisation ratio.

**2. Core Content**
Credit utilisation is the ratio of current credit balances to total available credit, generally calculated both per individual account and in aggregate across all revolving credit accounts. This factor is commonly cited as carrying significant weight in credit scoring models (echoing Lesson 19's discussion), separate from whether payments are actually made on time — a person could pay their full balance every single month without ever missing a payment, yet still see their utilisation ratio affected by high balances relative to their credit limits at the specific moment their credit is checked or reported.

A commonly referenced general guideline suggests keeping utilisation meaningfully below 30% of available credit, both per card and in aggregate, for stronger credit scoring outcomes, though lower utilisation is generally viewed even more favorably. This is a factor that can shift relatively quickly compared to length of credit history, making it one of the more directly controllable levers for someone actively working to improve their credit score in a shorter timeframe.

**3. Worked Example**
Julio (from Lesson 18) has a credit card with a £10,000 limit and typically carries a £4,000 balance when his statement closes each month, even though he pays the full balance before any interest accrues — a 40% utilisation ratio (£4,000 divided by £10,000), above the commonly referenced 30% guideline. Learning that utilisation is calculated based on the balance reported at statement closing, not necessarily his final zero balance after payment, he adjusts his practice by making a payment before his statement closing date specifically to reduce his reported balance and utilisation ratio, even though he was already paying his balance in full every month with no interest charges.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is credit utilisation?
   a) The interest rate on a credit card
   b) The ratio of current credit balances to total available credit
   c) The length of a person's credit history
   d) A type of tax deduction
2. What is a commonly referenced general utilisation guideline?
   a) Keeping utilisation above 90%
   b) Keeping utilisation meaningfully below 30% of available credit
   c) Utilisation has no effect on credit scores at all
   d) Utilisation only matters for business credit
3. True or False: Paying a credit card balance in full every month automatically guarantees a low reported utilisation ratio.
4. Why did Julio's utilisation ratio remain high despite paying his balance in full every month?
   a) Utilisation has no relationship to reported balances
   b) Utilisation is generally calculated based on the balance reported at statement closing, not his final zero balance after payment
   c) He never actually paid his balance
   d) His credit limit was too high

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Credit utilisation is the ratio of current credit balances to total available credit.
2. A commonly referenced guideline suggests keeping utilisation meaningfully below 30% of available credit.
3. Utilisation is generally calculated based on the statement-closing balance, which can be high even if later paid in full.
4. Julio's high statement-closing balance created a high reported utilisation ratio, despite his full, on-time payments before interest accrued.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Credit utilisation is the ratio of current credit balances to total available credit, calculated per account and in aggregate.
- This factor carries significant weight in credit scoring, separate from on-time payment history.
- A commonly referenced guideline suggests keeping utilisation meaningfully below 30%.
- Utilisation is generally based on the statement-closing balance, meaning even full monthly payoffs can still show high reported utilisation.
