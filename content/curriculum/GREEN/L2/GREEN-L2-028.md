## GREEN-L2-028
### Dependents and Tax Credits: Child Tax Credit and Others

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general concept of a dependent for tax purposes.
- Identify common credits associated with having dependents.
- Reinforce the refundable-versus-nonrefundable distinction from Lesson 6.

**2. Core Content**
A "dependent" for tax purposes is generally a qualifying child or qualifying relative meeting specific relationship, residency, age, and support requirements, entitling the taxpayer claiming them to certain tax benefits. Common credits associated with dependents include a child tax credit (subject to income phase-outs and specific eligibility rules) and, in certain situations, a child and dependent care credit addressing care expenses that allow a taxpayer to work or look for work.

Reinforcing Lesson 6's refundable-versus-nonrefundable distinction, some dependent-related credits include a refundable component (potentially providing value even to taxpayers with little or no tax liability before the credit), while others are entirely nonrefundable, making the specific credit's refundability status a meaningful factor in understanding its actual value to a given taxpayer's specific situation.

**3. Worked Example**
Filing her tax return as a single parent, Teresa (from Lesson 5) claims her child as a dependent, qualifying her for a child tax credit that includes both a nonrefundable and a partially refundable component under the specific rules applicable to her filing year. Understanding this distinction, echoing Lesson 6's broader refundable-versus-nonrefundable discussion, helps her recognize why her actual credit benefit depends not just on the credit's stated maximum amount, but also on her specific tax liability and the credit's refundability structure for her situation.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Claiming a dependent generally requires meeting specific relationship, residency, age, and support requirements.
2. This lesson reinforces Lesson 6's refundable-versus-nonrefundable distinction, applied specifically to dependent-related credits.
3. Dependent-related credits can include both refundable and nonrefundable components, depending on the specific credit and rules.
4. Teresa's actual credit benefit depended on her specific tax liability and the credit's particular refundability structure.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A dependent for tax purposes generally requires meeting specific relationship, residency, age, and support requirements.
- Common dependent-related credits include the child tax credit and child and dependent care credit.
- These credits can include both refundable and nonrefundable components, reinforcing Lesson 6's distinction.
- A credit's actual value depends on the taxpayer's specific liability and the credit's refundability structure.
