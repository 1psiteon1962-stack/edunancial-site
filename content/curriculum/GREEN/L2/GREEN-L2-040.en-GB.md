## GREEN-L2-040
### Charitable Giving and Taxes: Deduction Basics

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general tax treatment of charitable contributions.
- Connect this concept to Lesson 7's itemising discussion.
- Recognise documentation requirements as important for substantiating charitable deductions.

**2. Core Content**
Charitable contributions to qualifying organisations are commonly deductible, but generally only for taxpayers who itemise (Lesson 7) rather than take the standard deduction, since charitable giving is generally categorised as an itemised deduction, not an above-the-line deduction (Lesson 8). This means a taxpayer whose total itemised deductions, including charitable contributions, do not exceed their available standard deduction may not receive any additional tax benefit from their giving, even though the contribution itself was genuine and valuable to the receiving organisation.

Documentation requirements generally apply, particularly for larger contributions, with specific record types required varying based on the contribution's size and whether it was cash or property — a taxpayer intending to claim a charitable deduction should maintain appropriate documentation from the outset, rather than assuming a deduction can be claimed based on memory or an informal record after the fact.

**3. Worked Example**
Making a meaningful charitable contribution during the year, Fatima (from Lesson 21) obtains and retains proper documentation from the receiving organisation at the time of her donation, rather than assuming she can reconstruct adequate records later at tax time. When preparing her return, she calculates her total itemised deductions, including this contribution, and compares the total to her available standard deduction (echoing Lesson 7), finding her itemised total, boosted by this contribution, now exceeds her standard deduction — meaning her generosity, combined with her other itemised deductions, provides an additional tax benefit that would not have been available had she taken the standard deduction instead.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. How are charitable contributions generally categorised for tax purposes?
   a) As an above-the-line deduction
   b) As an itemised deduction, generally requiring itemising to receive a tax benefit
   c) As a nonrefundable tax credit
   d) They have no tax categorisation at all
2. What might happen if a taxpayer's itemised deductions, including charitable giving, do not exceed the standard deduction?
   a) They automatically receive double the benefit
   b) They may not receive any additional tax benefit from their giving, since the standard deduction would be used instead
   c) Their charitable contribution becomes invalid
   d) They are required to itemise regardless
3. True or False: Documentation for charitable contributions can always be reliably reconstructed from memory at tax time, with no need for records at the time of donation.
4. Why did Fatima obtain proper documentation at the time of her donation, rather than later?
   a) Documentation has no relevance to charitable deductions
   b) Proper documentation from the outset supports substantiating the deduction, rather than relying on memory later
   c) She had no intention of claiming any deduction
   d) Documentation is only relevant for non-cash gifts

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Charitable contributions are generally categorised as an itemised deduction, requiring itemising to receive a tax benefit.
2. If itemised deductions do not exceed the standard deduction, a taxpayer may not receive additional tax benefit from their giving.
3. Reliable documentation should generally be obtained and retained at the time of donation, not reconstructed later from memory.
4. Fatima's proactive documentation practice supported her ability to substantiate her charitable deduction accurately.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Charitable contributions are generally an itemised deduction, requiring itemising (Lesson 7) to provide additional tax benefit.
- A taxpayer whose itemised total does not exceed the standard deduction may not see additional tax benefit from giving.
- Documentation requirements generally apply, varying by contribution size and type.
- Obtaining proper documentation at the time of donation, not later, supports reliable deduction substantiation.
