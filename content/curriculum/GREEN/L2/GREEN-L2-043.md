## GREEN-L2-043
### Tax Implications of Life Events: Marriage, Divorce, and Death

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify general tax implications of marriage, divorce, and death.
- Connect this concept to Lesson 5's filing status discussion.
- Reinforce the importance of updating tax-related documents after major life events.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson consolidates several threads from this level into a life-events framework. Marriage generally changes filing status options (Lesson 5) and can affect combined household tax outcomes, sometimes favorably and sometimes less favorably depending on each spouse's specific income situation — a phenomenon sometimes informally called a "marriage bonus" or "marriage penalty" depending on the specific circumstances. Divorce generally requires reassessing filing status, potentially affects dependent-related credits (Lesson 28), and often involves specific tax treatment of asset division and, in some cases, support payments, requiring careful professional guidance given the complexity involved.

Death, beyond the estate and inheritance tax considerations from Lesson 42, generally requires a final tax return for the deceased individual, and can affect a surviving spouse's filing status for a period afterward — this cluster of events also directly echoes PURPLE-L2-016's healthcare directive and PURPLE-L2-017's beneficiary designation discussions, reinforcing why comprehensive planning across both PURPLE legal instruments and GREEN tax considerations matters together, not as separate, unrelated concerns.

**3. Worked Example**
Following her divorce, Teresa (from Lesson 5) works with her tax professional to properly reassess her filing status, now potentially qualifying for Head of Household given her custodial situation with her dependent child, and to understand the specific tax treatment of the asset division from her divorce settlement. She also updates her beneficiary designations (echoing PURPLE-L2-017) as part of this same broader life transition, recognizing that a major life event like divorce touches both her PURPLE-track legal documents and her GREEN-track tax situation simultaneously, not just one or the other in isolation.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Marriage generally changes filing status options and can affect combined household tax outcomes.
2. Divorce generally requires reassessing filing status, dependent-related credits, and tax treatment of asset division.
3. Major life events connect directly to PURPLE-track instruments, such as beneficiary designations and healthcare directives.
4. Teresa's comprehensive approach recognized that divorce affected both her legal documents and her tax situation together.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Marriage generally changes filing status options and can produce a "marriage bonus" or "penalty" depending on circumstances.
- Divorce generally requires reassessing filing status, dependent credits, and asset division tax treatment.
- Death requires a final tax return and can affect a surviving spouse's filing status, connecting to estate tax (Lesson 42) considerations.
- These life events connect directly to PURPLE-track legal instruments, reinforcing coordinated legal and tax planning.
