## GREEN-L2-047
### Common Tax Mistakes and Why They Happen

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify several common tax mistakes.
- Connect each mistake back to specific earlier lessons in this level.
- Reinforce this level's cumulative structure, echoing the mistakes lessons in other colors.

**2. Core Content**
Echoing RED-L2-048 and WHITE-L2-047's format, this lesson reviews common tax mistakes, each connected to concepts already covered. Assuming a bracket-crossing raise reduces total after-tax income (Lessons 3–4) often stems from the widely repeated marginal-rate misunderstanding this level directly debunked. Underestimating self-employment tax when pricing freelance work (Lesson 10) often stems from failing to account for the full FICA-equivalent burden. Not adjusting Form W-4 after a major life event (Lesson 34) often stems from treating the form as a one-time task rather than an ongoing management tool. Assuming a large refund is a financial win (Lesson 33) often stems from not recognizing it as overwithheld personal money returned, interest-free.

As in other colors, a broader pattern underlies most of these mistakes: treating tax concepts as fixed, one-time facts rather than as an ongoing, evolving system requiring periodic attention as income, life circumstances, and decisions change — precisely the discipline this entire level has built toward.

**3. Worked Example**
Reflecting on his own early tax mistakes, Marcus (from Lesson 10) recognizes he initially underpriced his freelance work by failing to account for self-employment tax, and separately failed to adjust his W-4 promptly after a significant income change, resulting in an unexpected balance due one year. Mapping each mistake back to the specific concept from this level that would have prevented it, he commits to applying the ongoing tax calendar practice (Lesson 46) and periodic planning check-ins (Lesson 39) going forward, rather than treating tax matters as a once-a-year concern.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. This mistake reflects the debunked myth that crossing into a higher bracket taxes all income at the new, higher rate.
2. Underpricing freelance work often results from failing to account for the full self-employment tax burden.
3. This lesson deliberately connects back to concepts already covered throughout GREEN-L2, reinforcing the level's structure.
4. Most mistakes trace back to treating tax matters as fixed, one-time facts rather than an ongoing system requiring periodic attention.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Common tax mistakes include bracket-crossing fears, underpriced self-employment work, and neglected W-4 updates.
- Each mistake traces back to a specific concept already covered earlier in this level.
- This lesson echoes the RED and WHITE-track mistakes lessons, reinforcing a consistent curriculum-wide structure.
- Most mistakes share a root cause: treating tax matters as fixed and one-time rather than ongoing and evolving.
