# Tax-Loss Harvesting Across Asset Classes: Orchestrating Genuine Losses, Wash-Sale Rules, and Portfolio Rebalancing

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a populous nation's real estate and consumer credit markets, after years of rapid expansion, beginning to show signs of a sharp correction as defaults start rising. As a credit-fueled market correction creates genuine losses across multiple asset classes simultaneously, a taxpayer discovers that orchestrating tax-loss harvesting across their full portfolio, rather than within a single asset class alone, requires coordinating genuine losses, wash-sale compliance, and rebalancing goals together.

- Explain the wash-sale rule's specific mechanics, REGION-SPECIFIC RULE given this area's technical, jurisdiction-specific nature, and why this rule must be orchestrated across all of a taxpayer's accounts, not just the specific account where a loss is realized.

## Core Content
Extending this curriculum's earlier tax-loss harvesting discussion, VERIFIED FACT, a taxpayer should specifically consider genuine losses across their full portfolio — across multiple asset classes and accounts — rather than harvesting losses within a single asset class in isolation, since a genuine loss in one asset class can offset a genuine gain in a completely different asset class for tax purposes, meaning a taxpayer evaluating tax-loss harvesting opportunities only within a single asset class risks missing genuine offsetting opportunities available elsewhere in their broader portfolio.

Here is the "why" the wash-sale rule must be orchestrated across all of a taxpayer's accounts, given this area's technical, jurisdiction-specific nature: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, confirm current specific wash-sale rule mechanics for your jurisdiction, since this area is technical and specific, but a wash-sale rule generally disallows a claimed tax loss if the taxpayer purchases a substantially identical security within a specific window before or after the loss sale, meaning this rule must be orchestrated across every account a taxpayer controls, including retirement accounts, since a purchase in one account can trigger a wash sale on a loss realized in a completely different, separate account. This is why a tax-loss harvesting decision should be orchestrated alongside genuine portfolio rebalancing goals: OPINION and an important practical planning principle, a taxpayer should specifically consider whether a tax-loss harvesting transaction also advances their own genuine portfolio rebalancing goals, since combining these two objectives into a single transaction can produce a more efficient overall outcome than pursuing tax-loss harvesting and rebalancing as two entirely separate, uncoordinated actions. This is why harvesting a genuine loss and immediately reinvesting in a genuinely similar, but not substantially identical, replacement security allows a taxpayer to capture the tax benefit while maintaining intended market exposure: a taxpayer can specifically sell a security at a genuine loss and immediately purchase a genuinely similar, but not substantially identical, replacement security, capturing the tax loss for current use while avoiding an extended period out of the market, meaning this specific technique orchestrates the genuine tax benefit against the taxpayer's own continued desired market exposure.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A taxpayer facing a broad market correction specifically reviews genuine losses across their full portfolio — a taxable brokerage account, a separate retirement account, and a real estate-adjacent investment — identifying offsetting opportunities across these different asset classes rather than considering only one account in isolation. This taxpayer specifically confirms no wash-sale violation would occur across any of their accounts, harvests a genuine loss in a specific holding, and immediately reinvests in a genuinely similar but not substantially identical replacement security, capturing the tax benefit while maintaining intended market exposure and advancing a genuine rebalancing goal simultaneously.
