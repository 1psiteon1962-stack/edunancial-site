# Behavioral Discipline in Tax Planning: Orchestrating Genuine Risk Tolerance for Aggressive Positions vs Audit Risk

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a systemically important financial institution suddenly revealing a large, previously undisclosed loss, raising fears about counterparty risk rippling through the broader banking system. As fears about counterparty risk make tax authorities in some jurisdictions correspondingly more vigilant, taxpayers considering more aggressive tax positions discover that genuine behavioral discipline — honestly assessing their own risk tolerance, maintaining thorough documentation, and understanding genuine audit risk — matters considerably more than in calmer periods.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the general spectrum from clearly conservative to genuinely aggressive tax positions, and why a taxpayer should honestly locate any specific position they are considering along this spectrum.
- Explain why thorough, contemporaneous documentation represents an essential companion to any tax position, particularly a genuinely more aggressive one, rather than an afterthought addressed only if the position is later questioned.
- Explain why a taxpayer's own genuine risk tolerance for the possibility of an audit and the resulting uncertainty and potential cost should be orchestrated into how aggressive a specific position the taxpayer actually pursues.
- Explain why a genuinely sound tax position must actually have a legitimate factual and legal basis, distinguishing genuine, defensible tax planning from mere reckless non-compliance.

## Core Content
VERIFIED FACT: tax positions exist along a general spectrum from clearly conservative (positions with strong, well-established support) to genuinely aggressive (positions with meaningfully less certain support, relying on a more debatable interpretation of applicable rules), meaning a taxpayer should honestly, specifically locate any position they are considering along this genuine spectrum rather than assuming every tax position carries an identical, uniform level of risk.

Here is the "why" thorough, contemporaneous documentation represents an essential companion to any tax position, particularly a genuinely more aggressive one: a taxpayer should specifically maintain thorough, contemporaneous documentation supporting any tax position taken, particularly a genuinely more aggressive one, since this genuine, contemporaneous documentation — created at the time of the actual transaction or decision rather than reconstructed later — provides considerably stronger support if this position is ever actually questioned than documentation assembled only after the fact, once a specific position has already been challenged. This is why a taxpayer's own genuine risk tolerance for audit possibility and resulting uncertainty should be orchestrated into position aggressiveness: OPINION and an important practical planning consideration, a taxpayer should specifically, honestly assess their own genuine risk tolerance for the possibility of an audit, the resulting uncertainty this can create, and any potential additional cost (including penalties and interest if a position is ultimately not sustained), orchestrating this genuine risk tolerance into how aggressive a specific position they actually pursue, rather than pursuing a genuinely aggressive position without regard to their own honest capacity to tolerate this genuine uncertainty. This is why a genuinely sound tax position must actually have a legitimate factual and legal basis, distinguishing genuine tax planning from reckless non-compliance: a genuinely defensible tax position, however aggressive along the spectrum this lesson has described, must actually be supported by a legitimate, good-faith factual and legal basis, meaning this lesson's discussion of position aggressiveness concerns genuinely defensible, good-faith tax planning choices along a legitimate spectrum, never reckless positions lacking any genuine, legitimate basis whatsoever, which represent a fundamentally different, non-compliant category this lesson does not endorse.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A taxpayer considering a specific, genuinely more aggressive but legitimately, good-faith supportable tax position specifically maintains thorough, contemporaneous documentation supporting this position from the time of the actual transaction itself, rather than assembling this documentation only later if the position is questioned. This taxpayer honestly assesses their own genuine risk tolerance for the possibility of an audit and the resulting uncertainty, confirming they are genuinely comfortable with this specific position's place along the aggressiveness spectrum before proceeding, having also confirmed the position rests on a legitimate, good-faith factual and legal basis rather than pursuing it recklessly without this genuine foundation.
