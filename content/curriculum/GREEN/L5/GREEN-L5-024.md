## Core Content
Extending this curriculum's earlier WHITE-L5 and BLUE-L5 trust structuring discussions, focusing specifically on the grantor-versus-non-grantor tax distinction, REGION-SPECIFIC RULE, confirm current specific grantor and non-grantor trust tax rules for your jurisdiction, since this area is technical and jurisdiction-specific, but VERIFIED FACT, a grantor trust generally has its income taxed to the grantor personally, at the grantor's own individual tax rates, while a non-grantor trust generally files and pays its own separate tax return, often at compressed tax brackets reaching genuinely higher marginal rates at considerably lower income thresholds than an individual taxpayer would face.

Here is the "why" this grantor-versus-non-grantor tax distinction is directly, mechanically linked to the genuine control-versus-protection tradeoff this curriculum's earlier trust discussions established: precisely echoing those earlier discussions' finding regarding a revocable trust generally allowing more control but providing more limited protection, while an irrevocable trust provides stronger protection precisely because the grantor has genuinely relinquished this control, a trust's grantor-versus-non-grantor tax status is typically directly, mechanically determined by this same underlying degree of retained control, meaning a trust structured to preserve genuine grantor control for asset protection or other purposes will often, correspondingly, be taxed as a grantor trust, while a trust structured to relinquish genuine control for stronger asset protection will often, correspondingly, be taxed as a non-grantor trust, meaning these tax and control-protection considerations are not actually two separate, independent decisions but rather two mechanically linked aspects of the same underlying structural choice. This is why a family should orchestrate genuine, honest priorities regarding control, asset protection, and tax treatment together when structuring a trust: given this mechanical linkage, a family should specifically, honestly orchestrate their own genuine priorities across all three of these considerations together — rather than treating tax treatment as a separate, independent decision from the control-versus-protection tradeoff — since the specific trust structure a family ultimately selects will typically determine all three of these outcomes simultaneously, meaning a family choosing based on tax treatment alone, without regard to the genuinely linked control and protection consequences, risks a structure poorly suited to their own actual, fuller set of priorities.
