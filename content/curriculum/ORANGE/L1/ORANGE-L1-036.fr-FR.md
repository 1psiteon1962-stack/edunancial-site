# Building a Simple Sales Script

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the purpose of a sales script and what it isn't
- Identify the basic sections a simple sales script should include
- Practice drafting a basic sales script for a simple offer

## Core Content

A sales script is a flexible outline of how a sales conversation should generally flow, not a word-for-word speech to recite. Scripts that sound memorized and robotic tend to hurt trust, because they feel impersonal and scripted rather than like a genuine conversation. A good script gives structure while leaving room for natural, real-time responses.

A basic script usually includes: an opening (a natural way to start the conversation and establish why you're reaching out), a discovery section (questions to understand the prospect's situation and problem), a bridge (connecting what you offer to what you just learned), and a close (a clear next step). This isn't meant to be followed rigidly — it's a map, not a script to be read verbatim.

The goal of a script is consistency without losing authenticity: making sure important elements (like asking discovery questions before pitching) don't get skipped, while still sounding like a real person having a real conversation.

## Worked Example

**Opening:** "Hi, I noticed you [specific detail relevant to them] — do you have a couple minutes?"
**Discovery:** "Before I say anything about what I do, can you tell me a bit about [relevant situation]?"
**Bridge:** "That makes sense — that's actually exactly the kind of problem [product/service] was built to solve, because [specific connection to what they said]."
**Close:** "Would it make sense to [specific, low-commitment next step]?"

This structure adapts to nearly any sales conversation while still sounding natural, because the actual words change based on what the prospect says during discovery.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is a sales script, and what should it not be?
2. Name the four basic sections of a simple sales script.
3. Why do memorized, robotic-sounding scripts hurt trust?
4. What is the goal of using a script, according to this lesson?

## Answer Key
1. A flexible outline of conversation flow, not a word-for-word speech to recite.
2. Opening, discovery, bridge, close.
3. They feel impersonal and scripted rather than like a genuine conversation, damaging trust.
4. Consistency (not skipping important elements) without losing authenticity.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This defines what a script is meant to be — a structural guide, not a rigid recitation.
2. These four sections form the basic architecture of most effective sales conversations.
3. Authenticity matters to trust-building, and rigid scripts undermine that authenticity.
4. Balancing structure with natural conversation is the core purpose behind using a script at all.

## Key Takeaways
- A sales script is a flexible structural guide, not a word-for-word speech.
- The basic sections are opening, discovery, bridge, and close.
- The goal is consistency in covering important elements, without sacrificing authenticity.
