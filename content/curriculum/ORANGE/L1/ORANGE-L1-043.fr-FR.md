# Building a Repeatable Sales System

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the difference between individual sales skill and a repeatable sales system
- Identify the components a basic repeatable system needs
- Understand why systems matter for a business's ability to scale

## Core Content

A skilled individual seller can close deals, but that skill lives in one person's head and habits. A repeatable system means the process — outreach, discovery, follow-up cadence, objection handling, closing — is documented and consistent enough that someone else could follow it and get reasonably similar results, without having to reinvent everything from scratch.

This matters for a simple reason: a business built entirely around one person's individual sales talent has a ceiling equal to that one person's time and energy. A business with a documented, repeatable system can train additional people, delegate parts of the process, and grow beyond what any single seller could accomplish alone.

Building a basic system doesn't require anything complicated — it means writing down what's actually working: the outreach approach that gets responses, the discovery questions that reveal real needs, the follow-up cadence that converts, and the objection responses that address common concerns. Once it's written down, it can be taught, refined, and repeated.

## Worked Example

A solo consultant who closes deals purely on natural talent eventually wants to hire a second salesperson. Without a documented system, the new hire has nothing to learn from except vague advice like "just build rapport." With a documented system — including the actual discovery questions the consultant asks, the specific way objections get handled, and the follow-up schedule that works — the new hire can be trained systematically and reach reasonable competency much faster.

4. In the worked example, what allowed the new hire to be trained effectively?

## Answer Key
1. Individual skill lives in one person's habits; a system is documented and repeatable by others.
2. Because growth is limited by that one person's available time and energy.
3. Writing down what's actually working — outreach, discovery questions, follow-up cadence, and objection responses.
4. A documented system with specific, teachable details instead of vague advice.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This distinction is the core concept connecting individual skill to scalable business systems.
2. A single person's capacity is a hard limit on growth without a repeatable, delegable process.
3. Documentation is the practical first step toward building any repeatable system.
4. Specificity in the documented system made real training and delegation possible.

## Key Takeaways
- Individual sales skill has a natural ceiling; a repeatable system allows growth beyond one person.
- Building a system means documenting what's actually working, not creating something complicated.
- A documented system enables training, delegation, and scaling a sales process beyond any single seller.
