# When to Walk Away From a Sale

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why not every possible sale is a good sale to pursue
- Identify warning signs that a sale may not be a good fit
- Recognize the long-term cost of closing a bad-fit sale

## Core Content

It might seem like more sales are always better, but that's not quite true. A sale to someone who isn't actually a good fit for what you offer often ends up costing more than it earns — through refunds, poor reviews, excessive support demands, or a customer who's unhappy no matter what you do because their expectations were never realistic to begin with.

Warning signs worth taking seriously include: a prospect whose expectations clearly exceed what the product or service can realistically deliver, a prospect who's dismissive or disrespectful during the sales process itself (a preview of how they'll likely behave as a customer), or a situation where saying yes would require making a promise you're not confident you can actually keep.

Walking away from a bad-fit sale isn't a failure — it's often the more experienced, disciplined move. It protects your reputation, your time, and your ability to focus on customers who are genuinely a good fit.

## Worked Example

A prospect wants a service delivered in a fraction of the normal time, at a significantly reduced price, and is already pushing back hard on reasonable terms during the very first conversation. A seller focused only on closing might agree just to get the sale. A more experienced seller recognizes the warning signs and says honestly: "Based on what you're describing, I don't think this is the right fit — I'd rather be upfront about that now than take this on and not be able to deliver what you're expecting." This protects both the seller's reputation and the prospect from a bad outcome.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why isn't more sales always better?
2. Name two warning signs that a sale may not be a good fit.
3. Why is walking away from a bad-fit sale sometimes the more disciplined move?
4. What did the seller in the worked example protect by walking away?

## Answer Key
1. A bad-fit sale can cost more in refunds, poor reviews, or excessive support than it earns.
2. Any two of: unrealistic expectations, dismissive or disrespectful behavior during the sales process, needing to promise something you're not confident you can deliver.
3. Because it protects reputation, time, and focus for customers who are genuinely a good fit.
4. Their reputation and the prospect's own outcome, by being upfront rather than overpromising.

## Answers and Explanations
1. This challenges the assumption that closing every possible sale is always the right goal.
2. These signals help identify mismatches before they become costly problems.
3. Discipline here means prioritizing long-term reputation over short-term revenue.
4. Honesty upfront avoided a likely bad outcome for both parties.

## Key Takeaways
- Not every possible sale is a good sale — some cost more than they're worth in the long run.
- Watch for unrealistic expectations, disrespectful behavior, or promises you can't confidently keep.
- Walking away from a bad-fit sale is often the more experienced, disciplined choice, not a failure.
