## ORANGE-L2-004
### Customer Acquisition Cost: What It Really Costs to Win a Customer

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) at a conceptual level.
- Calculate a simple CAC given marketing spend and number of new customers.
- Explain why CAC should account for all acquisition-related costs, not just advertising spend.

**2. Core Content**
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is the total cost of acquiring a new customer, generally calculated by dividing total acquisition-related spending over a period by the number of new customers acquired during that same period. A common, costly mistake is calculating CAC using only advertising spend, when a more complete calculation should include all acquisition-related costs: advertising, marketing salaries or contractor fees, sales team compensation attributable to acquisition, and any tools or software supporting the acquisition process — echoing BLUE-L2-024's discussion of calculating the true total cost of an employee, now applied to the true total cost of a customer.

Understanding a business's actual, complete CAC is foundational to nearly every decision covered in this level: it directly feeds into the CAC-to-LTV comparison in Lesson 6, informs marketing budget allocation (Lesson 41), and helps a business understand whether its current growth strategy is actually financially sustainable, not merely generating sales volume without regard to what that volume actually costs to produce.

**3. Worked Example**
Calculating her business's CAC, a business owner initially considers only her advertising spend, arriving at a seemingly low, attractive figure. Recalculating more completely to include her marketing contractor's fees, the portion of her own time spent on sales activities (valued appropriately), and her marketing software subscription costs, she finds her true CAC is considerably higher than her advertising-spend-only calculation suggested — a more accurate, complete figure that will inform her subsequent decisions about marketing budget allocation and overall growth strategy sustainability.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)?
   a) The total revenue generated by a single customer
   b) The total cost of acquiring a new customer, generally total acquisition spending divided by new customers acquired
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) The price a customer pays for a product
2. What common, costly mistake does this lesson identify regarding CAC calculation?
   a) Including too many cost categories in the calculation
   b) Calculating CAC using only advertising spend, omitting other acquisition-related costs
   c) CAC calculation is always perfectly accurate regardless of method
   d) CAC has no relationship to marketing spend at all
3. True or False: A complete CAC calculation should include only advertising spend, with no other cost categories considered.
4. Why was the business owner's recalculated CAC considerably higher than her original, advertising-only figure?
   a) Her advertising spend had increased dramatically
   b) Her more complete calculation included contractor fees, her own time, and software costs, which her original figure had omitted
   c) She acquired significantly more customers in her recalculation
   d) CAC calculation method has no effect on the resulting figure

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. CAC is the total cost of acquiring a new customer, generally total acquisition spending divided by new customers acquired.
2. Calculating CAC using only advertising spend, omitting other acquisition-related costs, is a common, costly mistake.
3. A complete CAC calculation should include all acquisition-related costs, not advertising spend alone.
4. The business owner's more complete calculation, including omitted cost categories, revealed a considerably higher, more accurate CAC.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- CAC is the total cost of acquiring a new customer, generally total acquisition spending divided by new customers acquired.
- A complete calculation should include advertising, personnel, and tool costs, not advertising spend alone.
- This echoes BLUE-L2-024's true-total-cost discussion, now applied to the true total cost of a customer.
- Understanding accurate CAC is foundational to the CAC-to-LTV comparison and marketing budget decisions covered later in this level.
