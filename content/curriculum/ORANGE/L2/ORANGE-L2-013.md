## ORANGE-L2-013
### The Customer Journey: Mapping Touchpoints From Awareness to Advocacy

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define customer journey mapping at a conceptual level.
- Explain how this concept extends the marketing funnel (Lesson 2) with specific touchpoints.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical journey map.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 2's funnel stages, customer journey mapping involves identifying the specific touchpoints — every interaction a customer has with a business, across every stage from initial awareness through eventual advocacy (a satisfied customer recommending the business to others) — and understanding the customer's experience, needs, and potential friction points at each specific touchpoint. This provides more granular detail than the funnel's broader stages alone: within the "awareness" stage, for example, specific touchpoints might include a social media post, a friend's recommendation, or a search engine result, each with distinct characteristics worth understanding individually.

Mapping this journey using a specific persona (Lesson 12) helps a business identify friction points — places where the customer's experience might create confusion, frustration, or unnecessary obstacles — and opportunities to improve the experience at each specific touchpoint, rather than only considering the customer's experience in broad, undifferentiated terms.

**3. Worked Example**
Mapping "Maria's" (Lesson 12) journey from awareness through advocacy, a business owner identifies specific touchpoints: Maria first discovers the business through a search engine result, then visits the website, signs up for a free trial, receives onboarding emails, becomes a paying customer, and eventually refers a colleague. Examining each specific touchpoint, she discovers a friction point in the onboarding email sequence that seems to confuse new trial users, prompting a specific improvement to that particular touchpoint rather than addressing her customer experience only in broad, undifferentiated terms.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does customer journey mapping generally involve?
   a) Only considering the broad marketing funnel stages, with no further detail
   b) Identifying specific touchpoints and understanding the customer's experience at each one, from awareness through advocacy
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A specific pricing strategy
2. How does journey mapping extend the marketing funnel discussion from Lesson 2?
   a) It has no relationship to the funnel concept
   b) It provides more granular detail, identifying specific touchpoints within each broader funnel stage
   c) It replaces the funnel concept entirely with an unrelated framework
   d) It only applies to the final action stage
3. True or False: Journey mapping should only consider the customer's experience in broad, undifferentiated terms, without identifying specific touchpoints.
4. What did the business owner's specific touchpoint mapping reveal about her onboarding process?
   a) Nothing useful was revealed
   b) A friction point in the onboarding email sequence confusing new trial users
   c) Her entire customer journey was flawless with no friction points at all
   d) She had no touchpoints to examine

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Journey mapping identifies specific touchpoints and the customer's experience at each one, from awareness through advocacy.
2. Journey mapping provides more granular detail than the funnel alone, identifying specific touchpoints within each broader stage.
3. Journey mapping specifically identifies granular touchpoints, rather than considering experience only in broad, undifferentiated terms.
4. The business owner's specific touchpoint examination revealed a friction point in her onboarding email sequence.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Customer journey mapping identifies specific touchpoints and experiences from awareness through advocacy.
- This extends Lesson 2's funnel stages with more granular, touchpoint-specific detail.
- Mapping with a specific persona (Lesson 12) helps identify friction points and improvement opportunities.
- This granular approach supports more targeted improvements than considering customer experience only in broad terms.
