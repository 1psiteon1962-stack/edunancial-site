## ORANGE-L2-021
### Marketing Channel Selection: Matching Channel to Audience and Budget

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Synthesize Lessons 14–20 into a channel selection framework.
- Apply this framework to a hypothetical channel decision.
- Explain why channel selection should be evidence-based, not trend-driven.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson serves as a synthesis point, consolidating the various channels discussed in Lessons 14–20 — inbound versus outbound, content marketing, SEO, paid advertising, social media, email, and referral marketing — into a practical selection framework: Where does the target audience (Lesson 10) and persona (Lesson 12) actually spend time and attention? What does the available budget (echoing BLUE-L2's capital constraint discussions) support, given each channel's different cost structures and time-to-results profiles (Lesson 17)? And what channel or channels align with the business's actual content creation strengths and capacity?

A common, costly mistake this synthesis lesson specifically warns against: selecting a channel primarily because it is currently trending or because a competitor uses it, rather than because genuine evidence suggests it fits the specific business's audience, budget, and capabilities — echoing this level's broader theme that evidence-based, deliberate decisions should replace impulse or imitation-driven ones.

**3. Worked Example**
Rather than adopting a trending marketing channel simply because competitors are using it, a business owner applies this lesson's synthesis framework: researching where her specific target audience actually spends time (echoing Lesson 18), evaluating her available budget against each channel's cost and time-to-results profile (echoing Lesson 17), and honestly assessing her own capacity to consistently produce the content type each channel requires. This evidence-based process leads her to a different channel combination than her competitors use, one genuinely suited to her specific situation rather than one chosen through imitation alone.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. This lesson synthesizes the various channels from Lessons 14–20 into a practical selection framework.
2. Selecting a channel primarily due to trends or competitor imitation, rather than genuine evidence, is identified as a common, costly mistake.
3. Channel selection should be evidence-based, specific to a business's own audience, budget, and capabilities, not trend-driven.
4. The business owner's evidence-based process appropriately led to a different, genuinely suited channel combination than her competitors' choices.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- This lesson consolidates Lessons 14–20's channels into a practical selection framework based on audience, budget, and capability fit.
- Channel selection should be evidence-based, not driven by trends or competitor imitation.
- The framework asks where the audience spends time, what the budget supports, and what content capacity the business has.
- This synthesis reinforces this level's broader theme of evidence-based, deliberate decision-making.
