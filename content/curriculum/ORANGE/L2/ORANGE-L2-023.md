## ORANGE-L2-023
### The Psychology of Pricing: Anchoring, Charm Pricing, and Bundling

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define anchoring, charm pricing, and bundling as specific pricing psychology techniques.
- Explain the general mechanism behind each technique.
- Recognize the ethical considerations these techniques raise, previewing Lesson 28.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 22, this lesson examines specific pricing psychology techniques. Anchoring involves presenting a higher reference price alongside a lower actual price, influencing customers' perception of the actual price as a good value relative to that reference point, even if the reference price itself was never a realistic transaction price. Charm pricing involves pricing just below a round number (a price ending in .99, for example), which research suggests can influence perceived value beyond the minimal actual price difference involved. Bundling involves combining multiple products or services into a single package price, which can increase perceived value and average transaction size compared to purchasing items individually.

These techniques raise legitimate ethical considerations worth acknowledging directly, previewing Lesson 28's broader persuasion ethics discussion: using a genuinely representative anchor price differs meaningfully from using an artificially inflated reference price never actually offered, and businesses should consider where the line falls between legitimate psychological pricing techniques and potentially misleading practices that could also raise the advertising law concerns discussed in PURPLE-L2-041.

**3. Worked Example**
Considering how to present her pricing, a business owner uses a genuine, previously-charged price as an anchor alongside her current promotional price, ensuring the anchor reflects an actual, honest reference point rather than an artificially inflated figure never actually charged. She also considers charm pricing for her standard offerings and a bundled package combining complementary products at a combined price offering genuine additional value, while being careful to ensure none of these techniques cross into the misleading territory this lesson previews as an ethical concern developed further in Lessons 28–29.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does anchoring generally involve, in pricing psychology?
   a) Never presenting any reference price at all
   b) Presenting a higher reference price alongside a lower actual price, influencing perceived value
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Only presenting the lowest possible price with no reference point
2. What ethical consideration does this lesson raise regarding anchoring?
   a) Anchoring has no ethical considerations whatsoever
   b) Using a genuinely representative anchor differs meaningfully from using an artificially inflated reference price never actually offered
   c) All anchoring techniques are automatically misleading regardless of the reference price's accuracy
   d) Ethical considerations only apply to charm pricing, never anchoring
3. True or False: Bundling always increases actual value to the customer, regardless of the specific products combined or the combined price.
4. Why did the business owner ensure her anchor price reflected an actual, previously-charged price?
   a) She wanted to use a misleading, artificially inflated reference price
   b) She recognized the ethical distinction between a genuine anchor and an artificially inflated one, previewing Lesson 28's discussion
   c) Anchor price accuracy has no relationship to ethical marketing practice
   d) She had no interest in her pricing's ethical implications

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Anchoring involves presenting a higher reference price alongside a lower actual price, influencing perceived value.
2. Using a genuinely representative anchor differs meaningfully from using an artificially inflated reference price never actually offered.
3. Bundling's value depends on the specific products and combined price; it does not automatically increase actual value in every case.
4. The business owner's use of an accurate, genuine anchor price reflected sound ethical practice, previewing this level's later ethics discussion.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Anchoring, charm pricing, and bundling are specific pricing psychology techniques influencing perceived value.
- Anchoring uses a reference price; charm pricing uses below-round-number pricing; bundling combines products into a package price.
- These techniques raise legitimate ethical considerations, particularly regarding the honesty of reference prices used.
- This previews Lesson 28's broader persuasion ethics discussion and connects to PURPLE-L2-041's advertising law discussion.
