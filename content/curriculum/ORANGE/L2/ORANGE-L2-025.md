## ORANGE-L2-025
### The Sales Process: From Prospecting to Close

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify the general stages of a typical sales process.
- Explain how this process connects directly to Lesson 3's sales funnel discussion.
- Apply this framework to a hypothetical sales scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 3, this lesson examines the sales process's specific general stages in more detail: prospecting (identifying potential customers who might genuinely benefit from the offering), qualifying (developed further in Lesson 26, assessing whether a specific prospect represents a genuine, viable opportunity), presenting (communicating how the offering specifically addresses the prospect's particular needs, echoing Lesson 9's UVP discussion), handling objections (developed further in Lesson 27), and closing (securing the prospect's actual commitment to purchase).

This is a general framework, not a rigid, universal script — the specific length, formality, and emphasis of each stage varies significantly by industry, price point, and sales context (a low-cost, high-volume retail sale involves a much more compressed version of this process than a complex, high-value business-to-business sale) — but recognizing these general stages provides a useful structure for understanding where a specific sales interaction currently stands and what comes next.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her own sales process for a relatively high-value business-to-business offering, a business owner maps her interactions against this general framework: identifying prospects through research and referrals (prospecting), assessing whether each prospect has a genuine need and budget (qualifying, Lesson 26), presenting a tailored proposal addressing their specific situation (presenting), addressing their specific concerns thoughtfully (handling objections, Lesson 27), and finally securing their commitment (closing). Recognizing where a specific prospect currently sits within this framework helps her determine the most appropriate next action for that particular relationship.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is "prospecting," as a general sales process stage?
   a) Securing a prospect's final commitment to purchase
   b) Identifying potential customers who might genuinely benefit from the offering
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) Addressing a prospect's specific concerns
2. Why is this framework described as general rather than a rigid, universal script?
   a) It applies identically, with no variation, across every industry and sales context
   b) The specific length, formality, and emphasis of each stage varies significantly by industry, price point, and context
   c) This framework has no practical application to any actual sales process
   d) Only one specific stage ever applies to any given sale
3. True or False: A low-cost, high-volume retail sale generally involves an identical, equally lengthy process to a complex, high-value business-to-business sale.
4. How did the business owner use this framework for her high-value offering?
   a) She ignored the framework entirely
   b) She mapped her specific interactions against the general stages, helping her determine the appropriate next action for each prospect
   c) She assumed every prospect required an identical, scripted approach with no variation
   d) She had no prospects to consider at any stage

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Prospecting involves identifying potential customers who might genuinely benefit from the offering.
2. This framework's specific length, formality, and emphasis varies significantly by industry, price point, and sales context.
3. A low-cost, high-volume retail sale generally involves a much more compressed process than a complex, high-value transaction.
4. The business owner's mapping of her specific process against this general framework helped her determine appropriate next actions for each prospect.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- The general sales process includes prospecting, qualifying, presenting, handling objections, and closing.
- This builds directly on Lesson 3's sales funnel discussion, examining its specific stages in more detail.
- This is a general framework, not a rigid script, varying significantly by industry, price point, and sales context.
- Recognizing these stages helps identify where a specific sales interaction stands and what comes next.
