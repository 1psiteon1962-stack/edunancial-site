## ORANGE-L2-026
### Qualifying a Lead: Not Every Prospect Is a Customer

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of lead qualification.
- Identify common qualification criteria.
- Connect this concept directly to Lesson 6's CAC-to-LTV discussion.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 25's sales process discussion, lead qualification is the practice of assessing whether a specific prospect represents a genuine, viable sales opportunity before investing significant additional time and resources in pursuing them further. Common qualification criteria include whether the prospect has a genuine need the offering addresses, whether they have the budget or financial capacity to purchase, whether they have the authority to make or influence the purchase decision, and whether their timeline aligns with a realistic sales process.

This connects directly to Lesson 6's CAC-to-LTV discussion: pursuing unqualified prospects — those lacking genuine need, budget, authority, or timeline fit — consumes sales resources without a realistic likelihood of producing revenue, effectively increasing the true acquisition cost of the customers who do eventually convert, since resources spent on unqualified prospects must be absorbed somewhere in the overall acquisition economics.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her inbound leads, a business owner applies qualification criteria before investing significant sales time in each one: confirming genuine need, assessing budget fit through preliminary conversation, identifying whether she is speaking with an actual decision-maker, and understanding their realistic timeline. She recognizes that pursuing every inbound inquiry with equal, extensive effort regardless of these factors would waste her limited sales resources on prospects unlikely to convert, ultimately raising her effective CAC (echoing Lesson 6) for the customers who do eventually purchase.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general purpose of lead qualification?
   a) To pursue every single prospect with identical, extensive effort regardless of fit
   b) To assess whether a specific prospect represents a genuine, viable sales opportunity before investing significant additional resources
   c) A type of tax credit
   d) A specific pricing strategy
2. What is one common qualification criterion, per this lesson?
   a) The prospect's favorite color
   b) Whether the prospect has a genuine need, budget, authority, and realistic timeline
   c) A criterion entirely unrelated to the prospect's actual purchasing situation
   d) Only the prospect's geographic location, with no other consideration
3. True or False: Pursuing unqualified prospects with equal effort as qualified ones has no effect on a business's overall acquisition economics.
4. Why did the business owner apply qualification criteria to her inbound leads?
   a) She wanted to waste her limited sales resources on unlikely prospects
   b) She recognized unqualified prospects would raise her effective CAC for the customers who do eventually convert
   c) Qualification has no relationship to sales resource allocation
   d) She had no interest in her business's acquisition economics

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Lead qualification assesses whether a specific prospect represents a genuine, viable opportunity before investing significant additional resources.
2. Genuine need, budget, authority, and realistic timeline are common qualification criteria.
3. Pursuing unqualified prospects consumes resources without realistic conversion likelihood, raising effective CAC for actual converting customers.
4. The business owner's qualification process appropriately protected her limited sales resources and overall acquisition economics.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Lead qualification assesses genuine viability before investing significant additional sales resources in a prospect.
- Common criteria include genuine need, budget, decision-making authority, and realistic timeline.
- This connects directly to Lesson 6's CAC-to-LTV discussion, since unqualified pursuit raises effective acquisition cost.
- Qualification protects limited sales resources for prospects genuinely likely to convert.
