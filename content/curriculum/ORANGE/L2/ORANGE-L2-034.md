## ORANGE-L2-034
### The Cost of Losing a Customer: Churn and Its Hidden Impact

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define churn at a conceptual level.
- Explain how churn directly affects LTV and overall business economics.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical churn-reduction scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 33, churn refers to the rate at which customers stop doing business with a company over a given period — a metric with direct, sometimes underappreciated impact on the LTV discussion from Lesson 5, since higher churn generally means shorter average customer relationships and correspondingly lower LTV, which in turn worsens the CAC-to-LTV ratio (Lesson 6) even if acquisition efforts themselves remain unchanged and effective.

Churn's impact often compounds in ways that are easy to underappreciate: a business experiencing significant churn must continuously acquire new customers merely to maintain its existing customer base size, let alone grow it, meaning a portion of every acquisition effort is effectively being consumed simply replacing lost customers rather than contributing to genuine net growth — making churn reduction (echoing Lesson 36's customer service discussion) a potentially high-leverage area for improving overall business economics, sometimes more impactful than additional acquisition spending alone.

**3. Worked Example**
Analyzing her subscription business's growth challenges, a business owner discovers that despite strong new customer acquisition, her overall customer base has grown only modestly, due to significant churn requiring continuous replacement of lost customers merely to maintain her existing base size. Recognizing this compounding effect, she shifts some focus toward understanding and addressing her churn causes (echoing Lesson 36's customer service discussion), recognizing that even a modest churn reduction could have a larger impact on her overall growth than additional acquisition spending alone, given how much of her current acquisition effort was being consumed simply replacing lost customers.

3. True or False: A business experiencing significant churn can achieve substantial net customer base growth using the same acquisition effort as a business with minimal churn.

   c) Churn has no relationship to her overall business growth
   d) She wanted to increase her churn rate further

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Churn is the rate at which customers stop doing business with a company over a given period.
2. Higher churn generally means shorter average customer relationships and correspondingly lower LTV.
3. Significant churn requires continuous replacement effort, consuming acquisition resources and limiting genuine net growth compared to lower-churn businesses.
4. The business owner's focus shift appropriately recognized churn reduction's potentially high-leverage impact on overall business economics.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Churn is the rate at which customers stop doing business with a company over a given period.
- Higher churn directly lowers LTV, worsening the CAC-to-LTV ratio even without changes to acquisition effectiveness.
- Significant churn requires continuous replacement effort, consuming acquisition resources without contributing to genuine net growth.
- Churn reduction can be a high-leverage area for improving overall business economics, connecting to Lesson 36's customer service discussion.
