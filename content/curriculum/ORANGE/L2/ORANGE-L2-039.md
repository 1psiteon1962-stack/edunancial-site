## ORANGE-L2-039
### Return on Ad Spend: Evaluating Marketing as an Investment

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) at a conceptual level.
- Calculate a simple ROAS given revenue and ad spend figures.
- Connect this concept directly to Lesson 38's meaningful metrics discussion.

**2. Core Content**
Building directly on Lesson 38, Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) is a meaningful metric calculated by dividing revenue generated from a specific advertising effort by the amount spent on that advertising, providing a direct measure of how efficiently a specific marketing investment is producing revenue — echoing BLUE-L2-012's break-even discussion, now applied specifically to evaluating marketing spend as a genuine investment decision rather than merely an operational cost.

Evaluating ROAS in isolation, however, has limits worth recognizing directly: a campaign might show an attractive immediate ROAS while acquiring customers with poor long-term retention (Lesson 34) or low LTV (Lesson 5), meaning a genuinely complete evaluation should consider ROAS alongside these other meaningful metrics from Lesson 38, not as a single, standalone sufficient measure of a campaign's true overall value to the business.

**3. Worked Example**
Calculating ROAS for a specific advertising campaign, a business owner divides the campaign's generated revenue by its total ad spend, arriving at a figure suggesting the campaign was reasonably efficient in the short term. Before concluding the campaign was genuinely successful, however, she also examines the resulting customers' retention and LTV (echoing Lesson 38's broader metrics discussion), discovering these particular customers churned at a notably higher rate than her average, meaning her campaign's attractive immediate ROAS did not tell the complete story of its actual long-term value to her business.

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. ROAS is revenue generated from a specific advertising effort divided by the amount spent on that advertising.
2. A campaign might show attractive immediate ROAS while acquiring customers with poor long-term retention or low LTV, limiting ROAS alone as a complete measure.
3. An attractive immediate ROAS figure does not guarantee a genuinely successful, sustainable investment without considering retention and LTV.
4. The business owner's broader examination appropriately recognized ROAS alone might not reveal the campaign's true overall long-term value.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- ROAS measures revenue generated from advertising divided by advertising spend, evaluating marketing efficiency.
- This echoes BLUE-L2-012's break-even discussion, applied specifically to marketing investment evaluation.
- ROAS alone has limits, since it may not reveal poor long-term retention or low LTV affecting a campaign's true value.
- A complete evaluation considers ROAS alongside the other meaningful metrics discussed in Lesson 38.
