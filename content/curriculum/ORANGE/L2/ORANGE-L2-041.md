## ORANGE-L2-041
### Marketing Budget Allocation: A Multi-Piece Decision

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Synthesize Lessons 21, 33, 38–40 into a budget allocation framework.
- Apply this framework to a hypothetical multi-channel budget scenario.
- Explain why budget allocation should be evidence-based and periodically revisited.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson synthesizes several threads into a practical marketing budget allocation framework: channel selection based on audience and budget fit (Lesson 21), the balance between acquisition and retention investment (Lesson 33), and ongoing evaluation using meaningful metrics, ROAS, and A/B testing (Lessons 38–40) to continuously refine where budget is actually producing the best results. This is inherently a multi-piece decision, echoing this level's opening lesson: allocating budget to one channel or activity affects what remains available for others, meaning allocation decisions should be made holistically, not by evaluating each potential expenditure in isolation.

Effective budget allocation is not a one-time decision made annually and left unchanged; it should be periodically revisited using the actual performance data this level has emphasized throughout, shifting resources toward what is demonstrably working and away from what is not, rather than maintaining a fixed allocation based on initial assumptions that may no longer reflect actual current performance.

**3. Worked Example**
Allocating her marketing budget, a business owner considers her various channels (Lesson 21), the balance between acquisition and retention spending (Lesson 33), and her ongoing performance data (Lessons 38–40) together, rather than making isolated decisions about each channel independently. She commits to revisiting this allocation quarterly, shifting resources based on actual demonstrated performance rather than treating her initial allocation as a fixed, unchanging plan disconnected from the real results her ongoing measurement efforts reveal.
