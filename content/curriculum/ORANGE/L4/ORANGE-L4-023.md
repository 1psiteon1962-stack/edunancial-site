# Value Proposition Design Analysis: Why the Proposition Must Address a Genuine Job the Customer Is Trying to Get Done

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once. As a widespread disruption changes how customers actually use products and services, businesses discover that customers were never really buying the specific features being advertised — they were hiring the product to accomplish some genuine underlying goal, and disruption-driven shifts in that underlying goal can leave a feature-focused value proposition suddenly disconnected from what customers actually need.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the 'jobs to be done' framework, and why it holds that customers 'hire' a product to accomplish a specific underlying goal, rather than simply purchasing a set of features.
- Explain why a value proposition framed around a genuine underlying customer goal is generally more compelling than one framed purely around product features.
- Explain why understanding a customer's genuine underlying job can reveal previously unrecognized competitors, since the true competition is whatever else could accomplish that same job.
- Explain why a shift in a customer's underlying circumstances can change what job they are actually trying to get done, requiring a business to reassess its value proposition accordingly.

## Core Content
The "jobs to be done" framework holds that customers do not simply purchase a product for its own sake, but rather "hire" that product to accomplish a specific underlying goal or job they are genuinely trying to get done in their own life or business. VERIFIED FACT: this framing shifts the analytical focus away from the product's own specific features and toward the customer's genuine underlying motivation for using that product in the first place, since two customers might purchase the identical product to accomplish two entirely different underlying jobs, meaning correctly understanding this underlying job matters considerably for how the value proposition should actually be framed.

Here is the "why" a value proposition framed around a genuine underlying customer goal is generally more compelling than one framed purely around product features: a feature-focused proposition ("our product has X specification") requires the customer to independently translate that specific feature into its actual relevant benefit for their own particular situation, while a proposition framed directly around the customer's genuine underlying goal ("our product helps you accomplish Y outcome") speaks directly to what the customer actually cares about, without requiring this additional translation step, meaning outcome-focused framing generally connects more immediately and more persuasively with a prospect's own actual motivation.

This is why understanding a customer's genuine underlying job can reveal previously unrecognized competitors: since the true competitive set for a specific job includes anything else that could accomplish that same underlying job, not merely other products in the same conventional product category, a business may discover that its genuine competition includes an entirely different type of product, or even a completely different, non-product-based solution (doing the job manually, or not addressing the job at all), that a narrower, category-based competitive analysis would have entirely overlooked, meaning correctly identifying the genuine underlying job broadens, and often meaningfully changes, a business's accurate understanding of its own actual competitive landscape. This is why a shift in a customer's underlying circumstances can change what job they are actually trying to get done, requiring a business to reassess its value proposition accordingly: since the genuine underlying job, not the specific product category, is what actually drives a customer's purchase decision, a significant change in the customer's own circumstances (such as those a widespread disruption might produce) can shift what job they are actually trying to accomplish, meaning a value proposition that remains framed around the previous underlying job, even if the product's own specific features have not changed at all, can become genuinely disconnected from what customers now actually need, requiring the business to reassess and potentially reframe its value proposition around the customer's newly relevant underlying job.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A meal-delivery service initially frames its value proposition around specific features (fresh ingredients, diverse recipe options), but deeper research reveals that its customers are actually "hiring" the service to accomplish a genuine underlying job: reducing the daily mental burden and time pressure of deciding what to cook after a long workday. Reframing its value proposition specifically around this underlying job ("never wonder what's for dinner again") connects more directly and persuasively with customers' actual motivation than the original feature-focused framing had, and this reframing also reveals that the service's genuine competition includes not just other meal-delivery services, but also takeout ordering and simply skipping cooking altogether — solutions to the same underlying job the original, narrower, feature-based competitive framing had entirely overlooked.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What does the 'jobs to be done' framework hold regarding why customers purchase a product?**
A) Customers purchase a product purely for its own sake, with no underlying goal or motivation involved
B) Customers 'hire' a product to accomplish a specific underlying goal or job they are genuinely trying to get done, rather than simply purchasing a set of features for their own sake

**Q2. Why is a value proposition framed around a genuine underlying customer goal generally more compelling than one framed around features?**
A) Feature-focused framing is always more compelling than outcome-focused framing
B) Outcome-focused framing speaks directly to what the customer actually cares about without requiring them to independently translate a feature into its relevant benefit, connecting more immediately and persuasively with their actual motivation
C) Value proposition framing has no actual relationship to how compelling a proposition is to a customer
D) Customers always prefer a value proposition listing every available product feature in detail

**Q3. Why can understanding a customer's genuine underlying job reveal previously unrecognized competitors?**
A) A business's competitive set is always limited exclusively to other products in the identical conventional product category
B) The true competitive set for a specific job includes anything else that could accomplish that same underlying job, potentially including entirely different types of products or non-product-based solutions a narrower, category-based analysis would overlook

**Q4. Why can a shift in a customer's underlying circumstances change what job they are actually trying to get done?**
A) A customer's underlying job never actually changes regardless of any shift in their circumstances
B) Since the genuine underlying job, not the specific product category, drives the purchase decision, a significant change in circumstances can shift what job the customer is actually trying to accomplish, potentially disconnecting an unchanged value proposition from what customers now actually need
C) Product features, not the underlying job, are the only thing that can ever actually change over time
D) Changes in customer circumstances have no actual relationship to which job they are trying to get done

**Q5. In the example, why did reframing around the underlying job reveal new competitive threats the original framing had missed?**
A) The original feature-focused framing had already correctly identified every genuine competitor
B) Reframing around the genuine underlying job (reducing daily mental burden and time pressure around dinner) revealed that takeout ordering and simply skipping cooking were also solutions to that same job, competitors the narrower, feature-based framing centered on meal-delivery specifics had entirely overlooked

## Answer Key
Q1: B
Q2: B
Q3: B
Q4: B
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** This focus on the customer's genuine underlying motivation, rather than the product's features alone, is precisely what the jobs to be done framework emphasizes.

**Q2.** This direct connection to genuine customer motivation, avoiding the extra translation step feature-focused framing requires, is precisely why outcome-focused framing tends to be more compelling.

**Q3.** This broader view of competition based on the underlying job, rather than conventional product category alone, is precisely why this framework can reveal previously unrecognized competitive threats or alternatives.

**Q4.** This link between changing circumstances and a shifting underlying job is precisely why a business may need to reassess and reframe its value proposition even when its product's own features remain unchanged.

**Q5.** This direct illustration of a broadened competitive view following reframing around the underlying job exemplifies precisely why this framework can reveal competition a narrower, product-category-based analysis would miss.

## Key Takeaways
- The jobs to be done framework holds that customers hire a product to accomplish a genuine underlying goal, not simply to acquire a set of features.
- A value proposition framed around this underlying goal connects more directly and persuasively with customer motivation than one framed purely around features.
- Understanding the genuine underlying job broadens a business's accurate view of its competition to include anything else that could accomplish that same job.
- A shift in customer circumstances can change the underlying job being sought, requiring a business to reassess and potentially reframe its value proposition accordingly.
