# Marketing at Level 5: Every Sales Decision Moves the Whole Board

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: When interest rates rise sharply, customers borrow less and buy more carefully. Ask across colors: Should the company cut prices (ORANGE) or protect margins? How does a price cut affect cash flow and loan covenants (BLUE)? Does offering customer financing create legal duties (PURPLE)? A single marketing move touches all of them.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why ORANGE decisions at Level 5 affect every other color
- Identify the non-marketing consequences of a marketing decision
- Apply a cross-color checklist before major sales moves

## Core Content
In Levels 1 through 4, ORANGE taught the pieces of sales and marketing one at a time: understanding customers, positioning, pricing, channels, negotiation, persuasion, and competitive strategy. At Level 4, you analyzed why some strategies win and others fail, and learned the tactical, offense-versus-defense side of competition.

Level 5 asks a harder question: what happens to the rest of the business when you make a marketing move?

Think of the chess analogy. Early on, you learned how each piece moves. Now you coordinate them toward a goal. ORANGE is often the piece that starts the action. It brings in revenue, which every other color depends on. But every ORANGE move pulls on the other pieces:
- BLUE: A new campaign costs cash before it earns cash. A large new customer may need credit terms that stretch working capital.
- GREEN: Discounts, rebates, and gifts can have tax effects. Selling into new regions can create new tax obligations. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- PURPLE: Marketing claims must be truthful. Contracts with customers and distributors create obligations. Customer data must be handled lawfully.
- WHITE: Customer concentration and revenue quality affect how investors value the company.
- BLACK: Sales goals and compensation plans shape behavior and culture.

A Level 5 cross-color checklist before any major sales move:
1. Cash: When does the money go out, and when does it come back?
2. Margin: After discounts, commissions, and taxes, what does the company actually keep?
3. Legal: What are we promising, and can we deliver it?
4. Risk: Does this make us too dependent on one customer, channel, or product?
5. People: What behavior does this reward in our team?

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Many businesses fail not because they cannot sell, but because they sell in ways the rest of the business cannot support. Growth that outruns cash, margin, or legal capacity can be as dangerous as no growth at all.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Grace runs a furniture store. Rates rise and sales slow. Her marketing manager proposes "0% financing for 24 months" to boost sales.

Grace runs the cross-color checklist:
- Cash (BLUE): If the store finances purchases itself, it waits up to 24 months for full payment while paying suppliers now. Cash would run short within months.
- Margin (BLUE/GREEN): If a third-party lender provides the financing, the store pays the lender a fee of several percent of each sale, cutting margin.
- Legal (PURPLE): Offering consumer credit may trigger lending disclosure rules. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Risk: Some customers may default, especially if the economy weakens.
- People (BLACK): Salespeople paid only on sales volume may push financing to customers who cannot afford it.

Grace's decision: Partner with a licensed third-party lender to offer 12-month financing, raise prices slightly on financed items to cover the fee, and pay commissions on paid sales rather than signed sales.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The campaign still boosts sales, but in a way her cash flow, margins, and legal obligations can support.

A) They only affect advertising
B) They are always simple

C) When money goes out and when it comes back
D) Which color to use

**Q3. Why can growth be dangerous?**
A) Growth is illegal
B) Growth that outruns cash, margin, or legal capacity can damage the business
C) Customers dislike growth
D) It raises taxes automatically

**Q4. Why did Grace change commissions to paid sales?**
A) To avoid rewarding salespeople for pushing financing to customers who cannot afford it
B) To lower pay
C) Because the law required it
D) To reduce taxes

**Q5. What did Grace do to protect margin?**
A) Raised prices slightly on financed items to cover the lender's fee
B) Cut prices
C) Stopped selling
D) Offered 36-month financing

## Answer Key
Q1: D
Q2: C
Q3: B
Q4: A
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Marketing moves pull on every other color.

**Q2.** Timing of cash flows is often the hidden constraint.

**Q3.** Unsupported growth can create crises.

**Q4.** Compensation shapes behavior.

**Q5.** Pricing adjusted for the cost of financing.

## Key Takeaways
- ORANGE starts the action, but every move pulls on the other colors
- Check cash, margin, legal, risk, and people before major sales moves
- Growth must be supportable by the rest of the business
- Incentives shape behavior; design them carefully
