# Pricing Orchestrated with Cash Flow and Tax

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: As rates rise, a service business considers switching from monthly billing to annual prepaid plans at a discount. Ask across colors: How does this change cash flow (BLUE)? How and when is prepaid revenue taxed and recognized (GREEN)? What must the contract say about refunds (PURPLE)? Will customers see this as a good deal (ORANGE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how pricing structure affects cash flow timing
- Connect pricing to tax and accounting treatment
- Design pricing that balances customer appeal and business health

## Core Content
At Level 3 and Level 4, ORANGE covered pricing strategies: cost-plus, value-based, competitive, and psychological pricing. At Level 5, pricing becomes an orchestration tool.

The same price can produce very different business results depending on its structure:
- Upfront vs. over time: Annual prepaid plans bring cash in early. Monthly plans spread it out. Payment on completion delays it.
- Deposits and milestones: Asking for a deposit or milestone payments reduces the risk of doing work and not being paid.
- Discounts for early payment: A small discount for paying within 10 days may cost less than borrowing to cover slow payers.
- Bundling: Selling products together can raise average order value but may hide which products are actually profitable.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Cash timing affects how much the business must borrow. With high rates, getting paid earlier has real value.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Prepaid revenue may be taxed and recognized differently from revenue earned over time. Some systems let businesses defer recognition of prepaid amounts until services are delivered; others do not. Sales taxes or VAT may apply at the time of payment.
- PURPLE: Prepaid plans need clear refund and cancellation terms. Consumer protection rules may limit automatic renewals. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- WHITE: Predictable pricing models (like subscriptions) often support higher business valuations.

The contribution margin concept helps here: price minus the variable costs of delivering one more unit. A price that looks profitable may not be after commissions, payment processing fees, discounts, and taxes on the sale.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Many small businesses set prices by looking at competitors and then wonder why they are always short on cash. Designing price structure around cash flow can matter as much as the price itself.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Leila runs a bookkeeping service. She charges $300 per month, billed monthly. With rates rising, her credit line costs more.

She offers an annual plan: $3,240 paid upfront (a 10% discount from $3,600).

Analysis:
- Cash (BLUE): If 40 of her 100 clients switch, she receives $129,600 upfront instead of $12,000 per month from those clients. She pays down her credit line and saves interest.
- Margin: She gives up $360 per client per year, or $14,400 total. Her interest savings and reduced collection effort offset part of this.
- Tax (GREEN): Her CPA explains how prepaid income is treated in her jurisdiction and when it must be reported. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. She sets aside cash for any tax due.
- Legal (PURPLE): Her new contract states a prorated refund policy if a client cancels.
- Customer appeal (ORANGE): Clients like the discount and the simplicity.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The annual plan is not just a marketing offer. It is a financing decision, a tax decision, and a legal decision all at once. Leila made it work by checking all of them.

**Q4.** Clear terms reduce legal risk and disputes.

**Q5.** Pricing structure touches several colors.

## Key Takeaways
- Price structure affects cash timing as much as price level
- Deposits, milestones, and prepayment reduce risk and borrowing needs
- Prepaid revenue has REGION-SPECIFIC tax and legal treatment
- Check contribution margin after all variable costs
