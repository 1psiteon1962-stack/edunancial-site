# Marketing Real Estate: Positioning, Pricing, and Timing

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a populous nation's real estate and consumer credit markets, after years of rapid expansion, beginning to show signs of a sharp correction as defaults start rising.

Apply it here: A housing market begins to correct. An investor must decide whether to sell a property now or lease it and wait. Ask across colors: How should the property be priced and positioned (ORANGE)? How are buyers' financing conditions affecting demand (BLUE/RED)? What disclosures are required (PURPLE)? What are the tax consequences of selling now versus later (GREEN)?

## Learning Objectives
- Apply positioning and pricing to real estate sales and leasing
- Connect marketing timing to financing and tax conditions
- Include legal disclosure requirements in property marketing

## Core Content
RED teaches how to evaluate and finance property. ORANGE teaches how to sell or lease it effectively. At Level 5, they work together.

Key marketing decisions for property:
- Target buyer or tenant: First-time buyers, investors, families, businesses? Each responds to different features and messages.
- Positioning: What makes this property the best choice for that target? Location, condition, income potential, flexibility?
- Pricing: Based on comparable sales or rents (RED's valuation tools), current demand, and how quickly you need to sell. Overpricing in a falling market often leads to a longer sale and a lower final price.
- Timing: Seasonal patterns, interest rate trends, and local supply all affect demand.
- Presentation: Photos, descriptions, showings, and repairs before listing.

How colors connect:
- RED: Valuation, condition, and income potential.
- BLUE: Buyers' borrowing costs shape what they can afford. When rates rise, some buyers drop out, and others need seller concessions such as help with closing costs or seller financing.
- PURPLE: Many jurisdictions require sellers to disclose known defects. Marketing descriptions must not be misleading. Fair housing rules limit how properties can be advertised. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Holding period, depreciation recapture, and exchange rules may affect whether selling now or later is better after taxes.
- WHITE: Compare expected after-tax proceeds from selling with the expected return from holding and leasing.

Sell vs. lease: In a correcting market, leasing may produce income while waiting for recovery, but it brings management work, vacancy risk, and possible landlord obligations (PURPLE-L5-018 and -027 in the law track).

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The biggest real estate marketing mistake in a falling market is anchoring on last year's price. Buyers price on today's conditions.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Dmitri owns a townhouse he bought as an investment. The market is correcting, and rates are high.

Option 1: Sell now. Comparable sales suggest $420,000, down from $460,000 a year ago. Dmitri is tempted to list at $460,000.
Option 2: Lease and wait. Market rent is $2,300 per month.

His analysis:
- Pricing (ORANGE/RED): Listing at $460,000 would likely sit unsold. He lists at $425,000 with a clear marketing message targeting first-time buyers: move-in ready, near transit.
- Financing (BLUE): To help buyers facing high rates, he offers a closing cost credit instead of a price cut, which keeps his sale price stronger for future comparables.
- Legal (PURPLE): He discloses a past roof repair as required. His listing avoids any language that could violate fair housing rules.
- Tax (GREEN): His CPA confirms he has held long enough for favorable treatment and estimates depreciation recapture. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Compare (WHITE): After-tax sale proceeds invested elsewhere vs. holding with rental income. The numbers are close.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Dmitri sells for $418,000 after concessions within 40 days. Pricing to the current market, not his hopes, made the sale happen.

## Key Takeaways
- Real estate marketing coordinates positioning, pricing, timing, and presentation
- Buyer financing conditions shape demand and concessions
- Disclosure and fair housing rules apply to property marketing
- Price to today's market, and compare after-tax outcomes
