# Raising Capital Is a Sales Process

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a systemically important financial institution suddenly revealing a large, previously undisclosed loss, raising fears about counterparty risk rippling through the broader banking system.

Apply it here: After a banking scare, investors become cautious and ask harder questions. A founder must raise money to survive. Ask across colors: How should the pitch be positioned for nervous investors (ORANGE)? What claims can legally be made (PURPLE)? What terms protect the founder's control (BLUE)? How should risks be presented honestly (BLACK)?

## Learning Objectives
- Apply the sales process to fundraising
- Position a company for specific investor types
- Balance persuasion with legal and ethical honesty

## Core Content
Raising money is selling. The product is a share of the company's future. The customer is the investor. Many sales skills from earlier ORANGE levels apply directly:

1. Prospecting: Find investors whose interests match your company. Some focus on certain industries, stages, or regions. Pitching the wrong investors wastes time.
2. Research: Understand what each investor cares about: growth, income, impact, control, or exit timing.
3. Positioning: Explain why your company is the right investment for them, now.
4. Storytelling: Show the problem, your solution, your traction, your team, and how the investment will be used.
5. Handling objections: Prepare for hard questions about risks, competition, and assumptions.
6. Closing: Move to term sheets, due diligence, and documents.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Securities laws apply (PURPLE-L5-033 in the law track). Claims must be truthful. Projections should be clearly labeled as estimates with stated assumptions. Public advertising of an offering may be restricted. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- BLUE: The terms matter as much as the amount: valuation, dilution, control rights, and investor protections.
- WHITE: Investors compare your company with other opportunities, including public markets. In cautious times, they want clearer paths to profit and lower risk.
- BLACK: Credibility is the founder's most important asset. Overpromising may win one round but damage every future relationship.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some jurisdictions offer tax incentives for investors in qualifying small businesses, which can be a genuine selling point.

In cautious markets, positioning often shifts from "fastest growth" to "efficient growth with a path to profitability."

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The best investor pitches are honest about risks and explain how the team will handle them. Experienced investors trust founders who show they understand what could go wrong.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Amara's company makes software for small clinics. She needs $1 million. After the banking scare, investors are nervous.

Her original pitch emphasized rapid growth: "We'll triple revenue next year!"

Her revised pitch:
- Prospecting: She targets investors who focus on healthcare software and have invested during downturns before.
- Positioning: "Efficient growth." She shows a 7-month customer payback period (see ORANGE-L5-003) and low churn.
- Story: Clinics save staff time, which matters more when labor costs rise.
- Honest risks: She names the two biggest risks (a large competitor and regulatory changes) and explains her plans for each.
- Projections: Labeled as estimates, with three scenarios (cautious, base, strong) and stated assumptions.
- Terms: She seeks a fair valuation rather than the highest possible, to avoid future down-round problems (see PURPLE-L5-007).

Two investors who passed on her growth pitch invest after the revised version. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Her honesty about risk increased investor confidence rather than reducing it.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Sales skills apply directly to fundraising.

**Q2.** Matching investors to the company improves results.

**Q3.** Honest labeling protects credibility and legal standing.

**Q4.** Cautious investors value efficiency and clear paths to profit.

**Q5.** Understanding risk builds credibility.

## Key Takeaways
- Fundraising uses the full sales process
- Match investors to your company and position for their priorities
- Securities rules and honesty limit what you can claim
- Credibility is the founder's most valuable asset
