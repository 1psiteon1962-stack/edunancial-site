# Building a Sales Team vs. Outsourcing Sales

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once.

Apply it here: After a long disruption, a company must rebuild its sales quickly. Ask across colors: Should it hire employees (BLACK) or use outside agents (ORANGE)? Which option costs less upfront and over time (BLUE)? What contracts and classification issues arise (PURPLE)? How does each option affect the families of the people involved?

## Learning Objectives
- Compare internal sales teams with outsourced options
- Evaluate cost, control, and flexibility trade-offs
- Consider legal obligations and employer responsibility

## Core Content
A business can sell through its own employees or through outside parties. Each approach has strengths.

Internal sales team:
- Full control over training, messaging, and customer experience.
- Deep product knowledge and loyalty over time.
- Higher fixed costs: salaries, benefits, payroll taxes, management.
- Employer responsibilities (BLACK/PURPLE).

Outsourced options:
- Independent sales agents or representatives: Paid mainly by commission. Lower fixed costs, but less control and often representing other companies too.
- Distributors: Buy products and resell them. Take inventory risk but also take margin and customer relationships.
- Outsourced sales firms: Provide trained teams for a fee.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Outsourcing lowers fixed costs and upfront investment. Internal teams usually cost more upfront but can be more efficient at scale.
- PURPLE: Agent and distributor agreements should cover territory, exclusivity, commission, termination, and who owns customer relationships. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some jurisdictions give sales agents or distributors special protections, such as compensation when terminated. Agents who work like employees may need to be classified as employees (PURPLE-L5-031).
- GREEN: Employees require payroll taxes. Payments to contractors and agents have different reporting rules. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- BLACK: The employer responsibility theme: hiring staff directly supports their families, especially in economies where formal jobs are scarce. Employees deserve dignity and fair treatment.
- WHITE: Businesses that own their customer relationships are often valued higher than those that depend on distributors.

A common path: start with agents or distributors to test markets cheaply, then build an internal team where sales are proven and volume justifies it.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Outsourcing sales can be smart early on, but a business that never builds its own customer relationships may find that its real asset belongs to someone else.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Kemi's company makes kitchen equipment for restaurants. After a long disruption, she needs to rebuild sales in three regions.

Option 1: Hire 6 salespeople. Annual cost: about $480,000 in salary, benefits, and payroll taxes, plus management time.
Option 2: Use independent agents in all three regions, paid 10% commission.
Option 3: Mixed approach.

She chooses Option 3:
- Region A (her largest market): Hires 3 employees. She wants control and direct customer relationships.
- Regions B and C: Signs agent agreements for 2 years, with clear terms on territory, commission, termination, and customer ownership. Her attorney checks local agent protection rules. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- She sets a plan to evaluate converting Region B to employees if sales exceed a set level.

Region A's employees earn stable incomes, and Kemi invests in their training. The agents bring quick coverage to smaller regions without large fixed costs.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Kemi matched each sales model to each market's size and strategic importance, rather than choosing one model for everything.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Internal teams offer control and loyalty.

**Q2.** Agents are paid mainly by results.

**Q3.** Customer relationships are valuable assets.

**Q4.** Agent protections vary. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q5.** Different markets justified different models.

## Key Takeaways
- Internal teams offer control; outsourcing offers flexibility and lower fixed costs
- Agent and distributor contracts must address territory, termination, and customer ownership
- Employer responsibility is part of the decision
- Match the sales model to each market
