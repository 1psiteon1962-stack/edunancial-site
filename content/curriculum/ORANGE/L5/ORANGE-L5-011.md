# Marketing Through Inflation: Raising Prices Without Losing Trust

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: Inflation is high, and a central bank keeps raising rates. A business's costs have risen 15%. Ask across colors: How much should prices rise, and how should the increase be explained (ORANGE)? What do existing customer contracts allow (PURPLE)? What happens to cash flow and loan covenants if margins shrink (BLUE)? How does inflation change the real value of money the business holds (WHITE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Decide how and when to raise prices during inflation
- Communicate price increases in ways that protect trust
- Coordinate pricing with contracts, cash flow, and financing

## Core Content
Inflation forces a hard choice. If a business does not raise prices, rising costs shrink its margins. If it raises prices poorly, customers may leave. Level 5 marketing handles this by coordinating pricing, communication, contracts, and finance.

Pricing approaches during inflation:
- Across-the-board increases: Simple but can hit price-sensitive customers hard.
- Targeted increases: Raise prices more on products where customers are less price-sensitive.
- Tiered offerings: Add a lower-priced option so price-sensitive customers can trade down instead of leaving.
- Smaller, more frequent increases vs. one large increase: Each has trade-offs in customer perception.
- Adding value: Pair increases with improved service, features, or guarantees.

Communication principles:
- Give advance notice.
- Explain honestly (rising costs of materials, labor, or energy).
- Emphasize the value customers continue to receive.
- Avoid misleading tactics such as quietly shrinking package sizes without disclosure, which may raise legal and trust issues. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Existing contracts may lock prices. Price adjustment clauses (PURPLE-L5-039 in the law track) help in future contracts.
- BLUE: Shrinking margins can break loan covenants that require certain profit levels. Protecting margin protects financing.
- WHITE: Inflation reduces the real value of cash. Getting paid faster and holding less idle cash can help.
- GREEN: Higher nominal prices can raise tax bills even if real profit has not grown. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- BLACK: Leaders must decide how much margin to sacrifice to keep customers.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Customers usually understand inflation. What damages trust is surprise, hidden changes, or increases that seem much larger than the costs behind them.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Nora runs a cleaning supply distributor. Costs rose 15%. Her margins fell from 25% to 14%. Her bank loan requires a minimum profit ratio she is close to breaking.

Her plan:
- Contracts (PURPLE): 30% of customers have fixed-price contracts expiring within 6 months. New contracts will include a price adjustment clause tied to a cost index.
- Pricing (ORANGE): A 10% average increase, higher on specialty products where customers are less price-sensitive, lower on basic items where competition is fierce.
- Tiering: She adds a value line of basic products at lower prices.
- Communication: Customers receive 45 days' notice with a letter explaining cost increases and listing ways to save, including the value line and bulk ordering.
- Finance (BLUE): She shows her bank the pricing plan and projected margins, and the bank agrees not to call the loan while the plan takes effect.

Results: Margins recover to 21%. A few customers switch to the value line; very few leave. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Nora protected margins, customers, and financing by coordinating all four instead of simply raising prices.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What happens to a business that does not raise prices during inflation?**
A) Rising costs shrink its margins
B) Margins grow
C) Taxes fall
D) Customers pay more automatically

**Q2. What is the purpose of a tiered offering during inflation?**
A) To confuse customers
B) To avoid contracts
C) To raise all prices
D) To let price-sensitive customers trade down instead of leaving

**Q3. Why can shrinking margins threaten financing?**
A) Banks dislike inflation
B) It cancels loans
C) It raises interest rates automatically
D) Loan covenants may require minimum profit levels

**Q4. What communication principle does the lesson recommend?**
A) Raise prices without notice
B) Blame competitors
C) Hide increases in smaller packages
D) Give advance notice and explain honestly

**Q5. What damages trust most, according to the lesson?**
A) Any price increase
B) Surprise, hidden changes, or increases much larger than costs
C) Advance notice
D) Offering alternatives

## Answer Key
Q1: A
Q2: D
Q3: D
Q4: D
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Costs rise while prices stay flat.

**Q2.** Lower-priced options keep customers.

**Q3.** Covenant breaks can trigger lender action.

**Q4.** Honesty and notice protect trust.

**Q5.** Customers accept honest increases more readily.

## Key Takeaways
- Inflation forces a choice between margin and customer retention
- Use targeted increases, tiers, and added value
- Communicate early and honestly; avoid hidden changes
- Protect margins to protect financing
