# International Market Entry: Translation Is Not Localization

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs.

Apply it here: New tariffs make a company's home market less attractive, so it plans to enter a new country. Ask across colors: Is translating the website enough (ORANGE), or must products, prices, and messages change? What legal registrations and compliance steps are needed (PURPLE)? What tax obligations arise (GREEN)? How much will entry cost before revenue arrives (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Distinguish translation from localization
- Identify the elements of a market entry plan
- Coordinate legal, tax, and financial preparation for entry

## Core Content
The curriculum's translation protocol states a principle that applies directly to marketing: translation is not localization, and language is not jurisdiction.

- Translation converts words from one language to another.
- Localization adapts the whole offering to a market: product features, pricing, payment methods, cultural references, examples, measurement units, legal requirements, and customer expectations.

Two countries may share a language but differ in laws, currency, culture, and buying habits. One country may have several languages.

Elements of a market entry plan:
1. Market research: Customer needs, competitors, price levels, and purchasing power.
2. Product adaptation: Features, sizes, packaging, and standards.
3. Pricing: Local purchasing power, competitor prices, currency, taxes, and shipping costs.
4. Messaging: Culturally appropriate examples, images, and tone.
5. Channels: How customers in that market prefer to buy.
6. Legal: Business registration, product compliance, trademark protection (PURPLE-L5-029 in the law track), consumer protection, and data privacy. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
7. Tax: Sales tax or VAT registration, income tax exposure from having a presence in the country, and customs duties. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
8. Finance: Upfront costs, time to first revenue, and currency risk.

How colors connect:
- ORANGE: Localization determines whether customers understand and trust the offering.
- PURPLE: Compliance and brand protection.
- GREEN: Tax obligations can start earlier than many businesses expect.
- BLUE: Entry costs and working capital.
- BLACK: Local leadership and partners who understand the market.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Many market entries fail not because the product was wrong, but because the company assumed customers abroad would think like customers at home.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Julia's company sells a personal budgeting app in its home country. It plans to enter a neighboring country that speaks a different language.

Translation-only approach (first draft): Translate the app and website. Launch.

Localization review reveals:
- Budget categories in the app reflect home-country expenses. The new market has different common expenses.
- Examples use home-country currency and salary levels.
- Customers in the new market prefer a different payment method.
- Data privacy laws require consent and data handling changes. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- The brand name is available as a trademark, but must be filed locally.
- VAT registration is required once digital sales begin. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Pricing: Local purchasing power is lower, so a lower-priced plan is needed.

Julia's localized launch:
- Adapted categories and examples.
- Local payment options.
- Adjusted pricing.
- Compliance changes and trademark filing.
- A local marketing advisor to review tone and images.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The localized launch costs more and takes longer. But it produces a product customers in the new market recognize as made for them, not simply translated for them.

**Q1.** Localization goes far beyond language.

**Q2.** Shared language does not mean shared rules.

**Q3.** Tax rules may apply from the first sale. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q4.** Pricing must fit the local market.

## Key Takeaways
- Translation is not localization; language is not jurisdiction
- Localize product, pricing, messaging, channels, and compliance
- Legal and tax obligations can begin at the first sale
- Local knowledge and partners improve market entry
