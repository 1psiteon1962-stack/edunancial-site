# Channel Strategy: Direct, Distributor, or Marketplace

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs.

Apply it here: Tariffs raise costs, and a manufacturer must decide which sales channels to keep. Ask across colors: Which channel leaves the most margin (ORANGE/BLUE)? Which gives the most control over customer relationships and data (PURPLE)? Which carries the most inventory and payment risk? How do channel choices affect the company's value (WHITE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Compare the main sales channels
- Evaluate margin, control, data, and risk trade-offs
- Manage channel conflict and contract terms

## Core Content
A channel is the path a product takes to reach customers. Channel choice affects nearly every color.

Main channels:
- Direct: Selling through your own store, website, or sales team. Highest margin and control, but you handle marketing, fulfillment, customer service, and payment risk.
- Distributors or wholesalers: They buy in bulk and resell. Lower margin for you, but less work and less direct risk. They may control customer relationships.
- Retailers: Stores that sell to end customers. They may require promotional spending, slotting fees, or return rights.
- Online marketplaces: Large platforms that provide traffic and fulfillment. They charge fees, set rules, and often control customer data. Policy changes can affect your business overnight.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Margins differ by channel. Distributors and marketplaces take a share. Payment terms differ too (retailers may pay in 60 to 90 days).
- PURPLE: Distribution agreements cover territory, exclusivity, pricing rules, returns, and termination (PURPLE-L5-030 in the law track). REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some jurisdictions restrict how manufacturers can control resale prices. Marketplace terms of service can change unilaterally.
- WHITE: Businesses that own customer relationships and data are often valued higher than those dependent on a single platform.
- ORANGE: Channel conflict occurs when channels compete, such as a manufacturer selling direct at prices that undercut its own distributors.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Selling directly into many regions can create sales tax or VAT obligations in each.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Depending on one channel, especially a platform you do not control, is a concentration risk. Many businesses do best with a mix, adjusted over time.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Ines makes handmade leather bags. Her sales: 50% through a large online marketplace, 30% through boutique retailers, 20% through her own website.

Tariffs raise her leather costs 15%.

Channel analysis:
- Marketplace: After fees and advertising, she keeps about 55% of the sale price. The platform controls customer data. A recent policy change raised fees.
- Retailers: They buy at 50% of retail price and pay in 60 days.
- Website: She keeps about 85% after payment and shipping costs, and owns customer data, but must pay for her own marketing.

Plan:
- Shift marketing toward her website, using email to past customers (with proper consent, see PURPLE-L5-036 in the law track).
- Raise marketplace prices slightly to protect margin.
- Keep retailers for brand visibility but negotiate 30-day payment terms.
- Avoid undercutting retailers on her website, to prevent channel conflict.
- Check sales tax obligations for direct sales. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

After a year, her website grows to 35% of sales. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Her business became more profitable and less dependent on a platform whose rules she could not control.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What is a sales channel?**
A) The path a product takes to reach customers
B) A TV station
C) A type of tax
D) A legal entity

**Q2. Which channel usually offers the highest margin and control?**
A) Distributor
B) Direct
C) Marketplace
D) Wholesaler

**Q3. What is channel conflict?**
A) A legal dispute
B) A tax issue
C) A shipping delay
D) Channels competing with each other, such as direct sales undercutting distributors

**Q4. Why can depending on one online marketplace be risky?**
A) Marketplaces are illegal
B) They pay too quickly
C) They have no customers
D) Policy and fee changes can affect your business overnight, and you may not own customer data

**Q5. What did Ines do to reduce platform dependence?**
A) Shifted marketing toward her own website and customer email list
B) Stopped selling
C) Raised all prices sharply
D) Sold only to retailers

## Answer Key
Q1: A
Q2: B
Q3: D
Q4: D
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Channels connect products with buyers.

**Q2.** Direct sales keep more margin but require more work.

**Q3.** Conflict can damage channel relationships.

**Q4.** Platform dependence is a concentration risk.

**Q5.** Building direct relationships reduced dependence.

## Key Takeaways
- Channels differ in margin, control, data, and risk
- Distribution and marketplace terms shape your business
- Avoid channel conflict and over-dependence on one channel
- Owning customer relationships often increases business value
