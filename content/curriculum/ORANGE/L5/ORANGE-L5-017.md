# Subscription Models: Revenue, Churn, and Valuation

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: Rising rates squeeze household budgets, and consumers start cancelling subscriptions. Ask across colors: Which customers are most likely to cancel, and how can the company keep them (ORANGE)? Do auto-renewal and cancellation practices follow the law (PURPLE)? How does rising churn affect revenue forecasts and company valuation (WHITE/BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how subscription models create predictable revenue
- Connect churn to revenue, cash flow, and valuation
- Design subscriptions that comply with renewal and cancellation rules

## Core Content
Subscription businesses charge customers regularly (monthly or yearly) for ongoing access to a product or service. Examples include software, memberships, content, and product boxes.

Why subscriptions are attractive:
- Predictable recurring revenue helps planning (BLUE).
- Customers can be served over a long relationship (ORANGE-L5-003 lifetime value).
- Investors often value recurring revenue more highly than one-time sales (WHITE).

Key metrics:
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR): Total monthly subscription revenue.
- Churn rate: The percentage of customers (or revenue) lost each period.
- Net revenue retention: Revenue from existing customers this period compared with last period, including upgrades and downgrades.

Churn is the critical risk. ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: At 2% monthly churn, a business loses about 22% of customers per year. At 5% monthly, it loses about 46%. The business must win many new customers just to stay the same size.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Many jurisdictions regulate automatic renewals, requiring clear disclosure, consent, reminders before renewal, and easy cancellation. Making cancellation deliberately difficult can violate consumer protection rules and damage trust.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Revenue recognition for prepaid subscriptions and sales tax on digital services vary.
- WHITE: Valuation often depends on growth and retention together. High churn reduces value sharply.
- BLUE: Annual plans bring cash earlier (ORANGE-L5-002).
- BLACK: Retention reflects product quality and customer service across the whole company.

Retention strategies: Improve onboarding, deliver ongoing value, offer pause or downgrade options instead of only cancellation, and listen to why customers leave.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Ethical retention focuses on giving customers reasons to stay, not making it hard to leave.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Sam runs a fitness app with 10,000 subscribers at $15 per month (MRR: $150,000). Churn rises from 3% to 6% per month as rates squeeze budgets.

At 6% churn, Sam loses 600 subscribers per month. He must add 600 just to stay even.

His response:
- Pause option: Subscribers can pause for up to 3 months instead of cancelling.
- Downgrade option: A $8 basic plan.
- Exit survey: Finds that many cancel because they stopped using the app, not because of price.
- Onboarding: New users receive a 30-day plan to build habits.
- Legal review (PURPLE): His attorney finds his cancellation process requires a phone call. In some of his markets, rules require online cancellation if sign-up was online. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. He adds one-click online cancellation and renewal reminders.

Results: Churn falls to 4%. Some customers downgrade instead of cancelling, preserving part of their revenue. Easy cancellation does not increase churn; it improves reviews.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Sam's valuation improves because retention improves. The legal fix and the marketing fixes worked together.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Predictability reduces uncertainty.

**Q2.** Churn shows how fast customers leave.

**Q3.** Consumer protection rules vary. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q4.** Usage, not price, drove much of the churn.

**Q5.** Value, not obstacles, should drive retention.

## Key Takeaways
- Subscriptions create predictable revenue when retention is strong
- Churn is the critical risk; small rate changes compound quickly
- Auto-renewal and cancellation rules are REGION-SPECIFIC and must be followed
- Retain customers through value, pause, and downgrade options
