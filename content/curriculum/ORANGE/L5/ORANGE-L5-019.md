# Content Marketing as an Owned Asset

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture two large economic powers escalating export controls against each other on a category of advanced technology components used across many industries.

Apply it here: Export controls and platform restrictions cause a popular social media app to become unavailable in some markets. Businesses that relied on it lose their audience overnight. Ask across colors: Did the business own its audience and content (ORANGE/PURPLE), or rent it from a platform? What is owned content worth to a buyer (WHITE)? What would rebuilding cost (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Distinguish owned, earned, and rented media
- Explain how content becomes a long-term business asset
- Protect content ownership and reduce platform dependence

## Core Content
Marketing channels can be grouped by who controls them:
- Owned media: Your website, email list, blog, courses, and content library. You control them.
- Earned media: Press coverage, reviews, and word of mouth. You influence but do not control them.
- Rented (or paid/platform) media: Social media followers, marketplace listings, and paid ads. The platform controls access, rules, and algorithms.

Content marketing creates useful material (articles, videos, guides, courses) that attracts and educates potential customers. Unlike an ad that stops working when you stop paying, good content can keep attracting customers for years.

How content becomes an asset:
- It builds trust and authority over time.
- It can attract search traffic continuously.
- It can be repurposed into courses, books, or licensed products (PURPLE-L5-030 in the law track).
- An email list built with proper consent (PURPLE-L5-036) lets you reach customers directly.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Copyright generally protects original content. Ensure the business owns content created by employees and contractors through written agreements (PURPLE-L5-028). Platform terms of service may grant platforms broad rights to content posted there.
- WHITE: Buyers often value businesses with owned audiences and content libraries more than those dependent on platforms.
- BLUE: Content takes time and money to build before it pays back. Consistency matters more than volume.
- ORANGE: Content builds relationships that make selling easier.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Content creation costs may be expensed or capitalized depending on rules.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Use rented platforms to reach new people, but guide them toward owned channels, such as your website and email list, where the relationship belongs to you.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Two personal finance educators each have large audiences.

Educator A: 200,000 followers on one social platform. No email list. Videos exist only on that platform.

Educator B: 50,000 social followers, but has consistently directed them to her website, where 30,000 people joined her email list with clear consent. She publishes articles on her site, keeps copies of all videos, and has written agreements confirming her company owns content made by her video editor.

The scenario hits: the platform becomes unavailable in several of their key markets.

Educator A loses access to most of her audience overnight. Rebuilding will take years.

Educator B loses some social followers, but her email list and website are unaffected. She emails subscribers, launches a course from her content library, and continues growing.

When a media company later offers to buy Educator B's business, it values her email list, content library, and clear ownership records highly.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Educator A built on rented land. Educator B built on land she owned.

## Key Takeaways
- Owned media is controlled by you; rented media is controlled by platforms
- Content can become a long-term, compounding asset
- Secure content ownership and consent-based email lists
- Use platforms for reach, but build owned channels
