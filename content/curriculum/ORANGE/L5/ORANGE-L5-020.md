# Marketing Compliance: Claims, Testimonials, and Disclosures in Practice

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a major energy-producing region hit by a sudden regional conflict that knocks out a large share of its production capacity overnight, tightening global energy supply with little warning.

Apply it here: An energy crisis creates demand for energy-saving products, and a company hires influencers to promote its product. Ask across colors: Are the influencers' claims accurate and substantiated (PURPLE)? Are paid relationships disclosed? How would a regulator's action affect finances and reputation (BLUE/BLACK)? Can compliant marketing still be persuasive (ORANGE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Build a practical marketing compliance process
- Manage influencer and testimonial compliance
- Keep marketing persuasive while staying within legal limits

## Core Content
PURPLE-L5-035 in the law track explained the principles of advertising law. This lesson turns them into an ORANGE operating process that marketing teams can follow every day.

A practical compliance process:
1. Claim inventory: List every objective claim in a campaign (numbers, comparisons, guarantees, "best," "fastest," "saves").
2. Substantiation file: For each claim, store the evidence (test results, studies, data) before the campaign launches.
3. Disclosure check: Identify important conditions, limits, fees, or material connections, and make sure they are clear and noticeable.
4. Testimonial review: Confirm testimonials are real, reflect honest experiences, and include context if results are not typical.
5. Endorser disclosure: Anyone paid, gifted, or otherwise connected to the company should disclose that relationship clearly. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
6. Influencer guidelines: Written instructions for influencers about disclosure, prohibited claims, and approval of scripts or posts.
7. Approval and records: A final sign-off and saved copies of campaigns.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Legal requirements and potential liability.
- ORANGE: Specific, proven claims are often more persuasive than vague exaggerations.
- BLUE: Regulatory penalties, refunds, and legal costs affect cash flow.
- BLACK: Leadership sets the tone. A culture that rewards aggressive claims creates risk.
- WHITE: Compliance failures can reduce company value and create liabilities in diligence.

Influencer marketing risk: Companies may be responsible for what their paid influencers say about their products. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. Clear contracts with influencers should require disclosure, prohibit unsupported claims, and allow the company to request corrections or removal.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Compliance works best when it is built into the marketing process, not added at the end as a final hurdle.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Oscar's company sells smart thermostats. During the energy crisis, he hires 10 influencers.

Without a process, the first influencer posts: "This thermostat cut my bills in half! Everyone should get one!" There is no disclosure that the influencer was paid, and there is no evidence of 50% savings.

Oscar pauses the campaign and builds a process:
- Claim inventory: The approved claim is "Up to 20% heating savings in independent testing; results vary by home and climate."
- Substantiation file: The lab report is saved.
- Influencer contract: Requires clear disclosure (such as "Paid partnership") at the start of posts, bans unsupported savings claims, and requires script approval.
- Testimonials: Influencers may share their actual experiences, with context that results vary.
- Monitoring: A team member reviews posts within 24 hours and requests corrections when needed.

The first influencer revises the post with proper disclosure and the approved claim. Engagement stays strong.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The compliant campaign still sells well, and Oscar's company avoids regulatory attention during a period when regulators are watching energy-saving claims closely.

## Key Takeaways
- Turn advertising law into an everyday marketing process
- Inventory claims, file evidence, and check disclosures
- Manage influencers with clear contracts and monitoring
- Compliant marketing can still be highly persuasive
