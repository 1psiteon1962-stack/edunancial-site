# Scaling Through Licensing and Franchising

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a mid-sized economy's currency losing a large share of its value against major reserve currencies over a few months, driven by capital fleeing the country and eroding investor confidence.

Apply it here: A restaurant brand's home currency collapses, making domestic expansion hard. Operators in other countries want to open locations under its name. Ask across colors: Should it license the brand or franchise the full system (ORANGE/PURPLE)? What regulations apply (PURPLE)? How would royalties in stronger currencies affect finances (BLUE/WHITE)? How would it protect quality (BLACK)?

## Learning Objectives
- Compare licensing and franchising as growth models
- Identify regulatory requirements for franchising
- Balance growth speed with brand control

## Core Content
Growing by opening your own locations takes capital and management. Licensing and franchising let others invest their money and effort to expand your brand or system, in exchange for fees and royalties.

Licensing: The owner grants permission to use IP (brand, recipes, designs) under specific terms (PURPLE-L5-030 in the law track). The licensee usually runs their own business their own way, within quality limits.

Franchising: The franchisor provides a complete business system: brand, operating methods, training, supplier relationships, marketing support, and ongoing assistance. Franchisees pay initial fees and ongoing royalties and follow the system.

REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Many jurisdictions regulate franchising, requiring detailed disclosure documents before sale, registration, waiting periods, or specific contract terms. Some arrangements called "licenses" may legally count as franchises if they include enough control and support.

Comparing the models:
- Speed: Both can grow faster than company-owned expansion.
- Capital: Operators invest their own money.
- Control: Franchising gives more control over operations; licensing usually less.
- Revenue: Royalties and fees, typically a share of the operator's sales.
- Risk: The brand depends on operators' performance. A poorly run location can damage reputation everywhere.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Disclosure, registration, contracts, trademark protection in each market (PURPLE-L5-029).
- BLUE: Royalty income can be steady, but building a franchise system requires upfront investment in manuals, training, and support.
- WHITE: Royalties in stronger currencies diversify income (scenario).
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Cross-border royalties may face withholding taxes.
- BLACK: Choosing and supporting operators is a leadership responsibility.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The system must work reliably before it is sold to others. Franchising an unproven concept transfers risk to franchisees who trusted the brand.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Mateo owns 8 successful bakeries. His home currency has fallen sharply. Operators in two stronger-currency countries want to open bakeries under his brand.

Option 1: License the brand and recipes only. Faster, simpler, but little control over quality.
Option 2: Franchise the full system.

Mateo chooses franchising because quality consistency is central to his brand. His preparation:
- Documents his operating system in manuals: recipes, training, customer service, suppliers.
- Registers trademarks in both countries.
- Hires franchise attorneys in each country. One country requires a detailed disclosure document and a waiting period before signing. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Sets fees: an initial fee plus 6% of sales in royalties, paid in the franchisee's currency.
- Tax advice on cross-border royalties and treaty benefits. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Selects experienced operators and provides on-site training.

Two years later, 5 franchise locations generate royalties in stronger currencies, helping balance his home-currency weakness.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Mateo's system was proven through 8 successful locations before he franchised it. That proof protected both his brand and the franchisees who invested in it.

## Key Takeaways
- Licensing and franchising let others invest in expanding your brand
- Franchising offers more control but more regulation
- Disclosure and registration rules are REGION-SPECIFIC
- Prove the system before selling it to others
