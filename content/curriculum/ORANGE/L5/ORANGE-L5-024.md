# Marketing a Company for Sale

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: With interest rates high, fewer buyers can finance acquisitions, and those who can are more selective. An owner wants to sell. Ask across colors: How should the business be positioned to attract the right buyers (ORANGE)? Are the financial records clean (GREEN/BLUE)? Are legal documents organized (PURPLE)? Would seller financing expand the buyer pool?

## Learning Objectives
- Apply marketing principles to selling a business
- Prepare financial, legal, and operational information for buyers
- Target and position for different buyer types

## Core Content
Selling a business is a sales process. The product is the company. The buyers range from individuals to competitors to investors. Good marketing can increase both the price and the chance of closing.

Types of buyers:
- Individual buyers: Often want a stable business with owner transition support. Often need financing.
- Strategic buyers: Competitors or related companies. May pay more for synergies such as customers, locations, or technology.
- Financial buyers: Investment groups seeking returns. Focus on cash flow, growth, and management strength.
- Insiders: Employees or family (see PURPLE-L5-015 in the law track).

Preparing the business:
- Financials (GREEN/BLUE): Clean, accurate statements for several years. Separate personal expenses. Explain one-time items.
- Legal (PURPLE): Organized records, contracts, IP ownership, and compliance (PURPLE-L5-043).
- Operations (BLACK): Documented systems and capable managers, so the business does not depend entirely on the owner. The systems epiphany applies: buyers pay for businesses, not jobs.
- Customers: Reduce concentration if possible (ORANGE-L5-018).

Positioning: What makes this business attractive to each buyer type? Stable cash flow for individuals, strategic fit for competitors, growth potential for investors.

Confidentiality: Sale processes usually use NDAs and share sensitive information in stages, to protect employees, customers, and competitive position.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Seller financing may expand the buyer pool when bank financing is tight (ORANGE-L5-007).
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Sale structure affects the seller's taxes (PURPLE-L5-023).
- WHITE: Valuation depends on earnings, risk, and growth.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The best-prepared businesses often sell for more and faster. Preparation should begin one to three years before a planned sale.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Lina owns an HVAC service company with $3 million in revenue. She wants to sell within two years. Rates are high.

Her preparation:
- Year 1: She works with her CPA to produce clean financial statements and remove personal expenses. She promotes her operations manager to general manager and documents service processes. She signs longer-term service contracts with commercial clients to add recurring revenue.
- Legal: Her attorney organizes records, confirms vehicle titles and licenses, and reviews employee agreements.
- Positioning: For individual buyers, "stable recurring service revenue with a strong manager in place." For strategic buyers, "customer base and technicians in a growing region."
- Financing: She offers to carry 20% of the price as a seller note (see PURPLE-L5-024), making it easier for buyers to finance the rest.
- Process: A business broker markets the company confidentially. Buyers sign NDAs before receiving detailed information.

She receives three offers, including one from a regional competitor that values her technicians and customer base. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Her preparation and positioning turned a hard selling environment into a competitive process.

## Key Takeaways
- Selling a business is a sales process with different buyer types
- Prepare clean financials, organized legal records, and transferable operations
- Position the business for each buyer type's priorities
- Seller financing can expand the buyer pool when credit is tight
