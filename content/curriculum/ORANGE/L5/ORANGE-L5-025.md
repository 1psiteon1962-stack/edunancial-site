# Earnouts and Seller Notes from the Seller's Seat

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once.

Apply it here: A business owner agrees to sell with part of the price paid through an earnout tied to future revenue. Then a long disruption hits, and revenue falls. Ask across colors: Did the earnout terms protect the seller (PURPLE)? Could the buyer's decisions have reduced revenue unfairly (BLACK)? How are earnout payments taxed (GREEN)? How much of the seller's wealth depends on the buyer (WHITE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the risks sellers take with earnouts and seller notes
- Negotiate protections for sellers
- Evaluate how deferred payments affect the seller's overall financial plan

## Core Content
ORANGE-L5-006 showed how earnouts and seller notes help buyers bridge price gaps. This lesson looks from the seller's seat, where these tools carry real risk.

Seller note risk: The seller becomes a lender (PURPLE-L5-024 in the law track). If the buyer runs the business poorly, the seller may not be paid. Protections include security, personal guarantees, covenants, and reporting requirements.

Earnout risk: Payment depends on future performance, usually revenue or profit. After the sale, the buyer controls the business. Risks include:
- The buyer changes strategy, cuts spending, or combines the business with others, making targets hard to reach.
- Accounting choices that reduce reported profit.
- Events outside anyone's control, like the scenario's disruption.
- Disputes about how results are measured.

Seller protections for earnouts:
- Clear metrics: Revenue is often easier to measure and harder to manipulate than profit.
- Defined accounting rules: Written in the agreement.
- Operating covenants: Buyer promises to run the business in the ordinary course and maintain reasonable resources.
- Information rights: Regular reports and audit rights.
- Acceleration: Full earnout paid if the buyer sells the business or breaches key terms.
- Shorter periods: 1 to 3 years reduces uncertainty.
- Dispute resolution: A named independent accountant for measurement disputes.

How colors connect:
- WHITE: Deferred payments concentrate the seller's wealth in the buyer's success. Sellers should consider diversification.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Earnout and installment payments may be taxed as received or differently, affecting timing and rates.
- BLACK: A seller who stays on during the earnout period needs clear authority.
- ORANGE: Negotiating leverage is strongest before signing.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Sellers should value an earnout at less than its maximum, because of the real chance it will not be fully paid. Cash at closing is worth more than a promise.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Farouk sells his software company for $4 million: $2.5 million at closing, a $500,000 seller note, and up to $1 million earnout over 2 years based on revenue.

His negotiated protections:
- Earnout metric: Revenue, not profit.
- Buyer covenant: Maintain the sales team at no fewer than 5 people and continue selling the product.
- Monthly revenue reports and annual audit rights.
- Acceleration: Full earnout if the buyer sells the business or discontinues the product.
- Seller note: Secured by business assets and personally guaranteed by the buyer's owner.
- Disputes: Independent accountant decides measurement issues.

The disruption hits in year 1. Revenue falls 20%. The earnout target is missed for year 1, reducing that year's payment. But the buyer also tries to cut the sales team to 3. Farouk points to the covenant, and the buyer restores the team.

In year 2, revenue recovers, and Farouk receives most of that year's earnout. His seller note is paid on schedule.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Farouk received about $3.7 million of the possible $4 million. Without the protections, he might have received far less.

**Q1. Why is a seller note risky for sellers?**
A) It is illegal
B) It eliminates taxes
C) It raises the price
D) The seller becomes a lender and may not be paid if the buyer struggles

**Q2. Why is revenue often preferred over profit as an earnout metric?**
A) Revenue is easier to measure and harder to manipulate
B) Revenue is always higher
C) Profit is illegal to measure
D) Buyers prefer it

**Q3. What is an acceleration clause in an earnout?**
A) Full earnout payment if the buyer sells the business or breaches key terms
B) A faster closing
C) A higher interest rate
D) A marketing campaign

**Q4. How did the covenant help Farouk?**
A) It lowered taxes
B) It required the buyer to restore the sales team
C) It canceled the earnout
D) It raised the price

**Q5. How should sellers value an earnout, according to the lesson?**
A) At its maximum
B) Twice its value
C) At zero
D) At less than its maximum, because it may not be fully paid

## Answer Key
Q1: D
Q2: A
Q3: A
Q4: B
Q5: D

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Sellers take credit risk on the buyer.

**Q2.** Profit can be affected by buyer accounting choices.

**Q3.** Acceleration protects sellers from buyer actions.

**Q4.** Operating covenants protected earnout potential.

**Q5.** Contingent payments carry real risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Seller notes and earnouts shift risk to the seller
- Protect earnouts with clear metrics, covenants, reporting, and acceleration
- Deferred payments concentrate wealth in the buyer's success
- Value earnouts below their maximum
