# Marketing Financial Products: Suitability, Fiduciary Duty, and Trust

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a systemically important financial institution suddenly revealing a large, previously undisclosed loss, raising fears about counterparty risk rippling through the broader banking system.

Apply it here: A banking scare makes customers nervous. A financial firm wants to market 'safe' products to attract worried savers. Ask across colors: Which products are actually appropriate for different customers (WHITE)? What standard of care applies to the firm's advice (PURPLE)? Can it call products 'safe' (ORANGE)? How do fees affect customer outcomes (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain suitability, best interest, and fiduciary standards
- Identify marketing practices that create legal and trust risks
- Market financial products and advice responsibly

## Core Content
Financial products and advice are regulated because customers often cannot easily judge quality or risk, and mistakes can be costly.

Standards of care (names and details REGION-SPECIFIC RULE):
- Suitability: Recommendations must be appropriate for the customer's situation, goals, and risk tolerance.
- Best interest: Recommendations must be in the customer's best interest, with conflicts managed or disclosed.
- Fiduciary: The advisor must put the customer's interests first, act with loyalty and care, and avoid or fully disclose conflicts.

Which standard applies depends on the type of professional, the product, and the jurisdiction.

Marketing risks:
- Calling products "safe," "guaranteed," or "risk-free" when they are not.
- Highlighting past performance without context, or implying it predicts future results.
- Hiding fees or conflicts of interest, such as commissions tied to specific products.
- Targeting vulnerable customers with complex products.
- Testimonials and endorsements in financial marketing may face special rules. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

How colors connect:
- WHITE: Understanding the product's real risk, return, liquidity, and costs.
- PURPLE: Licensing, disclosure, advertising, and conduct rules. Regulators often closely monitor financial marketing.
- BLUE: Fees compound over time and can greatly reduce customer returns.
- BLACK: Ethical leadership and compensation design (ORANGE-L5-008) affect advisor behavior.
- GREEN: Tax treatment affects product comparisons. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

The curriculum itself follows a related principle: it provides general education, not personalized financial, legal, or tax advice.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: In financial services, trust is the real product. Marketing that sacrifices trust for short-term sales damages the entire business.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: After the banking scare, two firms market to nervous savers.

Firm A advertises: "Our Secure Income Fund: Safe, guaranteed returns of 7%!" The fund invests in lower-rated corporate bonds and carries meaningful risk. Advisors earn higher commissions for selling it.

Firm B advertises: "Worried about your savings? Learn your options." It offers a free guide explaining:
- Which accounts are covered by deposit insurance or investor protection schemes, and their limits. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Differences between insured savings, government bonds, and other products.
- Clear fee disclosures.
- An invitation to meet with an advisor who follows a best-interest or fiduciary standard, as applicable.

When bond prices fall later that year, Firm A's fund loses value. Customers who believed it was "guaranteed" complain. Regulators investigate Firm A's advertising.

Firm B's customers, who understood their options, are more satisfied, and many refer friends.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Firm A's short-term sales created long-term legal and reputation damage. Firm B's honest approach built lasting trust.

## Key Takeaways
- Financial marketing is shaped by suitability, best interest, and fiduciary standards
- Avoid 'safe,' 'guaranteed,' and misleading performance claims
- Disclose fees and conflicts clearly
- Trust is the core product in financial services
