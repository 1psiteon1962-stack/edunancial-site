# Tax-Aware Promotions: Discounts, Rebates, and Gifts

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: With consumers squeezed by high rates, a retailer plans promotions to drive sales. Ask across colors: Is a discount, rebate, or gift card better for customers (ORANGE)? How does each affect sales tax and income reporting (GREEN)? Do unused gift cards or loyalty points create obligations (PURPLE)? Which structure is best for cash flow (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Compare common promotional structures
- Explain how promotions can differ in tax and accounting treatment
- Choose promotions that coordinate marketing goals with cash flow and compliance

## Core Content
Promotions can look similar to customers but work very differently for the business.

Common promotional structures:
- Price discounts: Lower price at purchase. Simple. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: In many systems, sales tax is calculated on the discounted price.
- Rebates: Customer pays full price, then claims money back later. Some customers never claim, which affects the true cost. The business may collect sales tax on the full price, depending on rules.
- Buy-one-get-one or free items: Tax and accounting treatment varies. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Gift cards: Customers prepay for future purchases. Cash comes in early. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Many jurisdictions regulate expiration dates, fees, and unclaimed balances, and accounting rules often treat unredeemed gift cards as a liability until used.
- Loyalty points: Customers earn future rewards. Accounting rules may require setting aside part of revenue for points not yet redeemed. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Business gifts and customer entertainment: Deductibility is often limited. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

How colors connect:
- ORANGE: Which promotion motivates the target customer?
- GREEN: Sales tax calculation, revenue recognition, and deductibility can differ by structure.
- BLUE: Gift cards bring cash early; rebates delay cash out; loyalty points create future obligations.
- PURPLE: Consumer protection rules on promotions, including clear terms, gift card rules, and restrictions on misleading "free" offers.
- WHITE: Deep, frequent discounts can train customers to wait for sales, weakening pricing power (ORANGE-L5-031).

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The best promotion is not always the most generous one. It is the one that achieves the marketing goal at the lowest true cost, after taxes, cash timing, and future obligations.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Clara runs a home goods store. She compares three promotions for a $100 item:

Option 1: 20% instant discount. Customer pays $80. Sales tax is calculated on $80 in her jurisdiction. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. Cost to Clara: $20 per sale.

Option 2: $20 mail-in rebate. Customer pays $100, then claims $20. Clara's CPA explains how the rebate affects her tax reporting. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. Some customers never claim, so her average cost is lower than $20. But customers often find rebates frustrating, which may hurt her brand.

Option 3: Buy $100, get a $20 gift card for a future visit. Customer pays $100. Clara receives full cash now. Her accountant explains that the gift card's value may need to be treated as a liability until used. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. Her attorney confirms gift card rules on expiration. Customers return to redeem the card, often spending more.

Clara chooses Option 3 during a slow season. It brings customers back, supports cash flow, and complies with gift card rules.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The "same" $20 promotion had three very different effects on cash, taxes, legal obligations, and customer behavior.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Rebates delay the customer's savings.

**Q2.** Accounting often treats them as obligations. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q3.** Frequent discounting can erode pricing power.

**Q4.** It balanced marketing, cash, and compliance.

**Q5.** True cost includes taxes, cash timing, and obligations.

## Key Takeaways
- Similar-looking promotions can have very different effects
- Tax, accounting, and legal treatment vary by structure and jurisdiction
- Gift cards and loyalty points create future obligations
- Choose promotions by true cost, not headline generosity
