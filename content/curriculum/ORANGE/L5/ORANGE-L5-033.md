# Selling Across Jurisdictions: Sales Tax and VAT

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs.

Apply it here: Tariffs push a company to sell directly to customers in many new regions online. Ask across colors: Does selling into each region create a tax collection obligation (GREEN)? Should prices shown to customers include tax (ORANGE)? What registration and filing duties apply (PURPLE)? Can the company's systems handle many tax rates (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how sales taxes and VAT generally work
- Identify when selling into a new region may create tax obligations
- Adapt pricing, systems, and marketing for multi-jurisdiction sales

## Core Content
Many jurisdictions tax sales of goods and services. The two main systems:
- Sales tax: Usually collected once, at the final sale to the consumer.
- Value-added tax (VAT) or goods and services tax (GST): Collected at each stage of production and distribution, with businesses generally able to recover tax they paid on inputs.

REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Rates, rules, exemptions, and thresholds vary widely.

When do obligations arise? Traditionally, a business had to collect tax where it had a physical presence. Many jurisdictions now also require collection when a seller exceeds certain sales amounts or transaction counts into that area, even without physical presence. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. Some jurisdictions require VAT registration for foreign sellers of digital services or goods from the first sale.

Marketplace rules: In some places, online marketplaces must collect tax on behalf of sellers. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

How colors connect:
- GREEN: Registration, collection, filing, and payment.
- ORANGE: Price display. In some markets, consumers expect tax-inclusive prices (common in VAT systems). In others, tax is added at checkout. Surprising customers with tax at checkout can hurt conversion.
- PURPLE: Failure to register and collect can lead to back taxes, interest, and penalties, sometimes with personal liability for responsible individuals. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- BLUE: Tax collected belongs to the government, not the business. Mixing it with operating cash is a common and dangerous mistake. Systems or software that calculate rates by location may be needed.
- WHITE: Uncollected tax liabilities reduce business value in diligence.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Expanding sales online without checking tax obligations is one of the most common and costly mistakes growing businesses make. Checking before expanding is far cheaper than fixing it afterward.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Tariq's company sells phone accessories online. He expands from his home region to customers across many regions and two foreign countries.

Tax review with his CPA:
- Home region: Already registered and collecting.
- Several other regions: His sales now exceed thresholds that require him to register and collect. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Marketplace sales: The marketplace collects tax for those sales in some regions. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Foreign Country 1: Requires VAT registration for foreign sellers of goods to consumers above a threshold, with tax-inclusive price display expected.
- Foreign Country 2: Collects import VAT at the border in some cases, which affects customer experience.

Tariq's actions:
- Registers where required.
- Installs tax calculation software.
- Shows tax-inclusive prices on his Country 1 site.
- Opens a separate bank account for collected taxes.
- Reviews thresholds quarterly as sales grow.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Tariq's compliance costs rose, but he avoided accumulating back taxes and penalties that could have threatened his business later.

## Key Takeaways
- Sales tax and VAT systems work differently and vary widely
- Selling into new regions can create obligations even without physical presence
- Adapt price display, systems, and registration
- Keep collected tax separate; it is not your money
