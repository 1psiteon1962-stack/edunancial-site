# Crisis Reputation Management

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once.

Apply it here: During a widespread disruption, a company's product fails for many customers, and complaints spread online. Ask across colors: What should the company say publicly, and how fast (ORANGE)? What legal risks come with admitting fault (PURPLE)? What remedies can the company afford (BLUE)? Who in leadership takes responsibility (BLACK)?

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the stages of crisis response
- Balance honesty, speed, and legal considerations
- Coordinate remedies, communication, and leadership accountability

## Core Content
A reputation crisis can come from a product failure, a service breakdown, an employee's misconduct, a data breach, or a viral complaint. How a business responds often matters more than the original problem.

Stages of crisis response:
1. Detect: Monitor customer feedback, reviews, and social media to catch problems early.
2. Assess: Understand what happened, how many people are affected, and whether safety is involved.
3. Respond quickly: Acknowledge the issue publicly, even before you have all the answers.
4. Take responsibility where appropriate: Explain what you are doing to fix it.
5. Offer remedies: Refunds, replacements, repairs, or other fair solutions.
6. Update: Keep people informed as you learn more.
7. Learn and improve: Fix the root cause and share what changed.

Balancing honesty and legal risk:
- PURPLE: Lawyers may worry that admitting fault increases liability. Communication should be honest while avoiding speculation or statements beyond known facts. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some jurisdictions have rules about apologies and admissions. Safety issues may trigger mandatory reporting or recall obligations.
- Coordinate legal and communication teams, but do not let legal caution cause silence, which often damages trust more.

How colors connect:
- ORANGE: Clear, human communication across channels.
- PURPLE: Legal obligations, insurance notification (PURPLE-L5-017 in the law track), and careful wording.
- BLUE: Remedies cost money. Insurance may cover some costs.
- BLACK: Visible leadership accountability.
- WHITE: Well-handled crises can limit damage to business value.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Customers often forgive mistakes. They rarely forgive silence, deflection, or dishonesty.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Leilani's company sells portable heaters. During a disruption, a supplier's faulty part causes some heaters to overheat. No injuries occur, but videos spread online.

Her response:
- Hour 1 (Detect/Assess): Her team confirms the issue affects a specific production batch.
- Hour 4 (Respond): She posts a clear statement: "We've learned some heaters from batch 2204 may overheat. Please stop using them. We are investigating and will provide free replacements or full refunds."
- Legal (PURPLE): Her attorney confirms reporting obligations to the product safety regulator and helps coordinate a voluntary recall. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE. She notifies her insurer.
- Remedies (BLUE): Free replacements or refunds, with prepaid return shipping.
- Leadership (BLACK): Leilani records a video explaining what happened and what the company is doing.
- Updates: Daily updates for the first week.
- Improvement: She adds testing of supplier parts before assembly and shares this change publicly.

Months later, customer surveys show trust has largely recovered. Several customers praise the response publicly.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The faulty part was not Leilani's fault directly, but taking responsibility quickly turned a crisis into a demonstration of trustworthiness.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Response determines reputation outcomes.

**Q2.** Early acknowledgment builds trust.

**Q3.** Careful honesty protects both trust and legal position.

**Q4.** Fixing and communicating improvements restored trust.

**Q5.** Honest responses preserve trust.

## Key Takeaways
- How you respond to a crisis often matters more than the crisis
- Respond quickly, honestly, and with fair remedies
- Coordinate legal and communication teams without defaulting to silence
- Leaders should be visibly accountable
