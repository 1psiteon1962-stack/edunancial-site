# Marketing to a Global Audience with Different Purchasing Power

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a mid-sized economy's currency losing a large share of its value against major reserve currencies over a few months, driven by capital fleeing the country and eroding investor confidence.

Apply it here: A currency collapse sharply reduces local incomes in one country, while customers in another remain relatively wealthy. A company sells a digital course in both. Ask across colors: Should prices be the same everywhere (ORANGE)? How do exchange rates and purchasing power affect revenue (WHITE/BLUE)? What legal or tax issues arise from regional pricing (PURPLE/GREEN)? How should success stories be framed for each audience?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain purchasing power differences across markets
- Design regional pricing and messaging
- Apply the relativity of achievement principle to global marketing

## Core Content
A price that feels small in one country may be a week's income in another. Global marketing must account for these differences.

Purchasing power parity (PPP) is a concept used to compare what money can buy in different countries. VERIFIED FACT: Economists use PPP-adjusted measures to compare living standards because exchange rates alone do not reflect local prices.

Regional pricing approaches:
- Uniform pricing: Same price everywhere. Simple, but may exclude customers in lower-income markets.
- PPP-adjusted pricing: Lower prices in markets with lower purchasing power.
- Tiered products: Different versions at different prices.
- Local payment methods and smaller packages: Making purchases accessible.

Risks of regional pricing:
- Arbitrage: Customers from higher-priced regions may try to buy at lower regional prices. Businesses may use location verification or local payment methods.
- Legal: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some jurisdictions restrict geographic price discrimination in certain situations.
- Tax: VAT and digital services taxes may apply differently by country (ORANGE-L5-033).

Messaging and relativity of achievement: The curriculum teaches that success is measured relative to starting point, not absolute scale. A small business earning a modest income in one economy may represent the same level of achievement and progress as a much larger business elsewhere. Marketing should reflect this:
- Use examples and success stories relevant to local conditions.
- Avoid implying that only large absolute numbers count as success.
- Respect local circumstances without assuming limitations.

How colors connect:
- ORANGE: Pricing and messaging.
- WHITE/BLUE: Currency and revenue effects (ORANGE-L5-015).
- PURPLE/GREEN: Legal and tax compliance.
- BLACK: Values and respect for diverse audiences.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Regional pricing can expand access and grow total revenue, especially for digital products where extra customers cost little to serve.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Kwabena sells an online bookkeeping course for $120.

In Market A (higher incomes), sales are steady. In Market B, after a currency collapse, $120 represents a much larger share of monthly income. Sales there fall to almost zero.

His changes:
- PPP-adjusted pricing: Market B price set at $35, paid in local currency through a local payment method.
- Arbitrage protection: Regional pricing tied to local payment methods and billing location.
- Tax: VAT registration where required. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Messaging: Market B marketing features a local student who started a small bookkeeping service for market vendors, showing her progress from her own starting point. Market A marketing features a student who grew a regional accounting firm.
- Legal review: Confirms regional pricing complies with rules in his markets. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

Results: Market B sales grow strongly. Because the course is digital, each additional sale costs very little to deliver. Total revenue increases.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Kwabena did not lower the course's value. He matched price and message to each audience's reality.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** PPP reflects local prices, not just exchange rates.

**Q2.** Arbitrage can undermine regional pricing.

**Q3.** Progress depends on where someone begins.

**Q4.** Access expanded the customer base.

**Q5.** Local, relative success stories resonated.

## Key Takeaways
- Purchasing power differs widely across markets
- Regional pricing and local payment methods expand access
- Manage arbitrage, legal, and tax issues
- Frame success relative to starting point
