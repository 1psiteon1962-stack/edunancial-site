# The Sales Pipeline as a Decision System

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a widespread public health disruption halting global shipping and manufacturing for an extended stretch, snarling supply chains across many industries at once.

Apply it here: A long disruption makes future sales uncertain. Leadership must decide whether to hire, cut costs, or invest. Ask across colors: What does the pipeline data actually show (ORANGE)? How should hiring and spending respond (BLUE/BLACK)? How much uncertainty is in the forecast (WHITE)? What commitments in existing contracts affect decisions (PURPLE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Use pipeline metrics to understand sales health
- Connect pipeline data to hiring, spending, and cash decisions
- Build decision rules based on pipeline signals

## Core Content
A sales pipeline tracks potential customers as they move through stages: lead, qualified opportunity, proposal, negotiation, and closed (won or lost). Many businesses use it only to track deals. At Level 5, it becomes a decision system.

Key pipeline metrics:
- Pipeline value: Total value of open opportunities.
- Weighted pipeline: Value adjusted by probability of closing (ORANGE-L5-028).
- Conversion rates: The percentage of opportunities moving from each stage to the next.
- Sales cycle length: Average time from first contact to close.
- Win rate: Percentage of opportunities won.
- Pipeline coverage: Weighted pipeline compared with sales targets.

Using the pipeline for decisions:
- Hiring (BLACK/BLUE): If the weighted pipeline consistently exceeds what the team can handle, hiring may be justified. If it shrinks, hiring should pause.
- Spending (BLUE): Marketing budgets can be adjusted based on whether the top of the pipeline is filling.
- Cash (BLUE): Expected close dates and payment terms help forecast cash flow.
- Strategy (ORANGE): Falling conversion at a specific stage reveals where the sales process needs work.
- Risk (WHITE): Pipeline concentration in a few large deals signals higher uncertainty.

Decision rules help leaders act consistently:
- ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: "If weighted pipeline for next quarter falls below 80% of costs, freeze hiring and review discretionary spending." "If it exceeds 150% of team capacity for two quarters, begin recruiting."

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Existing contracts (leases, employment agreements, supplier commitments) limit how quickly costs can change.
- BLACK: Decision rules prevent emotional overreaction to single good or bad weeks.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: A pipeline is only as good as the honesty of the data in it. Salespeople who inflate probabilities to look good can lead leaders into bad decisions.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Obinna runs a commercial cleaning company. During a disruption, clients pause contracts and new bids slow.

His pipeline review:
- Weighted pipeline for next quarter: $180,000, down from $300,000.
- Conversion from proposal to close: fell from 40% to 25%.
- Sales cycle: grew from 30 to 55 days.
- Two large bids represent 60% of the weighted pipeline (concentration risk).

His decision rules (set before the disruption):
- Weighted pipeline below 80% of quarterly costs ($250,000 x 80% = $200,000): freeze hiring and cut discretionary spending. Triggered.
- Conversion below 30%: review proposals and pricing. Triggered.

Actions:
- Freezes hiring and reduces spending (BLUE).
- Reviews lost proposals and learns clients now want flexible, month-to-month contracts. He adjusts offerings (ORANGE).
- Checks his lease and equipment contracts to see which costs he can adjust (PURPLE).
- Requires salespeople to justify probabilities with specific evidence to improve data honesty (BLACK).

Two quarters later, conversion recovers to 35% with the new flexible offering. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The pipeline did not just track decline; it told Obinna what to change.

**Q5.** Decisions depend on accurate data.

## Key Takeaways
- A pipeline is a decision system, not just a tracking tool
- Use conversion, cycle length, coverage, and concentration metrics
- Set decision rules for hiring, spending, and strategy
- Pipeline value depends on honest data
