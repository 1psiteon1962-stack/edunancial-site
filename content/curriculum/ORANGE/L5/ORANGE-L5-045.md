# The Integrated Go-to-Market Plan

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture two large economic powers escalating export controls against each other on a category of advanced technology components used across many industries.

Apply it here: Export controls force a company to launch a redesigned product in new markets. Ask across colors: Who is the target customer and why will they buy (ORANGE)? How will the launch be financed (BLUE)? What legal and IP protections are needed (PURPLE)? What tax obligations arise in each market (GREEN)? Who leads each part (BLACK)? This is the whole board in one plan.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the components of an integrated go-to-market plan
- Coordinate marketing decisions with finance, legal, tax, and leadership
- Build a plan that adapts as results come in

## Core Content
A go-to-market (GTM) plan describes how a business will bring a product or service to customers. Level 5 GTM plans integrate every color into one strategy.

Components of an integrated GTM plan:
1. Target customer (ORANGE): Who, what problem, and why now?
2. Positioning (ORANGE): Why choose us over alternatives?
3. Pricing (ORANGE/BLUE/GREEN): Price level, structure, cash timing, and taxes (ORANGE-L5-002).
4. Channels (ORANGE/PURPLE): Direct, distributors, marketplaces, and their contracts (ORANGE-L5-016).
5. Marketing and sales (ORANGE/BLACK): Campaigns, team, and compensation (ORANGE-L5-008).
6. Financing (BLUE/WHITE): Capital needed, runway, and staged milestones (ORANGE-L5-039).
7. Legal (PURPLE): Trademarks, claims compliance, privacy, contracts, and product regulations.
8. Tax (GREEN): Sales tax or VAT, cross-border obligations, and structure. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
9. Operations and supply (BLUE): Suppliers, inventory, and fulfillment (ORANGE-L5-040).
10. Metrics and decision rules (BLACK): What results trigger expansion, adjustment, or pause (ORANGE-L5-044).
11. Risks and contingencies: What could go wrong, and what is the plan for each?

Why integration matters: A brilliant marketing plan fails if the business runs out of cash, violates advertising rules, cannot deliver products, or faces unexpected taxes. Each part depends on the others.

The chess analogy: Each component is a piece. The GTM plan coordinates them toward checkmate: customers buying profitably, sustainably, and legally.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The best GTM plans are living documents. They set a clear starting strategy, then change as customer data and market conditions reveal what works.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Hye-jin's company must launch a redesigned air purifier after export controls cut off a key component. She builds an integrated GTM plan:

- Target: Small offices and clinics in two new regional markets.
- Positioning: "Reliable clean air with local service support."
- Pricing: Mid-range price with a service subscription (recurring revenue, ORANGE-L5-017). Annual prepay option.
- Channels: Direct sales to clinics; regional distributor for small offices, with a contract specifying territory and customer ownership.
- Sales: Two salespeople with salary plus margin-based commission.
- Financing: Staged launch, with Stage 1 funded by existing reserves; Stage 2 funded by a credit line if milestones are met.
- Legal: Trademark filed in both markets; air quality claims backed by independent testing (PURPLE-L5-035); product certification in each market. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Tax: VAT registration in both markets. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Supply: Two component suppliers to avoid concentration.
- Metrics: Stage 2 begins when 50 clinics sign and subscription churn is below 3% monthly.
- Risks: Component delays (backup supplier), slow adoption (flexible pilots).

Six months in, clinic sales exceed targets while small-office sales lag. She shifts resources toward clinics. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The plan worked because it was integrated from the start and flexible enough to adjust.

## Key Takeaways
- Integrated GTM plans coordinate every color
- Include customer, positioning, pricing, channels, financing, legal, tax, supply, metrics, and risks
- Marketing fails if other pieces break
- Treat the plan as a living document
