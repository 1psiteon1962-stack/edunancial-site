# Torts vs. Contracts: Two Different Kinds of Legal Claims

## Learning Objectives

- Define a tort in plain terms.
- Distinguish tort claims from contract claims.
- Identify examples relevant to RED, WHITE, and BLUE contexts.

## Core Content

A tort is a civil wrong that causes harm to another person, independent of any contract between them — negligence, property damage, or defamation are common examples. This is different from a contract dispute, which arises from a broken promise between parties who had an agreement. Torts can happen between people who never had any contract at all — a contractor's careless work damaging a neighboring property is a tort claim, not a contract claim, if the neighbor and contractor had no agreement between them.

This distinction matters practically across this curriculum: a property owner (RED) can be sued in tort if unsafe conditions on their property injure someone, regardless of any lease or purchase contract. A business owner (BLUE) can face tort liability for harm caused by a product or a careless employee, separate from any contract with a customer. Understanding that legal risk isn't limited to broken promises — that harm caused outside of any agreement can still create liability — is an important expansion of legal literacy beyond just contracts.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A property owner's poorly maintained staircase injures a delivery driver who has no contract with the owner at all. What kind of legal claim does this likely represent?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, this sounds like a tort claim, since there's no contract between the owner and the driver — the harm exists independently of any agreement.

**Guide:** Why does the absence of a contract matter here?

**Student:** Because it shows that legal liability isn't limited to broken promises between contracting parties — harm caused outside any agreement can still create legal exposure.

**Guide:** Exactly — this is exactly why property owners need to think about tort risk, not just contract risk.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Torts are fundamentally about causing harm, not about broken agreements.

**2.** The absence of a contract is exactly what distinguishes many tort scenarios from contract disputes.

**3.** This scenario is a classic illustration of tort liability arising with no contractual relationship at all.

**4.** Recognizing this broader liability landscape rounds out a more complete picture of legal risk for property and business owners.

## Key Takeaways

- A tort is a civil wrong causing harm, distinct from a broken contract.
- Torts can arise even between parties with no contract at all.
- Property owners and business owners face tort risk separate from any contract risk.
- Legal literacy includes understanding harm-based liability, not just agreement-based liability.
