# Civil vs. Criminal Law: Why the Distinction Matters

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish civil law from criminal law.
- Explain the different consequences and burdens of proof involved.
- Recognize why PURPLE's scope is primarily civil, not criminal.

## Core Content

Civil law governs disputes between private parties — contract breaches, torts, property disputes — where the typical remedy is money or a court order, not imprisonment. Criminal law governs offenses against society itself, prosecuted by the government, where the potential consequence includes fines and imprisonment. The two systems also differ in their burden of proof: criminal cases require proof "beyond a reasonable doubt," a very high standard, while civil cases typically use a lower standard, often "preponderance of the evidence" (more likely than not).

Almost everything covered in this PURPLE track — contracts, property law, securities regulation, business structures — lives on the civil side of this distinction. That's intentional: the legal risks most directly relevant to building wealth through real estate, investing, and business are civil risks (being sued, losing a deal, facing regulatory penalties), not criminal risks. Criminal law matters, but it's a different, more specialized area, and serious criminal exposure is exactly the kind of situation that calls for immediate, dedicated legal representation rather than general literacy.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A business owner is sued by a former customer over a contract dispute. Is this a civil matter or a criminal matter, and why does the distinction matter practically?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, that's a civil matter — a private dispute over a contract, not an offense being prosecuted by the government.

**Guide:** How does that affect what's actually at stake for the business owner?

**Student:** The typical consequence would be money or a court order, not imprisonment, and the burden of proof the customer would need to meet is lower than a criminal case's 'beyond a reasonable doubt' standard.

**Guide:** Exactly — understanding which system you're in changes what's realistically at stake and what standard applies.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Civil law addresses private disputes with remedies typically limited to money or court orders, not incarceration.

**2.** The heightened criminal standard reflects the severity of potential consequences, including loss of liberty.

**3.** Contracts, property, securities, and business structure — this track's core content — are all civil law topics.

**4.** Because civil risk is what most directly threatens wealth-building activities, that's where this track's literacy focus belongs.

## Key Takeaways

- Civil law governs private disputes; criminal law governs offenses against society.
- Criminal law's burden of proof is much higher than civil law's.
- PURPLE focuses almost entirely on civil law, which is directly relevant to RED, WHITE, and BLUE.
- Serious criminal exposure calls for immediate dedicated legal representation, beyond this track's scope.
