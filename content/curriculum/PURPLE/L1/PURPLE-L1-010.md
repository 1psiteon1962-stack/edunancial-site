# The Doctor Analogy Revisited: When to Call a Lawyer

## Learning Objectives

- Recall the doctor analogy and apply it specifically to legal decisions.
- Identify concrete signals that a situation warrants a lawyer.
- Reinforce that legal literacy and legal representation are complementary, not substitutes.

## Core Content

This curriculum has used the doctor analogy repeatedly: when something is complex, high-stakes, or uncertain, a professional's fee is insurance against a much larger downside, not just a cost to avoid. Nowhere does this apply more directly than law. Legal mistakes — a poorly drafted contract, a missed disclosure requirement, an improperly formed business entity — can be extraordinarily expensive to fix after the fact, far more than the cost of getting it right upfront.

Concrete signals that a situation warrants a lawyer include: any transaction involving significant money (a real estate purchase, a business sale), any document you don't fully understand before signing, forming or restructuring a business entity, facing an actual dispute or lawsuit, and any situation where the "standard" approach doesn't quite fit your specific facts. As with earlier lessons on this theme, the goal of PURPLE isn't to make you your own lawyer — it's to make you a sharper, more informed client, someone who can ask better questions, understand the advice you receive, and recognize early when a situation has crossed into professional-help territory.

## Worked Example

**Question:** Someone drafts their own real estate purchase contract using a template found online, for a significant six-figure property purchase, without any legal review. What does this lesson suggest about that approach?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, a significant-money transaction like that is exactly the kind of situation that calls for professional legal review, not just a generic template.

**Guide:** What's the real cost comparison being made here?

**Student:** The cost of a lawyer's review upfront versus the potentially much larger cost of a legal mistake discovered later, once real money and a real property are already involved.

**Guide:** Exactly — that's the doctor analogy applied directly to this situation.

## Key Takeaways

- The doctor analogy applies directly to law: professional fees are insurance against a larger downside.
- Significant transactions, unfamiliar documents, entity formation, and disputes are all clear signals to call a lawyer.
- PURPLE aims to make you a sharper, more informed client, not a substitute for legal representation.
