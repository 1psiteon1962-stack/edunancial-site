# Sole Proprietorship: Legal Exposure of the Simplest Structure

## Learning Objectives

- Define a sole proprietorship legally.
- Explain why it offers no separation between business and personal liability.
- Recognize the practical risk this creates.

## Core Content

A sole proprietorship is the default structure for a single-owner business with no formal entity registration — legally, the business and the owner are treated as the exact same entity. This is the simplest structure to start (often no formal paperwork required at all), but it comes with a critical legal tradeoff: there is no liability separation between the business and the owner's personal assets.

This means if the business is sued, defaults on a debt, or otherwise faces a legal claim, the owner's personal assets — their home, personal savings, personal property — are directly exposed, not just whatever the business itself owns. This is a meaningful legal risk that many new business owners don't fully appreciate when they start operating informally without setting up a more protective structure. Simplicity has a real cost here, and it's specifically a legal, liability-related cost, distinct from the tax simplicity covered in GREEN.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A freelance consultant operates as a sole proprietorship and gets sued by a client over a dispute, resulting in a judgment larger than the business's assets. What happens to the consultant's personal home and savings?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, since there's no legal separation between the business and the owner, the consultant's personal assets could be exposed to satisfy that judgment.

**Guide:** Would a different business structure have changed this outcome?

**Student:** Potentially — a structure with genuine liability separation might have protected personal assets from a business-related judgment in a way a sole proprietorship doesn't.

**Guide:** Exactly — that's the core legal tradeoff of choosing simplicity over liability protection.

## Key Takeaways

- A sole proprietorship offers no legal separation between business and owner.
- This means personal assets are directly exposed to business-related legal claims.
- Simplicity comes with a real legal liability cost.
- This is a distinct concern from the tax treatment of sole proprietorships covered in GREEN.
