# Piercing the Corporate Veil: When 'Limited' Liability Isn't

## Learning Objectives

- Define 'piercing the corporate veil.'
- Identify common reasons courts pierce the veil.
- Reinforce the practical habits that protect against this risk.

## Core Content

'Piercing the corporate veil' refers to a court disregarding the liability protection an LLC or corporation normally provides, holding the owners personally liable despite the formal structure. This isn't common, but it isn't rare enough to ignore either, and it directly undermines the entire point of forming a protective entity in the first place — which is exactly why understanding what triggers it matters.

Common reasons courts pierce the veil include: failing to maintain separation between business and personal finances (using the business bank account for personal expenses, commingling funds), failing to follow basic corporate formalities (not maintaining proper records, acting as if the entity doesn't really exist), being significantly undercapitalized relative to the business's obvious risks, or using the entity specifically to commit fraud or evade existing legal obligations. The practical lesson is direct: forming an LLC or corporation is necessary but not sufficient for liability protection — you also have to actually operate it as a genuinely separate entity, consistently, or the protection you paid to set up can evaporate exactly when you need it most.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A business owner forms an LLC but routinely uses the business bank account to pay personal bills, never keeps separate records, and treats business and personal money as basically interchangeable. What risk does this create?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, this kind of commingling is exactly the kind of behavior that could lead a court to pierce the corporate veil, disregarding the LLC's liability protection.

**Guide:** Why would a court do that, given that the LLC was properly formed on paper?

**Student:** Because if the owner never actually treated the business as a genuinely separate entity in practice, the court may reasonably conclude the separation was never real, and disregard the protection accordingly.

**Guide:** Exactly — forming the entity is necessary but not sufficient; you have to actually operate it as separate.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** This concept specifically describes the collapse of liability protection due to how the entity was actually operated.

**2.** Commingling funds is one of the most commonly cited triggers for a court disregarding entity protection.

**3.** Formation alone doesn't guarantee protection — ongoing, genuine operational separation is what actually sustains it.

**4.** Consistent financial and formal separation is the practical safeguard against this risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Piercing the corporate veil means a court disregards LLC/corporate protection, exposing owners personally.
- Commingling funds and ignoring formalities are common triggers.
- Forming the entity is necessary but not sufficient — you must actually operate it as genuinely separate.
- Consistent, disciplined separation is the practical defense against this risk.
