# Business Licences and Permits: The Legal Right to Operate

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish entity formation from licensing/permitting.
- Explain the general purpose of business licences and permits.
- Recognise the risk of operating without proper authorisation.

## Core Content

Forming a business entity (an LLC, corporation, etc.) is a separate legal step from obtaining the licences and permits actually required to legally operate that business. Licensing and permitting requirements vary enormously by industry, location, and business type — a restaurant needs health permits a consulting business doesn't; a contractor needs a trade licence a retail shop doesn't. This is another area where "the rules" aren't one universal list, but a jurisdiction- and industry-specific requirement that has to be researched for each specific situation.

Operating without required licences or permits carries real risk: fines, forced closure, invalidated contracts, and, in some cases, personal liability exposure even for an otherwise properly formed entity. A common and understandable mistake is assuming that forming an LLC or corporation is the only legal step needed to "be legitimate" — but a properly formed entity operating without required licences is still operating illegally in a meaningful sense, and that gap is worth identifying and closing before, not after, a problem surfaces.

For UK readers: in the UK many licences are issued by the local council (for example for food businesses, alcohol or street trading), while some sectors are licensed nationally, so check GOV.UK's licence finder for your activity and area.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A newly formed LLC starts operating a business immediately after formation, without researching whether any industry-specific licences or permits are required. What gap does this illustrate?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, forming the LLC handled entity formation, but licensing and permitting is a separate legal requirement that hasn't been addressed at all.

**Guide:** What risk does operating without required licences create, even for a properly formed LLC?

**Student:** Real risk — fines, forced closure, or even exposure that undermines the protection the LLC was supposed to provide, since operating illegally can complicate things even with a properly formed entity.

**Guide:** Exactly — entity formation and legal authorisation to operate are two separate steps, both necessary.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Forming an entity establishes its legal existence; licensing establishes its legal permission to actually operate.

**2.** The specific requirements are determined by industry and jurisdiction, not a single universal checklist.

**3.** Ignoring this step exposes the business to a range of real, avoidable consequences.

**4.** This lesson directly names and corrects the common assumption that formation alone covers full legal compliance.

## Key Takeaways

- Entity formation and licensing/permitting are two separate legal requirements.
- Licensing requirements vary by industry, location, and business type.
- Operating without required licences carries real risk, including fines and forced closure.
- Forming an entity alone doesn't make a business fully legally authorised to operate.
