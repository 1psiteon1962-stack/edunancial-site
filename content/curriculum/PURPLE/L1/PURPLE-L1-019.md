# Non-Compete and Non-Disclosure Agreements: What They Actually Do

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish a non-compete agreement from a non-disclosure agreement.
- Explain the general purpose of each.
- Recognize that enforceability varies significantly by jurisdiction.

## Core Content

A non-disclosure agreement (NDA) restricts a party from sharing specified confidential information — protecting trade secrets, business plans, or proprietary processes shared during a business relationship. A non-compete agreement restricts a party (often a former employee or business seller) from competing with the other party for a specified time and geographic area — protecting a business from a departing insider immediately using inside knowledge to directly compete.

Both are common across BLUE-track business contexts — employment agreements, business sale agreements, partnership dissolutions — but they differ significantly in how enforceable they actually are. NDAs are generally quite enforceable, since they simply restrict disclosure without broadly restricting someone's ability to earn a living. Non-competes are much more variable: some jurisdictions enforce them readily if reasonable in scope, some restrict them heavily, and some ban them for most workers almost entirely, reflecting a policy tension between protecting legitimate business interests and not unreasonably restricting someone's ability to work. This variability is exactly the kind of detail that makes "is my non-compete enforceable" a genuinely jurisdiction-specific question worth professional review, not something to assume either way.

## Answer Key

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** NDAs are specifically about protecting confidential information, not restricting someone's broader ability to work.

**2.** Non-competes specifically restrict future competitive activity within defined limits.

**3.** NDAs face fewer enforceability obstacles since they don't broadly restrict earning a living the way non-competes can.

**4.** This underlying policy tension is exactly why different jurisdictions strike the balance differently.

## Key Takeaways

- NDAs protect confidential information; non-competes restrict future competition.
- NDAs are generally more consistently enforceable than non-competes.
- Non-compete enforceability varies significantly by jurisdiction, reflecting a real policy tension.
- This variability makes non-compete enforceability a genuinely jurisdiction-specific question worth professional review.
