# Liens and Encumbrances: When Property Isn't Fully 'Free'

## Learning Objectives

- Define a lien and an encumbrance.
- Identify common types of liens.
- Explain why liens matter practically for buyers and owners.

## Core Content

An encumbrance is any legal claim, restriction, or interest attached to a property that isn't held by the current owner outright — a broader category that includes liens, easements, and certain restrictions. A lien specifically is a legal claim against a property, usually securing a debt, giving the lienholder a right to force a sale of the property to satisfy that debt if it isn't paid. Common types include a mortgage lien (securing the loan used to purchase the property), a tax lien (for unpaid property taxes), and a mechanic's lien (filed by a contractor who wasn't paid for work done on the property).

Liens matter practically because they generally attach to the property itself, not just to the person who incurred them — meaning a lien can follow a property through a sale if it isn't properly identified and cleared beforehand. This is exactly why title searches (from the previous lesson) matter so much: an unresolved lien discovered before closing can be addressed, but one that slips through can become the new owner's problem. Understanding that a property can carry these attached obligations, separate from the physical property itself, is a core piece of real estate legal literacy.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A contractor completes work on a property but is never paid, and later files a mechanic's lien against that property. If the property is then sold to a new buyer without the lien being resolved, what risk does the new buyer face?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, since liens generally attach to the property itself, not just the person who incurred them, the new buyer could inherit that unresolved lien as their own problem.

**Guide:** How could this have been prevented?

**Student:** A proper title search before the purchase should have identified the lien, allowing it to be resolved or addressed before the sale closed.

**Guide:** Exactly — this is precisely why the title search process from the last lesson matters so much in practice.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Encumbrance is the broader umbrella category covering various attached claims and restrictions.

**2.** A lien's defining feature is its role as security for an unpaid debt.

**3.** Mechanic's liens specifically protect contractors and suppliers who performed work without being compensated.

**4.** This property-attached nature is exactly why unresolved liens are a real risk for unsuspecting buyers.

## Key Takeaways

- An encumbrance is any attached claim or restriction on a property; a lien is a debt-securing claim specifically.
- Common lien types include mortgage liens, tax liens, and mechanic's liens.
- Liens generally attach to the property, not just the person who incurred them.
- This is exactly why a thorough title search before purchase matters so much.
