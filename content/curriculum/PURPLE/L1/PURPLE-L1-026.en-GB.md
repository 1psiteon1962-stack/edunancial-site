# Planning Law: What You Can and Can't Do With Property

## Learning Objectives

- Define planning in plain terms.
- Explain the general purpose of planning regulations.
- Recognise why planning matters for both current use and development plans.

## Core Content

Planning is local government regulation dividing land into designated categories (residential, commercial, industrial, mixed-use, etc.) that restrict what can legally be built or operated on a given property. This is a government-imposed limitation on the bundle-of-rights concept covered earlier — even outright property ownership doesn't include an unlimited right to use land however the owner wishes, since planning law constrains what's actually permitted.

Planning matters in two distinct practical ways for RED-track investors. First, for current use: a property zoned residential generally can't legally be used to run a manufacturing business, regardless of what the owner might prefer. Second, and often overlooked, for future development plans: an investor planning to renovate, expand, or convert a property's use needs to confirm the plan is actually permitted under current planning — or pursue a variance (a specific, sometimes difficult-to-obtain exception) if it isn't. A property that seems perfect on paper can become a costly problem if its intended use doesn't align with its actual planning designation, which is exactly why planning verification belongs early in due diligence, not as an afterthought discovered after purchase.

## Worked Example

**Question:** An investor buys a residentially zoned property, planning to convert it into a small retail shop, without checking planning rules first. What risk does this illustrate?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, the property's planning might not actually permit retail use, which could mean the investor's plan isn't legally allowed without pursuing a variance, if one is even available.

**Guide:** When should this kind of verification have happened, per this lesson?

**Student:** Early in due diligence, before the purchase, rather than being discovered as a costly problem afterward.

**Guide:** Exactly — planning verification belongs at the front of the process, not as an afterthought.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** Planning is specifically a government tool for regulating permitted land use categories.

**2.** This directly limits the otherwise broad use-rights within the property ownership bundle.

**3.** Both dimensions — what's allowed now and what's allowed for future plans — carry real practical weight for investors.

**4.** Front-loading this verification avoids the costly surprise of an unusable or restricted purchase.

## Key Takeaways

- Planning is local government regulation restricting permitted land use.
- Planning limits even outright ownership's use rights, per the bundle-of-rights concept.
- Planning matters for both current legal use and future development or conversion plans.
- Planning verification belongs early in due diligence, not discovered as a problem after purchase.
