# Real Estate Contracts: Purchase Agreements Explained

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the general contract framework specifically to a real estate purchase agreement.
- Identify key terms unique to real estate purchase agreements.
- Recognize common contingencies and their purpose.

## Core Content

A real estate purchase agreement is a direct application of the contract principles covered earlier in this track: it contains an offer (the proposed price and terms), acceptance (the seller's agreement to those terms), and consideration (the exchange of money for property). Beyond the general contract structure, real estate purchase agreements typically include several terms specific to this transaction type worth understanding.

**Contingencies** are conditions that must be satisfied for the deal to proceed — a financing contingency (the deal is off if the buyer can't secure a loan), an inspection contingency (the buyer can back out or renegotiate based on inspection findings), and an appraisal contingency (protecting the buyer if the property appraises for less than the agreed price). **Earnest money** is a deposit showing good faith, typically held by a neutral third party and applied toward the purchase price at closing, or forfeited under certain circumstances if the buyer backs out without a valid contingency reason. Understanding these specific mechanisms — not just the general contract concepts from earlier lessons — is what turns contract literacy into real estate contract literacy specifically.

## Worked Example

**Question:** A buyer signs a purchase agreement with a financing contingency, then is unable to secure a loan. What does this contingency generally allow the buyer to do?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, a financing contingency generally means the deal is off — the buyer can back out — if they can't secure financing, rather than being forced to complete the purchase or forfeit their earnest money.

**Guide:** How does this compare to a buyer backing out without any valid contingency reason at all?

**Student:** Without a valid contingency, backing out risks forfeiting the earnest money deposit, since there's no protected reason for walking away from the deal.

**Guide:** Exactly — contingencies are specifically what create a protected way to exit the deal under defined circumstances.


## Practice Quiz


**Question 1.** A real estate purchase agreement contains which general contract elements?

A) None of the elements covered earlier
B) Offer, acceptance, and consideration, applied to a property transaction
C) Only a signature, nothing else
D) Elements unrelated to contract law


**Question 2.** A financing contingency generally protects the buyer by:

A) Guaranteeing loan approval
B) Allowing the buyer to back out if they can't secure financing
C) Requiring the buyer to pay cash regardless of financing
D) Having no real protective function


**Question 3.** Earnest money is:

A) The full purchase price paid upfront
B) A good-faith deposit, typically applied toward the purchase price or forfeited under certain conditions
C) A type of insurance
D) Irrelevant to real estate contracts


**Question 4.** What happens if a buyer backs out without a valid contingency reason?

A) Nothing — earnest money is always fully refunded
B) They risk forfeiting the earnest money deposit
C) The seller is automatically penalized instead
D) The contract is automatically voided with no consequence


## Answer Key

1. B, 2. B, 3. B, 4. B

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** This is a direct, concrete application of the offer/acceptance/consideration framework to a specific transaction type.

**2.** Financing contingencies specifically protect buyers from being forced to complete a purchase they can't actually finance.

**3.** Earnest money functions as a good-faith commitment, with defined rules for its application or forfeiture.

**4.** Lack of a valid contingency reason is exactly the scenario where earnest money forfeiture risk becomes real.

## Key Takeaways

- A real estate purchase agreement applies the general offer/acceptance/consideration framework.
- Contingencies (financing, inspection, appraisal) create protected conditions for backing out of a deal.
- Earnest money is a good-faith deposit, applied at closing or potentially forfeited.
- Understanding these specific mechanisms is what makes contract literacy applicable to real estate specifically.
