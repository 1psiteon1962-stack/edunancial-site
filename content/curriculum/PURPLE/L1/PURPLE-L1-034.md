# Disclosure Requirements: Why Companies Must Tell the Truth

## Learning Objectives

- Identify general categories of required disclosure.
- Explain the legal consequences of false or misleading disclosure.
- Connect disclosure quality to informed investment decision-making.

## Core Content

Building on the earlier lesson explaining why disclosure requirements exist, this lesson looks at what's actually generally required. Companies offering securities to the public are typically required to disclose: financial statements (a truthful picture of the company's actual financial condition), material risks (factors that could significantly affect the investment's value or the company's prospects), and material facts relevant to an investor's decision (significant events, conflicts of interest, or other information a reasonable investor would want to know before investing).

The legal consequences of false or misleading disclosure are serious, reflecting how central truthful disclosure is to the entire system covered in the last two lessons: companies and individuals can face regulatory penalties, civil lawsuits from harmed investors, and in serious cases, criminal liability for fraud. For an investor, understanding that disclosure documents carry real legal weight — they aren't just marketing material — should shape how those documents are read: as a genuine, legally significant source of information worth reading carefully, not skipping past to get to the exciting parts of an investment pitch.

## Worked Example

**Question:** An investor skims past a company's disclosed risk factors section, focusing only on the exciting growth projections in an investment prospectus. What does this lesson suggest about that approach?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, that risk factors section carries real legal weight and is specifically meant to inform the investor's decision — skipping it means missing information the company was legally required to disclose.

**Guide:** Why does the legal seriousness of false disclosure make this section particularly worth reading carefully?

**Student:** Because companies face real legal consequences for misleading disclosure, the disclosed risks are likely to reflect genuine, material concerns the company is legally obligated to be honest about, not just boilerplate to skip past.

**Guide:** Exactly — disclosure documents deserve to be read as legally significant information, not marketing filler.

**2.** The seriousness of these consequences underscores how central truthful disclosure is to the entire regulatory system.

## Key Takeaways

- Required disclosures generally include financial statements, material risks, and material facts.
- False or misleading disclosure carries serious legal consequences, including potential fraud liability.
- This legal weight means disclosure documents deserve careful, serious reading, not skimming.
- This builds directly on the earlier lesson explaining why securities disclosure requirements exist.
