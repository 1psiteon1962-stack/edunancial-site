# Fiduciary Duty: What It Means When Someone Manages Your Money

## Learning Objectives

- Define fiduciary duty.
- Distinguish a fiduciary standard from a lesser suitability standard.
- Explain why this distinction matters when choosing who to trust with money.

## Core Content

A fiduciary duty is a legal obligation to act in someone else's best interest, above one's own, when managing their money or making decisions on their behalf — one of the highest standards of care and loyalty recognized in law. Certain professionals, in certain roles, are legally bound by this duty; others are held to a lower standard, and understanding the difference matters practically for anyone across RED, WHITE, or BLUE who relies on a professional to manage money or give financial guidance.

The key practical distinction is between a fiduciary standard (legally required to act in the client's best interest, even above the advisor's own financial interest) and a lower suitability standard (only required to recommend something reasonably suitable for the client, which can still allow a recommendation that benefits the advisor, like a higher commission product, as long as it's not clearly unsuitable). This isn't a minor technicality — it can meaningfully affect the quality and objectivity of advice received. A practical piece of legal literacy: asking directly whether an advisor is legally bound by a fiduciary duty, rather than assuming all financial professionals operate under the same standard.

## Worked Example

**Question:** An investor assumes that every financial advisor they might work with automatically has a legal duty to act purely in the investor's best interest. What does this lesson suggest about that assumption?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, that's not necessarily accurate — some professionals are held to a fiduciary standard, but others are only held to a lower suitability standard, which is a real, meaningful difference.

**Guide:** What practical question should this prompt the investor to ask before working with any advisor?

**Student:** Directly asking whether that advisor is legally bound by a fiduciary duty, rather than assuming it, since the answer meaningfully affects the objectivity of the advice they might receive.

**Guide:** Exactly — this is a concrete, actionable piece of legal literacy, not just an abstract legal concept.

## Answers and Explanations

**1.** The fiduciary standard is specifically defined by prioritizing the client's interest above the fiduciary's own.

**2.** The suitability standard permits a wider range of recommendations, including some that benefit the advisor.

**3.** This distinction directly shapes whose interests actually drive the advice being given.

**4.** This concrete question is presented as genuinely actionable legal literacy for the learner to apply.

## Key Takeaways

- A fiduciary duty requires acting in someone else's best interest, above one's own.
- A lower suitability standard allows recommendations that are merely reasonable, not necessarily optimal for the client.
- This distinction meaningfully affects the objectivity of financial advice received.
- Directly asking about an advisor's legal standard is a concrete, practical piece of legal literacy.
