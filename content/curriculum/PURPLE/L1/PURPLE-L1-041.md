# Liability: Understanding What You Could Legally Owe

## Learning Objectives

- Define legal liability in plain terms.
- Recognize the different sources of liability covered throughout this track.
- Reinforce liability as the unifying concept behind most of PURPLE's content.

## Core Content

Liability, in the legal sense, refers to being legally responsible for something — most commonly, owing money or another remedy as a result of a legal obligation or wrongdoing. This lesson steps back to name explicitly what's actually been the connecting thread running through much of this track: contract liability (owing damages for breach, covered early in this track), tort liability (owing damages for harm caused outside any contract), and business structure liability (whether personal assets are exposed to business obligations) are all, fundamentally, different sources of the same underlying concept.

Recognizing liability as this unifying thread helps organize everything covered so far into a coherent picture rather than a list of disconnected topics: contracts create liability if broken; torts create liability when harm is caused; business structure determines whose assets are actually exposed to that liability; and insurance and asset protection, covered in the surrounding lessons, are tools specifically aimed at managing liability exposure once it's understood as a real, ever-present risk across RED, WHITE, and BLUE activities alike. Every specific legal topic in this track, in some sense, is really a variation on this same core question: under what circumstances could you become legally responsible for something, and what determines the extent of that responsibility?

## Worked Example

**Question:** Looking back across this entire track, how would you explain to someone that contracts, torts, and business structure are actually all connected topics, not separate ones?

**Student:** Based on this lesson, they're all different sources of the same underlying concept — legal liability — being legally responsible for something, whether it comes from a broken contract, harm caused outside a contract, or exposure tied to business structure.

## Key Takeaways

- Legal liability means being legally responsible for something, typically owing money or another remedy.
- Contract liability, tort liability, and business structure liability are all variations on this same underlying concept.
- This unifying view organizes the whole track's content around one coherent core question.
- Insurance and asset protection, covered around this lesson, are tools for managing liability exposure.
