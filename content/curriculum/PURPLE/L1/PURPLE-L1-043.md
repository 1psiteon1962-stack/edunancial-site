# Trusts: Legal Tools for Holding and Protecting Assets

## Learning Objectives

- Define a trust in general terms.
- Explain the general roles involved (settlor, trustee, beneficiary).
- Connect trusts to both asset protection and succession planning.

## Core Content

A trust is a legal arrangement in which one party (the settlor, or grantor) transfers assets to be held and managed by another party (the trustee) for the benefit of a third party (the beneficiary), according to terms the settlor sets out. Trusts serve multiple legal purposes relevant to this curriculum: some trust structures offer a degree of asset protection, separating legal ownership from personal ownership in ways that can shield assets from certain future claims; others serve primarily as succession planning tools, directly connecting to the estate planning concepts covered in GOLD's Pass On section.

The specific legal effects, protections, and requirements of different trust types vary enormously by jurisdiction and by the specific type of trust used — this is genuinely complex legal territory, more so than most other topics in this track, and represents a clear case for professional guidance rather than DIY implementation. The practical takeaway at this literacy level is recognizing what a trust is structurally (a three-party arrangement separating legal and beneficial ownership) and that it's a real, legitimate tool worth discussing with a professional for both asset protection and succession planning purposes, rather than a mysterious or exclusively wealthy-person concept.
