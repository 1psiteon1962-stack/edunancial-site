## PURPLE-L2-002
### Contracts vs. Agreements: What Makes a Contract Enforceable

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish a legally enforceable contract from a general agreement or understanding.
- Identify the general elements required for contract enforceability.
- Recognize this as a jurisdiction-specific area requiring professional guidance for actual disputes.

**2. Core Content**
Not every agreement between people rises to the level of a legally enforceable contract. A general agreement or understanding may reflect a genuine intention between parties, but without certain elements, a court may not enforce it if a dispute arises. Generally, an enforceable contract requires an offer, acceptance, and consideration (something of value exchanged, explored further in Lesson 3), along with the parties' mutual intent to be legally bound and their legal capacity to enter into the agreement (such as being of legal age and sound mind).

This distinction matters practically: a handshake agreement to "help out with a project sometime" likely lacks the specificity and mutual intent to be legally binding, while a detailed written agreement specifying scope, payment, and deadlines, signed by both parties, is far more likely to be enforceable if a dispute later arises. Specific enforceability rules vary by jurisdiction and by contract type, making this an area where general education establishes the concept, while an actual dispute requires a qualified attorney.

**3. Worked Example**
Two neighbors verbally agree that one will help the other move furniture "sometime next month" in exchange for "a nice dinner." When the helper is unavailable and the other neighbor is upset, no enforceable contract likely exists here — the agreement lacks the specificity, consideration clarity, and mutual intent to be legally bound that a court would look for. This contrasts sharply with a written contract specifying an exact moving date, exact compensation, and both parties' signatures, which would carry far more weight as an actual enforceable obligation if a dispute arose.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Enforceable contracts generally require offer, acceptance, consideration, and mutual intent to be legally bound.
2. Vague agreements often lack the specificity and clear mutual intent courts look for in enforcement.
3. Not every agreement rises to the level of an enforceable contract; specific elements must generally be present.
4. The neighbors' vague, informal terms lacked the specificity and clear intent needed for enforceability.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Not every agreement is a legally enforceable contract; specific elements are generally required.
- These elements include offer, acceptance, consideration, and mutual intent to be legally bound.
- Vague, informal agreements often lack the specificity courts look for in enforcement.
- Enforceability rules vary by jurisdiction and contract type, requiring professional guidance for actual disputes.
