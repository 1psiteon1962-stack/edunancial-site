## PURPLE-L2-005
### Reading a Contract: Key Clauses to Look For

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify common contract clauses worth specific attention.
- Explain the general purpose of each clause type.
- Apply this checklist to a hypothetical contract review.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 1's multi-piece framing, this lesson provides a practical checklist for reading a contract critically. Key clauses commonly worth specific attention include: the scope of work or obligations (exactly what each party must do); payment terms (amount, timing, and consequences for late payment); term and termination (how long the contract lasts and how either party can end it); liability and indemnification clauses (addressed further in Lessons 26–27); dispute resolution provisions (arbitration versus litigation, Lesson 28); and choice of law/venue clauses (Lesson 46).

Reading a contract critically does not require becoming an attorney, but it does require resisting the temptation to skim past dense-looking sections, since the sections that look most tedious (indemnification, dispute resolution, termination) are often exactly where the most consequential terms are hiding.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing a vendor services contract before signing, a business owner works through this checklist deliberately: confirming the scope of work matches her actual expectations, noting the payment terms and any late fees, checking how either party can terminate the agreement and with how much notice, and specifically reading the liability and dispute resolution sections rather than skipping past them as "boilerplate." This structured review reveals a termination clause requiring 90 days' notice — far longer than she had assumed — prompting her to renegotiate that specific term before signing.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Term and termination provisions are a key clause type worth specific attention when reviewing a contract.
2. Dense-looking sections often contain the most consequential terms, making them worth extra attention, not less.
3. A structured, careful reading approach does not require becoming an attorney, though attorney review remains valuable for significant contracts.
4. The business owner's careful review revealed an unexpectedly long termination notice requirement.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Key contract clauses worth specific attention include scope, payment terms, term/termination, liability, and dispute resolution.
- Dense-looking sections often hide the most consequential terms and deserve careful attention, not skimming.
- A structured review checklist supports more informed contract review without requiring legal training.
- Careful reading can surface unexpected terms worth renegotiating before signing.
