## PURPLE-L2-006
### Breach of Contract: What Happens When Someone Doesn't Perform

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define breach of contract at a conceptual level.
- Distinguish material breach from minor breach.
- Explain why the type of breach affects available responses.

**2. Core Content**
A breach of contract occurs when a party fails to perform an obligation specified in an enforceable contract, without a legally recognized excuse. Not all breaches are treated identically: a material breach is significant enough that it undermines the core purpose of the contract, generally allowing the non-breaching party to treat the contract as terminated and pursue remedies (Lesson 7). A minor breach (sometimes called a partial breach) is a less significant failure that does not undermine the contract's core purpose, generally still requiring the non-breaching party to perform their own obligations, while allowing them to seek compensation for the specific harm the minor breach caused.

Determining whether a specific breach is material or minor depends on the facts of the situation and the contract's own terms, making this a fact-specific and often contract-specific determination, not a bright-line rule appliable identically to every situation.

**3. Worked Example**
A contractor is hired to renovate a kitchen by a specific deadline, with a wall color specified as "soft white." The contractor finishes on time but paints the wall a very slightly different shade of white than specified — likely a minor breach, since the core purpose of the renovation (a functional, renovated kitchen) was still achieved, though the homeowner might reasonably seek a small compensation or a repaint for the color discrepancy. If, instead, the contractor failed to complete the renovation at all, missing the deadline by months with no walls painted, floors installed, or fixtures connected, this would likely constitute a material breach, undermining the contract's core purpose and potentially allowing the homeowner to terminate the contract and seek more substantial remedies.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a breach of contract?
   a) Successfully completing a contract's terms
   b) A failure to perform a contractual obligation without a legally recognized excuse
   c) A type of consideration
   d) A government filing requirement
2. What generally distinguishes a material breach from a minor breach?
   a) There is no distinction
   b) A material breach undermines the contract's core purpose; a minor breach does not
   c) Material breaches are always intentional; minor breaches are always accidental
   d) Only material breaches involve any consequence at all
3. True or False: Every breach of contract, no matter how small, allows the non-breaching party to terminate the entire contract.
4. Why was the slightly wrong wall color likely a minor breach rather than a material breach?
   a) The renovation's core purpose was still substantially achieved
   b) No renovation occurred at all
   c) The contractor never showed up
   d) The homeowner had no remedy available at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. a

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A breach of contract is a failure to perform a contractual obligation without a legally recognized excuse.
2. A material breach undermines the contract's core purpose; a minor breach does not, though it may still cause some harm.
3. Only a material breach generally allows termination of the entire contract; a minor breach generally requires continued performance alongside compensation for the specific harm.
4. The slightly wrong wall color did not undermine the renovation's core, functional purpose, making it more consistent with a minor breach.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A breach of contract is an unexcused failure to perform a contractual obligation.
- A material breach undermines the contract's core purpose; a minor breach does not.
- Material breaches generally allow contract termination and broader remedies; minor breaches generally allow compensation while performance continues.
- Determining breach severity is fact-specific, not governed by a single bright-line rule.
