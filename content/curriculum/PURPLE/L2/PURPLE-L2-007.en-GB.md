## PURPLE-L2-007
### Remedies for Breach: Damages, Specific Performance, and Rescission

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify common legal remedies available for breach of contract.
- Distinguish damages, specific performance, and rescission at a conceptual level.
- Explain why the appropriate remedy depends on the specific situation.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 6, this lesson introduces the general remedies available when a breach occurs. Damages — monetary compensation intended to put the non-breaching party in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed — are the most common remedy. Specific performance is a court order requiring the breaching party to actually perform the contractual obligation, generally reserved for situations where monetary damages would not adequately address the harm, such as a contract for a unique piece of real estate or a one-of-a-kind item, where no amount of money can truly substitute for the specific thing promised. Rescission cancels the contract entirely, generally returning both parties as closely as possible to their position before the contract was formed, often used when a contract was formed based on fraud, misrepresentation, or a significant mistake.

The appropriate remedy depends heavily on the specific facts, the type of contract, and the nature of the harm — courts generally favour damages as the default remedy, reserving specific performance and rescission for situations where damages alone would not adequately address the harm.

**3. Worked Example**
A buyer contracts to purchase a specific, unique parcel of land with a rare geological feature, but the seller later breaches by refusing to convey the property. Because this specific land cannot be readily replaced with any amount of money, a court might order specific performance, requiring the seller to actually convey the property as originally agreed, rather than awarding only monetary damages that could not truly substitute for this unique asset. This contrasts with a more typical breach involving readily available, replaceable goods, where monetary damages (covering the buyer's cost of purchasing a substitute elsewhere) would generally be the appropriate and sufficient remedy.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Damages, monetary compensation for the breach, are the most common contract remedy.
2. Specific performance is generally reserved for situations involving unique subject matter where damages would not adequately address the harm.
3. Courts generally favour damages as the default remedy, reserving specific performance for special circumstances.
4. The land's uniqueness meant no amount of money could truly substitute for it, unlike readily replaceable goods.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Damages, specific performance, and rescission are the primary remedies available for breach of contract.
- Damages provide monetary compensation and are the most common, default remedy.
- Specific performance orders actual performance, generally reserved for unique subject matter.
- Rescission cancels the contract entirely, often used in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or significant mistake.
