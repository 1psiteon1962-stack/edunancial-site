## PURPLE-L2-009
### Non-Compete Agreements: Purpose, Limits, and Enforceability

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of a non-compete agreement.
- Identify common factors courts consider in evaluating enforceability.
- Recognise this as a strongly jurisdiction-specific area with widely varying rules.

**2. Core Content**
A non-compete agreement generally restricts a party (often a former employee or a business seller) from engaging in competing business activities within a specified geographic area and time period after a relationship ends. The general purpose is protecting legitimate business interests, such as trade secrets (Lesson 24), client relationships, or specialised training investments, from being immediately used to directly compete against the same business that provided them.

Enforceability of non-competes varies dramatically by jurisdiction — some jurisdictions enforce them fairly broadly if reasonable in scope, while others have significantly restricted or even banned their use for employees, particularly lower-wage workers, reflecting differing policy views about balancing business protection against worker mobility. Courts that do enforce non-competes commonly evaluate whether the geographic scope, time duration, and restricted activities are reasonable and no broader than necessary to protect the legitimate business interest at stake — an overly broad non-compete (unlimited duration, an entire country's geographic scope, for a role with no genuine trade secret exposure) is more likely to be found unenforceable, even in jurisdictions generally willing to enforce reasonable ones.

**3. Worked Example**
A regional bakery asks a newly hired counter employee to sign a non-compete restricting her from working at any bakery anywhere in the country for five years after leaving. Given her limited role with no exposure to genuine trade secrets or specialised proprietary training, and the extremely broad geographic and time scope relative to any legitimate business interest at stake, this non-compete would likely be found unenforceable in many jurisdictions, even those generally willing to enforce more reasonably scoped agreements — illustrating why the same instrument (a non-compete) can be enforceable in one context and completely unenforceable in another, depending on its actual scope and the interest it purports to protect.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general purpose of a non-compete agreement?
   a) To guarantee lifetime employment
   b) To protect legitimate business interests like trade secrets or client relationships from immediate competition
   c) To eliminate all competition in an industry permanently
   d) A type of tax form
2. What do courts commonly evaluate when assessing non-compete enforceability?
   a) Nothing — all non-competes are automatically enforceable
   b) Whether the geographic scope, time duration, and restricted activities are reasonable relative to the legitimate interest
   c) Only the employee's personal preference
   d) Only the size of the company
3. True or False: Non-compete enforceability rules are identical in every jurisdiction.
4. Why was the bakery counter employee's non-compete likely unenforceable?
   a) It was too narrow in scope
   b) Its broad geographic scope, long duration, and lack of connection to any genuine protectable interest made it unreasonable
   c) Non-competes are illegal everywhere
   d) The employee had signed a mutual NDA instead

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Non-competes generally aim to protect legitimate business interests from immediate post-relationship competition.
2. Courts commonly assess reasonableness of geographic scope, duration, and restricted activities relative to the protectable interest.
3. Non-compete enforceability varies dramatically by jurisdiction, with some banning or heavily restricting their use.
4. The bakery non-compete's excessive scope, relative to the limited role and lack of genuine protectable interest, made it likely unenforceable.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Non-compete agreements generally aim to protect legitimate business interests from immediate post-relationship competition.
- Enforceability varies dramatically by jurisdiction, reflecting different policy views on business protection versus worker mobility.
- Courts commonly assess whether the scope, duration, and restrictions are reasonable relative to the actual interest at stake.
- An overly broad non-compete is more likely to be found unenforceable, even where such agreements are generally permitted.
