## PURPLE-L2-010
### Non-Competes vs. Non-Solicitation vs. Non-Disparagement: A Comparison

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish non-compete, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement clauses.
- Explain why non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses are often more readily enforceable than non-competes.
- Apply this comparison to a hypothetical departing-employee scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 9, this lesson compares three related but distinct restrictive covenants. A non-compete (Lesson 9) restricts working in a competing business or industry. A non-solicitation clause restricts a departing party from soliciting the former employer's clients or employees to leave with them, without necessarily restricting them from working in the same industry at all. A non-disparagement clause restricts a party from making negative or damaging public statements about the other party.

Because non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses are generally narrower in scope — restricting specific harmful actions rather than an entire category of employment or business activity — they are often more readily enforceable across a broader range of jurisdictions than non-competes, even in jurisdictions that heavily restrict or ban non-competes generally. This is why many employment agreements include some combination of all three, or rely more heavily on non-solicitation and non-disparagement specifically in jurisdictions where non-competes face significant restrictions.

**3. Worked Example**
Leaving her position at a marketing agency in a jurisdiction that significantly restricts non-competes, Farida is not barred from working at a competing agency (a broad non-compete restriction would likely be unenforceable there), but she remains bound by a non-solicitation clause preventing her from actively recruiting her former colleagues to join her new employer, and a non-disparagement clause preventing her from publicly criticising her former employer. This combination allows her former employer some meaningful protection even without an enforceable non-compete, since the narrower restrictions address specific harmful actions rather than her broader ability to work in the industry.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a non-solicitation clause generally restrict?
   a) Working in any competing business
   b) Soliciting the former employer's clients or employees to leave
   c) Making any public statements at all
   d) Nothing of legal significance
2. Why are non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses often more readily enforceable than non-competes?
   a) They are broader in scope
   b) They are generally narrower, restricting specific harmful actions rather than an entire category of activity
   c) They have no legal basis at all
   d) They only apply to non-competes
3. True or False: Non-compete, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement clauses are identical in scope and enforceability.
4. Why could Farida's former employer still gain some protection despite the unenforceable non-compete in her jurisdiction?
   a) No protection was available at all
   b) The narrower non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses remained enforceable
   c) She was legally barred from working in marketing entirely
   d) Non-solicitation clauses do not exist

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Non-solicitation clauses generally restrict soliciting a former employer's clients or employees, not broader competitive employment.
2. Their narrower scope, targeting specific harmful actions, makes non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses more readily enforceable across jurisdictions.
3. These three restrictive covenants differ meaningfully in scope and, consequently, in their general enforceability.
4. The narrower, more readily enforceable non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses provided some protection despite the unenforceable non-compete.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Non-competes restrict competitive employment broadly; non-solicitation restricts recruiting clients/employees; non-disparagement restricts negative public statements.
- Non-solicitation and non-disparagement clauses are generally narrower and often more readily enforceable than non-competes.
- Many agreements combine all three, relying more heavily on the narrower restrictions where non-competes face legal limits.
- Comparing these related instruments clarifies which protections remain available even where broader restrictions are unenforceable.
