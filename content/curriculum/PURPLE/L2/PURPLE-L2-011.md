## PURPLE-L2-011
### Wills: The Basic Legal Instrument for Passing Property

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a will at a conceptual level.
- Identify general components typically included in a will.
- Recognize will requirements as jurisdiction-specific.

**2. Core Content**
A will is a legal document specifying how a person's property should be distributed after death, and often naming an executor (the person responsible for carrying out the will's instructions) and, where relevant, guardians for minor children. Building on RED-L2-040's and WHITE-L2-046's introduction of estate planning concepts, this lesson examines the will itself as a foundational legal instrument.

General components typically included in a will are: identification of the person making the will (the "testator"), instructions for distributing specific property or the overall estate, naming of an executor, and, where applicable, guardianship designations for minor children. For a will to be valid, it generally must meet specific jurisdiction-specific formalities — such as being in writing, signed by the testator, and witnessed by a specified number of people not benefiting from the will — and failing to meet these formalities can render an otherwise clear, well-intentioned will legally invalid.

**3. Worked Example**
Drafting his first will with an attorney, a father specifies how his property should be divided among his children, names his sister as executor to handle the process, and designates his brother and sister-in-law as guardians for his minor children in the event both he and his spouse are unable to care for them. His attorney ensures the will meets his state's specific signing and witnessing requirements, since a will that seemed complete in its content but failed to meet these formalities could be challenged or invalidated entirely, regardless of how clearly it expressed his actual wishes.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A will is a legal document specifying how a person's property should be distributed after death.
2. An executor is the person responsible for carrying out a will's instructions.
3. Will formalities vary by jurisdiction and must be verified for a specific will to be valid.
4. Failing to meet specific formalities can invalidate an otherwise clear and well-intentioned will.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A will is a foundational legal instrument specifying property distribution and often naming an executor and guardians.
- General components include the testator's identification, distribution instructions, executor naming, and guardianship designations.
- Wills must generally meet specific jurisdiction-specific formalities to be valid, such as signing and witnessing requirements.
- Failing to meet these formalities can invalidate an otherwise clear, well-intentioned will.
