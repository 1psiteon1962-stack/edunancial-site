## PURPLE-L2-012
### Trusts: How They Differ From a Will

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a trust at a conceptual level.
- Explain the general roles of settlor, trustee, and beneficiary.
- Distinguish a trust's basic function from a will's.

**2. Core Content**
A trust is a legal arrangement in which one party (the "settlor" or "grantor") transfers property to be held and managed by another party (the "trustee") for the benefit of designated beneficiaries, according to the terms the settlor establishes. Unlike a will, which generally only takes effect and directs distribution after death, a trust can take effect during the settlor's lifetime, actively managing and distributing property according to its terms while the settlor is still alive, in addition to (or instead of) directing distribution after death.

This creates a meaningful functional difference from a will: a trust can provide ongoing management of property — useful, for example, if a beneficiary is a minor, has special needs, or the settlor wants distributions to occur gradually over time rather than all at once — while a will generally provides a one-time distribution instruction that takes effect only upon death, without this kind of ongoing management capability.

**3. Worked Example**
Rather than simply leaving a lump sum to her young grandchildren through a will, which would generally transfer directly to them (or a court-appointed guardian) upon her death, a grandmother establishes a trust naming a trustee to manage the funds and make distributions gradually over time — for education expenses now, and larger distributions once the grandchildren reach specified ages. This ongoing management structure, unavailable through a simple will alone, reflects her specific goal of guiding how and when the funds are actually used, rather than providing an immediate, one-time lump sum distribution.

   a) They are functionally identical
   b) A trust can provide ongoing management and distribution over time, while a will generally provides a one-time distribution after death

   d) Trusts have no legal function at all
3. True or False: A will and a trust serve identical functions with no meaningful differences.

   a) She wanted an immediate, one-time lump sum distribution
   b) She wanted ongoing management and gradual distributions over time, which a trust could provide
   c) Trusts are always simpler than wills
   d) She had no specific goals for how the funds would be used

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A trust is a legal arrangement where a trustee holds and manages property for beneficiaries according to the settlor's terms.
2. A trust can provide ongoing management and distribution over time, unlike a will's generally one-time, post-death distribution.
3. Wills and trusts serve meaningfully different functions, despite both being estate planning tools.
4. The grandmother's goal of gradual, guided distributions over time was better served by a trust's ongoing management capability.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A trust involves a trustee holding and managing property for beneficiaries according to the settlor's terms.
- Unlike a will, a trust can take effect during the settlor's lifetime and provide ongoing management.
- This makes trusts useful for gradual distributions, minor beneficiaries, or special needs situations.
- A will generally provides a one-time distribution instruction taking effect only after death.
