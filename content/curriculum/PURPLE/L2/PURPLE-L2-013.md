## PURPLE-L2-013
### Revocable vs. Irrevocable Trusts: A Comparison

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish revocable trusts from irrevocable trusts.
- Explain general tradeoffs between flexibility and asset protection for each type.
- Recognize this as an individualized decision requiring professional guidance.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 12, this lesson compares two general trust categories. A revocable trust (sometimes called a "living trust") generally allows the settlor to modify, amend, or entirely revoke the trust during their lifetime, retaining significant flexibility and control, but generally providing more limited asset protection or tax benefits, since the settlor's continued control means the assets are often still treated as belonging to the settlor for certain legal and tax purposes. An irrevocable trust generally cannot be easily modified or revoked once established, meaning the settlor gives up significant control, but this reduced control can, depending on the specific structure and jurisdiction, provide stronger asset protection or tax benefits, since the assets are more clearly treated as separate from the settlor once transferred.

This represents a genuine tradeoff between flexibility and protection, rather than one type being universally superior — the right choice depends on the settlor's specific goals, risk tolerance regarding giving up control, and the specific protections or benefits sought, all of which require guidance from a qualified estate planning attorney given the significant, sometimes difficult-to-reverse consequences of this choice.

**3. Worked Example**
Comparing trust options with her estate planning attorney, Renata considers a revocable trust, which would let her retain full control and flexibility to make changes as her circumstances evolve, against an irrevocable trust, which would provide potentially stronger asset protection for a specific goal she has in mind, but would require her to give up the ability to modify or reclaim the assets once transferred. Given her priority on maintaining flexibility during a period of significant life changes, she chooses a revocable trust for now, with her attorney noting this decision could be revisited alongside her broader estate plan as her circumstances stabilize.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a revocable trust generally allow the settlor to do?
   a) Nothing — it cannot be changed once created
   b) Modify, amend, or revoke the trust during their lifetime
   c) Automatically avoid all taxes
   d) Transfer assets with no legal formalities at all
2. What is a general tradeoff of an irrevocable trust?
   a) No tradeoffs exist
   b) Reduced settlor control in exchange for potentially stronger asset protection or tax benefits
   c) Complete flexibility with no loss of control
   d) Guaranteed elimination of all estate taxes
3. True or False: One trust type is universally superior to the other for every settlor and situation.
4. Why did Renata choose a revocable trust for her current situation?
   a) She wanted to give up all control immediately
   b) She prioritized flexibility during a period of significant life changes
   c) Revocable trusts are always required by law
   d) She had no specific goals at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A revocable trust generally allows the settlor to modify, amend, or revoke it during their lifetime.
2. An irrevocable trust generally trades reduced settlor control for potentially stronger asset protection or tax benefits.
3. The right choice depends on the settlor's specific goals and circumstances, not a universal superiority of either type.
4. Renata's priority on flexibility during a period of change made a revocable trust the better fit for her current situation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Revocable trusts allow ongoing modification and control but generally offer more limited asset protection.
- Irrevocable trusts reduce settlor control but can provide stronger asset protection or tax benefits, depending on structure.
- This represents a genuine flexibility-versus-protection tradeoff, not a universal superiority of either type.
- This decision requires guidance from a qualified estate planning attorney given its significant, sometimes irreversible consequences.
