## PURPLE-L2-014
### Wills vs. Trusts: Probate, Privacy, and Control Compared

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Compare wills and trusts across probate, privacy, and control dimensions.
- Explain the general concept of probate.
- Apply this comparison to a hypothetical estate planning scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lessons 11–13, this lesson directly compares wills and trusts across three practical dimensions. Probate is the court-supervised process of validating a will and overseeing the distribution of an estate, which a will generally must go through, potentially involving court fees, delays, and — since probate proceedings are generally public record — reduced privacy regarding the estate's contents and distribution. A properly funded trust (meaning assets have actually been transferred into it, not merely mentioned in its terms) can often allow assets to pass to beneficiaries without going through probate, potentially offering faster distribution and greater privacy, since trust terms and administration generally remain private rather than becoming public court record.

Control also differs across the trust types discussed in Lesson 13: a revocable trust retains significant settlor control during their lifetime, while a will provides no ongoing control mechanism at all, taking effect only as a one-time instruction after death. This comparison does not mean trusts are universally superior to wills — many estate plans use both instruments together, with a will addressing property not transferred into the trust, and the specific right combination depending on the individual's assets, goals, and jurisdiction.

**3. Worked Example**
Comparing options with her attorney, a business owner recognises that relying solely on a will would mean her estate, including sensitive business ownership details, becomes part of the public probate record after her death, and that probate could delay her business's ownership transition during a potentially vulnerable period. She instead establishes a properly funded revocable trust to hold her business ownership interests, allowing for faster, more private transition to her successor, while still maintaining a simple "pour-over" will to address any assets not formally transferred into the trust before her death.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Probate is the court-supervised process of validating a will and overseeing estate distribution.
2. A properly funded trust generally allows assets to bypass public probate, offering greater privacy than a will alone.
3. Many estate plans use both wills and trusts together, with the right combination depending on individual circumstances.
4. The business owner's trust provided faster, more private transition for her sensitive business ownership interests, avoiding probate.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Probate is the court-supervised, generally public process a will must go through for validation and distribution.
- A properly funded trust can often bypass probate, offering faster distribution and greater privacy.
- Control also differs: a revocable trust retains settlor control during life, while a will only takes effect after death.
- Many estate plans use both instruments together, with the right combination depending on individual circumstances.
