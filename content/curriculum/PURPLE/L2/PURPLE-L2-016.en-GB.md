## PURPLE-L2-016
### Healthcare Directives and Living Wills: A Different Kind of Instrument

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a healthcare directive and a living will at a conceptual level.
- Distinguish these instruments from a financial power of attorney (Lesson 15).
- Explain why these instruments address medical, not financial, decisions.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 15's financial power of attorney discussion, this lesson introduces a related but distinct category of instrument addressing medical, rather than financial, decisions during incapacity. A healthcare power of attorney (sometimes called a healthcare proxy) designates a specific person to make medical decisions on the principal's behalf if they become unable to do so themselves. A living will (sometimes called an advance directive) specifically documents the principal's own wishes regarding certain medical treatments — such as life-sustaining measures — providing guidance for both the designated healthcare agent and medical providers, even in the absence of the agent's specific input.

These instruments serve a different function than the financial power of attorney from Lesson 15: financial POAs address bill-paying and asset management, while healthcare directives address medical treatment decisions, and a comprehensive incapacity plan generally includes both types, since they cover fundamentally different categories of decision-making.

**3. Worked Example**
As part of comprehensive estate and incapacity planning, a woman establishes both a durable financial power of attorney (Lesson 15), naming her brother to manage her finances if needed, and separately a healthcare power of attorney naming her sister to make medical decisions on her behalf, along with a living will documenting her specific wishes regarding life-sustaining treatment in certain defined circumstances. This comprehensive approach ensures both her financial and medical affairs are addressed by trusted people with clear guidance, rather than relying on a single document to address these fundamentally different categories of decisions.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a healthcare power of attorney generally designate?
   a) Someone to manage financial accounts
   b) Someone to make medical decisions on the principal's behalf if they become unable to do so
   c) A type of insurance policy
   d) A tax filing status
2. What does a living will generally document?
   a) How property should be distributed after death
   b) The principal's own wishes regarding certain medical treatments
   c) Business ownership transfer instructions
   d) A financial power of attorney's specific terms
3. True or False: A financial power of attorney and a healthcare power of attorney address identical categories of decisions.
4. Why did the woman establish both types of powers of attorney, plus a living will?
   a) One document alone would have covered every possible decision
   b) Financial and medical decisions are fundamentally different categories requiring distinct instruments
   c) She had no need for any medical decision guidance
   d) Living wills replace the need for a healthcare power of attorney entirely

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A healthcare power of attorney designates someone to make medical decisions on the principal's behalf during incapacity.
2. A living will documents the principal's own wishes regarding certain medical treatments.
3. Financial and healthcare powers of attorney address fundamentally different categories of decisions.
4. The woman's comprehensive approach addressed both financial and medical decision-making, recognising they require distinct instruments.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A healthcare power of attorney designates someone to make medical decisions during incapacity.
- A living will documents the principal's own wishes regarding specific medical treatments.
- These instruments address medical decisions, distinct from the financial power of attorney covered in Lesson 15.
- A comprehensive incapacity plan generally includes both financial and healthcare instruments together.
