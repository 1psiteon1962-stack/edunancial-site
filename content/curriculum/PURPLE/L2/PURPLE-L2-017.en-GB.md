## PURPLE-L2-017
### Beneficiary Designations vs. Wills: Which Controls?

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Reinforce the WHITE-L2-046 beneficiary designation discussion from a legal instrument perspective.
- Explain the general precedence rule between beneficiary designations and wills.
- Apply this concept to a hypothetical estate planning scenario.

**2. Core Content**
Revisiting WHITE-L2-046's discussion of beneficiary designations, this lesson examines the legal relationship between these designations and a will more directly. Certain assets — retirement accounts, life insurance policies, and some bank or brokerage accounts with a "payable on death" or "transfer on death" designation — generally pass directly to the named beneficiary upon the account holder's death, according to the designation on file with the institution, largely independent of what a will might separately state about that same asset.

This means a beneficiary designation generally takes precedence over a conflicting will provision for that specific asset — an outdated designation (naming a former spouse, for example) can override even a carefully drafted, more recent will's instructions for that specific account, precisely the gap illustrated in WHITE-L2-046's example. This is a critical, sometimes underappreciated legal reality: comprehensive estate planning requires reviewing and updating beneficiary designations directly with each institution, not merely updating a will and assuming it covers everything.

**3. Worked Example**
After completing a comprehensive new will reflecting his current wishes following a remarriage, a man assumes his estate planning is complete. During a review with his attorney, they discover his retirement account still lists his former spouse as the designated beneficiary from years earlier — a designation that would legally control distribution of that specific account regardless of what his new, carefully drafted will states. He immediately updates the beneficiary designation directly with his retirement account provider, closing a gap his new will alone could not have addressed.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What generally controls the distribution of an asset with a valid beneficiary designation, such as a retirement account?
   a) The will, regardless of any beneficiary designation
   b) The beneficiary designation on file with the institution, largely independent of the will
   c) Neither the will nor the designation has any legal effect
   d) A court always overrides both automatically
2. Why is updating beneficiary designations directly with each institution important?
   a) It is unnecessary once a will is updated
   b) A designation generally takes precedence over a conflicting will provision for that specific asset
   c) Beneficiary designations have no legal significance
   d) Wills always override designations automatically
3. True or False: Updating a will automatically updates all beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and insurance policies.
4. What gap did the man's attorney discover during his estate planning review?
   a) His will was invalid
   b) His retirement account still listed his former spouse as beneficiary, despite his new will
   c) He had no retirement account at all
   d) His new spouse was already correctly listed

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A beneficiary designation generally controls distribution of that specific asset, largely independent of the will's provisions.
2. Because designations generally take precedence over a conflicting will for that asset, direct updates with each institution are essential.
3. Updating a will does not automatically update beneficiary designations held separately with each institution.
4. The man's outdated retirement account designation would have controlled distribution despite his updated will's different intentions.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Certain assets pass according to beneficiary designations, largely independent of what a will states.
- A beneficiary designation generally takes precedence over a conflicting will provision for that specific asset.
- Updating a will does not automatically update separately held beneficiary designations.
- Comprehensive estate planning requires directly reviewing and updating designations with each relevant institution.
