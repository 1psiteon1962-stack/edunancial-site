## PURPLE-L2-031
### Statute of Limitations: Why Legal Deadlines Matter

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define statute of limitations at a conceptual level.
- Explain why these deadlines exist and their general consequence if missed.
- Recognise specific time limits as jurisdiction- and claim-type-specific.

**2. Core Content**
A statute of limitations is a law establishing a deadline within which a legal claim must be filed, after which the claim generally becomes barred entirely, regardless of its underlying merit — a claim that might have been entirely valid and winnable can become completely unenforceable simply because the filing deadline passed. These deadlines exist for several policy reasons: encouraging timely resolution of disputes while evidence and memories remain fresh, and providing eventual certainty and finality for potential defendants who might otherwise face claims indefinitely.

Specific statute of limitations periods vary significantly by jurisdiction and by the type of claim involved (contract disputes, personal injury claims, and other categories often have different specific deadlines even within the same jurisdiction), and the calculation of exactly when the clock starts running can itself be a fact-specific legal question in some situations. This makes prompt consultation with an attorney important whenever someone believes they may have a legal claim, since delaying that consultation risks the claim becoming time-barred before it is ever filed.

**3. Worked Example**
Believing she may have a valid contract dispute claim against a former business partner, a business owner initially delays consulting an attorney for over a year while attempting informal resolution on her own. When she finally consults an attorney, she learns her jurisdiction's statute of limitations for this type of contract claim was shorter than she had assumed, and her window to file may have already closed or be dangerously close to closing — a risk that prompt attorney consultation at the outset could have identified and addressed before her ability to pursue the claim was potentially lost entirely.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a statute of limitations?
   a) A law guaranteeing a claim will always succeed
   b) A law establishing a deadline within which a legal claim must be filed
   c) A type of contract clause
   d) A tax filing requirement
2. What generally happens if a claim is filed after the statute of limitations deadline has passed?
   a) Nothing — deadlines have no real effect
   b) The claim generally becomes barred entirely, regardless of its underlying merit
   c) The deadline is automatically extended indefinitely
   d) The claim is automatically strengthened
3. True or False: Statute of limitations periods are identical for every type of claim in every jurisdiction.
4. Why did the business owner's delay in consulting an attorney create risk for her potential claim?
   a) Delay never creates any risk regarding legal claims
   b) Her jurisdiction's statute of limitations deadline may have already passed or been dangerously close during her delay
   c) Statutes of limitations do not apply to contract disputes
   d) She had unlimited time to file regardless of any deadline

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A statute of limitations establishes a deadline within which a legal claim must be filed.
2. Missing this deadline generally bars the claim entirely, regardless of its underlying merit.
3. Statute of limitations periods vary significantly by jurisdiction and claim type.
4. The business owner's delay risked her claim becoming time-barred before she ever consulted an attorney to understand the applicable deadline.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A statute of limitations establishes a filing deadline, after which a claim generally becomes barred regardless of merit.
- These deadlines encourage timely resolution and provide eventual certainty for potential defendants.
- Specific periods vary significantly by jurisdiction and claim type, and calculating the start date can itself be complex.
- Prompt attorney consultation upon believing a claim may exist helps avoid the risk of a claim becoming time-barred.
