## PURPLE-L2-032
### Torts vs. Contracts: Two Different Legal Foundations

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish tort law from contract law at a conceptual level.
- Explain the general source of each type of legal obligation.
- Apply this distinction to a hypothetical scenario involving both.

**2. Core Content**
This lesson introduces tort law as a distinct legal foundation from the contract law covered throughout Lessons 1–10. A contract obligation generally arises from an agreement the parties voluntarily entered into (Lessons 2–3) — the specific duties exist because the parties agreed to them. A tort is a civil wrong causing harm to another person, where the resulting legal obligation (to compensate for the harm) arises not from any agreement between the parties, but from a general legal duty owed to others, regardless of whether any contractual relationship exists between them.

A single situation can sometimes involve both types of claims simultaneously: a contractor who breaches a renovation contract (a contract claim, Lesson 6) might also have been negligent in a way that caused property damage extending beyond the contract's scope (a tort claim, Lesson 33), and a plaintiff might potentially pursue both types of claims arising from the same underlying situation, though the specific facts and applicable law determine what claims are actually available.

**3. Worked Example**
A contractor hired to renovate a kitchen fails to complete the specified work by the agreed deadline (a contract breach, Lesson 6) and, separately, negligently causes a water leak that damages flooring in an area of the house entirely outside the renovation's contracted scope (a potential tort claim for negligence, Lesson 33). The homeowner's contract claim addresses the contractor's failure to perform the specific renovation obligations they agreed to; the potential tort claim addresses the contractor's separate, general duty to avoid negligently causing harm, regardless of what the specific renovation contract said — two distinct legal foundations potentially both arising from the same overall situation.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general source of a contract obligation?
   a) A general legal duty owed to everyone
   b) An agreement the parties voluntarily entered into
   c) A government regulation with no relationship to agreement
   d) A criminal statute
2. What is a tort?
   a) A type of enforceable contract
   b) A civil wrong causing harm, with the obligation arising from a general legal duty, not an agreement
   c) A type of intellectual property
   d) A government tax filing
3. True or False: A single situation can never involve both a contract claim and a tort claim simultaneously.
4. In the worked example, what distinguished the contract claim from the potential tort claim?
   a) They were identical in every respect
   b) The contract claim addressed the specific agreed obligations; the tort claim addressed a separate general duty to avoid negligent harm
   c) Only the tort claim had any legal basis
   d) The contractor had no contractual obligations at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A contract obligation generally arises from the parties' voluntary agreement.
2. A tort is a civil wrong causing harm, with the obligation arising from a general legal duty rather than an agreement.
3. A single situation can potentially give rise to both contract and tort claims simultaneously, depending on the specific facts.
4. The contract claim and potential tort claim addressed genuinely different legal foundations arising from the same overall situation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Contract obligations arise from voluntary agreement; tort obligations arise from a general legal duty owed to others.
- A single situation can potentially give rise to both contract and tort claims simultaneously.
- Distinguishing these foundations helps clarify what specific legal duties and remedies might apply to a given situation.
- This distinction sets up the negligence discussion in Lesson 33, examining tort law in more depth.
