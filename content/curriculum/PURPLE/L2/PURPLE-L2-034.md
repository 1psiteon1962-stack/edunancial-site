## PURPLE-L2-034
### Strict Liability vs. Negligence: A Comparison

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define strict liability at a conceptual level.
- Compare strict liability to negligence (Lesson 33).
- Identify general contexts where strict liability commonly applies.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 33's negligence framework, strict liability represents a different legal standard: liability can be imposed regardless of whether the defendant exercised reasonable care, meaning the plaintiff generally does not need to establish a breach of a duty of care (Lesson 33's second element) at all — simply engaging in the specific activity or conduct that the law treats as subject to strict liability, combined with resulting harm, can be sufficient for liability, even if the defendant took every reasonable precaution.

Strict liability commonly applies in specific, defined contexts, such as certain defective product liability claims (echoing consumer protection themes, Lesson 37) and activities considered abnormally dangerous regardless of care taken (such as certain uses of explosives). This represents a deliberate policy choice in these specific contexts: the law places the risk of harm on the party engaging in the inherently risky activity or introducing the defective product, rather than requiring the injured party to prove a specific failure of reasonable care, which can be particularly difficult to establish in these contexts.

**3. Worked Example**
A manufacturer produces a product with a genuine design defect that causes injury to a consumer using the product as intended, despite the manufacturer having followed generally reasonable manufacturing and testing procedures throughout production. Under strict product liability principles applicable in many jurisdictions, the manufacturer may still be held liable for the resulting harm, without the injured consumer needing to prove the manufacturer was negligent in a traditional sense (Lesson 33) — the mere existence of the defect and resulting harm, in this specific legal context, can be sufficient for liability, reflecting the policy choice to place this risk on the manufacturer rather than the consumer.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does strict liability generally not require the plaintiff to establish, unlike negligence?
   a) Any harm at all
   b) A breach of a duty of reasonable care
   c) The defendant's identity
   d) Any legal claim whatsoever
2. In what type of context does strict liability commonly apply?
   a) Only in ordinary contract disputes
   b) Certain defective product liability claims and abnormally dangerous activities
   c) Every possible type of legal claim, without exception
   d) Only in employment disputes
3. True or False: Strict liability requires the plaintiff to prove the defendant failed to exercise reasonable care, identical to negligence.
4. Why might the manufacturer be held liable under strict product liability, despite following reasonable procedures?
   a) Strict liability requires no showing of harm
   b) The specific legal context (product defect) does not require proving negligence in the traditional sense
   c) The manufacturer admitted to negligence
   d) No liability was actually established

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Strict liability generally does not require the plaintiff to establish a breach of a duty of reasonable care.
2. Strict liability commonly applies in contexts like certain defective product claims and abnormally dangerous activities.
3. Strict liability specifically does not require proving a breach of reasonable care, unlike negligence.
4. The specific strict liability context applicable to product defects allowed liability without requiring proof of traditional negligence.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Strict liability can impose liability regardless of whether reasonable care was exercised, unlike negligence.
- This standard commonly applies in specific contexts like defective product claims and abnormally dangerous activities.
- This reflects a deliberate policy choice to place certain risks on specific parties, rather than requiring proof of a care failure.
- Comparing strict liability to negligence clarifies these two distinct legal standards for civil liability.
