## PURPLE-L2-042
### Corporate Governance Basics: Bylaws, Boards, and Fiduciary Duty

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define corporate governance at a conceptual level.
- Identify the general roles of bylaws and a board of directors.
- Preview the fiduciary duty concept developed further in Lesson 43.

**2. Core Content**
Corporate governance refers to the structures and processes through which a corporation (or, with variations, other entity types) is directed and controlled, echoing BLUE-L2-002's introduction of the corporation as a more formal entity structure. Bylaws are internal governing documents establishing how the corporation will operate — how directors are elected, how meetings are conducted, and other procedural and structural rules — generally required for corporations and adopted at or near formation.

A board of directors generally oversees major corporate decisions and management, owing fiduciary duties (introduced here, developed in Lesson 43) to the corporation and its shareholders. This governance structure exists precisely because a corporation, as its own legal entity, requires defined mechanisms for humans to make decisions on its behalf — bylaws and board oversight provide that structure, distinct from and more formal than the governance structures typically used for sole proprietorships or simpler LLCs.

**3. Worked Example**
Forming a corporation for her growing business rather than continuing as an LLC, a founder works with her attorney to draft bylaws establishing how her board of directors will be elected, how often it will meet, and what major decisions require board approval versus what she can decide independently as an officer. This formal governance structure, required for her corporation, contrasts with the simpler operating agreement structure her LLC had used previously, reflecting the more formal governance generally expected of the corporate entity structure.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What are bylaws, in a corporate governance context?
   a) A type of tax filing
   b) Internal governing documents establishing how a corporation will operate
   c) A type of intellectual property protection
   d) A marketing document
2. What does a board of directors generally do?
   a) Nothing of significance
   b) Oversee major corporate decisions and management, owing fiduciary duties to the corporation and shareholders
   c) Handle only day-to-day customer service
   d) Replace the need for any bylaws
3. True or False: Corporate governance structures like bylaws and boards are identical to the governance structures typically used for sole proprietorships.
4. Why did the founder need to draft bylaws when forming her corporation?
   a) Bylaws are optional and irrelevant for corporations
   b) Bylaws establish the formal governance structure generally required for and expected of a corporation
   c) Her LLC's operating agreement was automatically sufficient for her new corporation
   d) She had no need for any governance structure

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Bylaws are internal governing documents establishing how a corporation operates.
2. A board of directors oversees major decisions and management, owing fiduciary duties to the corporation and shareholders.
3. Corporate governance structures are generally more formal than those used for sole proprietorships or simpler entity types.
4. Forming a corporation generally requires the more formal governance structure that bylaws establish.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Corporate governance refers to the structures and processes directing and controlling a corporation.
- Bylaws are internal governing documents establishing operational and procedural rules.
- A board of directors oversees major decisions, owing fiduciary duties to the corporation and shareholders.
- This formal structure is generally required for corporations, more formal than typical sole proprietorship or simple LLC governance.
