## PURPLE-L2-043
### Fiduciary Duty: What It Means and Who Owes It

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define fiduciary duty at a conceptual level.
- Identify common relationships where fiduciary duty arises.
- Explain general components of fiduciary duty, such as duty of care and duty of loyalty.

**2. Core Content**
Building on Lesson 42's introduction, a fiduciary duty is a heightened legal obligation requiring a party (the fiduciary) to act in the best interests of another party (the beneficiary of the duty), generally arising in relationships involving significant trust, reliance, or control over another's interests. Common relationships involving fiduciary duties include corporate directors and officers toward their corporation and shareholders (Lesson 42), trustees toward trust beneficiaries (echoing Lesson 12's trust discussion), and attorneys toward their clients.

Fiduciary duty generally includes a duty of care (acting with the competence and diligence a reasonably prudent person would exercise in a similar position) and a duty of loyalty (prioritising the beneficiary's interests over the fiduciary's own personal interests, avoiding self-dealing or conflicts of interest). This heightened standard, well beyond ordinary contractual or tort obligations, reflects the significant trust and reliance inherent in these specific relationships.

**3. Worked Example**
As a director on a corporation's board, a businessperson is offered a personal opportunity to purchase, at an attractive price, a piece of property that the corporation itself might reasonably want to acquire for its own business purposes. Recognising his fiduciary duty of loyalty to the corporation, he does not simply purchase the property for himself without first disclosing the opportunity to the board and allowing the corporation the chance to pursue it if genuinely interested — a director's fiduciary duty generally prohibits diverting a corporate opportunity for personal benefit without proper disclosure and, where applicable, corporate waiver of the opportunity.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a fiduciary duty?
   a) An ordinary contractual obligation, identical to any other contract
   b) A heightened legal obligation to act in the best interests of another party
   c) A type of intellectual property protection
   d) A government tax filing requirement
2. What does the duty of loyalty generally require?
   a) Prioritising the fiduciary's own personal interests above all else
   b) Prioritising the beneficiary's interests over the fiduciary's own personal interests, avoiding self-dealing
   c) Nothing specific at all
   d) Only applies to trustees, never to corporate directors
3. True or False: Fiduciary duty is identical to, and no different from, an ordinary contractual obligation.
4. Why did the corporate director disclose the property opportunity to the board rather than purchasing it himself?
   a) His fiduciary duty of loyalty generally prohibited diverting a corporate opportunity for personal benefit without proper disclosure
   b) He had no fiduciary duty at all
   c) The property had no value to the corporation
   d) Disclosure was purely optional with no legal significance

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. a

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A fiduciary duty is a heightened legal obligation to act in the best interests of another party.
2. The duty of loyalty requires prioritising the beneficiary's interests over the fiduciary's own personal interests.
3. Fiduciary duty represents a heightened standard well beyond ordinary contractual obligations.
4. The director's fiduciary duty of loyalty required proper disclosure before pursuing a personal opportunity that the corporation might reasonably want.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Fiduciary duty is a heightened legal obligation to act in another party's best interests, arising in relationships of significant trust.
- Common fiduciary relationships include corporate directors/officers, trustees, and attorneys toward their respective beneficiaries.
- Fiduciary duty generally includes a duty of care and a duty of loyalty.
- This heightened standard reflects the significant trust and reliance inherent in these specific relationships.
