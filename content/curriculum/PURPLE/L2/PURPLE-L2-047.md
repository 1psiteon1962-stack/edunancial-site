## PURPLE-L2-047
### Working With an Attorney: Engagement Letters and Scope

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of an attorney engagement letter.
- Identify key elements typically included in an engagement letter.
- Reinforce the BLUE-L2-033 ongoing attorney relationship discussion from the client's perspective.

**2. Core Content**
Building on BLUE-L2-033's discussion of ongoing attorney relationships, this lesson examines the engagement letter — the document formalizing the specific scope, terms, and fee structure of an attorney-client relationship for a particular matter. Key elements typically include the specific scope of representation (what the attorney will and will not handle), the fee structure (hourly, flat fee, contingency, or another arrangement), billing and payment terms, and each party's general responsibilities.

Understanding and reviewing an engagement letter carefully matters for the same reasons Lesson 5's contract-reading checklist applies broadly: a vague or overly narrow scope of representation can create confusion later about what the attorney is actually responsible for handling, and unclear fee terms can create disputes or surprises regarding billing. Clients should feel comfortable asking clarifying questions about an engagement letter before signing, just as this level has emphasized throughout for any significant contract.

**3. Worked Example**
Before hiring an attorney for a specific business matter, a business owner carefully reads the proposed engagement letter, noting that the scope of representation is defined narrowly to cover only the specific contract negotiation at hand, not any broader ongoing legal advice. Recognizing this scope limitation, she clarifies with the attorney whether a separate, related question she has about entity structure would fall within this engagement or require a separate arrangement, avoiding the surprise of later discovering a matter she assumed was covered was actually outside the engagement's defined scope.

- Clients should carefully review and ask clarifying questions about engagement letters, echoing this level's broader contract-reading discipline.
