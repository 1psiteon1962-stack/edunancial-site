# Law as the Board: Why Every Level 5 Decision Has a Legal Layer

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: When borrowing suddenly gets more expensive, a real estate investor (RED) may want to refinance, a business owner (BLUE) may want to renegotiate a loan, and an investor (WHITE) may want to sell bonds. Ask a single question across all three: what legal document decides what each person is actually allowed to do? The loan agreement, the operating agreement, and the account agreement all set the rules before any move is made.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why every financial decision at Level 5 runs through a legal structure
- Describe how PURPLE connects to the other seven colors
- Identify the legal document that controls a given financial move

## Core Content
In Levels 1 through 4, you learned PURPLE one piece at a time: what a contract is, what a trust does, how an LLC differs from a corporation, why a non-compete matters. Each piece made sense on its own. Level 5 asks a harder question: what happens when all of those pieces are on the board at the same time?

Think back to the chess analogy. In early levels you learned how each piece moves. In Level 5, you coordinate them toward a goal. Law is different from the other colors in one important way. Law is not just another piece. Law is closer to the board itself and the rules of the game. It decides which moves are allowed, which are forbidden, and what happens when two players disagree.

VERIFIED FACT: Nearly every financial relationship is created or governed by some legal instrument. A mortgage is a contract secured by real property. A share of stock is a set of legal rights. A business partnership runs on an agreement, written or not. A bank account comes with terms you agreed to when you opened it.

That means a RED decision (buy a rental), a BLUE decision (take on a partner), a GREEN decision (elect a tax treatment), and a WHITE decision (open a brokerage account inside a trust) all have a PURPLE layer underneath them. If that layer is weak, the whole move can fail even if the math was right.

Here is the Level 5 habit this track builds. Before you commit to any major decision, ask three legal questions:
1. What document governs this decision, and what does it actually say?
2. Who has the right to decide, and who has the right to say no?
3. If something goes wrong, who carries the loss, and how is a dispute settled?

REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: The answers depend heavily on where you live and where the asset sits. Contract rules, property law, entity law, and inheritance law differ between countries, and often between states or provinces inside one country. This track teaches the questions and the logic. The specific rules must always be confirmed locally.

This track does not give personalized legal advice. Instead, it trains you to walk into a lawyer's office with better questions, which is exactly what the doctor analogy from earlier levels teaches: paying a professional is insurance against a much larger loss, not a cost to dodge.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Dana owns two rental houses and a small landscaping company. Rates jump sharply. She wants to (a) refinance one house, (b) bring in a partner to buy new equipment for the business, and (c) move some savings from bonds into a stock index fund.

Dana runs the three legal questions on each move:
- Refinance (RED + BLUE): Her current mortgage has a prepayment clause. Reading it shows a penalty if she pays the loan off in the first five years. That changes the math before she even calls a new lender.
- Partner (BLUE + PURPLE): Her company is a single-member LLC. Adding a partner means rewriting the operating agreement: who votes, who gets paid first, and what happens if the partner wants out.
- Savings shift (WHITE + GREEN): The bonds sit in a retirement account. The account's legal status decides whether selling triggers taxes now.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Dana's best decision is not about any single move. It is about sequencing all three so the legal limits on each one are respected.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Law sets which moves are allowed and who carries the risk, so it sits underneath every other color's decisions.

**Q2.** You cannot make a sound move without knowing which document sets the rules.

**Q3.** Contract, property, entity, and inheritance law all vary by jurisdiction. That is why they are labeled REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q4.** The prepayment clause added a cost she had to include before comparing new loans.

**Q5.** Paying for expert advice early is usually far cheaper than fixing a legal failure later.

## Key Takeaways
- Law is the board and the rules, not just another piece
- Every RED, BLUE, GREEN, and WHITE move has a legal document underneath it
- Ask: what governs this, who decides, and who carries the loss
- Specific rules are REGION-SPECIFIC and must be confirmed locally with a professional
