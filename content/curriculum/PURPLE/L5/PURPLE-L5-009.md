# Personal Guarantees in Business Lending

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: A central bank raises rates sharply, and a small business's variable-rate loan payment jumps. Ask across colors: If the business cannot pay, can the lender reach the owner's house (RED) and savings (WHITE), even though the loan is in the company's name (BLUE)? The answer depends on what the owner signed (PURPLE).

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how a personal guarantee affects entity liability protection
- Compare unlimited, limited, and burn-off guarantees
- Negotiate guarantee terms as part of a full financing decision

## Core Content
At Level 2, you learned that an LLC or corporation can separate business debts from personal assets. At Level 5, you learn the most common way owners give that protection back: the personal guarantee.

VERIFIED FACT: Many lenders, especially for small and new businesses, require owners to personally guarantee the loan. If the business defaults, the lender can pursue the guarantor's personal assets, subject to local law.

That means the entity still protects you from many other risks, such as some customer claims or supplier disputes. But for that specific loan, the wall between business and personal assets has a door in it, and you gave the lender the key.

Types of guarantees:
- Unlimited: You guarantee the full loan plus interest and collection costs.
- Limited: You guarantee only a set dollar amount or percentage.
- Burn-off: The guarantee shrinks or ends once the business meets certain targets, such as a number of on-time payments or a debt-to-income ratio.
- Several vs. joint and several: With multiple owners, "joint and several" usually means the lender can collect the full amount from any one guarantor. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

This coordinates several colors:
- BLUE: Loan size, rate, and term.
- PURPLE: Guarantee terms, spousal consent rules, and which assets may be protected under local exemption laws (REGION-SPECIFIC RULE).
- RED: Whether the lender also wants a lien on your home.
- WHITE: Whether your savings or investment accounts are reachable.
- BLACK: How much personal risk you are willing to take for this business.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Guarantees are often negotiable, especially for businesses with a track record. A limited or burn-off guarantee may cost a slightly higher rate, and that trade can be well worth it.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Sofia's company borrows $300,000 to open a second location. The lender's first draft asks for an unlimited personal guarantee from Sofia and her business partner, joint and several, plus a lien on Sofia's home.

Sofia's counteroffer, prepared with her attorney:
- Guarantee limited to 50% of the outstanding balance.
- Guarantee burns off after 36 on-time payments if the business keeps a set debt-service coverage ratio.
- No lien on her home; instead, the lender gets a lien on the new location's equipment.
- Each partner guarantees their own share (several), not the whole amount.

The lender accepts the limit and the burn-off but insists on joint and several liability. In exchange, it adds 0.25% to the rate.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Sofia pays a bit more interest but caps her personal exposure and keeps her home out of the deal. She also signs a written agreement with her partner that each will reimburse the other for any amount paid beyond their share.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** A guarantee reopens personal exposure for that specific debt.

**Q2.** Burn-off terms reduce personal exposure as the business proves itself.

**Q3.** Each guarantor may be liable for the full debt. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q4.** She accepted 0.25% more interest in exchange for capped exposure.

## Key Takeaways
- A personal guarantee reopens personal exposure for that specific loan
- Unlimited, limited, and burn-off guarantees carry very different risk
- Joint and several liability can make one partner pay for all
- Guarantee terms are often negotiable; a small rate increase may be worth a cap
