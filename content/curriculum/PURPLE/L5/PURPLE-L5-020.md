# Marital Agreements and Business Ownership

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a mid-sized economy's currency losing a large share of its value against major reserve currencies over a few months, driven by capital fleeing the country and eroding investor confidence.

Apply it here: A currency crisis causes a family business's value to swing wildly. The owners' marriages, and their spouses' claims, suddenly affect the company's stability. Ask across colors: If an owner divorces, could part of the company pass to an ex-spouse (PURPLE)? How would the company value be set (BLUE)? Could the business be forced to sell assets to pay a settlement (RED/WHITE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how marriage and divorce can affect business ownership
- Describe what marital agreements can and cannot usually do
- Coordinate marital agreements with operating agreements and buy-sell terms

## Core Content
This lesson is about planning, not suspicion. Marriage is a legal relationship with property consequences. When one spouse owns a business, real estate, or large investments, those consequences can affect partners, employees, and family members who were never part of the marriage.

VERIFIED FACT: Many legal systems have rules for dividing property at divorce or death. Some treat most property acquired during marriage as shared. Some divide property based on fairness factors. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: The rules differ widely.

A prenuptial agreement (before marriage) or postnuptial agreement (during marriage) lets a couple decide in advance how certain property will be treated. Common uses:
- Identifying a business or property owned before the marriage as separate.
- Deciding how growth in a business's value during the marriage will be shared.
- Setting a valuation method in advance.
- Providing for a spouse in other ways, such as other assets or payments, while keeping the business intact.

Limits: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Many jurisdictions require full financial disclosure, independent lawyers for each spouse, time to review, and fairness. Some matters, like child support, often cannot be waived in advance.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Partners and investors may worry about an ex-spouse becoming a co-owner.
- PURPLE: Operating agreements and buy-sell agreements (PURPLE-L5-008) often include divorce as a trigger, allowing the company or partners to buy back any interest awarded to an ex-spouse.
- GREEN: Transfers between spouses at divorce may have special tax treatment. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- BLACK: Stability reassures employees and lenders.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The most respectful marital agreements protect both spouses. They give certainty to the business owner and fair, clear treatment to the other spouse. Written in good faith with independent advice, they can reduce conflict rather than create it.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Ines co-owns a construction company with two partners. She plans to marry Daniel, who works as a teacher.

With separate lawyers and full financial disclosure, they sign a prenuptial agreement:
- Ines's ownership stake as of the wedding date stays her separate property.
- Growth in the business's value during the marriage is shared by a set formula, paid in cash or other assets, not company shares.
- They agree to use an independent appraiser if needed.
- Daniel receives a fair share of their home and joint savings, plus a specific payment if the marriage ends.

The company's operating agreement separately says that if any partner's interest is transferred due to divorce, the company can buy it back at appraised value.

Years later, the marriage ends amicably. Daniel receives his share in cash and home equity. The company's ownership is untouched. Ines's partners and 40 employees are unaffected. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Both agreements worked together because they were written in good faith, in advance, with independent advice.

## Key Takeaways
- Marriage has property consequences that can affect business partners and employees
- Prenuptial and postnuptial agreements can define business treatment in advance
- Disclosure, independent advice, and fairness are commonly required
- Coordinate marital agreements with operating and buy-sell agreements
