# Reps, Warranties, and Indemnities in an Acquisition

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs.

Apply it here: Right after a buyer purchases a company, new tariffs hit and a customer claims the company broke a supply contract before the sale. Ask across colors: Did the seller promise (PURPLE) there were no pending disputes? Is part of the purchase price (BLUE) held back to cover that promise? How does the negotiation (ORANGE) shape who carries this risk?

## Learning Objectives
- Define representations, warranties, and indemnities
- Explain how escrows, caps, and baskets limit or secure indemnity claims
- Connect risk allocation to purchase price and financing

## Core Content
When you buy a business, you cannot see everything. The seller knows more than you do. The purchase agreement handles that gap through three tools:

- Representations: Statements of fact by the seller as of a certain date ("There are no pending lawsuits." "The financial statements are accurate.").
- Warranties: Promises that those statements are true, often used together with representations.
- Indemnities: Promises to pay the other side's losses if a representation turns out to be false or a specific risk occurs.

Common limits and protections:
- Survival period: How long after closing the buyer can make a claim.
- Basket (or deductible): Small claims below a set amount are not paid, to avoid endless minor disputes.
- Cap: The maximum the seller will pay, often a percentage of the price.
- Escrow or holdback: Part of the purchase price is held for a period to secure indemnity claims.
- Specific indemnities: Separate promises for known risks, such as a pending tax audit.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: A buyer who cannot get strong reps may lower the price or use seller financing, which gives the buyer a way to offset claims against note payments (if the agreement allows it).
- ORANGE: Everything here is negotiated. A seller with many interested buyers can resist broad indemnities. A buyer with the only offer has more leverage.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Indemnity payments may be treated as price adjustments for tax purposes.
- PURPLE: Due diligence (PURPLE-L5-022) and reps work together. Diligence finds what it can. Reps cover what it cannot.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: A buyer should not treat reps as a substitute for diligence. A seller who has already spent the sale money can be hard to collect from. Escrows and holdbacks turn a promise into cash.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Maya buys a distribution company for $1.5 million.

Key terms:
- Seller represents no pending or threatened customer disputes.
- Indemnity cap: 20% of price ($300,000). Basket: $15,000.
- Escrow: $150,000 held for 18 months.
- Survival: 18 months for general reps; longer for tax reps.
- Maya also pays $300,000 of the price through a seller note, and the agreement allows her to offset valid indemnity claims against the note.

Three months after closing, a customer claims $90,000 for a pre-sale contract breach. The seller had received a warning letter before closing but did not disclose it.

Maya's claim: $90,000 exceeds the $15,000 basket. Depending on the contract's wording, she recovers either the full amount or the amount above the basket from escrow. If escrow were exhausted, she could offset against the seller note.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The escrow and offset right mattered more than the rep itself. Without them, Maya would be suing a seller who had already moved on.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Reps state facts the buyer relies on.

**Q2.** Indemnities shift the cost of hidden problems.

**Q3.** Escrow turns an indemnity promise into available cash.

**Q4.** Baskets avoid disputes over minor amounts.

**Q5.** Secured cash is more reliable than a promise.

## Key Takeaways
- Reps state facts; warranties promise them; indemnities pay for breaches
- Caps, baskets, survival periods, and escrows shape real protection
- Seller notes with offset rights add another layer
- Reps supplement due diligence; they do not replace it
