# Asset Purchase vs. Share Purchase

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: With interest rates high, a buyer has less borrowing power and wants to limit risk in an acquisition. Ask across colors: Buying only the assets (PURPLE) may leave old liabilities behind, but it may require every contract to be reassigned. Which structure leads to better tax results (GREEN)? Which is easier to finance (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the difference between asset and share purchases
- Compare how each shifts liabilities and contract obligations
- Explain why buyer and seller often prefer different structures

## Core Content
There are two basic ways to buy a business:

- Share (stock) purchase: The buyer buys the owners' shares or membership interests. The company itself stays the same, with all its assets, contracts, and liabilities, known and unknown.
- Asset purchase: The buyer's company buys selected assets (equipment, inventory, customer lists, IP, sometimes real estate) and assumes only the liabilities it agrees to take. The seller's company keeps everything else.

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: In a share purchase, contracts usually stay in place (unless they have change-of-control clauses). In an asset purchase, many contracts, licenses, and permits must be assigned, which may require the other party's consent. Some licenses cannot be transferred at all. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- PURPLE (liability): Asset purchases generally let buyers leave unknown liabilities behind, though some laws impose successor liability in certain situations, such as some tax or employment obligations. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: In many systems, buyers prefer asset purchases because they can often assign new, higher tax values to assets and depreciate them. Sellers often prefer share sales because the sale may be taxed more favorably to them. The difference is a common negotiation point.
- BLUE: Lenders may prefer asset purchases because the purchased assets can serve as clean collateral.
- ORANGE: Because buyers and sellers want different things, price often adjusts. A seller who agrees to an asset sale may ask for a higher price to offset worse tax treatment.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Neither structure is always better. The best structure is the one where the combined legal, tax, and financing results work for both sides, with the price adjusted to share the benefit fairly.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Farah wants to buy a print shop. The seller asks $900,000.

Option A: Share purchase at $900,000.
- All customer contracts and the lease stay in place automatically.
- Farah inherits unknown liabilities, protected only by reps and escrow.
- Her tax basis in the equipment stays at the seller's old, low value, so depreciation deductions are small.

Option B: Asset purchase at $900,000.
- Farah's new company buys equipment, customer lists, and the brand.
- The landlord must consent to assigning the lease; two major customers must sign new contracts.
- Old liabilities mostly stay with the seller.
- Farah can assign higher tax values to the equipment and depreciate them. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- The seller's tax bill is higher.

Negotiation: The seller agrees to an asset sale if the price rises to $960,000. Farah's CPA estimates her tax savings over several years exceed $60,000, and her lender prefers the asset structure. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Both sides end up better off than in their first positions.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** The buyer takes the company as it is, including unknown liabilities.

**Q2.** Assets and contracts must be transferred individually, often with consent.

**Q3.** A new tax basis can create larger deductions. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q4.** A price adjustment shared the buyer's tax benefit.

**Q5.** The combined legal, tax, and financing result matters.

## Key Takeaways
- Share purchases take the whole company, liabilities included
- Asset purchases pick assets and liabilities but require transfers and consents
- Buyers and sellers often prefer different structures for tax reasons
- Price adjustments can share the benefit of the best structure
