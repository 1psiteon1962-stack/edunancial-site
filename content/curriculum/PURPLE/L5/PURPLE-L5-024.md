# Seller Financing Documents: Notes, Security, and Default

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation.

Apply it here: When bank rates spike, more buyers turn to seller financing to buy businesses and properties. Ask across colors: What documents (PURPLE) make the seller's loan enforceable? What collateral (RED/BLUE) secures it? What happens if the buyer misses payments, and how does the seller report the income (GREEN)?

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the core documents in a seller-financed deal
- Explain how security and priority protect the seller
- Describe default, cure, and remedy terms that protect both parties

## Core Content
At Level 3, BLUE introduced seller financing for business acquisitions, and RED showed how it can help buy property. At Level 5, you learn the legal documents that make the arrangement enforceable and fair.

Core documents:
1. Promissory note: The buyer's written promise to repay. It sets the amount, interest rate, payment schedule, maturity date, late fees, and prepayment rights.
2. Security instrument:
   - For real estate: a mortgage or deed of trust recorded against the property. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
   - For businesses: a security agreement giving the seller a claim on business assets, often made public through a filing so other creditors know about it. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
3. Guarantee: The buyer may personally guarantee the note (see PURPLE-L5-009).
4. Subordination agreement: If a bank also lends, the bank usually requires the seller's loan to be second in line. This agreement sets that priority.

Key terms that protect both sides:
- Default definition: What counts as default? Missed payments, failure to insure, selling the collateral without consent.
- Notice and cure: How much time the buyer gets to fix a default before the seller can act.
- Remedies: What the seller can do after default, such as accelerating the loan, foreclosing, or taking back business assets. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Covenants: Promises such as keeping insurance, paying taxes, and not taking on more debt without consent.
- Offset rights: Whether the buyer can reduce payments to cover indemnity claims (PURPLE-L5-021).

GREEN connection: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Sellers may be able to report the gain over time as payments are received (installment treatment), and interest must often be at or above certain minimum rates.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The seller becomes a lender. A seller who would never lend money to a stranger without documents should not sell on credit without them either.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Walter sells his auto repair shop to Kenji for $600,000. A bank lends $360,000. Walter carries a $180,000 note. Kenji pays $60,000 down.

Documents:
- Promissory note: $180,000, 7% interest, 7-year payoff, monthly payments.
- Security agreement: Walter has a second-position claim on shop equipment and inventory, filed publicly.
- Subordination agreement: The bank's loan is paid first.
- Kenji personally guarantees the note, with the guarantee dropping to 50% after 36 on-time payments.
- Default: 15 days late after written notice, or failure to keep insurance.
- Covenant: Kenji cannot sell major equipment without Walter's consent.

In year 3, Kenji misses two payments after losing a large fleet customer. Walter sends notice. Kenji cures within the cure period by using a line of credit. They also agree in writing to a three-month interest-only period.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The documents gave Walter enforceable rights, but they also gave both men a clear process. That clarity made a practical workout possible instead of a fight.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** The note defines the loan's amount and terms.

**Q2.** Security lets the seller recover from collateral.

**Q3.** It sets priority between the bank and the seller.

**Q4.** Cure periods give a fair chance to correct problems.

**Q5.** Clarity allowed practical renegotiation.

## Key Takeaways
- Seller financing makes the seller a lender; document it like one
- Core documents: note, security instrument, guarantee, and subordination
- Default, cure, remedies, and covenants protect both sides
- Tax reporting and minimum interest rules are REGION-SPECIFIC
