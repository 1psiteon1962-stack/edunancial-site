# Landlord-Tenant Law Across Jurisdictions

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a populous nation's real estate and consumer credit markets, after years of rapid expansion, beginning to show signs of a sharp correction as defaults start rising.

Apply it here: During a housing correction, two investors each own rentals in different places. One jurisdiction allows fairly quick eviction for non-payment. The other has long procedures and rent limits. Ask across colors: How does local law (PURPLE) change each investor's cash flow risk (RED)? Their financing risk (BLUE)? Their property's resale value (WHITE comparison)?

## Learning Objectives
- Identify major areas where landlord-tenant laws differ
- Explain how legal differences affect investment returns and risk
- Include legal research in real estate due diligence

## Core Content
Landlord-tenant law is one of the most local areas of law. Two properties with identical numbers can carry very different risks because of where they sit.

Major areas of variation (all REGION-SPECIFIC RULE):
- Rent regulation: Some places limit how much or how often rent can rise. Others have no limits.
- Eviction process: The grounds allowed, notice periods, court timelines, and tenant protections vary widely.
- Security deposits: Maximum amounts, where they must be held, interest requirements, and return deadlines.
- Habitability and repairs: Minimum standards and tenants' rights when repairs are not made.
- Registration and licensing: Some places require rental registration, inspections, or licenses.
- Short-term rental rules: Many cities restrict or ban short-term rentals.
- Anti-discrimination rules: Categories protected and how rules are enforced.

How colors connect:
- RED: Legal rules directly affect vacancy costs, collection losses, and how quickly a problem tenant situation can be resolved.
- BLUE: Lenders may consider local rules when underwriting. Cash reserves should be larger where resolution takes longer.
- WHITE: When comparing real estate to paper assets, legal friction is part of real estate's risk and illiquidity.
- GREEN: Rules on deposits and repairs affect expense planning.
- PURPLE: Leases must comply with local law. Clauses that violate local law may be unenforceable.

This lesson describes differences neutrally. Some jurisdictions prioritize tenant stability. Others prioritize owner flexibility. Each approach reflects local policy choices, and investors should understand them rather than assume every place works like the one they know.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: An investor who buys in a new place without studying its landlord-tenant law is buying an unknown risk. That research belongs in due diligence alongside inspection and appraisal.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Grace compares two similar fourplexes, each priced at $800,000 with similar rents.

Property A: In a jurisdiction with no rent regulation and a relatively short eviction timeline for non-payment.
Property B: In a jurisdiction with annual rent increase limits and eviction procedures that often take several months.

Her analysis:
- Rent growth: Property A may raise rents with the market. Property B's increases are capped. Over ten years, projected income is lower for B.
- Collection risk: In B, a non-paying tenant may occupy a unit for several months. Grace increases her reserve for B.
- Tenant stability: B's rules may lead to longer average tenancies and lower turnover costs.
- Resale: Buyers of B will price in the same rules.

Grace does not conclude that one is "good" and one is "bad." ASSUMPTION/OPINION: She concludes Property B should cost less to reflect its lower flexibility, and offers accordingly. The seller of B, facing similar offers from other informed buyers, accepts a lower price.

## Key Takeaways
- Landlord-tenant law is highly local and REGION-SPECIFIC
- Rent rules, eviction timelines, deposits, and licensing affect returns
- Price legal friction into offers and reserves
- Legal research belongs in real estate due diligence
