# Intellectual Property as a Balance-Sheet Asset

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture two large economic powers escalating export controls against each other on a category of advanced technology components used across many industries.

Apply it here: Export controls cut a company off from buying a key technology component. Its only path forward is to design its own version. Ask across colors: Who will own the new design (PURPLE)—the company, the engineers, or outside contractors? Can the new design be licensed to others for income (ORANGE/BLUE)? Does it raise the company's value (WHITE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the main types of intellectual property
- Explain how businesses secure ownership of IP
- Connect IP to financing, licensing, and business value

## Core Content
Intellectual property (IP) is legal ownership of creations of the mind. For many modern businesses, IP is worth more than buildings or equipment.

Main types (details REGION-SPECIFIC RULE):
- Trademarks: Brand names, logos, and slogans that identify a business's goods or services.
- Copyrights: Original creative works such as writing, software code, videos, and designs.
- Patents: Inventions that meet legal requirements, granted for a limited time in exchange for public disclosure.
- Trade secrets: Valuable information kept confidential, such as recipes, processes, or customer data, protected as long as reasonable steps keep it secret.

Securing ownership is where many businesses fail:
- Employees: In many systems, work created by employees within their job may belong to the employer, but not always. Clear written agreements help.
- Contractors and freelancers: Often own what they create unless there is a written assignment. (See PURPLE-L5-022's example.)
- Founders: IP created before a company existed may belong to the founder personally until assigned.
- Registration: Registering trademarks and patents, where available, strengthens enforcement.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Investors and buyers check IP ownership in diligence. Unclear ownership can reduce value or kill a deal.
- ORANGE: Brands and content are marketing assets. Licensing them can create new income (PURPLE-L5-030).
- WHITE: IP-heavy companies are often valued on earnings potential more than on physical assets.
- GREEN: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: IP development costs, licensing income, and IP transfers between related companies carry tax rules.
- PURPLE: NDAs (from PURPLE-L2) protect trade secrets during discussions.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The cheapest time to secure IP ownership is when it is created. Fixing it later, when a contractor knows the company needs their signature, can be expensive.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Nadia runs a small company that makes smart garden sensors. In the scenario, she must redesign a component in-house.

Her IP steps:
- Every employee signs an agreement assigning inventions created in their job to the company.
- She hires an outside engineering contractor under a contract with a clear IP assignment clause, signed before work begins.
- She files for patent protection on the new design where it qualifies. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- She registers her brand name as a trademark in her main markets.
- The design files are kept confidential, with access limited and NDAs used for supplier discussions.

Two years later, a larger company offers to license the design for a royalty and later explores buying Nadia's company. Their diligence team finds clean IP ownership, which supports a strong valuation.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Nadia's IP work cost a modest amount upfront. It turned a crisis-driven redesign into a new income stream and a more valuable company.

**Q1. Which type of IP protects a brand name or logo?**
A) Patent
B) Copyright only
C) Trade secret
D) Trademark

**Q2. Who often owns work created by an independent contractor without a written assignment?**
A) The company automatically
B) The government
C) The contractor
D) No one

**Q3. How is a trade secret protected?**
A) By taking reasonable steps to keep it confidential
B) By registration only
C) By publishing it
D) By patenting it

**Q4. Why does clean IP ownership matter in an acquisition?**
A) It lowers taxes
B) It is required for all loans
C) Buyers check it in diligence, and unclear ownership can reduce value or kill a deal
D) It changes the company's name

**Q5. When is the cheapest time to secure IP ownership?**
A) When the IP is created
B) After a lawsuit
C) When selling the company
D) Never

## Answer Key
Q1: D
Q2: C
Q3: A
Q4: C
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Trademarks identify the source of goods or services.

**Q2.** Contractors often keep ownership unless rights are assigned in writing.

**Q3.** Trade secret protection depends on secrecy.

**Q4.** IP ownership is a core diligence item.

**Q5.** Fixing ownership later can be expensive.

## Key Takeaways
- IP can be a company's most valuable asset
- Trademarks, copyrights, patents, and trade secrets each protect different things
- Secure ownership in writing when IP is created, especially from contractors
- Clean IP supports licensing income and higher valuations
