# Restrictive Covenants in a Growing Company

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture two large economic powers escalating export controls against each other on a category of advanced technology components used across many industries.

Apply it here: A tech company facing export controls must protect its new in-house designs. A senior engineer leaves for a competitor. Ask across colors: Does her NDA (PURPLE) protect the company's secrets? Is her non-compete enforceable where she lives? How does this affect the company's value to investors (BLUE/WHITE) and its hiring strategy (BLACK)?

## Learning Objectives
- Compare non-competes, non-solicitation clauses, and NDAs
- Explain why enforceability varies across jurisdictions
- Use restrictive covenants appropriately in hiring, sales, and exits

## Core Content
At Level 2, PURPLE introduced NDAs and non-competes as foundational instruments. At Level 5, you see how they fit together with growth, fundraising, and acquisitions.

Three main tools:
- Non-disclosure agreement (NDA): Protects confidential information. Generally the most widely enforceable of the three.
- Non-solicitation: Prevents someone from recruiting employees or soliciting customers for a period after leaving.
- Non-compete: Prevents someone from working for a competitor or starting a competing business for a period and within an area.

REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Enforceability varies greatly. Some jurisdictions ban most employee non-competes. Others enforce them if they are reasonable in time, geography, and scope, and protect a legitimate business interest. Some require payment during the restricted period. Non-competes tied to the sale of a business are often treated more favorably than those in ordinary employment.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: Buyers of a business often require the seller to sign a non-compete so the seller does not open a competing business next door. This protects the goodwill the buyer paid for.
- PURPLE: Trade secret protection (PURPLE-L5-028) depends partly on having NDAs and access controls in place.
- BLACK: Overly broad restrictions can hurt recruiting and morale. Great people may avoid companies with aggressive non-competes.
- ORANGE: Customer non-solicitation clauses protect sales relationships when salespeople leave.
- WHITE: Investors assess whether key knowledge is protected.

The Level 5 principle: use the narrowest tool that protects the legitimate interest. An NDA plus a customer non-solicitation clause may protect a business better than a broad non-compete that a court might refuse to enforce.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: A company that relies mainly on non-competes to keep talent may be ignoring the better tool: treating people well enough that they want to stay.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Yusuf's company develops specialized software. It operates in two jurisdictions: one that generally does not enforce employee non-competes and one that enforces reasonable ones.

His approach:
- All employees sign NDAs and invention assignment agreements.
- Sales staff sign 12-month customer non-solicitation clauses.
- Non-competes are used only for a few senior leaders in the jurisdiction that enforces them, limited to 12 months, a narrow list of direct competitors, and paired with continued salary during the restricted period. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Access to source code is limited and logged.

When a senior engineer leaves for a competitor in the non-enforcing jurisdiction, Yusuf cannot stop her from taking the job. But his NDA, access logs, and trade secret protections allow his attorney to send a clear reminder of her obligations, and the competitor agrees to wall her off from the directly competing project.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Yusuf's layered approach protected what mattered most without relying on a single clause that might not hold up.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. Which restrictive covenant is generally the most widely enforceable?**
A) Non-compete
B) Non-solicitation
C) NDA
D) None are enforceable

**Q2. Why are non-competes in a business sale often treated more favorably?**
A) They are shorter
B) They require no signature
C) They are secret
D) They protect goodwill the buyer paid for

**Q3. What does the Level 5 principle recommend?**
A) Use the narrowest tool that protects the legitimate interest
B) Use the broadest restriction possible
C) Avoid all agreements
D) Use only verbal promises

**Q4. Why couldn't Yusuf stop the engineer from joining the competitor?**
A) She had no NDA
B) The competitor was abroad
C) Her jurisdiction generally does not enforce employee non-competes
D) She was a contractor

**Q5. What better tool for keeping talent does the lesson suggest?**
A) Treating people well enough that they want to stay
B) Longer non-competes
C) Higher penalties
D) Secret contracts

## Answer Key
Q1: C
Q2: D
Q3: A
Q4: C
Q5: A

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** NDAs protecting confidential information are widely enforced.

**Q2.** Sale-related non-competes protect purchased value.

**Q3.** Narrow tools are more likely to be enforced and less harmful to recruiting.

**Q4.** Enforceability depends on jurisdiction. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

**Q5.** Retention through good treatment often works better than restrictions.

## Key Takeaways
- NDAs, non-solicitation clauses, and non-competes protect different interests
- Non-compete enforceability is highly REGION-SPECIFIC
- Sale-related non-competes protect purchased goodwill
- Use the narrowest effective tool and treat people well
