# Crowdfunding and Honest Investor Communication

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs.

Apply it here: A consumer product startup launches a crowdfunding campaign. Midway through, new tariffs raise its manufacturing costs by 25%. Ask across colors: What did the campaign promise backers (PURPLE/ORANGE)? Must the company tell investors about this new risk? How does it affect delivery dates and financial projections (BLUE)?

## Learning Objectives
- Distinguish rewards, donation, and investment crowdfunding
- Explain why investment crowdfunding is regulated
- Describe ongoing communication duties and best practices

## Core Content
Crowdfunding means raising money from many people, usually online. Different types carry very different legal consequences.

Types:
- Donation: People give money with nothing expected in return.
- Rewards (pre-sale): Backers receive a product or perk. Legally, this often looks like a sale or pre-order, and consumer protection laws may apply. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Lending: Backers lend money and expect repayment with interest.
- Equity or investment crowdfunding: Backers receive shares, notes, or revenue interests. This is generally a securities offering (PURPLE-L5-033), often allowed through special regulated platforms with limits on raise size and investment amounts. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

How colors connect:
- ORANGE: Crowdfunding campaigns are marketing campaigns. But claims about products and returns must be truthful (PURPLE-L5-035). Exciting language that becomes misleading can create liability.
- PURPLE: Campaign terms, platform rules, and securities disclosures define what was promised.
- BLUE: Money raised must be used as described. Delivery dates and cost estimates should include realistic buffers.
- WHITE: Investment crowdfunding interests are often illiquid and high-risk, which is why investment limits exist.
- BLACK: Founders must communicate bad news promptly and honestly.

Ongoing duties: REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: Some investment crowdfunding rules require annual reports. Even where not required, regular updates build trust. When something material changes, such as a cost increase or a delivery delay, telling backers quickly and clearly is both a legal safeguard and a relationship safeguard.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Crowdfunding backers often forgive delays. What they rarely forgive is silence or spin. Honest communication is a strategy, not just an obligation.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Theo's company raises $120,000 on a rewards platform for a portable water filter, promising delivery in six months. It also raises $300,000 through a regulated investment crowdfunding platform.

In month three, tariffs raise manufacturing costs 25%.

Theo's response:
- Rewards backers: He posts a clear update explaining the tariff, the new cost, and a revised delivery date two months later. He offers refunds to anyone who prefers not to wait, as the campaign terms allowed.
- Investors: His securities attorney advises that this is a material development. He sends an investor update explaining the cost impact, revised projections, and his plan to shift part of production to a different supplier.
- Marketing: He removes a phrase from the campaign page that said "guaranteed delivery by spring."

Most rewards backers choose to wait. A few take refunds. Investors appreciate the early warning. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Theo's company still faces a hard year, but it keeps its reputation and reduces its legal risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Crowdfunding types range from donations to regulated investment offerings
- Campaigns are marketing, but claims must be truthful
- Material changes should be communicated promptly
- Honest communication protects both legal standing and relationships
