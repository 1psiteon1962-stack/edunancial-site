# Cross-Border Contracts: Choice of Law, Forum, and Arbitration

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a mid-sized economy's currency losing a large share of its value against major reserve currencies over a few months, driven by capital fleeing the country and eroding investor confidence.

Apply it here: A supplier in a country whose currency has collapsed stops delivering to a foreign buyer. Ask across colors: Which country's law governs the contract (PURPLE)? Where can the buyer sue? Would a judgment or award actually be collectible (BLUE)? Would currency swings (WHITE) change what is owed?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain choice-of-law and forum selection clauses
- Compare court litigation and arbitration for cross-border disputes
- Consider enforceability when drafting international contracts

## Core Content
When two parties in different countries sign a contract, three questions matter if things go wrong:
1. Which law applies? (Choice of law)
2. Where will disputes be decided? (Forum selection)
3. Can the result actually be enforced where the other party's assets are? (Enforceability)

Choice of law: The contract can name which jurisdiction's law governs it. Without that clause, courts apply conflict-of-law rules to decide, which can be unpredictable. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.

Forum selection: The contract can name which courts will hear disputes. Litigating in a foreign court can mean unfamiliar procedures, language barriers, local lawyers, and travel.

Arbitration: Instead of courts, parties can agree to private arbitration, often under the rules of an established arbitration institution. Features:
- Parties can choose neutral arbitrators and a neutral location.
- Proceedings are often more private than courts.
- VERIFIED FACT: International treaties allow arbitration awards to be recognized and enforced in many countries that have joined them, which can make awards easier to enforce across borders than some court judgments.
- Appeals are usually very limited.
- Costs can be significant.

How colors connect:
- BLUE: A contract right is only as valuable as your ability to collect. Knowing where the other party has assets matters.
- WHITE: Contracts should state the currency of payment and how exchange rates apply (see PURPLE-L5-039).
- ORANGE: Dispute clauses are negotiated like any other term. A party with more leverage often gets its home forum.
- PURPLE: Mediation steps before arbitration can resolve many disputes cheaply.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: For many cross-border business contracts, a neutral arbitration clause with a mediation step first offers a practical balance. But the right choice depends on the size of the contract, where assets sit, and the relationship.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Kofi's company buys specialty fabric from a supplier abroad. Their original contract had no choice-of-law or dispute clause.

After the supplier's currency collapses, the supplier stops shipping and demands a 40% price increase. Kofi has paid a $60,000 deposit.

Problems:
- Unclear which law applies.
- Suing in his own country's courts may produce a judgment the supplier's country may not enforce. REGION-SPECIFIC RULE.
- Suing in the supplier's country would require hiring local lawyers and working in another language.

Kofi eventually negotiates a partial refund and a revised price. For his next supplier contract, he includes:
- Governing law of a neutral, well-developed commercial jurisdiction.
- Mediation first, then arbitration under an established institution, in a neutral city.
- Prices stated in a stable reserve currency.
- Deposits held in escrow until shipment.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: The first contract cost Kofi time and money. The second contract cannot prevent problems, but it gives him a clear, enforceable path if they occur.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** It removes uncertainty about which law applies.

**Q2.** It sets where disputes are decided.

**Q3.** Treaty frameworks support cross-border enforcement.

**Q4.** Missing clauses created uncertainty.

**Q5.** Enforceability determines real value.

## Key Takeaways
- Cross-border contracts should state governing law and dispute forum
- Arbitration can offer neutrality and cross-border enforceability
- Know where the other party's assets are
- Add mediation first and state payment currency clearly
