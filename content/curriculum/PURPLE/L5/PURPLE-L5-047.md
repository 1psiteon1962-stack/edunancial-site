# Legal Readiness: Preparing Before the Moment Arrives

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a major energy-producing region hit by a sudden regional conflict that knocks out a large share of its production capacity overnight, tightening global energy supply with little warning.

Apply it here: A sudden energy shock creates opportunities and threats at the same time: distressed assets come up for sale, contracts are disrupted, and competitors struggle. Ask across colors: Could this business act within days (PURPLE)? Are its records clean (GREEN)? Is financing pre-arranged (BLUE)? Does leadership already know its decision criteria (BLACK)?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why readiness must be built before opportunities appear
- Build a legal readiness checklist
- Connect readiness to the ability to act decisively under the knife-fight doctrine

## Core Content
The second part of the knife-fight doctrine is readiness: building the capacity to act before you need it. You cannot build a strong position in the moment the window opens. You can only use what you already prepared.

Legal readiness has several parts:
1. Organized records: Entity documents, ownership records, contracts, and filings, all current and easy to find (PURPLE-L5-043). A buyer, lender, or partner can move quickly with you only if your documents are ready.
2. Known rights and deadlines: A list of your options, rights of first refusal, renewal dates, and contract termination windows. You cannot use a right you forgot you had.
3. Counsel relationships: An attorney who already knows your business can act in days. Finding and onboarding a new attorney in a crisis costs time you may not have.
4. Standard documents: Templates for NDAs, letters of intent, and board or member resolutions, prepared in advance and reviewed by counsel.
5. Pre-arranged financing: Credit lines, lender relationships, or reserves ready to support a legal move (BLUE).
6. Decision criteria: Written conditions under which you will act, so emotion does not drive the decision (BLACK).
7. Protected IP: Trademarks and key IP registered in planned markets before you need them (PURPLE-L5-029).

How colors connect:
- PURPLE: Documents, rights, and counsel.
- BLUE: Financing and cash reserves.
- GREEN: Clean books and tax filings that support diligence.
- BLACK: Decision criteria and authority to act.
- ORANGE: Knowing competitors and market signals.

This is the "fall back and regroup" principle in reverse. Sometimes readiness means building strength during quiet times so you can advance when the moment comes.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Readiness is boring when nothing is happening. It becomes priceless the moment something does.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Nkechi owns a regional logistics company. During calm years, she builds legal readiness:
- Keeps a digital data room with all entity, contract, and financial documents updated quarterly.
- Maintains a list of every contract renewal and termination window.
- Meets her business attorney twice a year to review strategy.
- Has counsel-approved templates for letters of intent and NDAs.
- Maintains an unused credit line and a cash reserve.
- Writes acquisition criteria: target size, location, maximum price multiple, and deal-breakers.

When the energy shock hits, a smaller competitor with good routes but weak finances decides to sell. Nkechi sends an NDA the same day, receives information within a week, and submits a letter of intent within two weeks. Her data room lets the seller's advisors verify her ability to close. Her financing is ready.

Two larger competitors are also interested, but they need weeks to organize documents and arrange financing. The seller accepts Nkechi's offer because it is credible and fast.

ASSUMPTION/OPINION: Nkechi did not win because she was bigger. She won because she was ready.

## Answer Key

Q2: C

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** The moment of opportunity is too late to build readiness.

**Q2.** Onboarding new counsel in a crisis costs time.

**Q3.** Pre-set criteria support disciplined decisions.

**Q4.** Readiness made her offer more attractive.

**Q5.** Preparation during calm enables action later.

## Key Takeaways
- Readiness must be built before the window opens
- Keep records, rights lists, counsel, templates, financing, and criteria ready
- Readiness turns opportunity into action
- Quiet times are for building strength
