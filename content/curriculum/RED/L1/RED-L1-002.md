## Learning Objectives

- Calculate gross rent, net operating income (NOI), and cash-on-cash return for a rental property
- Apply the 1% rule and 50% rule as quick screening tools
- Identify the expenses that must be accounted for in a cash flow analysis
- Distinguish between a performing and non-performing rental property

## Core Content

Cash flow analysis is the most important skill a rental property investor can develop. Buying a property without analyzing cash flow is the leading cause of real estate investing losses.

### The 1% Rule

The 1% rule is a quick screening tool. A property passes if its monthly rent equals at least 1% of the purchase price.

**Example:** A $150,000 property should generate at least $1,500/month in rent to pass the 1% test.

The 1% rule is a filter, not a guarantee. It eliminates obvious losers quickly.

### Full Cash Flow Analysis

A complete analysis accounts for all real costs:

**Gross Scheduled Income (GSI)**
Total rent if fully occupied for 12 months.

**Vacancy Allowance (5–10%)**
Even great properties have turnover. Budget for empty months.

**Operating Expenses**
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Property management (8–12% of rent)
- Maintenance and repairs (5–10% of rent)
- Utilities (if owner-paid)
- HOA fees (if applicable)
- Capital expenditure reserves (roof, HVAC, appliances)

**Net Operating Income (NOI)**
NOI = GSI − Vacancy − Operating Expenses

**Debt Service**
Monthly mortgage payment (principal + interest).

**Cash Flow**
Cash Flow = NOI − Debt Service

### The 50% Rule

The 50% rule estimates that operating expenses (not including mortgage) will average 50% of gross rent. It is a faster way to estimate NOI.

**Example:** $1,200/month rent → Estimated expenses = $600 → Estimated NOI = $600

### Cash-on-Cash Return

Cash-on-Cash Return = (Annual Cash Flow / Total Cash Invested) × 100

A 10%+ cash-on-cash return is generally considered strong. Below 6% is marginal.

### What Makes a Property Perform

A property performs when:
- Rents exceed all expenses including mortgage
- The neighborhood supports stable or rising rents
- Deferred maintenance is minimal or priced into the purchase
- The tenant pool is reliable and demand is strong

### Common Mistakes

- Forgetting capital expenditure reserves
- Underestimating property management costs
- Ignoring vacancy rate
- Buying at retail and expecting investor returns

## Knowledge Check

1. A property costs $200,000. What monthly rent is needed to pass the 1% rule?
2. If a property earns $1,400/month in rent, what does the 50% rule estimate for monthly operating expenses?
3. Calculate NOI: $18,000 annual rent, $3,000 vacancy, $6,000 operating expenses.
4. You invest $40,000 cash and generate $4,200 annually in cash flow. What is your cash-on-cash return?

## Key Terms

- **NOI (Net Operating Income)**: Gross income minus vacancy and operating expenses
- **Cash-on-Cash Return**: Annual cash flow divided by total cash invested
- **Cap Rate**: NOI divided by property value; used to compare investments
- **Vacancy Rate**: Percentage of time a unit is unoccupied
- **Capital Expenditure (CapEx)**: Large, infrequent repair or replacement costs
