## Learning Objectives

- Identify the phases of a real estate market cycle
- Analyze a local market using vacancy rates, days-on-market, and price trends
- Distinguish between landlord markets and tenant markets
- Recognize which market conditions favor buying, holding, or selling

## Core Content

Real estate markets are local, cyclical, and driven by supply and demand. National headlines rarely reflect what is happening in your target market. Successful investors learn to read their local market independently.

### The Real Estate Market Cycle

Markets move through four phases:

**1. Recovery**
Following a downturn, vacancy rates are high, rents are soft, and construction is slow. Smart investors buy during recovery when prices are depressed.

**2. Expansion**
Employment grows, demand increases, vacancy drops, and rents rise. New construction begins. This is a strong buying and holding phase.

**3. Hyper-Supply**
Construction has caught up or exceeded demand. Vacancy rises and rent growth slows. Exercise caution buying in this phase.

**4. Recession**
Oversupply, declining rents, rising vacancies. Investors who over-leveraged during expansion face distress. Buyers with cash benefit from motivated sellers.

### How to Analyze a Local Market

**Vacancy Rate**
Low vacancy (below 5%) indicates strong rental demand. High vacancy (above 8%) may signal overbuilding or economic weakness.

**Days on Market (DOM)**
Properties selling in fewer days indicate strong buyer demand. Long DOM may mean overpricing or weak demand.

**Price-to-Rent Ratio**
Divide the median home price by the annual median rent. A ratio below 15 typically favors buying; above 20 may favor renting.

**Population and Employment Trends**
Growing populations and diversified employment bases support long-term real estate value. Single-employer cities carry concentration risk.

**New Construction Permits**
Rising permits signal builders expect demand. Excessive permits may lead to future oversupply.

### Landlord vs. Tenant Markets

**Landlord Market**: Low vacancy, rising rents, multiple applicants per unit. Landlords can be selective and raise rents.

**Tenant Market**: High vacancy, flat or falling rents, concessions common. Tenants have leverage to negotiate terms.

### Buying in Any Market

A good deal is a good deal in any market. Buying at the right price — below market, with strong fundamentals — protects investors from cycles. Never overpay expecting appreciation to bail you out.

### National vs. Local Data

National real estate statistics are averages that can obscure local reality. One city can be in recession while a neighboring market is in full expansion. Always research at the ZIP code level, not the national level.

## Key Terms

- **Market Cycle**: The recurring pattern of recovery, expansion, hyper-supply, and recession in real estate
- **Vacancy Rate**: Percentage of rental units unoccupied at a given time
- **Days on Market (DOM)**: Average time a listed property takes to sell
- **Price-to-Rent Ratio**: Median home price divided by annual median rent
- **Landlord Market**: A market with low vacancy and rising rents favoring property owners
