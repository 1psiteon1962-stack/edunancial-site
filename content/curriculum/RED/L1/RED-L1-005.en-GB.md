# Principal vs. Interest: Where Your Payment Actually Goes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define principal and interest separately.
- Explain why the split between them changes over time.
- Explain why this matters for building equity.

## Core Content

### Two Parts of Every Payment

Every mortgage payment is split into two parts: principal (which reduces
the actual loan balance owed) and interest (the cost of borrowing the
money, paid to the lender). Both are part of the same monthly payment,
but they do very different jobs.

### Why the Split Changes Over Time

Early in a mortgage, a much larger share of each payment goes toward
interest, with only a small amount reducing the principal. Over time,
that ratio gradually flips — later payments put more toward principal
and less toward interest. This pattern is often called amortization.

### Why This Matters for Equity

Since equity grows partly from paying down principal, the early years of
a mortgage build equity more slowly through payments than the later
years do — even though the payment amount itself often stays the same.

## Worked Example

On a $200,000 mortgage, an early payment might be $1,200, with $1,000
going to interest and only $200 reducing principal. Years later, that
same $1,200 payment might split closer to $400 interest and $800
principal — same payment amount, very different effect on the loan
balance.

## Practice Quiz

1. Principal refers to:
   A. The cost of borrowing money
   B. The portion of a payment that reduces the loan balance
   C. A type of property tax
   D. The home's insurance premium

2. True or False: The split between principal and interest stays the
   same throughout the entire loan.

3. What is the gradual shift from mostly-interest to mostly-principal
   payments commonly called?
   A. Appreciation
   B. Amortization
   C. Depreciation
   D. Escrow

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Principal is the part of a payment that reduces the
actual loan balance.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The split shifts over time — more interest early,
more principal later.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. This gradual shift is called amortization.

## Key Takeaways

- Every mortgage payment splits into principal and interest.
- Early payments are interest-heavy; later payments are principal-heavy.
- This shift (amortization) affects how quickly equity builds from
  payments alone.
