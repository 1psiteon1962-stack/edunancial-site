## Learning Objectives

- Explain how tax liens are created and what rights a lien certificate holder has
- Identify the states that offer tax lien investing and their typical redemption periods and interest rates
- Calculate the return on a tax lien investment given a specific interest rate and holding period
- Describe the process of foreclosing on a tax lien certificate when the owner fails to redeem

## Core Content

Tax lien investing is one of the most misunderstood — and most powerful — real estate investment strategies available to the average investor. It requires no renovation, no tenants, and no property management. It is, at its core, lending money to the government.

### How Tax Liens Are Created

When a property owner fails to pay property taxes, the county government is deprived of funds it has already budgeted. To resolve this, counties sell the delinquent tax debt to investors as a tax lien certificate.

The investor pays the outstanding tax bill. The property owner now owes the investor — not the county — plus statutory interest. If the owner redeems the certificate by paying the debt within the redemption period, the investor receives their principal back plus interest. If the owner fails to redeem, the investor can foreclose and acquire the property.

### Redemption Periods and Interest Rates

These vary by state:

| State | Interest Rate | Redemption Period |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| Iowa | 2% per month (24%/yr) | Up to 2 years |
| Florida | Up to 18%/yr | 2 years |
| Illinois | Up to 36%/yr (bid down) | 2–3 years |
| Arizona | 16%/yr | 3 years |
| New Jersey | Up to 18%/yr | 2 years |

Always verify current statutory rates with the county; rates change.

### The Tax Lien Auction Process

1. County publishes a list of delinquent properties (typically 4–6 weeks before auction)
2. Investors research properties and assess risk
3. Auction occurs online or at the courthouse
4. Winning bidder pays the delinquent taxes and receives a certificate
5. Redemption period begins; investor earns interest
6. At end of redemption period, if unredeemed, investor initiates foreclosure

### Risk Management

**Due Diligence Before Bidding**
- Verify property has value (title search, drive-by inspection)
- Check for senior liens (federal tax liens, mortgages) that survive the tax lien process in some states
- Assess whether the property is worth acquiring if the owner does not redeem

**Redemption Rate**
In practice, 95–98% of tax liens are redeemed. Investors get interest. The remaining 1–5% convert to foreclosure opportunities.

### Calculating Your Return

If you purchase a $3,000 tax lien certificate in a state with 18% annual interest and the owner redeems in 18 months:

Return = $3,000 × 18% × (18/12) = $3,000 × 0.27 = $810

Total received: $3,810 on a $3,000 investment — 27% return in 18 months.

### Starting Small

Tax liens allow investors to start with as little as a few hundred dollars. This makes it ideal for building capital while learning the process before investing in physical properties.

## Key Terms

- **Tax Lien Certificate**: A legal claim on a property sold by a county to recover unpaid taxes
- **Redemption Period**: The time an owner has to repay the lien plus interest before foreclosure can proceed
- **Statutory Interest Rate**: The state-mandated rate of interest paid on redeemed tax lien certificates
- **Foreclosure**: Legal process by which a lien holder gains ownership of a property when the debt is unpaid
- **Senior Lien**: A lien with higher priority that may survive tax lien foreclosure in some states
