# What Is a Down Payment, and Why Does It Matter So Much?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a down payment in plain terms.
- Explain how the down payment size affects the loan and monthly payment.
- Explain why lenders care about down payment size beyond the math.

## Core Content

### What a Down Payment Is

A down payment is the portion of a property's purchase price paid
upfront, in cash, rather than borrowed. If a home costs $300,000 and the
buyer pays $30,000 upfront, that's a 10% down payment, with the
remaining $270,000 financed through a mortgage.

### How It Affects the Loan

A larger down payment means a smaller loan amount, which generally means
a smaller monthly payment and less interest paid over the life of the
loan. It directly changes the numbers, not just the buyer's comfort
level.

### Why Lenders Care Beyond the Math

A larger down payment also means the buyer already has real equity (and
real money) in the property from day one, which reduces the lender's
risk — a buyer with meaningful equity has more to lose by defaulting.
This is part of why smaller down payments often come with additional
costs, like PMI, covered in the next lesson.

## Worked Example

A $300,000 home with a 20% down payment ($60,000) requires financing
$240,000. The same home with a 5% down payment ($15,000) requires
financing $285,000 — a larger loan, larger monthly payment, and more
total interest paid over time.

## Practice Quiz

1. A down payment is:
   A. A monthly fee paid to the lender
   B. The portion of the purchase price paid upfront in cash
   C. A type of home insurance
   D. A tax paid at closing

2. True or False: A larger down payment generally results in a smaller
   monthly mortgage payment.

3. Why do lenders generally view a larger down payment as lower risk?
   A. It guarantees the home's value will rise
   B. The buyer has more of their own money and equity at stake
   C. It eliminates the need for a credit check
   D. It shortens the loan term automatically

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A down payment is the upfront cash portion of the
purchase price.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. A larger down payment reduces the loan amount,
which reduces the monthly payment.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. More of the buyer's own money at stake reduces the
lender's risk of default.

## Key Takeaways

- A down payment is the upfront cash portion of a home purchase.
- A larger down payment means a smaller loan, smaller payments, and less
  total interest.
- Lenders view larger down payments as lower risk because the buyer has
  more at stake.
