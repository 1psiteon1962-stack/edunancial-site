# Fixed-Rate vs. Adjustable-Rate Mortgages

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a fixed-rate mortgage and an adjustable-rate mortgage.
- Explain the trade-off between predictability and potential savings.
- Identify one risk unique to adjustable-rate mortgages.

## Core Content

### Fixed-Rate Mortgages

A fixed-rate mortgage locks in the same interest rate for the entire
loan term. The monthly principal-and-interest payment stays the same
from the first payment to the last, making budgeting predictable.

### Adjustable-Rate Mortgages (ARMs)

An adjustable-rate mortgage starts with a rate that's often lower than a
comparable fixed rate, but that rate can change periodically after an
initial fixed period, based on broader interest rate conditions. Payments
can go up or down over time.

### The Real Trade-Off

Fixed-rate loans trade a potentially higher starting rate for long-term
predictability. ARMs trade predictability for a potentially lower
starting cost — with real risk that payments could rise later. Neither
is universally better; it depends on how long someone plans to keep the
loan and their tolerance for payment uncertainty.

## Worked Example

A fixed-rate borrower pays the same amount in year 1 and year 15. An ARM
borrower might pay less in year 1 than the fixed-rate borrower, but if
rates rise, could be paying more by year 6 — the exact opposite could
also happen if rates fall.

## Practice Quiz

1. A fixed-rate mortgage:
   A. Changes rate every year automatically
   B. Keeps the same interest rate for the entire loan term
   C. Is only available to first-time buyers
   D. Always has a higher payment than an ARM

2. True or False: An adjustable-rate mortgage's payment can never
   increase after the initial period.

3. What is the main trade-off between fixed and adjustable-rate
   mortgages?
   A. Predictability vs. potentially lower starting cost with risk of
      change
   B. Legal vs. illegal loan types
   C. Government-backed vs. private loans
   D. Short-term vs. long-term property ownership

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. A

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A fixed-rate mortgage keeps the same rate for the
full loan term.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. ARM payments can rise (or fall) after the initial
fixed period, based on rate conditions.

### Question 3

Correct answer: A. The core trade-off is long-term predictability versus
a potentially lower starting cost with future rate risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Fixed-rate mortgages offer a locked, predictable rate for the full
  term.
- ARMs often start lower but can change after an initial period.
- The right choice depends on how long someone plans to keep the loan
  and their comfort with payment uncertainty.
