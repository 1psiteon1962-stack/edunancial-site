# What Is PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) and When Do You Pay It?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define PMI in plain terms.
- Explain who PMI actually protects.
- Explain when PMI is typically required, and when it can go away.

## Core Content

### What PMI Is

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is insurance that protects the lender —
not the borrower — if the borrower stops paying the loan. It's an
additional monthly cost added to the mortgage payment.

### Who It Actually Protects

This is the most misunderstood part: PMI doesn't protect the homeowner
from losing their home. It exists to reduce the lender's risk on loans
where the buyer has less equity cushion, so the lender is more willing
to approve the loan in the first place.

### When It's Required, and When It Ends

PMI is typically required on conventional loans when the down payment is
below a certain threshold (often 20%), because a smaller down payment
means less equity cushion for the lender. It can often be removed once
the borrower's equity in the home reaches a similar threshold, either
through payments or appreciation.

## Worked Example

A buyer puts down 10% instead of 20% on a home. Because their equity
cushion is smaller, the lender requires PMI as a condition of approving
the loan — an added monthly cost until enough equity builds up.

## Practice Quiz

1. PMI primarily protects:
   A. The homeowner
   B. The lender
   C. The real estate agent
   D. The local government

2. True or False: PMI is typically required when the down payment is
   below a certain threshold, often 20%.

3. Can PMI ever be removed from a loan?
   A. No, it lasts the entire loan term
   B. Yes, often once enough equity has built up
   C. Only if the borrower refinances with a different lender
   D. Only after the loan is fully paid off

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. PMI protects the lender's risk, not the homeowner.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Smaller down payments (below roughly 20% on many
conventional loans) often trigger a PMI requirement.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. PMI can often be removed once equity reaches a
sufficient threshold.

## Key Takeaways

- PMI protects the lender, not the homeowner.
- It's typically required when the down payment is below a certain
  threshold.
- PMI can often be removed once enough equity has built up.
