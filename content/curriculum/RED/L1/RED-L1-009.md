## Learning Objectives

- Describe the key responsibilities of a property manager and the cost-benefit of hiring vs. self-managing
- Apply a tenant screening process that reduces vacancy risk and legal liability
- Identify the clauses every lease should contain
- Outline the legal framework for security deposits and evictions

## Core Content

Property management is where many investors abandon their real estate strategy. Managing properties poorly eliminates profits. Managing them well — or hiring someone competent to do so — is the difference between a wealth-building asset and a liability.

### Self-Managing vs. Hiring a Property Manager

**Self-managing**:
- No management fee (saves 8–12% of gross rent)
- Requires time and availability
- Works well for investors with 1–5 units in their local market
- Risk: you become the 2 a.m. emergency call

**Professional property manager**:
- Costs 8–12% of gross rent monthly, plus leasing fee (often one month's rent)
- Provides local expertise, vendor relationships, and legal compliance knowledge
- Essential for out-of-state investing
- Good managers pay for themselves through reduced vacancy and better tenant quality

**Rule**: If you have more than 5 units, are investing out of state, or value your time above the management fee, hire a professional.

### Tenant Screening Process

A rigorous screening process is your most important tool to minimize vacancy losses and evictions.

**Standard screening criteria**:
- Income: gross monthly income ≥ 3× monthly rent
- Credit: 620+ FICO minimum (adjust for your market)
- Rental history: no prior evictions, no more than one late payment in 12 months
- Criminal background: felonies within 7 years are disqualifying (consult HUD guidance on blanket bans)
- Employment: verified employment or other stable income documentation

Apply your criteria consistently to all applicants to comply with Fair Housing laws.

### The Lease Agreement

A strong lease protects both parties. Essential clauses include:

- **Rent amount and due date**: Specify the day and grace period
- **Late fees**: State-specific limits; typically $50–75 or 5–10% of monthly rent
- **Lease term**: Month-to-month vs. annual
- **Occupancy limits**: Who may legally reside in the unit
- **Pet policy**: Allowed breeds, sizes, deposits, and pet rent
- **Maintenance responsibilities**: Who handles what (lawn, HVAC filters, etc.)
- **Entry notice**: Typically 24–48 hours required by state law
- **Lease termination**: Notice periods and early termination fees
- **Subletting restriction**: Usually prohibited without written permission

### Security Deposits

Security deposit law is highly state-specific. Know your state's rules:
- Maximum allowed deposit (typically 1–2 months' rent)
- Required interest on deposits (required in some states)
- Itemization deadline after move-out (14–30 days in most states)
- Return deadline (14–30 days after move-out)

Violating security deposit laws can expose you to treble damages and attorney fees.

### The Eviction Process

Eviction is a legal process and cannot be shortcut:

1. **Written notice**: Pay or quit, cure or quit, or unconditional quit — state law dictates form and timeline
2. **File for eviction**: If notice period expires without compliance
3. **Court hearing**: Typically 1–4 weeks after filing
4. **Judgment**: If you prevail, the tenant has a set time to vacate
5. **Writ of possession**: Sheriff enforces removal if tenant does not leave voluntarily

Self-help evictions (changing locks, removing belongings) are illegal in all states and expose landlords to significant liability.

## Key Terms

- **Property Manager**: A licensed professional who manages rental properties on behalf of owners for a fee
- **Tenant Screening**: The process of evaluating rental applicants using income, credit, and rental history criteria
- **Security Deposit**: Upfront funds held by the landlord to cover unpaid rent or damages beyond normal wear and tear
- **Eviction**: Legal process to remove a tenant from a property for violation of the lease or failure to pay rent
- **Fair Housing Act**: Federal law prohibiting discrimination in housing based on protected classes
