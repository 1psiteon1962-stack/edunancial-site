# What Is a Lien on a Property?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a lien in plain terms.
- Name at least two common types of liens.
- Explain why a lien can affect a property sale.

## Core Content

### What a Lien Is

A lien is a legal claim against a property, usually securing a debt.
Common examples include a mortgage lien (the lender's claim, covered
earlier), a tax lien (unpaid property taxes), and a mechanic's lien (an
unpaid contractor's claim for work done on the property).

### How a Lien Attaches

A lien is typically recorded with the local government, attaching to the
property itself rather than just to the owner personally — meaning it
can follow the property even through a sale if not resolved first.

### Why It Can Block a Sale

Because a lien is attached to the property, most buyers and lenders will
require it to be cleared before a sale closes. An unresolved lien is a
common reason a property sale gets delayed or falls through.

## Worked Example

A homeowner hires a contractor for a renovation and doesn't pay the
final invoice. The contractor can file a mechanic's lien against the
property, which would need to be resolved before the home could be sold
with a clear title.

## Practice Quiz

1. A lien is best described as:
   A. A type of homeowner's insurance
   B. A legal claim against a property, usually securing a debt
   C. A government subsidy for homebuyers
   D. A required home inspection

2. True or False: A lien only affects the current owner personally, not
   the property itself.

3. What is a mechanic's lien?
   A. A claim filed by an unpaid contractor for work done on the
      property
   B. A type of car loan
   C. A government tax on new construction
   D. A form of title insurance

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. A

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A lien is a legal claim against a property, usually
tied to a debt.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Liens attach to the property itself, which is
why they can follow it through a sale.

### Question 3

Correct answer: A. A mechanic's lien is filed by an unpaid contractor
for work performed on the property.

## Key Takeaways

- A lien is a legal claim against a property, usually securing a debt.
- Liens attach to the property, not just the owner personally.
- Unresolved liens commonly block or delay property sales.
