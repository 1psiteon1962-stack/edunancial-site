## Learning Objectives

- Describe the BRRRR strategy and how it enables accelerated portfolio growth
- Calculate the equity recycled from a BRRRR deal to fund the next acquisition
- Identify the legal structures appropriate for holding investment properties
- Create a personal portfolio growth plan with defined milestones

## Core Content

Acquiring one investment property is an achievement. Building a portfolio that generates meaningful passive income requires a systematic strategy for recycling capital and managing growing complexity.

### The BRRRR Strategy

BRRRR stands for: **Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat**

This strategy recycles your initial capital across multiple properties:

1. **Buy** a distressed property below market value
2. **Rehab** it to increase value and rental appeal
3. **Rent** it to a qualified tenant
4. **Refinance** with a cash-out refinance based on the new appraised value
5. **Repeat** — use the returned capital for the next acquisition

**Example**:
- Buy: $80,000
- Rehab: $20,000 (Total invested: $100,000)
- After-Repair Value: $150,000
- Refinance at 75% LTV: $112,500 cash-out loan
- Capital returned: $112,500 − closing costs ≈ $108,000
- Net out-of-pocket after refi: approximately $0 or small amount remaining

This is called "infinite return" — positive cash flow with little or no capital remaining in the deal.

### Scale: The Numbers That Matter

One property generating $300/month cash flow provides $3,600/year.
Five properties: $18,000/year.
Ten properties: $36,000/year.
Twenty properties: $72,000/year.

At twenty properties averaging $300/month each, a real estate investor earns over $70,000/year in passive income. This is achievable in 10 years through disciplined BRRRR cycling.

### Legal Structures for Holding Properties

**Personal Name**
The simplest structure. No asset protection. If a tenant sues, personal assets are at risk.

**Single-Member LLC**
An LLC for each property (or small group) limits liability to assets held within that LLC. Used by most serious investors. Requires operating agreements, separate bank accounts, and compliance formalities.

**Series LLC**
Available in certain states. One parent LLC with multiple protected series — a more efficient structure for large portfolios. Consult an attorney on availability in your state.

**Land Trust**
Provides privacy by holding title anonymously. Often combined with LLC beneficiary structure for both privacy and liability protection.

**Important**: Do not hold investment properties personally once you have equity worth protecting. Consult a real estate attorney about the right structure for your situation.

### Building Your Team

A scalable portfolio requires a team:
- **Real estate attorney**: Contracts, LLCs, closings
- **CPA**: Tax strategy, depreciation, entity structure
- **Lender (or broker)**: Financing, rate shopping, DSCR products
- **Property manager**: Day-to-day operations
- **Contractor**: Rehab and repairs
- **Insurance agent**: Landlord policies, umbrella coverage

### Your 10-Year Portfolio Plan

A realistic plan for a first-time investor with $50,000 in starting capital:

| Year | Properties | Monthly Cash Flow |
|------|-----------|------------------|
| 1–2 | 1–2 | $300–$600 |
| 3–4 | 3–5 | $900–$1,500 |
| 5–7 | 6–10 | $1,800–$3,000 |
| 8–10 | 11–20 | $3,300–$6,000 |

The plan compounds through BRRRR recycling, reinvested cash flow, and natural appreciation.

### The Decision to Stop or Scale

Some investors reach their target income and stop acquiring. Others continue to scale. Neither is wrong. The key is intentionality: know your number, track your progress, and adjust based on your life goals — not someone else's definition of success.

## Key Terms

- **BRRRR**: Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat — a portfolio-building strategy that recycles capital
- **LLC (Limited Liability Company)**: A business entity providing personal asset protection for its members
- **Cash-Out Refinance**: Replacing an existing mortgage with a larger one and taking the difference as cash
- **LTV (Loan-to-Value)**: Loan amount divided by appraised property value, expressed as a percentage
- **Infinite Return**: A scenario where all original capital is recovered via refinancing while retaining the asset and its cash flow
