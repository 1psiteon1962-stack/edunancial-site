# What Is an Appraisal, and Why Isn't It the Same as the Price?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define an appraisal in plain terms.
- Explain who typically orders an appraisal and why.
- Explain why appraised value and sale price can be different numbers.

## Core Content

### What an Appraisal Is

An appraisal is a professional, independent estimate of a property's
value, performed by a licensed appraiser. It's based on factors like
comparable sales, condition, and location.

### Who Orders It, and Why

Lenders typically require an appraisal before approving a mortgage, to
confirm the property is worth roughly what's being borrowed against it —
protecting the lender's collateral, not just helping the buyer.

### Why the Numbers Can Differ

The appraised value and the agreed sale price are two separate numbers
determined two separate ways: the sale price is whatever a buyer and
seller agreed to, while the appraisal is an independent professional
estimate. If a home appraises below the agreed sale price, it can create
a real problem for financing, since the lender generally won't loan more
than the appraised value supports.

## Worked Example

A buyer agrees to pay $320,000 for a home, but the appraisal comes back
at $305,000. The lender will typically only finance based on the lower
appraised value, meaning the buyer may need a larger down payment or the
sale price may need to be renegotiated.

## Practice Quiz

1. An appraisal is:
   A. The price a buyer and seller agree to
   B. An independent professional estimate of a property's value
   C. A type of homeowner's insurance
   D. A government tax assessment

2. True or False: Lenders typically require an appraisal to protect
   their own collateral, not just to help the buyer.

3. What can happen if a home appraises below the agreed sale price?
   A. Nothing — the sale proceeds exactly as agreed
   B. The lender may only finance up to the appraised value, creating a
      financing gap
   C. The appraisal is automatically overturned
   D. Property tax is reduced

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An appraisal is an independent, professional value
estimate — separate from the negotiated price.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. The appraisal protects the lender's collateral
position on the loan.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. A low appraisal can create a financing gap that the
buyer, seller, or both must resolve.

## Key Takeaways

- An appraisal is an independent estimate of value, not the sale price.
- Lenders require it to protect their own collateral.
- A low appraisal relative to the sale price can create real financing
  problems.
