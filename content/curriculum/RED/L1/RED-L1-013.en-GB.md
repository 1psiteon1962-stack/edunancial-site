# What Is a Comp? (Comparable Sales, Explained)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a comp in plain terms.
- Name the factors that make a comp genuinely useful.
- Explain why a poorly chosen comp can be misleading.

## Core Content

### What a Comp Is

A comp (short for "comparable sale") is a recently sold property used as
a reference point to estimate the value of another property. Comps are
central to both appraisals and everyday buyer/seller pricing decisions.

### What Makes a Comp Useful

A genuinely useful comp is typically close in location (often within a
mile or two), similar in size and specs (bedrooms, bathrooms, square
footage), and sold recently (often within the past six to twelve
months) — because markets and property conditions change over time.

### Why a Poor Comp Misleads

Using a comp that's too far away, too different in size or condition, or
sold too long ago can produce a badly skewed value estimate. A five
-bedroom renovated home isn't a reliable comp for a two-bedroom fixer
-upper, even if they're on the same street.

## Worked Example

To value a 3-bedroom, 2-bath home, an appraiser looks for recently sold
3-bedroom, 2-bath homes within a mile or two, adjusting for differences
like a finished basement or an extra garage — rather than comparing it to
a very different property just because it's nearby.

## Practice Quiz

1. A comp is:
   A. A type of mortgage
   B. A recently sold property used as a reference for estimating value
   C. A government property tax record
   D. A legal document transferring ownership

2. True or False: Any nearby sold property makes a good comp, regardless
   of size or condition.

3. Why does recency matter for a comp?
   A. It doesn't matter at all
   B. Markets and conditions change over time, so older sales may not
      reflect current value
   C. Older comps are always more accurate
   D. Recency only matters for commercial property

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A comp is a recently sold, similar property used as a
value reference.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A useful comp needs to be similar in size,
condition, and location — not just nearby.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Markets shift over time, so a sale from several years
ago may not reflect current value.

## Key Takeaways

- A comp is a recently sold, similar property used to estimate value.
- Useful comps are close in location, similar in size/condition, and
  recent.
- A poorly chosen comp can produce a misleading value estimate.
