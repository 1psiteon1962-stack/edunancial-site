# What Is a Home Inspection Actually Checking For?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a home inspection in plain terms.
- Explain how it's different from an appraisal.
- Explain what a buyer can do with inspection results.

## Core Content

### What a Home Inspection Is

A home inspection is a professional evaluation of a property's physical
condition — checking systems like the roof, foundation, plumbing,
electrical, and HVAC for existing or potential problems.

### How It's Different from an Appraisal

An appraisal estimates value; an inspection assesses condition. A home
can appraise at a high value while still having significant physical
problems the appraisal wouldn't necessarily catch in the same detail —
they answer different questions.

### What a Buyer Can Do With the Results

Depending on the purchase agreement, a buyer who receives a concerning
inspection report may be able to negotiate repairs, request a price
reduction, or in some cases walk away from the deal — the specific
options depend on the contract terms agreed to beforehand.

## Worked Example

An inspection reveals an aging roof likely to need replacement within a
few years. The buyer can use this information to negotiate a price
reduction or ask the seller to address it before closing, depending on
what the purchase agreement allows.

## Practice Quiz

1. A home inspection primarily evaluates:
   A. The property's market value
   B. The property's physical condition
   C. The buyer's creditworthiness
   D. The local property tax rate

2. True or False: An appraisal and a home inspection answer the same
   question.

3. What might a buyer be able to do after a concerning inspection
   report, depending on the contract?
   A. Nothing at all
   B. Negotiate repairs, a price reduction, or potentially walk away
   C. Automatically cancel the mortgage
   D. Force the seller to lower the property tax

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A home inspection evaluates physical condition, not
market value.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. An appraisal estimates value; an inspection
assesses condition — different purposes.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Depending on contract terms, a buyer may have several
options after a concerning inspection.

## Key Takeaways

- A home inspection evaluates physical condition, not value.
- It serves a different purpose than an appraisal.
- Inspection results can give a buyer real negotiating leverage,
  depending on the contract.
