# What Is Title, and Why Does "Clear Title" Matter?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define title in plain terms.
- Explain what "clear title" means.
- Name a common issue that can cloud a title.

## Core Content

### What Title Actually Is

Title is the legal concept of ownership — the recognized right to a
property. It's distinct from a deed, which is the physical document that
transfers title from one party to another.

### What "Clear Title" Means

Clear title means there are no competing claims, unresolved liens, or
ownership disputes attached to the property. Buyers and lenders
generally require clear title before a sale can close, because an unclear
title creates real legal risk for whoever takes ownership.

### What Can Cloud a Title

A title can be "clouded" by things like an unresolved lien, a boundary
dispute, an error in public records, or a missing heir with a legal
claim from a previous owner's estate. These issues typically need to be
resolved before a sale can proceed cleanly.

## Worked Example

A title search on a property reveals an old, unpaid contractor's lien
from a previous owner that was never resolved. This clouds the title and
must be cleared before the current sale can close.

## Practice Quiz

1. Title refers to:
   A. The physical document transferring ownership
   B. The legal concept of ownership itself
   C. A type of property insurance
   D. A government tax record

2. True or False: Clear title means there are no competing claims or
   unresolved issues on the property.

3. What is one thing that can cloud a title?
   A. A recent paint job
   B. An unresolved lien from a previous owner
   C. A new roof
   D. A change in property tax rate

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Title is the legal concept of ownership; a deed is
the document that transfers it.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Clear title means no competing claims or unresolved
issues.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. An unresolved lien is a common example of a title
cloud.

## Key Takeaways

- Title is the legal right of ownership, distinct from the deed
  document.
- Clear title means no competing claims or unresolved issues.
- Liens, disputes, or record errors can cloud a title and must typically
  be resolved before a sale closes.
