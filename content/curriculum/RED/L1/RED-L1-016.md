# What Is Title Insurance?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define title insurance in plain terms.
- Explain what it protects against.
- Explain why it's typically a one-time cost.

## Core Content

### What Title Insurance Is

Title insurance protects the owner (and separately, the lender) against
financial loss from title problems that existed before the purchase but
weren't discovered during the title search — such as an undisclosed
heir, a forged document in the property's history, or an error in public
records.

### What It Protects Against

Unlike most insurance, which covers future events, title insurance
covers past, undiscovered problems. If a covered issue surfaces after
closing, the policy can cover legal costs and financial losses tied to
defending or resolving the claim.

### Why It's a One-Time Cost

Because title insurance covers historical risk rather than ongoing risk,
it's typically paid once, at closing, rather than as a recurring premium
like homeowner's insurance.

## Worked Example

Two years after buying a home, a previously unknown heir of a past owner
files a claim on the property. The buyer's title insurance policy covers
the legal costs of resolving the claim.

## Practice Quiz

1. Title insurance primarily protects against:
   A. Future storm damage
   B. Past, undiscovered title problems
   C. A drop in market value
   D. Late mortgage payments

2. True or False: Title insurance is typically paid as a recurring
   monthly premium, like homeowner's insurance.

3. Which of these is an example of something title insurance might
   cover?
   A. A new roof needed after a storm
   B. An undisclosed heir with a claim from the property's past
   C. A decline in the local real estate market
   D. A late mortgage payment fee

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Title insurance covers past, undiscovered issues —
not future events like storm damage.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. It's typically a one-time payment at closing.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. An undisclosed heir is a classic example of a
historical title problem this insurance covers.

## Key Takeaways

- Title insurance covers past, undiscovered title problems.
- It's typically a one-time payment made at closing.
- It protects against financial loss and legal costs if a covered issue
  surfaces later.
