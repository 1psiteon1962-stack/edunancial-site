# What Is Escrow?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define escrow in plain terms.
- Explain the two common uses of the term in real estate.
- Explain why a neutral third party is involved.

## Core Content

### Escrow, Generally

Escrow refers to money or documents held by a neutral third party until
specific conditions are met. In real estate, the term is used in two
related but distinct ways.

### Escrow During a Purchase

During a home purchase, an escrow company or agent holds the buyer's
deposit and relevant documents until all conditions of the sale (like
inspections and financing) are satisfied, then releases funds and
documents to complete the closing.

### Escrow After Purchase (For Taxes and Insurance)

After closing, many mortgage lenders maintain an ongoing escrow account,
collecting a portion of property tax and homeowner's insurance costs
with each monthly payment, then paying those bills on the homeowner's
behalf when due.

## Worked Example

A buyer's earnest money deposit is held in escrow during the purchase
process. After closing, their new lender also sets up an ongoing escrow
account to collect and pay property taxes and insurance automatically
each year.

## Practice Quiz

1. Escrow refers to:
   A. A type of home inspection
   B. Money or documents held by a neutral third party until conditions
      are met
   C. A government property tax
   D. A form of title insurance

2. True or False: Escrow is only used during the home-buying process,
   never afterward.

3. What might an ongoing mortgage escrow account be used for?
   A. Paying the homeowner's grocery bills
   B. Collecting and paying property tax and insurance
   C. Covering the down payment
   D. Paying the real estate agent's commission

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Escrow is a neutral third-party holding arrangement.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Escrow is also commonly used on an ongoing basis
for tax and insurance payments after closing.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Ongoing escrow accounts commonly handle property tax
and insurance payments.

## Key Takeaways

- Escrow is a neutral third-party holding arrangement.
- It's used both during a purchase and, often, on an ongoing basis for
  taxes and insurance.
- The neutral third party protects both buyer and seller (or lender and
  borrower) during the process.
