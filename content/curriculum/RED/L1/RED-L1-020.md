# Single-Family vs. Multi-Family: What's the Real Difference for an Owner?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define single-family and multi-family properties.
- Explain how income potential differs between the two.
- Explain how management responsibility differs between the two.

## Core Content

### What Each Type Is

A single-family property houses one household in one independent unit.
A multi-family property contains multiple separate units (like a duplex,
triplex, or fourplex) within one property, each capable of housing a
different household.

### Income Potential

A single-family home generally generates rental income from one source
(if rented at all). A multi-family property can generate income from
multiple units simultaneously, which can mean more total income — but
also depends on more units being successfully rented and maintained.

### Management Responsibility

More units generally mean more day-to-day management: more tenants, more
potential maintenance requests, more turnover events. This trade-off
between income potential and management complexity is a real
consideration when comparing property types, not just a difference in
price.

## Worked Example

A single-family rental generates one rent check per month. A fourplex
generates up to four rent checks per month from the same overall
property — but also up to four tenants' worth of maintenance requests
and turnover to manage.

## Practice Quiz

1. A multi-family property is best described as:
   A. A property with one independent housing unit
   B. A property containing multiple separate housing units
   C. A property with no housing units
   D. A type of commercial office building

2. True or False: Multi-family properties generally involve more
   day-to-day management than single-family properties.

3. What is one real trade-off between single-family and multi-family
   ownership?
   A. There is no trade-off; multi-family is always better
   B. Multi-family offers more income potential but generally more
      management complexity
   C. Single-family always generates more total income
   D. Multi-family properties cannot be rented out

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Multi-family properties contain multiple separate
housing units.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. More units generally mean more tenants and more
management responsibility.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. More income potential typically comes with more
management complexity.

## Key Takeaways

- Single-family properties house one unit; multi-family properties
  house multiple units.
- Multi-family properties offer more income potential from multiple
  units.
- That income potential comes with real trade-offs in management
  complexity.
