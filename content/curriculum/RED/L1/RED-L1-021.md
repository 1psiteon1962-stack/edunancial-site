# Commercial Real Estate Fundamentals

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Name the four main categories of commercial real estate.
- Explain how commercial property is valued differently from a typical home.
- Identify key differences in leases and risk between commercial and residential property.

## Core Content

### The Four Main Categories

Commercial real estate is property used to generate business income rather than to serve as someone's home. It is commonly grouped into four categories: office (buildings where businesses work), retail (stores, shopping centers, and restaurants), industrial (warehouses, distribution centers, and light manufacturing), and multifamily (apartment buildings large enough to be financed and valued as a business — often five or more units in U.S. lending practice).

### Valued by Income, Not by Comparable Homes

A typical home is valued mainly by comparing it to similar homes that recently sold nearby (the comps covered earlier in this track). Commercial property is valued mainly by the income it produces. Two metrics from this level do most of the work: net operating income (NOI), the income left after operating expenses, and cap rate, which relates that income to the property's value. Raising NOI can raise the value of a commercial building directly.

### Leases and Risk Work Differently

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: lease rules, tenant protections, and lending standards vary significantly by state/country.)* Commercial leases are often longer than residential leases — commonly several years — and many shift some operating costs, such as taxes, insurance, or maintenance, to the tenant. Longer leases can make income more stable, but a vacancy can last much longer and cost much more, because finding a new business tenant usually takes more time than finding a new residential renter. Commercial loans also tend to require larger down payments and shorter terms than typical home mortgages.

## Worked Example

*ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:* A small retail building produces $60,000 in annual net operating income. In a market where similar properties trade at a 7.5% cap rate, its estimated value is $60,000 ÷ 0.075 = $800,000. If the owner signs a new tenant and NOI rises to $66,000, the estimated value at the same cap rate becomes $880,000 — the higher income itself raises the value, which is not how a single-family home is typically valued.

## Practice Quiz

1. Which of these is one of the four main categories of commercial real estate?
   A. A single-family home the owner lives in
   B. Industrial property, such as a warehouse
   C. A vacation cabin used only by the owner's family
   D. A savings account
2. True or False: Commercial property is valued mainly by its income, not mainly by comparable home sales.
3. Why can a vacancy be especially costly in commercial real estate?
   A. Commercial tenants never leave
   B. Finding a new business tenant usually takes longer, so lost income can last longer
   C. Commercial buildings cannot be leased again after a vacancy
   D. Vacancies only affect residential property

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Industrial property is one of the four main categories, along with office, retail, and multifamily.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Commercial value is driven mainly by income, using measures like NOI and cap rate.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Re-leasing to a business tenant often takes much longer, extending the loss of income.

## Key Takeaways

- Commercial real estate generally falls into four categories: office, retail, industrial, and multifamily.
- Commercial property is valued mainly by the income it produces, measured through NOI and cap rate.
- Longer leases can stabilize income, but vacancies tend to last longer and cost more than in residential property.
