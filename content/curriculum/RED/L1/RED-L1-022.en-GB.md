# What Is Cash Flow in Real Estate?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define cash flow in a real estate context.
- Calculate basic cash flow given income and expenses.
- Explain why collecting rent doesn't guarantee positive cash flow.

## Core Content

### What Cash Flow Means

Cash flow is the money left over from a rental property after all
expenses (mortgage payment, taxes, insurance, maintenance, and more) are
subtracted from the rental income collected.

### The Basic Formula

Cash Flow = Rental Income − Total Expenses.

If a property collects $2,000 in rent and has $1,700 in total monthly
expenses, its cash flow is $300 — positive cash flow. If expenses exceed
income, cash flow is negative.

### Why Collecting Rent Isn't Enough

A property can collect substantial rent and still have negative cash
flow if expenses are high enough. New investors sometimes look only at
rental income without fully accounting for expenses like maintenance
reserves or vacancy periods, leading to an overly optimistic picture.

## Worked Example

A rental collects $1,800/month. Expenses total $1,600/month (mortgage,
taxes, insurance, and a maintenance reserve). Cash flow = $1,800 −
$1,600 = $200/month positive.

## Practice Quiz

1. Cash flow is calculated as:
   A. Rental income plus expenses
   B. Rental income minus total expenses
   C. The property's total value
   D. The down payment amount

2. True or False: Collecting rent automatically guarantees positive cash
   flow.

3. If a property collects $2,200 in rent and has $2,400 in total
   expenses, what is the cash flow?
   A. Positive $200
   B. Negative $200
   C. $0
   D. $4,600

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Cash flow = rental income minus total expenses.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. High enough expenses can still produce negative
cash flow.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $2,200 − $2,400 = −$200, a negative cash flow.

## Key Takeaways

- Cash Flow = Rental Income − Total Expenses.
- Positive cash flow isn't guaranteed just because rent is collected.
- Accounting for all real expenses, not just the mortgage, is essential
  to an accurate picture.
