# What Is Cash Flow in Immobilier?

## Objectifs d’apprentissage

À la fin de cette leçon, vous devriez pouvoir :

- Define flux de trésorerie in a real estate context.
- Calculate basic flux de trésorerie given revenu and dépenses.
- Explain why collecting rent doesn't guarantee positive flux de trésorerie.

## Contenu principal

### What Cash Flow Means

Cash flow is the money left over from a rental propriété after all
dépenses (hypothèque payment, impôtes, assurance, maintenance, and more) are
subtracted from the rental revenu collected.

### The Basic Formula

Cash Flow = Rental Income − Total Expenses.

If a propriété collects $2,000 in rent and has $1,700 in total monthly
dépenses, its flux de trésorerie is $300 — positive flux de trésorerie. If dépenses exceed
revenu, flux de trésorerie is negative.

### Why Collecting Rent Isn't Enough

A propriété can collect substantial rent and still have negative cash
flow if dépenses are high enough. New investors sometimes look only at
rental revenu without fully accounting for dépenses like maintenance
reserves or vacancy periods, leading to an overly optimistic picture.

## Exemple pratique

A rental collects $1,800/month. Expenses total $1,600/month (hypothèque,
impôtes, assurance, and a maintenance reserve). Cash flow = $1,800 −
$1,600 = $200/month positive.

## Questionnaire pratique

1. Cash flow is calculated as:
   A. Rental revenu plus dépenses
   B. Rental revenu minus total dépenses
   C. The propriété's total valeur
   D. The down payment amount

2. Vrai ou faux: Collecting rent automatically guarantees positive cash
   flow.

3. If a propriété collects $2,200 in rent and has $2,400 in total
   dépenses, what is the flux de trésorerie?
   A. Positive $200
   B. Negative $200
   C. $0
   D. $4,600

## Corrigé

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Réponses et explications

### Question 1

Bonne réponse: B. Cash flow = rental revenu minus total dépenses.

### Question 2

Bonne réponse: False. High enough dépenses can still produce negative
flux de trésorerie.

### Question 3

Bonne réponse: B. $2,200 − $2,400 = −$200, a negative flux de trésorerie.

## Points clés

- Cash Flow = Rental Income − Total Expenses.
- Positive flux de trésorerie isn't guaranteed just because rent is collected.
- Accounting for all real dépenses, not just the hypothèque, is essential
  to an accurate picture.
