# Qu’est-ce que le taux de capitalisation? (Introduction)

## Objectifs d’apprentissage

À la fin de cette leçon, vous devriez pouvoir :

- State the basic cap rate formula.
- Explain what a cap rate estimates.
- Recognize that cap rate is a starting comparison tool, not a complete
  valuation method.

## Contenu principal

### The Basic Formula

Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Propriété Value.

Net operating revenu (NOI) is a propriété's revenu after operating
dépenses, but before hypothèque payments — a more detailed introduction to
NOI comes in Level 2.

### What It Estimates

A cap rate gives a rough estimate of a propriété's potential return as an
all-cash investment, expressed as a percentage — useful for quickly
comparing multiple propriétés on a similar basis.

### A Starting Point, Not a Full Picture

*ASSUMPTION (for teaching purposes):* Cap rate is a useful first-pass
comparison tool, but it doesn't account for financing details, future
appréciation, or propriété-specific risk — a fuller treatment comes in
Level 2.

## Exemple pratique

A propriété with $24,000 in annual net operating revenu and a valeur of
$300,000 has a cap rate of $24,000 ÷ $300,000 = 8%.

## Questionnaire pratique

1. The basic cap rate formula is:
   A. Rental Income ÷ Down Payment
   B. Net Operating Income ÷ Propriété Value
   C. Propriété Value ÷ Hypothèque Balance
   D. Total Expenses ÷ Rental Income

2. Vrai ou faux: Cap rate accounts for hypothèque financing details.

3. What is cap rate generally used for?
   A. Calculating propriété impôt
   B. Quickly comparing potential returns across multiple propriétés
   C. Determining a home inspection's cost
   D. Setting a HOA fee

## Corrigé

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Réponses et explications

### Question 1

Bonne réponse: B. Cap Rate = NOI ÷ Propriété Value.

### Question 2

Bonne réponse: False. Cap rate is calculated before financing costs are
factored in.

### Question 3

Bonne réponse: B. It's a quick comparison tool across propriétés.

## Points clés

- Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Propriété Value.
- It estimates potential return as if the propriété were bought in cash.
- It's a useful starting comparison, not a complete valuation method.
