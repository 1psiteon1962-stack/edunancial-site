# What Is a 1031 Exchange? (What It Is, in Plain Terms)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain the basic idea behind a 1031 exchange.
- Explain what "deferring" tax means, as opposed to eliminating it.
- Recognize that strict rules and timelines apply.

## Core Content

### The Basic Idea

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this is a U.S. tax provision; not available in
other countries.)* A 1031 exchange allows an investor to sell one
investment property and reinvest the proceeds into another "like-kind"
property, deferring the capital gains tax that would normally be due on
the sale.

### Deferring, Not Eliminating

This is a critical distinction: the tax isn't erased, it's postponed.
The deferred gain generally carries forward into the new property, and
tax may eventually be owed later, depending on what happens with future
transactions.

### Strict Rules Apply

*ASSUMPTION (for teaching purposes):* 1031 exchanges involve specific
identification and closing timelines and procedural requirements. This
introductory lesson doesn't cover those specifics — the goal here is
understanding the core concept before the mechanics.

## Worked Example

An investor sells a rental property with a significant gain and, instead
of paying capital gains tax immediately, uses a 1031 exchange to roll
the proceeds into a new rental property — deferring that tax bill rather
than paying it right away.

## Practice Quiz

1. A 1031 exchange primarily allows an investor to:
   A. Eliminate capital gains tax permanently
   B. Defer capital gains tax by reinvesting in a like-kind property
   C. Avoid property tax entirely
   D. Skip the need for a home inspection

2. True or False: A 1031 exchange eliminates the tax owed rather than
   postponing it.

3. What does "like-kind" generally refer to in this context?
   A. Two identical houses
   B. Investment properties that qualify for exchange treatment under
      the rules
   C. Any two assets of equal price
   D. A specific brand of property

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A 1031 exchange defers, not eliminates, capital gains
tax.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The tax is postponed, not erased.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. "Like-kind" refers to qualifying investment property
types under the applicable rules, not identical properties.

## Key Takeaways

- A 1031 exchange defers capital gains tax by reinvesting proceeds into
  another qualifying property.
- Deferring is not the same as eliminating the tax.
- Strict rules and timelines apply, covered in more depth in later
  lessons.
