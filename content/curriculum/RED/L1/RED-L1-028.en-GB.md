# Landlord Basics: What Are You Actually Responsible For?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Name core landlord responsibilities.
- Distinguish landlord responsibilities from tenant responsibilities.
- Explain why understanding this split matters before renting out a
  property.

## Core Content

### Core Landlord Responsibilities

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: exact obligations vary by state/country and
lease terms.)* Landlords are generally responsible for maintaining the
property in a habitable condition — this typically includes major
systems (plumbing, heating, structural issues) and compliance with local
housing codes.

### What Tenants Are Typically Responsible For

Tenants are generally responsible for day-to-day upkeep of their living
space, following lease terms, and often reporting problems promptly —
the specific split is usually spelled out in the lease itself.

### Why the Split Matters

A new landlord who doesn't understand these responsibilities risks legal
exposure (for failing habitability obligations) or unnecessary costs (for
covering things that were actually the tenant's responsibility). Clear
lease terms help prevent disputes on both sides.

## Worked Example

A tenant reports a broken heater in winter. This is typically a landlord
responsibility (a major system affecting habitability), not something the
tenant is expected to fix themselves.

## Practice Quiz

1. Landlords are generally responsible for:
   A. Day-to-day tidiness of the tenant's space
   B. Maintaining the property in a habitable condition, including major
      systems
   C. The tenant's personal belongings
   D. The tenant's employment status

2. True or False: The exact split of landlord and tenant responsibilities
   is identical everywhere, regardless of location or lease.

3. Why does understanding this responsibility split matter for a new
   landlord?
   A. It doesn't matter at all
   B. It helps avoid legal exposure and unnecessary costs
   C. It only matters for commercial property
   D. It has no connection to the lease

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Landlords generally handle habitability and major
systems.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Responsibilities vary by location and lease
terms.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Understanding the split helps avoid legal and
financial problems.

## Key Takeaways

- Landlords generally handle habitability and major systems.
- Tenants generally handle day-to-day upkeep and following lease terms.
- Clear lease terms help prevent disputes over responsibility.
