# What Is a Lease, and What Should Be In One?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a lease in plain terms.
- Name core terms a lease should address.
- Explain why a verbal agreement is a real risk.

## Core Content

### What a Lease Is

A lease is a legally binding contract between a landlord and tenant,
setting the terms under which the tenant may occupy the property.

### Core Terms a Lease Should Address

A solid lease typically addresses: rent amount and due date, lease term
length, security deposit terms, maintenance responsibilities, and rules
around things like pets or subletting. Vague or missing terms are a
common source of disputes.

### Why "We'll Figure It Out" Is a Real Risk

A verbal or informal agreement, without clear written terms, leaves both
landlord and tenant exposed if a disagreement arises later — there's no
clear record of what was actually agreed to. A written lease protects
both parties, not just the landlord.

## Worked Example

A landlord and tenant verbally agree on rent but never document a move
-out notice requirement. When the tenant leaves without notice, the
landlord has no written term to point to, creating a real dispute that a
clear lease could have prevented.

## Practice Quiz

1. A lease is:
   A. An informal handshake agreement
   B. A legally binding contract setting occupancy terms
   C. A type of property tax
   D. A government housing subsidy

2. True or False: A verbal lease agreement carries the same protection
   as a clear written lease.

3. Which of these is a core term a lease should typically address?
   A. The tenant's favorite color
   B. Rent amount and due date
   C. The landlord's personal hobbies
   D. The weather forecast

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A lease is a legally binding contract.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A written lease provides much clearer protection
than a verbal agreement.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Rent amount and due date are core lease terms.

## Key Takeaways

- A lease is a legally binding contract setting occupancy terms.
- Core terms include rent, term length, deposits, and responsibilities.
- A written lease protects both landlord and tenant far better than a
  verbal agreement.
