# Security Deposits: What Landlords Can and Can't Do

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a security deposit in plain terms.
- Explain what it's generally intended to cover.
- Recognize that landlords face real legal limits on its use.

## Core Content

### What a Security Deposit Is

A security deposit is money paid by a tenant upfront, held by the
landlord to cover potential unpaid rent or property damage beyond normal
wear and tear, once the tenancy ends.

### What It's Generally Intended to Cover

Security deposits are typically meant for unpaid rent or actual damage —
not for normal wear and tear that naturally happens from ordinary living
(like minor carpet wear or small nail holes from hanging pictures).

### Real Legal Limits

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: specific limits, timelines, and rules vary
significantly by state/country.)* Many jurisdictions place legal limits
on deposit amounts, require itemized deductions, and set timelines for
returning the deposit after move-out. A landlord who ignores these rules
can face real legal consequences.

## Worked Example

A tenant moves out after two years, leaving minor carpet wear from normal
use. A landlord who tries to deduct the full cost of new carpet from the
deposit may be overstepping — normal wear and tear is typically not a
valid deduction.

## Practice Quiz

1. A security deposit is generally intended to cover:
   A. The landlord's personal expenses
   B. Unpaid rent or actual damage beyond normal wear and tear
   C. Normal wear and tear from ordinary living
   D. The next tenant's move-in costs

2. True or False: Landlords can generally deduct for any wear on the
   property, including normal use.

3. What often applies to how and when a deposit must be returned?
   A. Nothing — it's entirely up to the landlord
   B. Legal limits and timelines that vary by jurisdiction
   C. A fixed global rule that applies everywhere identically
   D. Federal law only, with no state variation

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Deposits generally cover unpaid rent or real damage,
not normal wear.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Normal wear and tear typically isn't a valid
deduction.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Legal limits and timelines vary by jurisdiction.

## Key Takeaways

- A security deposit covers unpaid rent or real damage, not normal wear.
- Landlords face real legal limits on deposit use.
- Rules and timelines for returning deposits vary by location.
