# What Is Flipping, and What Can Go Wrong?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define flipping in plain terms.
- Name at least two real risks involved in flipping.
- Explain why flipping requires more active involvement than many other
  strategies.

## Core Content

### What Flipping Is

Flipping means buying a property, typically improving it through repairs
or renovation, and reselling it relatively quickly for a profit.

### What Can Go Wrong

Flipping carries real risks: renovation costs commonly run over budget,
timelines often extend longer than planned (increasing holding costs
like the mortgage and taxes), and the local market can shift between
purchase and sale, changing the expected resale value.

### Why It Requires Active Involvement

Unlike buy-and-hold rental investing, flipping is a hands-on, active
strategy requiring real project management skill (or a trusted, well
-managed contractor relationship), not a passive investment.

## Worked Example

An investor buys a fixer-upper expecting a 3-month renovation and a
quick resale. Unexpected structural repairs extend the project to 7
months, and the local market softens slightly in that time — turning a
projected profit into a much thinner margin.

## Practice Quiz

1. Flipping generally means:
   A. Renting a property long-term
   B. Buying, improving, and quickly reselling a property for profit
   C. Holding a property indefinitely without changes
   D. Assigning a purchase contract without ownership

2. True or False: Flipping is generally considered a passive investment
   strategy.

3. What is one real risk commonly involved in flipping?
   A. Guaranteed profit regardless of costs
   B. Renovation costs and timelines running over budget
   C. No exposure to market changes
   D. Zero holding costs

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Flipping means buying, improving, and quickly
reselling for profit.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Flipping is an active, hands-on strategy.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Cost and timeline overruns are common real risks.

## Key Takeaways

- Flipping means buying, improving, and quickly reselling for profit.
- Real risks include cost overruns, timeline delays, and market shifts.
- Flipping is an active strategy, not a passive one.
