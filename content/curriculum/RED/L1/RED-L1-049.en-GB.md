# Buying Your First Property: What Order Do These Steps Actually Go In?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- List the general order of steps in a typical property purchase.
- Explain why this order generally makes sense.
- Connect each step back to a concept covered earlier in this level.

## Core Content

### The General Order

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: exact order and requirements vary by location
and transaction type.)* A typical purchase generally moves through:
getting pre-approved for financing, searching for and making an offer on
a property, securing the mortgage (including the appraisal), completing
inspections, clearing title, and finally closing (including paying
closing costs).

### Why This Order Generally Makes Sense

Pre-approval comes first because it tells a buyer what they can actually
afford before falling in love with a property. Inspections generally
happen before finalizing the purchase so problems can be addressed or
negotiated. Title needs to be clear before closing, since an unclear
title (covered earlier) could derail the whole transaction.

### Connecting Back to Earlier Lessons

Each step in this order connects to a concept from earlier in this
level: the down payment and mortgage type (Lessons 4-8), the appraisal
and comps (Lessons 12-13), the inspection (Lesson 14), title and title
insurance (Lessons 15-16), escrow (Lesson 17), and closing costs
(Lesson 18).

## Worked Example

A first-time buyer gets pre-approved for a $280,000 mortgage before
searching, then makes an offer on a $270,000 home, orders an inspection,
addresses a few issues found, and moves to closing once title comes back
clear and financing is finalized.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why does getting pre-approved for financing typically come early in
   the process?
   A. It doesn't matter when it happens
   B. It tells a buyer what they can actually afford before searching
   C. It's legally required to happen last
   D. It replaces the need for an appraisal

2. True or False: Inspections typically happen after closing, once the
   buyer already owns the property.

3. Why must title be clear before closing?
   A. It doesn't need to be clear at all
   B. An unclear title creates real legal risk for the new owner
   C. Title only matters for commercial property
   D. Clear title is only relevant for cash purchases

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Pre-approval establishes a realistic budget before
searching.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Inspections typically happen before closing, not
after.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. An unclear title creates real legal risk, as covered
earlier in this level.

## Key Takeaways

- A typical purchase moves through pre-approval, search/offer, financing
  and appraisal, inspection, title clearance, and closing.
- This order exists for practical reasons at each step.
- Each step connects back to a specific concept covered earlier in this
  level.
