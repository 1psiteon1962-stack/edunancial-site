# Acheter votre première propriété : dans quel ordre les étapes se déroulent-elles?

## Objectifs d’apprentissage

À la fin de cette leçon, vous devriez pouvoir :

- List the general order of steps in a typical propriété purchase.
- Explain why this order generally makes sense.
- Connect each step back to a concept covered earlier in this level.

## Contenu principal

### The General Order

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: exact order and requirements vary by location
and transaction type.)* A typical purchase generally moves through:
getting pre-approved for financing, searching for and making an offer on
a propriété, securing the hypothèque (including the appraisal), completing
inspections, clearing title, and finally clôture (including paying
clôture costs).

### Why This Order Generally Makes Sense

Pre-approval comes first because it tells a acheteur what they can actually
afford before falling in love with a propriété. Inspections generally
happen before finalizing the purchase so problems can be addressed or
negotiated. Title needs to be clear before clôture, since an unclear
title (covered earlier) could derail the whole transaction.

### Connecting Back to Earlier Lessons

Each step in this order connects to a concept from earlier in this
level: the down payment and hypothèque type (Lessons 4-8), the appraisal
and comps (Lessons 12-13), the inspection (Lesson 14), title and title
insurance (Lessons 15-16), escrow (Lesson 17), and clôture costs
(Lesson 18).

## Exemple pratique

A first-time acheteur gets pre-approved for a $280,000 hypothèque before
searching, then makes an offer on a $270,000 home, orders an inspection,
addresses a few issues found, and moves to clôture once title comes back
clear and financing is finalized.

## Questionnaire pratique

1. Why does getting pre-approved for financing typically come early in
   the process?
   A. It doesn't matter when it happens
   B. It tells a acheteur what they can actually afford before searching
   C. It's legally required to happen last
   D. It replaces the need for an appraisal

2. Vrai ou faux: Inspections typically happen after clôture, once the
   acheteur already owns the propriété.

3. Why must title be clear before clôture?
   A. It doesn't need to be clear at all
   B. An unclear title creates real legal risk for the new propriétaire
   C. Title only matters for commercial propriété
   D. Clear title is only relevant for cash purchases

## Corrigé

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Réponses et explications

### Question 1

Bonne réponse: B. Pre-approval establishes a realistic budget before
searching.

### Question 2

Bonne réponse: False. Inspections typically happen before clôture, not
after.

### Question 3

Bonne réponse: B. An unclear title creates real legal risk, as covered
earlier in this level.

## Points clés

- A typical purchase moves through pre-approval, search/offer, financing
  and appraisal, inspection, title clearance, and clôture.
- This order exists for practical reasons at each step.
- Each step connects back to a specific concept covered earlier in this
  level.
