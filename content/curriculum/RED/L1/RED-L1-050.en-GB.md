# Capstone: Walking Through a Real Property Purchase, Start to Finish

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Apply the core concepts from RED Level 1 to one connected scenario.
- Identify which concept from this level applies at each stage of a
  purchase.
- Explain how the pieces of a property purchase fit together as a whole,
  not just as isolated terms.

## Core Content

### The Scenario

Maria wants to buy her first home. She has saved for a down payment and
wants to understand what she's actually buying — not just memorize terms,
but see how everything from this level connects in a real situation.

### Walking Through It

Maria starts by understanding that a home purchase means buying land, a
structure, and legal rights (Lesson 1) — not just a building. She weighs
renting vs. owning (Lesson 2) and decides owning fits her plans. She gets
pre-approved for a mortgage (Lessons 4-8), understanding her payment
splits into principal and interest (Lesson 5), and that her down payment
size affects her loan and whether she'll need PMI (Lessons 6, 8).

She finds a home, and the lender orders an appraisal based on comps
(Lessons 12-13) to confirm its value. She gets a home inspection
(Lesson 14) and negotiates a small repair credit after it reveals an
aging water heater. Her title company confirms clear title (Lesson 15)
and issues title insurance (Lesson 16). Her earnest money sits in escrow
(Lesson 17) until closing, where she pays her closing costs (Lesson 18)
in addition to her down payment.

Over the following years, Maria's equity (Lesson 3) grows two ways: she
pays down her loan, and her home appreciates (Lesson 25) — though she
understands that appreciation was never guaranteed.

### Why This Matters

None of these concepts function in isolation in a real purchase — they
connect as one process. Understanding each piece individually, and how
they fit together, is what separates genuinely useful financial
literacy from memorized vocabulary.

## Worked Example

If Maria's home cost $300,000 with a $30,000 (10%) down payment, she
financed $270,000, likely required PMI (since her down payment was below
20%), and her monthly payment split between principal and interest from
day one — all concepts covered individually earlier in this level, now
working together in one real transaction.

## Practice Quiz

1. What does a home purchase actually include, according to Lesson 1?
   A. Only the structure
   B. Land, structure, and legal rights
   C. Only the legal right to sell later
   D. Only the land

2. True or False: Because Maria's down payment was below 20%, she likely
   needed PMI.

3. What two things caused Maria's equity to grow over time?
   A. Rent payments and property tax
   B. Paying down her loan and the property appreciating
   C. Her home inspection and title insurance
   D. Her closing costs and escrow account

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A property purchase bundles land, structure, and
legal rights, as covered in Lesson 1.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. A down payment below roughly 20% commonly triggers
a PMI requirement, as covered in Lesson 8.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Equity grows from loan paydown and appreciation —
two separate sources, as covered in Lesson 3.

## Key Takeaways

- Understanding how the pieces fit together is the actual goal of this
