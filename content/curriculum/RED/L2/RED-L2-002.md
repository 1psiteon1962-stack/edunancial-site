## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Explain what a VA loan is, who qualifies, and its key advantages over conventional financing.
2. Define creative financing and name three common structures.
3. Explain how seller financing works from both the buyer's and seller's perspective.
4. Identify situations where creative financing may be more useful than a conventional or VA loan.

## Core Content

Once you know where the residential-to-commercial line sits (RED-L2-001), the next question is: how do you actually pay for a residential property (1–4 units)? Beyond the conventional mortgage, two other paths open up — VA loans for eligible veterans and service members, and creative financing that doesn't route through a bank at all.

### Topic 1: VA Loans — Financing Backed by Service

A **VA loan** is a mortgage backed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, available to eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and some surviving spouses, for financing 1–4 unit residential properties.

**VERIFIED FACT:** VA loans are guaranteed (not directly issued) by the Department of Veterans Affairs; the actual loan is originated by a private lender (bank, credit union, or mortgage company).

Key advantages over a conventional mortgage:
- Often **no down payment required** (0% down is common, subject to lender and loan limits)
- **No private mortgage insurance (PMI)** — which conventional loans typically require below 20% down
- Competitive interest rates, often below conventional market rates
- No prepayment penalty

**REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:** VA loans are a U.S. program. Learners outside the U.S. should research whether their country has equivalent veteran or government-backed mortgage programs.

### Topic 2: Creative Financing — Buying Without a Traditional Mortgage

**Creative financing** refers to structures that let a buyer acquire real estate without obtaining a conventional bank mortgage. The three most common structures:

**1. Seller Financing (Owner Financing)**
The seller acts as the lender. The buyer makes monthly payments directly to the seller under a contract that specifies the interest rate, term, and conditions. The buyer takes possession; the seller holds a lien (or retains title depending on structure) until paid in full.

**2. Subject-To Financing**
The buyer acquires the property while the seller's existing mortgage remains in place — the buyer takes title "subject to" the existing loan. The buyer makes mortgage payments, but the loan remains in the seller's name.

**REGION-SPECIFIC RULE:** Subject-to transactions may trigger a "due on sale" clause in the existing mortgage. Consult a real estate attorney before pursuing this structure.

**3. Lease Option (Rent-to-Own)**
The buyer rents the property with an option to purchase it at a predetermined price within a set time frame. A portion of rent may credit toward the purchase price.

### Topic 3: When to Use Creative Financing

Creative financing may be more useful than a conventional or VA loan when:
- The buyer cannot qualify for a traditional mortgage (credit issues, self-employment, unconventional income)
- The buyer wants to move quickly without a lengthy bank underwriting process
- The seller wants to spread out capital gains recognition rather than receive a lump sum
- The property has issues that make traditional financing difficult (deferred maintenance, title issues)

**ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE:** A self-employed real estate investor with irregular income may struggle to qualify for a conventional mortgage despite having consistent cash flow. A seller who is motivated to close quickly may agree to seller financing at a slightly above-market interest rate, allowing the buyer to acquire the property and refinance into conventional financing once income documentation stabilizes.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming VA eligibility automatically means the best deal — VA loans have a funding fee that adds to overall cost.
- Entering a subject-to deal without legal counsel — triggering the due-on-sale clause can create serious risk.
- Treating creative financing as a last resort only — it can be a strategic first choice when the terms favor the buyer.
- Neglecting to document seller financing properly — verbal agreements are unenforceable in most jurisdictions.

## Key Takeaways

- VA loans offer powerful benefits (0% down, no PMI) to eligible veterans and service members for 1–4 unit residential properties.
- Creative financing covers a range of structures (seller financing, subject-to, lease option) that bypass the conventional mortgage process.
- Each structure has specific risks and legal requirements — always involve a real estate attorney.
- Knowing multiple financing tools makes you a more capable buyer and investor across different market conditions.

## Practical Exercise

A veteran with a 680 credit score wants to purchase a duplex. She has enough cash for a 5% down payment on a conventional loan, but researching further, she discovers she has full VA entitlement.

1. What are the advantages of using a VA loan instead of a conventional loan in her situation?
2. What is one cost associated with VA loans she should evaluate?
3. What creative financing structure would be most appropriate if the property had title issues preventing conventional or VA financing?

*(Answer: 1. No down payment required; no PMI. 2. The VA funding fee. 3. Lease option or seller financing, pending legal review of the title issues.)*
