## RED-L2-010
### Property Management: Self-Manage vs. Hire — What Changes

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Compare self-management and professional property management.
- Identify what changes in cost, time, and risk between the two approaches.
- Explain how portfolio size and personal circumstances affect this decision.

**2. Core Content**
Owning a rental property requires management: finding tenants, collecting rent, handling repairs, and addressing problems. An investor can self-manage or hire a professional property manager, and this choice changes several pieces at once.

Self-management saves the management fee — often a percentage of collected rent, commonly in a range investors should verify locally — but costs time, requires the owner to be reachable (or have a system) for emergencies, and demands some knowledge of landlord-tenant law, which varies significantly by jurisdiction.

Professional management costs money but can save time, bring market knowledge and vendor relationships, and provide a buffer between the owner and tenant disputes. It becomes increasingly valuable as an investor's portfolio grows beyond what they can personally manage, or if the property is far from where the investor lives.

Neither choice is universally correct. A single nearby property might be very manageable alone; a portfolio of properties across several states typically requires professional management, or at least local boots on the ground.

**3. Worked Example**
Raj owns one rental property fifteen minutes from his home and enjoys handling tenant communication himself. He self-manages, saving the management fee, and has built a small local network of reliable contractors for repairs.

As Raj expands to five properties across two states over several years, the math changes. He can no longer personally screen tenants, coordinate repairs, and handle emergency calls across multiple time zones while working his full-time job. He hires a property management company for the out-of-state properties, treating the management fee as the cost of being able to scale beyond his personal time capacity.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does self-management typically save?
   a) Property taxes
   b) The property management fee
   c) Insurance premiums
   d) Closing costs
2. What is a key advantage of professional property management as a portfolio grows?
   a) It eliminates all expenses
   b) It replaces the owner's limited time capacity with a dedicated service
   c) It guarantees higher rents
   d) It removes the need for insurance
3. True or False: Professional management is always the better choice regardless of portfolio size or location.
4. Why did Raj hire management for his out-of-state properties but not his local one?
   a) He no longer liked managing property
   b) His time and proximity could no longer support self-management at that scale
   c) It was legally required
   d) The local property was more valuable

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Self-management primarily saves the fee that would otherwise go to a property manager.
2. As portfolios grow, professional management substitutes for time and local presence the owner may no longer have.
3. The right choice depends on portfolio size, location, and the owner's personal capacity — not a universal rule.
4. Raj's decision followed his changing capacity and geography, not a change in his preferences alone.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Self-management saves money but costs time and requires landlord-tenant knowledge.
- Professional management costs money but can save time and provide expertise, especially at scale or distance.
- The right choice depends on portfolio size, proximity, and the owner's available time and expertise.
- This decision should be revisited as circumstances — not just preferences — change.
