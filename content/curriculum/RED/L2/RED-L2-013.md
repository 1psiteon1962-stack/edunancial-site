## RED-L2-013
### How Entity Choice Changes Financing, Taxes, and Liability

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain how holding a property in an entity versus personal name affects financing options.
- Describe general tax treatment differences at a conceptual level.
- Reinforce how a single structural choice ripples across multiple pieces.

**2. Core Content**
Building on the previous lesson, entity choice is a clear example of one decision moving several pieces simultaneously. Financing: some conventional residential loan programs are designed for individual borrowers and may not be available to LLCs, pushing entity-held properties toward commercial-style financing, which can carry different rates, terms, and down payment requirements. Some investors initially finance in their personal name, then transfer the property into an LLC after closing — a strategy that itself carries considerations, including potential due-on-sale clause implications (introduced in Lesson 5) and financing/insurance updates that a professional should review.

Taxes: entity type affects how rental income and expenses are reported, and rules vary by jurisdiction and specific entity election. This is not a place for assumptions — a tax professional should confirm treatment for an investor's specific situation.

Liability: as covered in the previous lesson, proper entity structuring can help separate personal and property-related liability, but only when maintained correctly.

The Level 2 takeaway is recognizing entity choice as a single lever connected to three separate systems — financing, taxation, and liability — each with its own rules and its own professional to consult.

**3. Worked Example**
Yuki is deciding how to hold a new rental property. She researches and finds that financing directly in an LLC's name would mean a commercial-style loan with a larger down payment and higher rate than a conventional residential mortgage in her personal name would offer.

She discusses options with her lender, attorney, and tax professional. Together, they map out a plan: finance the property personally to access more favorable residential terms, then transfer title into her LLC afterward — with her attorney handling the transfer properly and confirming insurance and lender requirements are met, and her tax professional confirming how this affects her tax reporting going forward.

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. LLC-held properties often require commercial-style financing, which can carry different terms than residential loans.
2. Tax treatment depends on the investor's specific facts and should be confirmed by a professional, not assumed from general education.
3. Transferring title can trigger due-on-sale and other contractual considerations that require professional review.
4. This lesson's core point is that entity choice simultaneously affects financing, taxes, and liability — three interconnected systems.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Entity choice affects financing options, often pushing LLC-held properties toward commercial-style loans.
- Tax treatment varies by entity type and jurisdiction and should be confirmed by a professional.
- Transferring a property into an LLC after financing carries legal and contractual considerations.
- One structural decision — entity choice — connects to financing, taxes, and liability simultaneously.
