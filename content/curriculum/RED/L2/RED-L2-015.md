## RED-L2-015
### Depreciation as a Financial Piece

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define depreciation at a conceptual level in a real estate context.
- Explain why depreciation is treated as a piece with tax implications, not investment advice.
- Recognize the importance of professional tax guidance for depreciation.

**2. Core Content**
Depreciation, in a general real estate and tax context, is a concept that allows the cost of certain property (typically the building, not land) to be accounted for over time, reflecting the idea that a structure wears down. In many jurisdictions, this can have tax implications for property owners, though specific rules, schedules, and eligibility vary significantly and change over time.

This lesson introduces depreciation strictly as an educational concept — a piece on the board that interacts with taxes — not as specific tax advice. The exact treatment of depreciation, including recapture considerations when a property is eventually sold, is a matter for a qualified tax professional, since rules are jurisdiction-specific, change with legislation, and depend on an owner's individual circumstances.

Understanding that this piece exists — and that it can meaningfully affect a property's overall tax picture — is a Level 2 skill. Knowing the exact numbers for any individual's situation is not something this curriculum will ever attempt to calculate or recommend.

**3. Worked Example**
Fatima owns a rental property and has heard other investors mention depreciation as a tax benefit. Rather than assuming she understands how it applies to her, she brings her closing documents and property records to a qualified tax professional, who explains how depreciation may apply to her specific situation, including how it could affect her taxes in the future if she sells the property.

Fatima leaves the meeting understanding the general concept clearly, but relying entirely on her tax professional — not general curriculum content or assumptions from other investors — for the actual numbers that apply to her.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Depreciation generally relates to accounting for a structure's cost over time, with tax implications in many jurisdictions.
2. Because these rules are jurisdiction-specific, change with legislation, and depend on individual facts, general education cannot responsibly give specific numbers.
3. This lesson deliberately avoids prescribing a specific depreciation strategy, staying at the conceptual, educational level.
4. Fatima appropriately sought individualized guidance from a qualified professional rather than generalizing from others' experiences.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Depreciation is a concept that accounts for a structure's cost over time and can carry tax implications.
- Specific rules, schedules, and eligibility vary by jurisdiction and change over time.
- This curriculum teaches the concept, not specific numbers or strategies — those require a qualified tax professional.
- Recognizing where general education ends and individualized professional advice begins is itself a Level 2 skill.
