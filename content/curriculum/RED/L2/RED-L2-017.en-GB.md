## RED-L2-017
### Refinancing: Cash-Out vs. Rate-and-Term

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish between a cash-out refinance and a rate-and-term refinance.
- Explain the general purpose of each.
- Identify how each type changes loan balance, payment, and equity differently.

**2. Core Content**
Refinancing means replacing an existing mortgage with a new one. Two common types are rate-and-term refinancing and cash-out refinancing.

A rate-and-term refinance changes the interest rate, the loan term, or both, without significantly increasing the loan balance beyond what is owed (plus applicable closing costs). Its general purpose is to reduce the monthly payment, shorten or lengthen the payoff timeline, or move from one loan structure to another (such as adjustable-rate to fixed-rate), without extracting additional cash from the property's equity.

A cash-out refinance replaces the existing mortgage with a larger loan, with the difference paid to the borrower in cash. This extracts equity from the property, turning a portion of an illiquid asset back into spendable funds, but it increases the loan balance and, generally, the monthly payment.

Choosing between these — or neither — depends on the borrower's specific goal: lowering costs (rate-and-term) versus accessing capital (cash-out), and each carries its own tradeoffs in equity position and payment size.

**3. Worked Example**
Two homeowners consider refinancing in the same interest-rate environment. Hana refinances her mortgage from a higher rate to a lower one, keeping roughly the same loan balance — a rate-and-term refinance that meaningfully lowers her monthly payment without touching her equity position.

Leon, needing capital to renovate a rental property, does a cash-out refinance on that property instead, taking on a larger loan balance and extracting a portion of his equity as cash. His monthly payment rises, but he now has funds to complete renovations that he expects will increase the property's rental income and value.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the general purpose of a rate-and-term refinance?
   a) To extract equity as cash
   b) To change the rate and/or term without significantly increasing the balance
   c) To sell the property
   d) To avoid property taxes
2. What happens to the loan balance in a cash-out refinance?
   a) It decreases
   b) It generally increases
   c) It stays exactly the same
   d) It becomes zero
3. True or False: A cash-out refinance typically leaves the monthly payment unchanged.
4. Why did Leon choose a cash-out refinance instead of a rate-and-term refinance?
   a) He wanted a lower monthly payment only
   b) He needed capital for renovations
   c) He wanted to sell the property
   d) His rate was already at its lowest possible level

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Rate-and-term refinancing focuses on adjusting rate or term without significantly increasing the loan balance.
2. A cash-out refinance increases the loan balance because the borrower receives the difference in cash.
3. The larger balance in a cash-out refinance generally raises the monthly payment.
4. Leon's specific need for renovation capital made a cash-out refinance the fitting tool, despite the higher payment.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Rate-and-term refinancing adjusts rate or term without significantly increasing loan balance.
- Cash-out refinancing extracts equity as cash by increasing the loan balance.
- Each serves a different goal: lowering cost versus accessing capital.
- The right choice depends on the borrower's specific financial objective, not a one-size-fits-all rule.
