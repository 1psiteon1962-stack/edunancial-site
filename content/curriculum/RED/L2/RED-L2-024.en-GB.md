## RED-L2-024
### Working With Contractors: Scope, Cost, and Timeline Interactions

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the relationship between scope, cost, and timeline in a renovation project.
- Identify common causes of cost and timeline overruns.
- Describe practical steps to manage contractor relationships responsibly.

**2. Core Content**
Scope, cost, and timeline are three interconnected pieces in any renovation project — often described informally as a triangle where changing one usually affects at least one other. Expanding scope (adding more work) tends to increase both cost and timeline. Compressing timeline (wanting work done faster) often increases cost (rush fees, overtime labour) or forces scope reductions. Cutting cost aggressively can extend timeline (fewer workers, cheaper materials with longer lead times) or force scope reductions.

Common causes of overruns include unclear or verbal-only agreements, scope creep (small additions that individually seem minor but accumulate), unexpected conditions discovered during work (such as issues behind walls), and contractors juggling too many simultaneous projects.

Practical management steps include detailed written contracts specifying scope and materials, phased payment schedules tied to completed milestones rather than large upfront payments, contingency budgets (commonly 10–20%, though this varies by project) for unexpected issues, and regular site visits or check-ins rather than only reviewing at the end.

**3. Worked Example**
Before starting a kitchen renovation, Bea gets a detailed written contract specifying exact materials, a payment schedule tied to milestones (not a large upfront deposit), and a written change-order process for any scope additions. She also sets aside a 15% contingency budget beyond the contracted price.

Midway through, the contractor discovers outdated wiring behind a wall that needs to be addressed for safety. Because Bea had a contingency budget and a clear change-order process already in place, this unexpected discovery becomes a manageable, documented adjustment rather than a budget crisis or a dispute over unclear expectations.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What tends to happen to cost and timeline when scope increases?
   a) Both tend to decrease
   b) They tend to increase
   c) Neither changes
   d) Cost decreases while timeline increases
2. What is "scope creep"?
   a) A pest control issue
   b) Small work additions that individually seem minor but accumulate over a project
   c) A type of financing
   d) A zoning violation
3. True or False: Verbal agreements are generally sufficient and just as protective as detailed written contracts for renovation projects.
4. Why was Bea able to handle the unexpected wiring discovery smoothly?
   a) She had no contract at all
   b) She had a contingency budget and a clear change-order process in place beforehand
   c) She paid the contractor the full amount upfront
   d) The wiring issue was not actually a problem

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Expanding scope generally increases both cost and timeline, all else equal.
2. Scope creep refers to small additions accumulating into significant unplanned cost and time.
3. Detailed written contracts provide far more protection and clarity than verbal-only agreements.
4. Bea's advance planning — contingency budget and change-order process — turned a surprise into a manageable adjustment.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Scope, cost, and timeline are interconnected; changing one typically affects the others.
- Common overrun causes include scope creep, unclear agreements, and unexpected discoveries.
- Detailed written contracts, milestone-based payments, and contingency budgets manage these risks.
- Proactive planning turns unexpected issues into manageable adjustments rather than crises.
