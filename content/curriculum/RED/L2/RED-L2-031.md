## RED-L2-031
### Title Insurance and Liens: Protecting Ownership

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define title insurance and its general purpose.
- Explain what a lien is and how it can affect a property.
- Describe the relationship between title search, title insurance, and liens.

**2. Core Content**
Building on the previous lesson, title insurance is a policy that protects a buyer (and typically a lender) against certain losses arising from defects in a property's title that were not discovered during the title search — such as a previously unknown heir with a claim, a forged historical document, or an unresolved prior lien. Unlike many insurance types that protect against future events, title insurance generally protects against past, undiscovered issues.

A lien is a legal claim against a property, often securing a debt or obligation, that must generally be resolved before clear title can transfer to a new owner. Liens can arise from unpaid property taxes, unpaid contractor bills (mechanic's liens), unresolved court judgments, or an existing mortgage that has not yet been paid off at closing.

The title search (previous lesson) is the investigative process; title insurance is the protective policy that stands behind that investigation, covering the buyer if something was missed. Together, they form a key layer of protection over what is often a buyer's largest single asset.

**3. Worked Example**
After closing on her new home, Renata discovers that a contractor hired by the previous owner years earlier had never been fully paid and had filed a mechanic's lien against the property — one that was somehow missed during the title search process.

Because Renata purchased an owner's title insurance policy at closing, her title insurer steps in to resolve the claim according to the policy's terms, protecting Renata from having to pay the old debt out of pocket or risk losing clear ownership of her home over an issue that existed before she ever purchased the property.

   c) Her mortgage lender's goodwill

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Title insurance generally protects against certain past, undiscovered title defects.
2. A lien is a legal claim against a property, typically securing an unpaid debt or obligation.
3. The title search investigates; title insurance protects against what that investigation might miss — they are complementary, not identical.
4. Renata's title insurance policy covered the undiscovered mechanic's lien according to its terms.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Title insurance protects against certain past, undiscovered defects in a property's title.
- A lien is a legal claim against a property that generally must be resolved before clear title can transfer.
- The title search investigates potential issues; title insurance provides a backstop if something is missed.
- Together, these protections guard one of a buyer's largest financial commitments.
