## RED-L2-038
### Property Diversification: One Property vs. a Portfolio

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general concept of diversification applied to real estate.
- Identify risks of concentrating real estate holdings in a single property or market.
- Describe general approaches to diversifying real estate exposure.

**2. Core Content**
Diversification, a concept introduced more broadly in other Edunancial tracks, applies to real estate as well. An investor holding a single property in a single market carries concentrated exposure to that specific property's condition and that specific local market's economic conditions — a factory closure, a natural disaster, or a local economic downturn could disproportionately affect an investor whose real estate wealth sits entirely in one place.

Diversification approaches include holding multiple properties across different markets (spreading geographic risk), holding different property types (residential, commercial, and so on, spreading sector risk), or blending direct ownership with more liquid, diversified vehicles like REITs (previous lesson).

This does not mean every investor needs, or can realistically achieve, broad diversification — many investors reasonably start and stay concentrated in a single property or local market due to capital constraints, familiarity, or management capacity. The Level 2 skill is recognising concentration risk as a real, identifiable factor, not eliminating it entirely, which may not be practical or necessary for every investor's goals.

**3. Worked Example**
Marco owns three rental properties, all in the same small town, all purchased because he grew up there and knows the market well. When the town's largest employer announces a major layoff, local rental demand and property values in that specific market face real pressure — a risk Marco had not previously considered, since all three of his properties share the same underlying local economic exposure.

After this experience, Marco decides that his next acquisition will be in a different market with a different economic base, not because his hometown was a bad market, but specifically to reduce his concentrated exposure to any single local economy going forward.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What risk does holding all properties in a single local market create?
   a) No additional risk
   b) Concentrated exposure to that specific market's local economic conditions
   c) Guaranteed appreciation
   d) Automatic diversification
2. What is one way to diversify real estate exposure, per this lesson?
   a) Buying only one property forever
   b) Holding properties across different markets or property types, or blending in REITs
   c) Avoiding real estate entirely
   d) Ignoring local economic conditions
3. True or False: Every investor must achieve broad geographic diversification to invest responsibly in real estate.
4. What prompted Marco to consider diversifying his next acquisition into a different market?
   a) He disliked his hometown
   b) He recognised concentrated local economic exposure after a layoff affected his market
   c) His properties lost all their value
   d) A new law required it

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Concentrating properties in a single market ties an investor's outcomes closely to that market's specific conditions.
2. Diversifying across markets, property types, or blending in REITs are all approaches to reducing concentration risk.
3. Broad diversification is not required or always practical for every investor; recognising the risk is the key Level 2 skill.
4. Marco's real experience with local economic exposure prompted him to consider diversifying future acquisitions.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Concentrating real estate holdings in one property or market creates concentrated exposure to that market's specific conditions.
- Diversification approaches include geographic spread, property type variety, and blending in liquid vehicles like REITs.
- Broad diversification is not mandatory or always practical, but recognising concentration risk is an important Level 2 skill.
- Real experience with local risk can reasonably inform future diversification decisions.
