## RED-L2-047
### Reading a Real Estate Market Report Like an Investor

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Identify common metrics found in real estate market reports.
- Explain how to interpret these metrics in combination, rather than individually.
- Apply earlier lessons (cap rate, market cycles, vacancy) to interpret a sample report.

**2. Core Content**
Real estate market reports commonly include metrics such as median sale price, days on market (average time a listing takes to sell), inventory levels (number of active listings), vacancy rates (Lesson 9), and sometimes cap rate ranges for investment property (Lesson 26). A Level 2 investor reads these metrics together, not in isolation, since each one adds context to the others.

For example, a rising median price alongside falling days on market and low inventory generally suggests a competitive, demand-heavy market (echoing Lesson 34's supply/demand discussion). A rising median price alongside rising days on market and growing inventory could suggest a market that is cooling even though prices haven't dropped yet — prices are often a lagging indicator, moving after shifts in the other metrics.

This lesson does not teach the learner to predict markets, but to read a market report critically, combining metrics into a coherent picture rather than fixating on a single headline number, such as median price alone.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing a local market report, Yasmin notices median sale price is still rising, but days on market has increased over the past two quarters and inventory has grown noticeably. Rather than concluding the market is simply "hot" because of the rising median price, she interprets the fuller picture as an early sign of a potentially cooling market, since buyer urgency (reflected in days on market and inventory) appears to be softening even before price growth has caught up.

This more complete reading informs Yasmin's own timeline for a potential purchase, encouraging her to negotiate a bit more assertively rather than assuming she needs to rush to compete in an overheated market, based on price alone.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does "days on market" generally measure?
   a) The property's age
   b) The average time a listing takes to sell
   c) The mortgage interest rate
   d) The property's insurance premium
2. Why might rising median price alongside rising days on market signal a cooling market?
   a) Price is often a lagging indicator, moving after shifts in demand-related metrics
   b) It never signals anything
   c) Rising days on market always means rising demand
   d) These metrics are unrelated
3. True or False: A single metric like median sale price tells the complete story of a market's condition.
4. What did Yasmin conclude from reading multiple metrics together, rather than price alone?
   a) The market was clearly overheating further
   b) The market showed early signs of potentially cooling, despite still-rising price
   c) Nothing could be concluded
   d) She should rush to buy immediately

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. a  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Days on market measures the average time it takes for listings to sell in a given period.
2. Price often lags other market indicators, so rising days on market alongside rising price can signal an early cooling trend.
3. A complete market picture requires combining multiple metrics, not relying on price alone.
4. Yasmin's combined reading of days on market, inventory, and price led her to a more nuanced, less reactive conclusion.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Market reports combine metrics like median price, days on market, inventory, and vacancy rates.
- These metrics should be read together, since each provides context for interpreting the others.
- Price is often a lagging indicator, meaning shifts in other metrics can signal changes before price reflects them.
- Reading a market report critically means avoiding fixation on a single headline number.
