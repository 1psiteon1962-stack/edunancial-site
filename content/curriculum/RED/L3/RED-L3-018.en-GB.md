# RED-L3-018: Why Seller Financing Can Beat a Bank Loan in the Right Deal

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain how seller financing works
- Explain why a seller might prefer to finance the sale themselves
- Identify a situation where seller financing could benefit both parties

## Core Content

In **seller financing**, instead of the buyer getting a loan from a bank, the seller acts as the lender: the buyer makes payments directly to the seller under agreed-upon terms, and the seller holds a lien on the property until it's paid off, similar to how a bank would.

Why would a seller agree to this instead of just taking a lump sum from a bank-financed buyer? A few reasons show up repeatedly: the seller may want a steady income stream (with interest) rather than a lump sum, especially in retirement. The property may not easily qualify for conventional financing (as in Lesson 17), making seller financing the only realistic path to a sale. Or the terms can be structured to benefit both sides — a higher price for the seller in exchange for below-market interest rate for the buyer, for instance.

[VERIFIED FACT] Seller financing terms are privately negotiated between buyer and seller rather than set by standardised bank guidelines, which allows more flexibility — but also means both parties typically benefit from clear legal documentation and, often, professional guidance to protect their interests.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] A retiring landlord owns a small rental property outright and wants steady monthly income rather than a large lump sum that would trigger a big capital gains tax bill in one year. She agrees to seller-finance the sale to a buyer at an agreed price, an agreed interest rate, and a 10-year term with a balloon payment at the end. The buyer avoids conventional loan qualification hurdles, and the seller receives predictable monthly income (with interest) instead of a single taxable lump sum.

## Practice Quiz

1. In seller financing, who acts as the lender?
2. Name two reasons a seller might prefer this over a conventional cash sale.
3. Why is clear legal documentation especially important in a seller-financed deal?

## Answer Key

1. The seller.
2. Any two of: steady income with interest instead of a lump sum, avoiding a large one-time capital gains tax hit, or enabling a sale when the property or buyer might not qualify for conventional financing.
3. Because the terms are privately negotiated rather than standardised by a bank, so a clear written agreement protects both parties if a dispute or default arises.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the defining structural feature of seller financing.
2. This checks understanding of the seller's motivations, which are the "why" behind this financing method existing.
3. This reinforces that flexibility without standardisation increases the importance of careful documentation.

## Key Takeaways
- In seller financing, the seller acts as the lender and the buyer makes payments directly to them.
- Sellers may prefer this for steady income, tax timing reasons, or to enable a sale that conventional financing couldn't support.
- Because terms are privately negotiated, clear legal documentation is especially important for both parties.
