# RED-L3-044: Why Government-Backed Loans Have Limits for Investors

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain why many government-backed loan programmes require owner-occupancy
- Explain why this limits their direct usefulness for a typical rental property investor
- Identify one indirect way these programmes can still fit into an investment strategy

## Core Content

Certain government-backed loan programmes offer advantages like lower down payments or more flexible qualification standards. Here's the "why" these usually don't apply directly to typical investment property purchases: many of these programmes exist specifically to support owner-occupied housing, and generally require the borrower to live in the property as their primary residence for a specified period, not use it purely as a rental investment from day one.

This creates a real limitation for a straightforward buy-and-hold rental investor: these programmes generally aren't designed as, or available for, pure investment property financing. [VERIFIED FACT] Owner-occupancy requirements, exact terms, and eligibility rules vary by specific programme and change over time, so current programme guidelines should always be verified directly rather than assumed.

However, this doesn't mean these programmes are irrelevant to an investor's broader strategy. A common indirect approach some investors use — sometimes called "house hacking" — involves purchasing a small multi-unit property (within the 1–4 unit residential range from Lesson 16), living in one unit to satisfy an owner-occupancy requirement, and renting out the other units, gaining real estate investing experience and rental income while still meeting the programme's occupancy rule.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor wants to use a government-backed loan programme's favourable terms but knows it requires owner-occupancy. Rather than purchasing a pure rental property (which wouldn't qualify), she buys a duplex, moves into one unit to satisfy the occupancy requirement, and rents out the second unit. [ASSUMPTION] After meeting the required occupancy period, she may have options to move out and convert the property to a full rental, having used the programme's terms as a stepping stone into her first investment property rather than a direct violation of its intended purpose.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why do many government-backed loan programmes generally not apply directly to pure investment property purchases?
2. What is "house hacking," as described in this lesson?
3. Why should current programme guidelines always be verified directly rather than assumed?

## Answer Key

1. Because these programmes generally require owner-occupancy for a specified period, since they're designed to support primary housing, not pure investment purchases.
2. Purchasing a small multi-unit property, living in one unit to satisfy an occupancy requirement, and renting out the other units.
3. Because eligibility rules, occupancy requirements, and exact terms vary by specific programme and can change over time.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the central limitation the lesson explains.
2. This provides the practical, indirect strategy some investors use to still benefit from these programmes.
3. This reinforces appropriate caution given how programme-specific and time-sensitive these rules can be.

## Key Takeaways
- Many government-backed loan programmes require owner-occupancy, generally limiting their direct use for pure investment property purchases.
- "House hacking" — living in one unit of a small multi-unit property while renting the others — is a common indirect way investors use these programmes as an entry point.
- Programme rules and eligibility requirements vary and change over time, so current guidelines should always be verified directly.
