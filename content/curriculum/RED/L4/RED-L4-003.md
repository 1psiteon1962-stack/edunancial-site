# IRR vs Cap Rate: Why Time Value Changes the Picture

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the core difference between a point-in-time metric and a time-weighted metric.
- Explain why the timing of cash flows changes an investment's real return.
- Identify situations where IRR is a more useful tool than cap rate.
- Interpret a basic IRR comparison between two investment scenarios.

## Core Content
Cap rate is a snapshot — it tells you the relationship between income and price at one moment. IRR (Internal Rate of Return) is a full-story metric — it accounts for every cash flow over the entire holding period, including the timing of when money comes in and goes out, and factors in the time value of money (a dollar today is worth more than a dollar in five years).

Here is the why: two deals can have the same average annual return but very different IRRs because of when the cash arrives. A property that pays you steadily every year has a different real value than one that pays you nothing for four years and then a large lump sum in year five, even if the total dollars are identical. Money received sooner can be reinvested sooner, so it is worth more.

This is why professional investors and syndicators use IRR to compare deals with different hold periods, different cash flow timing, or a mix of cash flow and a future sale. Cap rate cannot do this because it only looks at one snapshot, usually year one.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Deal A returns $10,000 a year for five years ($50,000 total). Deal B returns nothing for four years, then $50,000 in year five (same $50,000 total). Both have the identical total dollar return, but Deal A has a meaningfully higher IRR because the investor received money earlier and could reinvest it, while Deal B's investor waited with their capital tied up and earning nothing until the final year.

## Practice Quiz
**Q1. What is the main advantage of IRR over cap rate?**
A) IRR is easier to calculate by hand
B) IRR accounts for the timing of all cash flows over the full holding period
C) IRR ignores the time value of money
D) IRR only applies to commercial property

**Q2. Why is a dollar received today worth more than a dollar received in five years?**
A) Inflation always makes future dollars worth more
B) The dollar today can be reinvested and grow over that time
C) Cap rates always rise over five years
D) Money loses no value over time

**Q3. Two deals return the same total dollars over five years. Deal A pays evenly each year; Deal B pays nothing until a lump sum in year five. Which will generally have the higher IRR?**
A) Deal B, because lump sums are always better
B) Deal A, because it delivers cash earlier for reinvestment
C) They will always have identical IRR
D) IRR cannot be calculated for either

**Q4. Cap rate is best described as which type of metric?**
A) A time-weighted, full-holding-period metric
B) A point-in-time snapshot metric
C) A metric that only applies after a property sells
D) A tax calculation tool

**Q5. Why would a syndicator comparing two multi-year deals with different cash flow timing prefer IRR over cap rate?**
A) Cap rate is illegal to use in syndications
B) IRR captures the full timing and size of all cash flows, making it comparable across different structures
C) IRR requires no math
D) Cap rate always overstates returns

## Answer Key
Q1: B
Q2: B
Q3: B
Q4: B
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** IRR incorporates when cash flows happen, not just their total amount, which cap rate cannot do.

**Q2.** The time value of money reflects the opportunity to reinvest and earn returns on money received sooner.

**Q3.** Earlier cash flows increase IRR because that money can be reinvested sooner, even when totals are equal.

**Q4.** Cap rate reflects one moment (usually year one), unlike IRR which spans the full hold.

**Q5.** IRR gives an apples-to-apples comparison across deals with different timing structures, which cap rate cannot provide.

## Key Takeaways
- Cap rate is a snapshot; IRR is a full-story, time-weighted return metric.
- The timing of cash flows changes real investment value, even when total dollars are equal.
- IRR is the right tool for comparing deals with different hold periods or cash flow patterns.
- Use cap rate for a quick first look, and IRR for a full comparison before committing capital.
