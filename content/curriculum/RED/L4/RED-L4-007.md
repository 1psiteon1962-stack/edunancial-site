# Interest Rate Sensitivity: Why Cap Rates and Rates Move Together

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the relationship between the cost of borrowing and required investor returns.
- Explain why rising interest rates put upward pressure on cap rates.
- Identify how rising cap rates affect property values at a constant NOI.
- Apply this relationship to timing decisions around buying or selling.

## Core Content
Interest rates and cap rates are connected because both reflect the cost, and the required return, of capital. When interest rates rise, borrowing money to buy real estate becomes more expensive, and investors can also earn more from safer alternatives like bonds. Over time, this typically pushes cap rates upward too, because buyers demand a bigger income cushion to make real estate competitive with those safer, now higher-yielding alternatives.

Here is the why this matters: at a fixed NOI, a rising cap rate means a falling property value, because Value = NOI / Cap Rate. This is a direct mathematical relationship, not just a market feeling. An investor holding property through a period of rising rates may see the market value of their holdings decline even if the property's income and occupancy have not changed at all.

This relationship is not instant or perfectly linked — REGION-SPECIFIC RULE and market-specific factors like local supply, demand, and investor sentiment also drive cap rates — but over multi-year periods, the direction is consistent enough that ignoring interest rate trends when analyzing a deal is a significant blind spot.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A property generates $60,000 NOI. At a 5% cap rate, it is worth $1,200,000. If rising interest rates push the market's required cap rate to 6% while NOI stays the same, the same property is now worth $60,000 / 0.06 = $1,000,000 — a $200,000 drop in value with zero change in the property's actual performance.

D) Selling is always better when rates are rising

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Higher rates raise the cost of capital and the return available elsewhere, pushing required real estate returns (cap rates) up too.

**Q2.** Since Value = NOI / Cap Rate, a higher cap rate with unchanged NOI produces a lower calculated value.

**Q3.** $80,000 / 0.08 = $1,000,000.

**Q4.** Understanding rate-driven cap rate movement helps an investor judge whether current market conditions favor selling or holding.

**Q5.** The relationship is directional and meaningful over time but is moderated by local market-specific factors.

## Key Takeaways
- Cap rates and interest rates tend to move in the same direction because both reflect the cost and required return of capital.
- At a fixed NOI, a rising cap rate mathematically lowers property value, and a falling cap rate raises it.
- This relationship is directional, not perfectly immediate, and is influenced by local market conditions too.
- Tracking interest rate trends helps time both acquisition and disposition decisions more effectively.
