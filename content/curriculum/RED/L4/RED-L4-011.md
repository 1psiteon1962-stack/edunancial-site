# Debt Service Coverage Ratio: Why Lenders Weight It Over Cap Rate

## Learning Objectives
- Calculate DSCR from NOI and annual debt service.
- Explain why lenders prioritize DSCR over cap rate.
- Explain how a low DSCR limits the loan amount a property can support.
- Identify how rising interest rates affect DSCR at a fixed loan amount.

## Core Content
Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) equals NOI divided by annual debt service (the total yearly mortgage payment, principal and interest). VERIFIED FACT: most commercial lenders require a minimum DSCR, commonly in the 1.20-1.35 range, though this varies by lender and property type and should always be confirmed with the specific lender.

Here is the why lenders care about DSCR more than cap rate: cap rate tells an investor about return relative to price, but it tells a lender nothing directly about whether the property generates enough income to safely cover the loan payment. DSCR answers exactly that question. A DSCR of 1.25 means the property generates 25% more income than needed to cover the debt payment — a cushion the lender needs in case income dips or expenses rise unexpectedly.

This is why DSCR, not just appraised value, often determines the maximum loan a lender will approve. If a property's NOI cannot support the required DSCR at a given loan amount, the lender will reduce the loan size regardless of how attractive the cap rate or appraisal looks — this is called being "DSCR constrained" rather than "value constrained." 

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A property has NOI of $90,000. A lender requires a minimum 1.25 DSCR. Maximum allowable annual debt service = $90,000 / 1.25 = $72,000. If the requested loan amount's annual payment would be $85,000, the lender will not approve that loan size, even if the appraised value supports a larger loan — the borrower must put more money down or accept a smaller loan to meet the DSCR requirement.

**Q5. How does a rising interest rate affect DSCR if the loan amount stays fixed?**

## Answer Key
Q1: B
Q2: B
Q3: B
Q4: B
Q5: B

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** DSCR = NOI / annual debt service, showing how much income cushion exists above the loan payment.

**Q2.** DSCR is the metric most directly tied to loan repayment safety, which is the lender's core concern.

**Q3.** $100,000 / 1.25 = $80,000 maximum annual debt service.

**Q4.** DSCR-constrained means income is the limiting factor on loan size, even if the property would otherwise appraise for more.

**Q5.** Higher rates increase debt service payments, which lowers DSCR (NOI unchanged, debt service up) at the same loan amount.

## Key Takeaways
- DSCR = NOI / annual debt service, and it measures income cushion above the loan payment.
- Lenders often prioritize DSCR over cap rate because it directly reflects repayment safety.
- A property can be DSCR constrained, capping the loan size even when appraised value would support more.
- Rising interest rates worsen DSCR at a fixed loan amount, which can reduce how much a property can be financed.
