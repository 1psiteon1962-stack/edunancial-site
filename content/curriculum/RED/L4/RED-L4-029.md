# Break-Even Analysis: Why Knowing Your Number Prevents Panic Selling

## Learning Objectives
- Calculate a property's break-even occupancy rate.
- Explain why knowing the break-even point in advance supports better decision-making during a downturn.
- Explain the relationship between leverage level and break-even occupancy.
- Apply break-even thinking to assess how much vacancy a property can withstand.

## Core Content
Break-even occupancy is the minimum occupancy rate a property needs to cover its operating expenses and debt service. VERIFIED FACT: it is calculated by dividing total fixed costs (operating expenses plus debt service) by potential gross rental income at full occupancy.

Here is the why calculating this number in advance matters: investors who do not know their break-even point tend to react emotionally when vacancy rises during a market downturn, sometimes panic-selling at a low point in the market cycle (recall Lesson 5) because they do not know whether their current occupancy level is actually a real problem or simply within a tolerable range. An investor who has already calculated that their property can sustain occupancy as low as 65% and still cover all costs can look at a temporary dip to 80% occupancy with calm confidence, rather than reactive fear.

This is also directly connected to leverage (Lesson 12): higher leverage increases fixed debt service, which raises the break-even occupancy rate needed to cover costs, leaving a smaller cushion against vacancy. A heavily leveraged property has less room to absorb a downturn than a conservatively financed one with the same NOI potential, which is an important part of matching leverage level to risk tolerance and market cycle position.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A property has potential gross rental income of $120,000 at full occupancy, with fixed operating expenses and debt service totaling $84,000 a year. Break-even occupancy = $84,000 / $120,000 = 70%. If a recession pushes occupancy down to 78%, the investor, knowing their break-even point is 70%, can recognize they still have an 8-percentage-point cushion and are in no immediate danger, rather than assuming any vacancy increase is a crisis requiring an emergency sale.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Break-even occupancy shows the minimum occupancy level needed to cover all fixed costs, calculated as fixed costs divided by full-occupancy potential income.

**Q2.** Understanding the safety cushion allows for calm, informed decisions rather than reactive fear during temporary vacancy increases.

**Q3.** Since debt service is a fixed cost included in the break-even calculation, higher leverage raises the occupancy threshold required to cover costs.

**Q4.** $90,000 / $150,000 = 0.60, or 60%.

**Q5.** A higher break-even occupancy threshold means less occupancy can be lost before the property fails to cover its costs.

## Key Takeaways
- Break-even occupancy shows the minimum occupancy needed to cover fixed operating and debt costs.
- Knowing this number in advance supports calm, informed decisions rather than panic selling during a downturn.
- Higher leverage raises break-even occupancy, reducing a property's cushion against vacancy.
- Break-even analysis should be paired with leverage decisions to match risk tolerance to market cycle exposure.
