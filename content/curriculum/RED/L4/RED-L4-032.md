# Syndication Analysis: Why the Waterfall Structure Matters

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the basic structure of a real estate syndication.
- Define preferred return and explain its role in a waterfall structure.
- Explain why the order of a profit split, not just the percentages, affects investor outcomes.
- Identify why understanding the waterfall matters before investing passively.

## Core Content
A real estate syndication pools capital from multiple passive investors (limited partners) to acquire a property managed by a sponsor (general partner) who sources, finances, and operates the deal. VERIFIED FACT: profits are typically distributed according to a "waterfall" structure — a defined sequence describing who gets paid, and how much, at each stage of the deal's cash flow and eventual sale proceeds.

Here is the why the waterfall structure matters as much as the headline projected return: a common structure includes a preferred return (often 6-8%, ESTIMATE and deal-specific) that limited partners receive first, before the general partner participates in any profit split above that threshold. After the preferred return is met, remaining profit is typically split according to an agreed percentage (for example, 70% to limited partners and 30% to the general partner, though structures vary widely), and some waterfalls include multiple tiers that shift the split further in the general partner's favor as returns increase further.

This is why two syndications advertising the same projected overall return can produce very different actual outcomes for a passive investor depending on the waterfall's structure — where the preferred return is set, whether it is cumulative or non-cumulative, and how many tiers exist above it. Reading and understanding the waterfall section of the offering documents is essential before committing capital, not an optional detail to skip past.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: Deal A offers a 7% preferred return with a straightforward 70/30 split above that threshold. Deal B advertises the same overall 15% projected return but structures it with a lower 5% preferred return and a 50/50 split above that threshold, meaning the general partner captures a larger share of any performance above the preferred return. If the deal performs better than projected, limited partners in Deal A capture more of that upside than limited partners in Deal B, even though both deals advertised similar headline projected returns going in.

## Key Takeaways
- A syndication pools passive investor capital under a general partner who sources and manages the deal.
- A preferred return typically pays limited partners first, before further profit is split with the general partner.
- The specific waterfall structure, not just the headline projected return, determines actual investor outcomes.
- Understanding the waterfall in the offering documents is essential before committing capital to a passive syndication.
