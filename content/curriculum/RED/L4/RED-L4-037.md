# BRRRR Strategy Analysis: Why Recycling Capital Accelerates Growth

## Learning Objectives
- Explain each step of the BRRRR strategy.
- Explain why recycling capital accelerates portfolio growth compared to using new capital for each purchase.
- Identify the key risk points where a BRRRR deal can fail to work as planned.
- Explain why accurate after-repair value estimation is critical to this strategy.

## Core Content
BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. An investor buys a property below market value (often distressed or in need of work), rehabs it to increase its value and rentability (connecting to Lesson 26's renovation ROI analysis), rents it out, then refinances based on the new, higher after-repair value to pull out most or all of their original invested capital (connecting to Lesson 13's refinance analysis), and repeats the process with a new property using the recycled capital.

Here is the why this accelerates growth compared to a traditional buy-and-hold approach: if an investor can successfully pull out most or all of their original capital through the refinance step, that same initial capital can be used again on the next property, and again after that, allowing portfolio growth that is not limited by how much new capital the investor can save or raise between purchases. In a well-executed BRRRR deal, the investor essentially achieves near-infinite return on their original invested capital, since that capital is no longer tied up in the property at all after refinancing.

This is why accurate after-repair value (ARV) estimation is critical — the entire strategy depends on the property appraising high enough post-renovation to support a refinance loan large enough to return most or all of the original capital, following the same DSCR (Lesson 11) and LTV (Lesson 12) constraints a lender applies to any refinance. An overly optimistic ARV estimate, or a rehab that costs more than budgeted, can leave meaningfully more capital trapped in the property than planned, undermining the strategy's core benefit.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: An investor buys a distressed property for $150,000 and spends $50,000 on rehab, for a total investment of $200,000. After renovation, the property appraises for an after-repair value of $280,000. Refinancing at 75% LTV provides a new loan of $210,000 — enough to pay off any short-term acquisition financing and return the investor's original $200,000 in full, leaving them owning a rented, cash-flowing property with essentially none of their own capital still tied up in the deal, ready to redeploy that same $200,000 into the next property.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** The strategy follows this specific five-step sequence: Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat.

**Q2.** Recovering most or all of the original investment through refinancing allows that capital to be redeployed repeatedly, rather than remaining locked in a single property.

**Q3.** Since the refinance loan is based on the post-renovation appraised value, an inaccurate ARV estimate directly threatens the strategy's ability to return the investor's capital.

**Q4.** The $210,000 refinance loan was enough to return the full $200,000 originally invested, leaving little to no capital trapped in the property.

**Q5.** Cost overruns or a lower-than-expected appraisal both directly threaten the amount of capital the investor can recover through refinancing.

## Key Takeaways
- BRRRR follows Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat, aiming to recycle the same capital across multiple properties.
- Successfully recovering most or all original capital through refinancing allows accelerated, less capital-constrained portfolio growth.
- Accurate after-repair value estimation is essential, since the refinance loan amount depends directly on the appraised value.
- Cost overruns or an overly optimistic ARV estimate are the key risks that can trap more capital in the deal than planned.
