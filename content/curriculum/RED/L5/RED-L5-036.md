# Real Estate Development Feasibility: Orchestrating Land Cost, Construction Cost, Market Absorption, and Financing Stack

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a large trading bloc suddenly imposing steep tariffs on a broad category of imported manufactured goods, raising costs for companies that depend on those imports as inputs. As tariffs create genuine uncertainty about construction costs mid-planning, a developer conducting a ground-up feasibility analysis discovers that land cost, construction cost, market absorption, and the full financing stack must be orchestrated together as a single, integrated feasibility model.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why land acquisition cost should be evaluated specifically against the property's genuine development potential rather than its current, as-is use.
- Synthesize this level's earlier Lesson 23 and Lesson 8 discussions into this lesson's treatment of total construction cost.
- Explain why genuine market absorption assumptions, connecting to this level's Lesson 31 discussion, must be honestly, conservatively orchestrated into the feasibility model.
- Explain why the full financing stack must be orchestrated together as a single, integrated model, rather than analyzing each financing layer in isolation.

## Core Content
VERIFIED FACT: land acquisition cost represents the foundational input into a development feasibility analysis, and should specifically be evaluated against the property's genuine development potential, echoing this level's earlier discussion of development value differing meaningfully from as-is value.

Here is the "why" total construction cost requires genuine cost uncertainty orchestration, synthesizing this level's earlier discussions: a development feasibility analysis's construction cost input should specifically incorporate a genuine, current cost assessment along with a realistic contingency allowance for potential further cost changes. This is why genuine market absorption assumptions must be honestly, conservatively orchestrated: a genuinely sound analysis should apply a realistic, appropriately conservative assumption regarding leasing or sale speed, rather than assuming an optimistic, immediate full-absorption scenario. This is why the full financing stack must be orchestrated together as a single, integrated model: construction debt, mezzanine or equity financing, and eventual permanent financing must be modeled together, since each layer's genuine availability and cost depends significantly on the other layers actually functioning as planned.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: A developer conducting a feasibility analysis specifically evaluates land acquisition cost against genuine development potential, orchestrates a genuine, current construction cost assessment with appropriate contingency, and applies a realistic, conservative market absorption assumption. This developer also models the full financing stack as a single, integrated system, confirming each layer's genuine availability and cost remain compatible throughout the project's full timeline.
