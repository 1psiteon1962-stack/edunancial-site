## Core Content

### What Are Paper Assets?

Paper assets are financial instruments that represent ownership or a creditor relationship. Unlike real estate (which is tangible) or a business (which you operate), paper assets exist as legal claims recorded on paper — or today, in digital ledgers.

The three primary categories of paper assets are:

1. **Equities** — stocks and funds representing ownership in companies
2. **Fixed Income** — bonds and notes representing loans to governments or corporations
3. **Funds** — pooled investment vehicles (mutual funds, ETFs, index funds) that hold equities or fixed income

Each category has different risk profiles, return patterns, and roles in a portfolio.

### How Paper Assets Generate Returns

Paper assets generate returns through two primary mechanisms:

**1. Capital Appreciation**
The price of the asset rises over time. A stock purchased at $50 that grows to $80 produces a $30 gain in capital value.

**2. Income**
Many paper assets pay regular income:
- **Dividends** — companies distribute a portion of profits to shareholders
- **Interest** — bonds pay a fixed or variable rate of interest (the coupon)
- **Fund distributions** — funds pass through the dividends and interest from their holdings

The combination of capital appreciation and income creates total return — the true measure of investment performance.

### The Difference Between Saving and Investing

Saving places money in accounts where it earns a guaranteed but low return (savings accounts, CDs, money market accounts). Saving preserves capital.

Investing places money in paper assets where the return is variable but historically higher. Investing builds wealth.

The average long-term return of a diversified equity index fund (such as a U.S. total market fund) has historically been approximately 8–10% per year, far above the rate of inflation. Savers lose purchasing power over time. Investors build it.

### Why Paper Assets Are the Starting Point for Most Investors

Paper assets are the most accessible asset class:

- **Low entry cost** — you can begin with as little as $1 through fractional share purchases
- **High liquidity** — most paper assets can be converted to cash within days
- **Passive management** — index funds require no active management
- **Tax-advantaged accounts** — 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, and HSA accounts defer or eliminate taxes on gains
- **Automatic investing** — payroll deductions and automatic monthly contributions make investing effortless

These characteristics make paper assets the foundation of financial competency for most people before they accumulate enough capital for real estate or business ownership.

### The Financial Competency Distinction

Knowing that the stock market exists is financial literacy.

Understanding how equities, bonds, and funds generate returns — and building a systematic investment practice — is financial competency.
