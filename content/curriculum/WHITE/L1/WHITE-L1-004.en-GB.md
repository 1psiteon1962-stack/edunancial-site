# What Is a Bond, Really? (You're Lending Money, Not Buying Ownership)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a bond in plain terms.
- Explain the core relationship between a bondholder and the issuer.
- Name the basic terms that define a bond.

## Core Content

### A Bond Is a Loan, Not Ownership

When you buy a bond, you're lending money to whoever issued it (a
company or government), not buying a piece of ownership the way a stock
would give you. This is the single most important distinction between
stocks and bonds.

### The Core Relationship

The issuer promises to pay back the original amount (the principal, or
"face value") at a set future date (maturity), and typically to make
periodic interest payments (called the coupon) along the way, in
exchange for the use of your money.

### The Basic Terms

Every bond has a face value (the amount repaid at maturity), a coupon
rate (the interest rate paid), and a maturity date (when the loan is
repaid). These terms are set when the bond is issued.

## Worked Example

An investor buys a $1,000 face value bond with a 4% coupon rate and a
10-year maturity. They receive $40 per year in interest (4% of $1,000)
and get their $1,000 back after 10 years.

## Practice Quiz

1. Buying a bond means:
   A. Buying partial ownership in a company
   B. Lending money to the issuer
   C. Buying a physical asset
   D. Becoming a company employee

2. True or False: A bond's coupon rate refers to the amount repaid at
   maturity.

3. What is a bond's face value?
   A. The interest rate paid
   B. The amount repaid to the bondholder at maturity
   C. The current market price of the bond
   D. The issuer's total debt

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A bond represents lending money, not ownership.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The coupon rate is the interest rate; face value
is the amount repaid at maturity.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Face value is the principal repaid at maturity.

## Key Takeaways

- A bond represents lending money, not ownership.
- The issuer promises to repay principal at maturity, plus periodic
  interest (the coupon).
- Face value, coupon rate, and maturity date are the core defining
  terms.
