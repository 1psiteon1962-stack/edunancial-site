# Stocks vs. Bonds: What's the Actual Trade-Off?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain the core structural difference between stocks and bonds.
- Explain why bondholders are generally repaid before shareholders.
- Avoid the oversimplified "stocks are risky, bonds are safe" framing.

## Core Content

### The Structural Difference

A stock represents ownership; a bond represents a loan. This single
difference drives most of the practical differences between them,
covered in this lesson.

### Repayment Priority

If a company runs into serious financial trouble, bondholders (as
lenders) generally have a legal claim to be repaid before shareholders
(as owners) receive anything. This is a real structural priority, not
just a general reputation for safety.

### Beyond "Risky vs. Safe"

Calling stocks simply "risky" and bonds simply "safe" oversimplifies a
more nuanced trade-off: stocks offer ownership upside (unlimited growth
potential, but no repayment guarantee) while bonds offer defined,
contractual repayment terms (but capped return potential, and their own
real risks like the issuer defaulting).

## Worked Example

If a company goes bankrupt, its bondholders are generally in line to be
repaid from remaining assets before its shareholders see anything —
shareholders' claim comes last, reflecting the ownership-vs-loan
structural difference.

## Practice Quiz

1. The core structural difference between stocks and bonds is:
   A. Stocks are always riskier than bonds
   B. Stocks represent ownership; bonds represent a loan
   C. Bonds always pay higher returns
   D. There is no real difference

2. True or False: In a company bankruptcy, bondholders generally have a
   repayment claim ahead of shareholders.

3. Why is "stocks are risky, bonds are safe" an oversimplification?
   A. It's completely accurate with no nuance
   B. Both have real, different types of risk and reward trade-offs
   C. Bonds have no risk at all
   D. Stocks always lose money

## Answer Key

1. B
2. True
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Ownership vs. lending is the core structural
difference.

### Question 2

Correct answer: True. Bondholders generally have repayment priority over
shareholders.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Both carry real, distinct types of risk — the
simple framing misses this nuance.

## Key Takeaways

- Stocks represent ownership; bonds represent a loan.
- Bondholders generally have repayment priority over shareholders.
- "Risky vs. safe" oversimplifies a more nuanced trade-off between the
  two.
