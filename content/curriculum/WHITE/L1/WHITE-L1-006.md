# What Is a Dividend?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a dividend in plain terms.
- Explain how dividends are typically paid.
- Explain why dividends are never guaranteed.

## Core Content

### What a Dividend Is

A dividend is a portion of a company's profit distributed to
shareholders, proportional to how many shares they own.

### How They're Typically Paid

Dividends are commonly paid in cash, on a regular schedule (often
quarterly), directly to shareholders based on how many shares they hold
on a specific record date.

### Why They're Never Guaranteed

A company's board of directors decides whether to pay a dividend, and
how much — there's no legal requirement to do so. Companies can reduce,
suspend, or never start paying dividends, often depending on their
profitability and growth plans.

## Worked Example

An investor owning 100 shares of a company paying a $0.50 per share
quarterly dividend would receive $50 that quarter — an amount that could
change in future quarters at the company's discretion.

## Practice Quiz

1. A dividend is:
   A. A guaranteed fixed payment
   B. A portion of company profit distributed to shareholders
   C. A type of bond interest payment
   D. A tax owed on stock ownership

2. True or False: Companies are legally required to pay dividends.

3. Who decides whether and how much dividend to pay?
   A. Individual shareholders
   B. The company's board of directors
   C. The stock exchange
   D. The government

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A dividend is a profit distribution to shareholders.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Dividend payments are discretionary, not legally
required.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. The board of directors decides on dividend payments.

## Key Takeaways

- A dividend is a portion of profit distributed to shareholders.
- Payments are typically cash, on a regular schedule.
- Dividends are never guaranteed — they're a board decision.
