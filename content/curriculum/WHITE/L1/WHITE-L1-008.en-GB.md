# What Is Market Capitalization?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- State the market capitalization formula.
- Calculate market cap given share price and shares outstanding.
- Explain why market cap isn't the same as a company's cash or total
  assets.

## Core Content

### The Formula

Market Capitalization = Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding. It
represents the total market value of all a company's shares combined.

### What It Actually Measures

Market cap reflects what investors collectively believe the company's
ownership is worth right now — a real-time market estimate, not an
audited or fixed figure.

### Why It's Not the Same as Cash or Total Worth

Market cap is not the same as how much cash a company has, its total
revenue, or even its total assets. A company can have a large market cap
while having relatively little cash on hand, because market cap reflects
investor expectations about future value, not a current balance sheet
snapshot.

## Worked Example

A company with 10,000,000 shares outstanding trading at $50 per share has
a market cap of 10,000,000 × $50 = $500,000,000 — regardless of how much
actual cash the company holds.

## Practice Quiz

1. Market capitalization is calculated as:
   A. Total revenue × number of employees
   B. Share price × total shares outstanding
   C. Total assets − total liabilities
   D. Annual profit ÷ shares outstanding

2. True or False: Market capitalization is the same thing as a
   company's cash on hand.

3. A company has 2,000,000 shares outstanding trading at $30 per share.
   What is its market cap?
   A. $6,000
   B. $60,000
   C. $6,000,000
   D. $60,000,000

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. D

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Market cap = share price × shares outstanding.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Market cap reflects market value, not cash
holdings.

### Question 3

Correct answer: D. 2,000,000 × $30 = $60,000,000.

## Key Takeaways

- Market Cap = Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding.
- It reflects the market's collective valuation of total ownership.
- It's not the same as a company's cash, revenue, or total assets.
