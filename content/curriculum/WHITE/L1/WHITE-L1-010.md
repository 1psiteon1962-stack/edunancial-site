## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Define the core components of a personal investment strategy
- Select an appropriate asset allocation based on your time horizon and risk tolerance
- Build a simple, low-cost portfolio using index funds
- Establish an automatic investment plan
- Define the ongoing maintenance required to keep the strategy on track

## Core Content

### The Complete WHITE Level 1 Framework

You now understand the building blocks:
- Paper assets: stocks, bonds, and funds
- How each generates returns: appreciation, dividends, interest
- How to hold them tax-efficiently: 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA
- How to allocate them: the three-fund portfolio
- How to manage risk: diversification, behavior, and policy

The next step is integration. A strategy is not a list of concepts. It is a specific, written, actionable plan that tells you exactly what to buy, how much to contribute, when to rebalance, and what to do when markets fall.

### Step 1: Define Your Investment Goal

Every strategy starts with a goal. Vague goals produce vague strategies. Specific goals produce executable plans.

Good goal: "I will build $1,200,000 in investable assets by age 65, which will support a $4,800/month withdrawal rate at a 4% withdrawal rate."

Poor goal: "I want to be financially independent someday."

Calculate the specific number you need. Work backward to determine what monthly contribution and rate of return is required to reach it. Use a retirement or compound interest calculator.

### Step 2: Select Your Asset Allocation

Based on your time horizon and risk tolerance from Lesson 6:
- More than 20 years: 80–100% equities / 0–20% bonds
- 10–20 years: 60–80% equities / 20–40% bonds
- Less than 10 years: 40–60% equities / 40–60% bonds

Write your allocation as a specific percentage. Example: 80% equities (60% U.S. / 20% international), 20% bonds.

### Step 3: Select Your Funds

For each allocation category, select one low-cost index fund:
- U.S. equities: VTI, FSKAX, or SWTSX
- International equities: VXUS, FZILX, or SWISX
- Bonds: BND, FXNAX, or SWAGX

Minimize the number of funds. Three funds are sufficient for complete diversification. Choose funds from the same provider as your brokerage account to minimize friction.

### Step 4: Optimize Account Selection

For each dollar you invest, use the most tax-efficient account available:

1. Employer 401(k) up to the match — always do this first
2. Roth IRA to the annual limit — tax-free growth is irreplaceable
3. Return to 401(k) to the annual limit
4. Taxable brokerage account — for additional investing beyond retirement account limits

Hold tax-inefficient assets (bonds, REITs, dividend-paying stocks) in tax-advantaged accounts. Hold tax-efficient assets (total market index funds) in taxable accounts if needed.

### Step 5: Automate

Manual investing fails. Automatic investing succeeds.

Set up:
- Payroll deduction to your 401(k)
- Monthly automatic transfer to your Roth IRA
- Automatic investment into your selected funds on a fixed schedule

Automation removes the decision from the equation. You will never forget. You will never hesitate during a downturn. The machine runs regardless of how you feel.

### Step 6: Define Your Maintenance Rules

- **Rebalance:** Once per year, or when any allocation drifts more than 5–10 percentage points
- **Review:** Once per year — check progress toward goal, not daily prices
- **Change:** Only when your life circumstances change (income, goals, time horizon), not when the market moves

Write these rules in your investment policy statement and commit to them in writing before your next significant market decline.

### The Financial Competency Line

WHITE Level 1 is complete. You understand the full landscape of paper asset investing. You have the framework for building a strategy that will work for decades.

The line between financial literacy and financial competency is execution. Knowledge without action produces no wealth.

Open the account. Set up the automatic transfer. Execute the first trade. The strategy becomes real only when money is invested.

## Application

Complete the following before closing this level:

1. Write your complete investment strategy in one page or less: goal, target amount, target date, monthly contribution, asset allocation, fund selection, and account allocation.
2. Identify one specific action you will complete within the next seven days to advance this strategy (open an account, increase your 401k contribution, set up automatic investing, or make your first purchase).
3. Set a calendar reminder for one year from today to review your portfolio against your plan.

Keep these notes. WHITE Level 2 builds on this foundation with intermediate investment strategies, including dividend investing, sector analysis, and tax-loss harvesting.
