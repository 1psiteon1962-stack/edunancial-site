# What Is a Mutual Fund, and How Is It Different from an ETF?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a mutual fund in plain terms.
- Explain how mutual funds and ETFs are similar.
- Name at least one practical difference between them.

## Core Content

### What a Mutual Fund Is

Like an ETF, a mutual fund pools money from many investors to buy a
collection of assets. Both share the basic "basket" concept from the
previous lesson.

### How They're Similar

Both mutual funds and ETFs offer diversified exposure to a basket of
holdings through a single investment, and both can be actively or
passively managed.

### A Key Practical Difference

Unlike ETFs, which trade throughout the day on an exchange at
constantly changing prices, mutual fund shares are typically priced and
traded only once per day, after markets close, based on that day's
calculated value.

## Worked Example

An investor buying an ETF can see its price change minute to minute
during market hours. An investor buying a mutual fund places their order
and receives a price calculated at the end of that trading day, not in
real time.

## Practice Quiz

1. A mutual fund is:
   A. A single company's stock
   B. A pooled fund investing in a collection of assets, similar in
      concept to an ETF
   C. A type of bond exclusively
   D. A government savings program

2. True or False: Mutual fund shares trade continuously throughout the
   day like ETF shares.

3. What is a key practical difference between mutual funds and ETFs?
   A. Mutual funds cannot hold stocks
   B. Mutual funds are typically priced once per day; ETFs trade
      throughout the day
   C. ETFs cannot be diversified
   D. There is no real difference

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A mutual fund pools money into a diversified basket,
similar to an ETF conceptually.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Mutual funds are typically priced once daily,
unlike ETFs.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Pricing frequency is a key practical difference.

## Key Takeaways

- A mutual fund pools investor money into a diversified basket, similar
  to an ETF.
- Both offer diversified exposure through a single investment.
- Mutual funds are typically priced once daily; ETFs trade throughout
  the day.
