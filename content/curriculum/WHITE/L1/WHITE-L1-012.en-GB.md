# What Is an Expense Ratio, and Why Should You Care About a Small Percentage?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define expense ratio in plain terms.
- Calculate the approximate annual cost given a fund balance and expense
  ratio.
- Explain why small percentage differences matter over time.

## Core Content

### What an Expense Ratio Is

An expense ratio is the annual fee a fund (like an ETF or mutual fund)
charges, expressed as a percentage of the amount invested, to cover the
fund's operating costs.

### Calculating the Cost

A 0.50% expense ratio on a $10,000 investment costs roughly $50 per
year, taken automatically from the fund's returns — investors typically
don't pay it as a separate bill.

### Why Small Percentages Matter Over Time

Because expense ratios are charged every year, even small differences
compound significantly over long periods. A 0.10% fee versus a 1.00% fee
on the same investment, held for decades, can add up to a meaningfully
different total cost, even though the yearly difference looks small.

## Worked Example

A $10,000 investment with a 1.00% expense ratio costs about $100/year in
fees. The same investment with a 0.10% expense ratio costs about
$10/year — a $90 difference that compounds meaningfully if held for
many years.

## Practice Quiz

1. An expense ratio is:
   A. A one-time purchase fee
   B. An annual fee charged as a percentage of the amount invested
   C. A tax owed on dividends
   D. A fee charged only when selling

2. True or False: Expense ratio differences are too small to matter over
   time.

3. A $20,000 investment has a 0.40% expense ratio. Approximately how
   much does that cost per year?
   A. $8
   B. $40
   C. $80
   D. $400

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. C

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An expense ratio is an annual percentage-based fee.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Small annual differences compound significantly
over long periods.

### Question 3

Correct answer: C. $20,000 × 0.40% = $80.

## Key Takeaways

- An expense ratio is an annual fee, charged as a percentage of assets
  invested.
- It's automatically deducted from fund returns, not billed separately.
- Small differences in expense ratio can compound into meaningful costs
  over time.
