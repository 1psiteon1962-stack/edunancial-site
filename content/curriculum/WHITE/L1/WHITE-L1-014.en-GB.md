# What Is Risk, and Why Isn't It the Same as "Bad"?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define investment risk in plain terms.
- Explain why risk and "bad" are different concepts.
- Explain the general relationship between risk and potential return.

## Core Content

### What Risk Means Here

In investing, risk generally refers to the uncertainty of an outcome —
specifically, the chance that an investment's actual return will differ
from what was expected, including the possibility of loss.

### Risk Isn't the Same as "Bad"

Risk describes uncertainty, not a guaranteed negative outcome. A
"risky" investment isn't automatically a bad choice — it means the range
of possible outcomes is wider, for better or worse, than a lower-risk
option.

### The General Risk-Return Relationship

Generally, investments with higher potential returns tend to carry
higher risk, and investments with more certainty/safety tend to offer
lower potential returns. This isn't a strict guarantee in every single
case, but it's a common pattern worth understanding.

## Worked Example

A government bond from a stable issuer is generally considered lower
risk, with a correspondingly modest return. A new company's stock is
generally considered higher risk, with the potential for a much higher
(or much lower) return than the bond.

## Practice Quiz

1. In investing, risk generally refers to:
   A. A guaranteed loss
   B. The uncertainty of an investment's actual outcome
   C. A type of dividend
   D. A government regulation

2. True or False: A "risky" investment is automatically a bad choice.

3. What is the general relationship between risk and potential return?
   A. Higher risk generally has no relationship to potential return
   B. Higher potential returns generally come with higher risk
   C. Lower risk always means higher returns
   D. Risk and return are always identical

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Risk refers to outcome uncertainty, not guaranteed
loss.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Risk describes uncertainty, not an automatic bad
outcome.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Higher potential returns generally correlate with
higher risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Risk refers to uncertainty of outcome, not a guaranteed bad result.
- A risky investment isn't automatically a poor choice.
- Higher potential returns generally come with higher risk, as a general
  pattern.
