# What Is Volatility?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define volatility in plain terms.
- Explain what volatility measures, specifically.
- Distinguish volatility from permanent loss.

## Core Content

### What Volatility Means

Volatility refers to how much and how quickly an asset's price moves up
and down over a given period. A highly volatile asset swings in price
significantly and often; a low-volatility asset moves more gradually.

### What It Specifically Measures

Volatility measures price movement, not necessarily the direction or
final outcome. An asset can be highly volatile while still ending up
higher over time — volatility describes the bumpiness of the ride, not
automatically the destination.

### Volatility vs. Permanent Loss

A temporary price drop in a volatile asset isn't the same as a
permanent loss — if the price later recovers, an investor who didn't
sell during the dip experiences volatility without a lasting loss. Loss
only becomes "locked in" if the asset is sold at the lower price.

## Worked Example

A stock's price swings between $80 and $120 over a few months before
settling near $115 — highly volatile along the way, but an investor who
held through the swings ended up close to a gain, not a loss.

## Practice Quiz

1. Volatility measures:
   A. Guaranteed loss
   B. How much and how quickly a price moves up and down
   C. A company's total profit
   D. The number of shares outstanding

2. True or False: A temporary price drop is automatically the same as a
   permanent loss.

3. When does a paper loss from volatility become a locked-in loss?
   A. Never
   B. When the asset is sold at the lower price
   C. Automatically after one year
   D. As soon as the price drops at all

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Volatility measures the degree of price movement.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A temporary drop isn't a permanent loss unless
the asset is actually sold at that lower price.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Selling at the lower price is what locks in the
loss.

## Key Takeaways

- Volatility measures how much and how quickly price moves.
- It describes price movement, not automatically a final outcome.
- A temporary drop isn't a permanent loss unless sold at that price.
