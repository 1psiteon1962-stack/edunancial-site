# What Is a Bull Market vs. a Bear Market?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a bull market and a bear market.
- Explain what these labels are typically based on.
- Explain why they describe a trend, not a guaranteed future.

## Core Content

### What Each Term Means

A bull market generally refers to a sustained period of rising prices
across a market. A bear market generally refers to a sustained period of
falling prices, often loosely defined as a drop of a significant
percentage (commonly around 20%) from a recent high.

### What the Labels Are Based On

These terms describe an observed trend over a period of time, not a
prediction. A market is typically labeled "bull" or "bear" based on
price movement that has already happened, looking backward or at a
current sustained pattern.

### Why They're Not a Guarantee

Being in a bull or bear market doesn't guarantee what happens next —
markets can shift from one to the other, and no one can reliably predict
exactly when that shift will happen. Treating current labels as a
forecast is a common but risky assumption.

## Worked Example

A market that has risen steadily for two years might be described as a
bull market — but that label describes what already happened, not a
guarantee that prices will keep rising.

## Practice Quiz

1. A bull market generally refers to:
   A. A sustained period of falling prices
   B. A sustained period of rising prices
   C. A single day of trading
   D. A type of bond

2. True or False: Being labeled a "bull market" guarantees prices will
   keep rising.

3. What are bull and bear market labels typically based on?
   A. A government announcement
   B. An observed price trend over a period of time
   C. A company's individual earnings report
   D. The weather

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A bull market refers to a sustained rising trend.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. The label describes past/current trend, not a
guaranteed future.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. These labels reflect an observed price trend.

## Key Takeaways

- A bull market is a sustained rising trend; a bear market is a
  sustained falling trend.
- These labels describe an observed trend, not a prediction.
- Markets can shift between the two, unpredictably.
