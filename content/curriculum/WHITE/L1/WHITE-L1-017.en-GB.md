# What Is Compound Interest? (The Idea Everyone Mentions but Few Explain)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define compound interest in plain terms.
- Distinguish it from simple interest.
- Calculate a basic example of compound growth over a few periods.

## Core Content

### What Compound Interest Is

Compound interest is interest calculated not just on the original
amount invested, but also on the interest that's already accumulated —
meaning growth builds on top of previous growth, not just the starting
amount.

### Compound vs. Simple Interest

Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount, every period.
Compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus all
previously earned interest — this is the entire reason compounding
accelerates over time.

### A Step-by-Step Example

Starting with $1,000 at 10% annual compound interest: after year 1, it's
$1,100. After year 2, interest is calculated on $1,100 (not just the
original $1,000), producing $1,210. After year 3, it's calculated on
$1,210, producing $1,331 — the growth accelerates because each year's
interest is calculated on a larger base.

## Worked Example

$1,000 at 10% simple interest earns exactly $100 every year: $1,100,
$1,200, $1,300. The same $1,000 at 10% compound interest earns $1,100,
$1,210, $1,331 — a small but growing gap that widens significantly over
longer periods.

## Practice Quiz

1. Compound interest is calculated on:
   A. Only the original amount invested
   B. The original amount plus previously earned interest
   C. A fixed government rate
   D. Only the most recent year's contribution

2. True or False: Simple interest and compound interest produce
   identical results over time.

3. $1,000 grows to $1,100 after year 1 at 10% compound interest. What
   would it grow to after year 2?
   A. $1,100
   B. $1,200
   C. $1,210
   D. $1,300

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. C

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Compound interest builds on both principal and
accumulated interest.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Compound interest grows faster over time than
simple interest.

### Question 3

Correct answer: C. $1,100 × 1.10 = $1,210 (10% growth on the new,
larger base).

## Key Takeaways

- Compound interest is calculated on principal plus accumulated
  interest.
- This is the key difference from simple interest, which only uses the
  original amount.
- The gap between compound and simple interest widens over longer
  periods.
