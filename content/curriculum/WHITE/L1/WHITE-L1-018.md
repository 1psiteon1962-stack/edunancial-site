# What Is a 401(k)?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a 401(k) in plain terms.
- Explain how contributions typically work.
- Explain what an employer match is.

## Core Content

### What a 401(k) Is

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this is a U.S.-specific retirement account
type.)* A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement savings account,
allowing employees to contribute a portion of their paycheck, often with
tax advantages, toward long-term retirement savings.

### How Contributions Typically Work

Employees generally choose a percentage or amount of their paycheck to
contribute automatically, which is then invested in options the employer

's plan offers (often a menu of funds, including options like the ETFs
and mutual funds covered earlier in this level).

### What an Employer Match Is

Many employers offer a "match" — contributing additional money based on
what the employee contributes, up to a certain limit. This is
effectively extra compensation tied specifically to participating in the
plan, and not taking full advantage of an available match is a commonly
cited missed opportunity.

## Worked Example

An employer offers to match 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of
salary. An employee earning $50,000 who contributes 6% ($3,000)
receives an additional $1,500 from the employer match — money they
wouldn't receive without contributing.

## Practice Quiz

1. A 401(k) is:
   A. A type of checking account
   B. An employer-sponsored retirement savings account
   C. A government pension paid automatically
   D. A type of bond

2. True or False: All employers are required to offer a matching
   contribution.

3. What is an employer match?
   A. A tax penalty
   B. Additional employer contributions based on what the employee
      contributes
   C. A type of loan
   D. A fixed government payment

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement account.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Employer matching is common but not universally
required or offered.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. A match is additional employer money tied to
employee contributions.

## Key Takeaways

- A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement savings account.
- Contributions are typically automatic, from paycheck deductions.
- An employer match, when offered, is effectively extra compensation for
  participating.
