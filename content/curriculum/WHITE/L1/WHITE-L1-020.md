# Traditional vs. Roth: What's Actually Different About the Tax Treatment?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain the core tax-timing difference between traditional and Roth
  accounts.
- Explain when a traditional account is taxed.
- Explain when a Roth account is taxed.

## Core Content

### The Core Tax-Timing Difference

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this describes a U.S. tax concept.)* Both
traditional and Roth accounts (available as either 401(k) or IRA
versions) offer tax advantages — the real difference is WHEN the tax
benefit applies, not whether one is better in every situation.

### Traditional Accounts

Contributions to a traditional account are often made before tax, and
withdrawals in retirement are generally taxed as income at that later
time.

### Roth Accounts

Contributions to a Roth account are made with after-tax money, but
qualified withdrawals in retirement are generally tax-free — the tax is
paid upfront instead of later.

## Worked Example

A traditional account contribution reduces taxable income now, with tax
paid later on withdrawals. A Roth contribution provides no tax break now,
but qualified withdrawals later are generally tax-free — the same
dollar, taxed at a different point in time.

## Practice Quiz

1. The core difference between traditional and Roth accounts is:
   A. Only traditional accounts allow investing in stocks
   B. When the tax advantage applies — now versus later
   C. Roth accounts are only for employers
   D. There is no real difference

2. True or False: Traditional account contributions are generally made
   with after-tax money.

3. When are qualified Roth withdrawals generally taxed?
   A. When contributed
   B. Generally not taxed at withdrawal, since tax was paid upfront
   C. Twice — both when contributed and withdrawn
   D. Never contributed with real money

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. The timing of the tax advantage is the core
difference.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Traditional contributions are often made before
tax.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Roth accounts pay tax upfront, so qualified
withdrawals are generally tax-free later.

## Key Takeaways

- Both traditional and Roth accounts offer tax advantages, at different
  times.
- Traditional: often tax-advantaged now, taxed later on withdrawal.
- Roth: taxed now, generally tax-free on qualified withdrawal later.
