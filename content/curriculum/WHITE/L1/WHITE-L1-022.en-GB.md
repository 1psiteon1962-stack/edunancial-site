# What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define dollar-cost averaging in plain terms.
- Explain how it interacts with price volatility.
- Recognize it as a strategy, not a guarantee of profit.

## Core Content

### What Dollar-Cost Averaging Is

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount of money at regular
intervals (like monthly), regardless of the current price, rather than
investing a large lump sum all at once.

### How It Interacts With Volatility

Because the investment amount stays fixed, more shares get purchased
when prices are lower, and fewer shares get purchased when prices are
higher — this naturally averages the purchase price over time, without
requiring the investor to predict price movements.

### Not a Guarantee

Dollar-cost averaging doesn't guarantee a profit or protect against
loss — it's a disciplined approach to investing over time, not a way to
eliminate risk entirely.

## Worked Example

An investor puts $200 into an investment every month. When the price is
low, that $200 buys more shares; when the price is high, it buys fewer
shares — over a year, this produces an average purchase price without
the investor needing to time the market.

## Practice Quiz

1. Dollar-cost averaging means:
   A. Investing a large lump sum all at once
   B. Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price
   C. Only investing when prices are low
   D. Avoiding all paper assets

2. True or False: Dollar-cost averaging guarantees a profit.

3. What happens to the number of shares purchased when the price is
   lower, under dollar-cost averaging?
   A. Fewer shares are purchased
   B. More shares are purchased for the same fixed dollar amount
   C. No shares are purchased at all
   D. The investment amount automatically increases

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Fixed amount, regular intervals, regardless of
price.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. It's a disciplined strategy, not a guarantee.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. A fixed dollar amount buys more shares when the
price is lower.

## Key Takeaways

- Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount at regular
  intervals.
- It naturally buys more shares when prices are low, fewer when high.
- It's a disciplined strategy, not a guarantee against loss.
