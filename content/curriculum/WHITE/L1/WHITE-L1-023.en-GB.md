# What Is a Stock Split?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a stock split in plain terms.
- Explain what changes and what doesn't change in a split.
- Calculate a basic post-split share count and price.

## Core Content

### What a Stock Split Is

A stock split divides each existing share into multiple new shares,
proportionally reducing the price per share while increasing the number
of shares outstanding.

### What Changes, and What Doesn't

The number of shares an investor owns increases, and the price per share
decreases proportionally. What does NOT change: the total value of the
investor's holding, and the company's overall market capitalization —
the pie is cut into more slices, but the pie itself is the same size.

### Why Companies Do It

Companies sometimes split shares to bring the per-share price down to a
more accessible range for investors, though this doesn't change the
company's underlying value at all.

## Worked Example

An investor owning 10 shares at $100 each ($1,000 total) experiences a
2-for-1 stock split. They now own 20 shares at $50 each — still $1,000
total, just divided into more, cheaper shares.

## Practice Quiz

1. A stock split:
   A. Increases a company's total market value
   B. Divides existing shares into more shares at a proportionally lower
      price
   C. Reduces the number of shares outstanding
   D. Changes the company's underlying business

2. True or False: A stock split changes the total value of an investor's
   holding.

3. An investor owns 5 shares at $200 each. After a 2-for-1 split, how
   many shares do they own, and at what price?
   A. 5 shares at $400
   B. 10 shares at $100
   C. 2.5 shares at $200
   D. 10 shares at $200

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A split divides shares proportionally without
changing total value.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Total holding value stays the same immediately
after a split.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. 5 shares × 2 = 10 shares; $200 ÷ 2 = $100 per share.

## Key Takeaways

- A stock split divides shares into more shares at a proportionally
  lower price.
- Total holding value and market cap don't change from the split itself.
- Companies split shares mainly to make the per-share price more
  accessible.
