# What Is Earnings Per Share (EPS)?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- State the EPS formula.
- Calculate a basic EPS given net income and shares outstanding.
- Explain what EPS represents in plain terms.

## Core Content

### The EPS Formula

Earnings Per Share (EPS) = Net Income ÷ Shares Outstanding. It divides a
company's total profit across each individual share.

### What EPS Represents

EPS represents how much profit is attributable to each individual
share — a way of expressing a company's total profit on a per-share
basis, making it easier to compare across companies of different sizes.

### Why This Matters Going Forward

EPS is the foundation for several valuation concepts covered in more
depth in Level 2 — understanding what it measures now sets up that
later material.

## Worked Example

A company with $5,000,000 in net income and 1,000,000 shares outstanding
has an EPS of $5,000,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = $5.00 per share.

## Practice Quiz

1. EPS is calculated as:
   A. Share price ÷ net income
   B. Net income ÷ shares outstanding
   C. Total revenue ÷ total expenses
   D. Shares outstanding ÷ net income

2. True or False: EPS represents a company's total profit, not a
   per-share figure.

3. A company has $2,000,000 in net income and 500,000 shares
   outstanding. What is its EPS?
   A. $0.25
   B. $2.50
   C. $4.00
   D. $250

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. C

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. EPS = Net Income ÷ Shares Outstanding.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. EPS specifically expresses profit on a per-share
basis.

### Question 3

Correct answer: C. $2,000,000 ÷ 500,000 = $4.00.

## Key Takeaways

- EPS = Net Income ÷ Shares Outstanding.
- It expresses a company's profit on a per-share basis.
- It's foundational to valuation concepts covered in more depth later.
