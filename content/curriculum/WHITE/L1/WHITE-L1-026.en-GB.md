# What Is a P/E Ratio? (Introductory Version — Full Method in Level 2)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- State the basic P/E ratio formula.
- Calculate a basic P/E ratio given share price and EPS.
- Explain, at an introductory level, what the ratio estimates.

## Core Content

### The Basic Formula

P/E Ratio = Share Price ÷ Earnings Per Share (EPS), building directly on
the EPS concept from the previous lesson.

### What It Estimates

The P/E ratio roughly answers: how much are investors paying today for
each dollar of a company's current earnings? A higher number means
investors are paying more per dollar of current profit.

### A Preview, Not the Full Picture

*ASSUMPTION (for teaching purposes):* This introductory lesson covers
only the basic formula. A full treatment — including how to compare
across industries, and how share buybacks can distort the ratio — is
covered in Level 2.

## Worked Example

A stock trades at $60 per share with an EPS of $3.00. P/E = $60 ÷ $3.00
= 20 — investors are paying $20 for every $1 of that company's current
earnings.

## Practice Quiz

1. The P/E ratio formula is:
   A. EPS ÷ Share Price
   B. Share Price ÷ EPS
   C. Net Income ÷ Share Price
   D. Shares Outstanding ÷ EPS

2. True or False: A higher P/E ratio means investors are paying less per
   dollar of current earnings.

3. A stock trades at $80 with an EPS of $4.00. What is its P/E ratio?
   A. 4
   B. 20
   C. 80
   D. 320

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. P/E = Share Price ÷ EPS.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A higher P/E means paying more per dollar of
earnings.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $80 ÷ $4.00 = 20.

## Key Takeaways

- P/E Ratio = Share Price ÷ EPS.
- It estimates how much investors are paying per dollar of current
  earnings.
- This is an introductory version — Level 2 covers deeper application
  and limitations.
