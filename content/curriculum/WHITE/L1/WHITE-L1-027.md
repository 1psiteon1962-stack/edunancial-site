# What Is Yield?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define yield in plain terms.
- Calculate a basic yield given income and price.
- Distinguish yield from price appreciation.

## Core Content

### What Yield Means

Yield generally measures the income an investment generates (like
dividends from a stock or interest from a bond), expressed as a
percentage of its price.

### The Basic Formula

Yield = Annual Income ÷ Current Price. For a stock, "income" typically
means dividends; for a bond, it typically means interest payments.

### Yield vs. Price Appreciation

Yield specifically measures income, not price gain. A stock could have a
high yield through dividends while its price stays flat, or a low yield
while its price rises substantially — these are two separate sources of
potential return, not the same thing.

## Worked Example

A stock trades at $50 and pays $2 in annual dividends. Yield = $2 ÷ $50
= 4%. This is separate from and doesn't include any change in the
stock's own price.

## Practice Quiz

1. Yield is calculated as:
   A. Price appreciation ÷ time held
   B. Annual income ÷ current price
   C. Total shares ÷ net income
   D. Share price ÷ EPS

2. True or False: Yield measures price appreciation, not income.

3. A bond pays $40 annually and is priced at $1,000. What is its yield?
   A. 0.4%
   B. 4%
   C. 40%
   D. 400%

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Yield = Annual Income ÷ Current Price.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Yield measures income, not price appreciation.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. $40 ÷ $1,000 = 4%.

## Key Takeaways

- Yield = Annual Income ÷ Current Price.
- It measures income (dividends or interest), not price appreciation.
- Income and price appreciation are two separate sources of potential
  return.
