# What Is a Bond's Maturity Date?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define maturity date in plain terms.
- Explain what happens at maturity.
- Recognize that maturity length affects a bond's behavior.

## Core Content

### What a Maturity Date Is

A bond's maturity date is the date the issuer has agreed to repay the
bond's face value (the principal) in full to the bondholder — the end of
the loan term first introduced in the earlier bond lesson.

### What Happens at Maturity

At maturity, the bondholder receives the bond's face value back, and
interest payments stop, since the loan is considered fully repaid.

### Why Maturity Length Matters

Bonds with longer maturities (further away repayment dates) are
generally more sensitive to changing interest rate conditions than
bonds with shorter maturities — a concept covered in more depth in a
later lesson on interest rate risk.

## Worked Example

A 2-year bond and a 20-year bond might have similar coupon rates today,
but the 20-year bond's price will typically react more significantly to
future interest rate changes, simply because of how much longer it has
until maturity.

## Practice Quiz

1. A bond's maturity date is:
   A. When interest payments begin
   B. When the issuer repays the bond's face value in full
   C. The date the bond was purchased
   D. A date set by the bondholder

2. True or False: Interest payments continue indefinitely after a bond
   reaches maturity.

3. Which type of bond is generally more sensitive to interest rate
   changes?
   A. A shorter-maturity bond
   B. A longer-maturity bond
   C. Maturity length has no effect on sensitivity
   D. Only bonds with no maturity date

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Maturity is when the face value is repaid in full.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Interest payments stop once the bond matures and
is repaid.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Longer-maturity bonds are generally more sensitive to
rate changes.

## Key Takeaways

- A bond's maturity date is when the face value is repaid in full.
- Interest payments stop once maturity is reached.
- Longer-maturity bonds are generally more sensitive to interest rate
  changes.
