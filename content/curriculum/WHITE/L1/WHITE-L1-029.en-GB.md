# What Is Credit Risk in a Bond?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define credit risk in plain terms.
- Explain how credit risk relates to a bond's interest rate.
- Recognize that not all bond issuers carry the same credit risk.

## Core Content

### What Credit Risk Is

Credit risk is the risk that a bond issuer won't be able to make
promised interest payments or repay the principal at maturity — in
other words, the risk of default.

### How It Relates to Interest Rate

Issuers seen as higher credit risk generally must offer a higher coupon
rate to attract lenders, compensating investors for taking on that
added risk. Issuers seen as very low risk (like stable governments) can
generally borrow at lower rates.

### Not All Issuers Are Equal

A large, financially stable company and a smaller, financially strained
company issuing bonds don't carry the same credit risk, even if their
bonds look similar on the surface — this is why bond ratings (covered in
Level 2) exist, to help assess this risk.

## Worked Example

A financially strong government might issue bonds at a low interest
rate, reflecting low credit risk. A financially struggling company might
need to offer a much higher interest rate on its bonds to attract
investors willing to take on the added default risk.

## Practice Quiz

1. Credit risk in a bond refers to:
   A. The risk of losing a paper certificate
   B. The risk the issuer won't repay interest or principal as promised
   C. The risk of a stock split
   D. The risk of a market-wide decline

2. True or False: Higher credit risk issuers generally offer lower
   interest rates.

3. Why might a financially struggling company need to offer a higher
   bond interest rate?
   A. To compensate investors for higher default risk
   B. Because the government requires it
   C. To reduce its market capitalization
   D. Bonds always carry the same rate regardless of risk

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. A

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Credit risk is the risk of default on interest or
principal.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Higher credit risk issuers generally must offer
higher rates.

### Question 3

Correct answer: A. Higher rates compensate investors for taking on
default risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Credit risk is the risk of an issuer defaulting on a bond.
- Higher credit risk generally means a higher offered interest rate.
- Not all bond issuers carry the same level of credit risk.
