# What Is Interest Rate Risk?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define interest rate risk in plain terms.
- Explain the basic inverse relationship between rates and bond prices.
- Explain why this matters to an existing bondholder.

## Core Content

### What Interest Rate Risk Is

Interest rate risk is the risk that changes in broader interest rates
will affect the market value of existing bonds — separate from credit
risk, which is about the issuer's ability to pay.

### The Inverse Relationship

When broader interest rates rise, the market price of existing bonds
with lower fixed rates generally falls, because new bonds are being
issued at the higher, more attractive current rate. When broader rates
fall, existing higher-rate bonds generally become more valuable by
comparison.

### Why It Matters to an Existing Bondholder

A bondholder who needs to sell before maturity is exposed to this price
movement. A bondholder who holds to maturity still receives the face
value regardless — interest rate risk mainly affects the bond's market
price before maturity, not the final repayment itself.

## Worked Example

An investor holds a bond paying 3% interest. Broader interest rates rise
to 5%. New bonds now offer the more attractive 5% rate, making the
investor's older 3% bond less attractive to a potential buyer — its
market price would generally fall if sold before maturity.

## Practice Quiz

1. Interest rate risk refers to:
   A. The risk of an issuer defaulting
   B. The risk that changing rates affect existing bond prices
   C. The risk of a stock split
   D. The risk of currency exchange

2. True or False: When broader interest rates rise, existing lower-rate
   bond prices generally rise too.

3. Does a bondholder who holds to maturity still receive full face value
   despite interest rate risk?
   A. No, they lose the difference
   B. Yes, face value is generally still paid at maturity regardless
   C. Only if rates fell
   D. Only if the bond was sold early

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Interest rate risk affects existing bond market
prices as rates change.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Rising rates generally push existing lower-rate
bond prices down.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Holding to maturity generally still delivers full
face value, regardless of interim price swings.

## Key Takeaways

- Interest rate risk affects existing bond prices as broader rates
  change.
- Rising rates generally push existing lower-rate bond prices down, and
  vice versa.
- Holding to maturity generally still delivers the face value regardless
  of interim price movement.
