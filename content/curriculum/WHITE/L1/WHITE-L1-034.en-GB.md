# What Is a CD (Certificate of Deposit)?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a CD in plain terms.
- Explain the basic trade-off a CD offers.
- Explain what typically happens with early withdrawal.

## Core Content

### What a CD Is

A CD (Certificate of Deposit) is a savings product offered by banks,
where money is deposited for a fixed period of time in exchange for a
fixed interest rate, generally higher than a standard savings account.

### The Basic Trade-Off

In exchange for a typically higher, guaranteed interest rate, the
depositor generally agrees not to withdraw the money until the CD's term
ends — trading liquidity (covered in the earlier lesson) for a better
rate.

### What Happens With Early Withdrawal

Withdrawing money from a CD before its term ends generally triggers a
penalty, often reducing or eliminating some of the interest earned —
this penalty is the real cost of breaking the liquidity trade-off.

## Worked Example

An investor puts $5,000 into a 1-year CD at a fixed rate, agreeing not to
touch it for a year. If they need the money after 6 months, they can
typically withdraw it, but will likely face a penalty reducing the
interest they would have otherwise earned.

## Practice Quiz

1. A CD generally offers:
   A. Full liquidity with no rate benefit
   B. A fixed interest rate in exchange for locking up money for a set
      term
   C. Ownership in a company
   D. Guaranteed high-growth returns

2. True or False: Withdrawing from a CD early is always free, with no
   penalty.

3. What does a CD trade for a typically higher interest rate?
   A. Diversification
   B. Liquidity — the money is generally locked up for a set term
   C. Ownership rights
   D. Credit risk protection

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A CD trades liquidity for a fixed, often higher
rate.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Early withdrawal generally triggers a penalty.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Liquidity is what's traded for the rate benefit.

## Key Takeaways

- A CD offers a fixed rate in exchange for locking up money for a term.
- This trades liquidity for a typically better interest rate.
- Early withdrawal generally triggers a real penalty.
