# What Is an Emergency Fund, and Why Does It Come Before Investing?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define an emergency fund in plain terms.
- Explain why it's generally recommended before other investing.
- Explain why it belongs in a liquid, low-risk place.

## Core Content

### What an Emergency Fund Is

An emergency fund is money set aside specifically to cover unexpected
expenses or income disruption (a job loss, an urgent repair, a medical
expense) — not for investing or planned spending.

### Why It Generally Comes First

Without an emergency fund, an unexpected expense might force someone to
sell investments at a bad time (possibly at a loss) or take on
high-interest debt. Having this cushion in place first protects other,
longer-term financial plans from being disrupted by short-term
emergencies.

### Why It Belongs Somewhere Liquid

Connecting back to the liquidity lesson earlier in this level, an
emergency fund needs to be accessible quickly, without penalty or risk
of loss — which is why it typically belongs in something like a savings
account or money market fund, not in stocks or a CD with withdrawal
penalties.

## Worked Example

Someone who invests all their savings in stocks, with no emergency fund,
might be forced to sell those stocks at a loss during a market downturn
just to cover an unexpected car repair — a situation an emergency fund
is specifically meant to prevent.

## Practice Quiz

1. An emergency fund is:
   A. Money invested for long-term growth
   B. Money set aside specifically for unexpected expenses or income
      disruption
   C. A type of retirement account
   D. A required insurance policy

2. True or False: An emergency fund is generally recommended to be
   invested in stocks for maximum growth.

3. Why is having an emergency fund recommended before other investing?
   A. It has no real benefit
   B. It prevents needing to sell investments at a bad time during an
      emergency
   C. It guarantees investment profits
   D. It replaces the need for insurance

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An emergency fund covers unexpected expenses or
income disruption.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. It generally belongs in a liquid, low-risk place,
not stocks.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. It prevents forced, poorly timed investment sales
during emergencies.

## Key Takeaways

- An emergency fund covers unexpected expenses, not planned investing.
- It generally comes before other investing to protect longer-term
  plans.
- It belongs in a liquid, low-risk place, accessible without penalty.
