# What Is a Bull Case vs. a Bear Case for a Stock?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a bull case and a bear case in plain terms.
- Explain why building both matters, not just one.
- Apply the concept to a basic hypothetical example.

## Core Content

### What a Bull Case Is

A bull case is a reasoned argument for why a stock (or other investment)
could perform well, based on specific factors like growth potential,
competitive advantages, or favorable trends.

### What a Bear Case Is

A bear case is a reasoned argument for why a stock could perform
poorly, based on specific risks, weaknesses, or unfavorable trends.

### Why Considering Both Matters

Building only a bull case (or only a bear case) creates a one-sided,
potentially overconfident view. Genuinely considering both perspectives
— even for an investment someone is excited about — leads to a more
realistic understanding of the actual risks and potential involved.

## Worked Example

An investor considering a growing company might build a bull case around
its expanding market and strong products, while also building a bear
case around new competition and rising costs — weighing both before
deciding.

## Practice Quiz

1. A bull case is:
   A. A reasoned argument for why an investment could perform poorly
   B. A reasoned argument for why an investment could perform well
   C. A guaranteed prediction of future performance
   D. A type of bond

2. True or False: Building only a bull case for an investment provides a
   complete, balanced view.

3. Why does considering both a bull case and a bear case matter?
   A. It doesn't matter at all
   B. It leads to a more realistic, balanced understanding of an
      investment
   C. It guarantees a profitable outcome
   D. It's only relevant for bonds

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A bull case argues for potential positive
performance.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A one-sided view misses real risks or
weaknesses.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Considering both leads to a more balanced, realistic
view.

## Key Takeaways

- A bull case argues for potential positive performance; a bear case
  argues for potential negative performance.
- Building only one side creates an incomplete, overconfident view.
- Considering both leads to more realistic investment decision-making.
