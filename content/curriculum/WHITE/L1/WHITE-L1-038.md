# What Is Insider Trading, and Why Is It Illegal?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define insider trading in plain terms.
- Explain the basic reasoning behind why it's prohibited.
- Recognize this as a real legal boundary, not just an ethical guideline.

## Core Content

### What Insider Trading Is

*(REGION-SPECIFIC RULE: this lesson describes the general concept;
specific laws vary by country.)* Insider trading generally refers to
buying or selling a stock based on material, non-public information —
information not yet available to the general public that could
reasonably affect the stock's price.

### The Basic Reasoning Behind the Prohibition

Markets are generally intended to operate on a level informational
playing field. Someone trading on non-public information has an unfair
advantage over other investors who don't have access to that same
information — undermining trust in fair market pricing for everyone
else.

### A Real Legal Boundary

This isn't just considered unethical — in many countries, including
described protections under U.S. securities law, it's a real, prosecutable
legal violation, not merely a matter of personal judgment.

## Worked Example

A company employee who learns, before it's publicly announced, that
their company is about to report disappointing earnings, and sells their
shares based on that non-public knowledge before the announcement, would
be engaging in insider trading.

## Practice Quiz

1. Insider trading generally refers to:
   A. Trading stock based on publicly available information
   B. Trading stock based on material, non-public information
   C. A type of dividend payment
   D. A required disclosure form

2. True or False: Insider trading is only an ethical guideline, not an
   actual legal violation.

3. Why is insider trading generally prohibited?
   A. It has no real justification
   B. It creates an unfair advantage over investors without access to
      the same information
   C. It's only illegal for company executives
   D. It only applies to bonds

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Insider trading involves trading on material,
non-public information.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. It's a real, prosecutable legal violation in
many jurisdictions, not just an ethical matter.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. It undermines fair, equal access to market-moving
information.

## Key Takeaways

- Insider trading means trading based on material, non-public
  information.
- It's prohibited because it creates an unfair informational advantage.
- It's a real legal violation, not just an ethical guideline.
