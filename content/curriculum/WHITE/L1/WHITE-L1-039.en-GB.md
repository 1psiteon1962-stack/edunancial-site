# What Is a Stock Exchange? (NYSE, Nasdaq — What They Actually Do)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a stock exchange in plain terms.
- Explain its core function beyond just "a place stocks trade."
- Name two well-known examples.

## Core Content

### What a Stock Exchange Is

A stock exchange is an organized marketplace where buyers and sellers
come together to trade shares of publicly listed companies, under a
common set of rules.

### Its Core Function

Beyond simply hosting trades, an exchange provides price discovery
(helping determine a fair current price through matching real buyers and
sellers) and enforces listing standards companies must meet to be traded
there, adding a layer of structure and oversight to the market.

### Well-Known Examples

*(VERIFIED FACT, subject to change over time — company listings shift.)*
The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the Nasdaq are two well-known
stock exchanges, each with somewhat different listing requirements and
trading structures.

## Worked Example

When an investor places an order to buy or sell a share, that order is
routed to the relevant exchange, which matches it with a corresponding
buy or sell order at an agreed price — the basic mechanism behind
"trading a stock."

## Practice Quiz

1. A stock exchange is:
   A. A single company's internal trading desk
   B. An organized marketplace for trading shares of listed companies
   C. A government tax agency
   D. A type of mutual fund

2. True or False: Stock exchanges only host trades, with no listing
   standards or oversight role.

3. Name one well-known stock exchange mentioned in this lesson.
   A. Federal Reserve
   B. NYSE or Nasdaq
   C. IRS
   D. SEC

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An exchange is an organized marketplace for trading
listed shares.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Exchanges also provide price discovery and
enforce listing standards.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. NYSE and Nasdaq are well-known examples.

## Key Takeaways

- A stock exchange is an organized marketplace for trading listed
  shares.
- It provides price discovery and enforces listing standards.
- NYSE and Nasdaq are two well-known examples.
