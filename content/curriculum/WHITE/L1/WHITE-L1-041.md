# What Is a Robo-Advisor?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a robo-advisor in plain terms.
- Explain how it typically builds a portfolio.
- Identify a basic trade-off versus a human advisor.

## Core Content

### What a Robo-Advisor Is

A robo-advisor is an automated service that builds and manages an
investment portfolio based on an investor's goals and risk tolerance,
generally using algorithms rather than a human advisor making individual
decisions.

### How It Typically Builds a Portfolio

A robo-advisor typically asks a series of questions about goals, time
horizon, and comfort with risk, then automatically allocates money
across a mix of investments (often ETFs, covered earlier in this level)
matching that profile.

### The Basic Trade-Off vs. a Human Advisor

Robo-advisors generally offer lower fees and easier access than a human
financial advisor, but typically offer less personalized guidance for
complex, individual financial situations — a real trade-off between cost
and customization.

## Worked Example

An investor with a straightforward goal (like general long-term
retirement saving) and a moderate risk tolerance might use a
robo-advisor to automatically build and maintain an appropriate
portfolio at a lower cost than hiring a human advisor.

## Practice Quiz

1. A robo-advisor is:
   A. A human financial advisor working remotely
   B. An automated service that builds and manages a portfolio using
      algorithms
   C. A type of bond
   D. A government retirement program

2. True or False: Robo-advisors generally charge higher fees than human
   financial advisors.

3. What is a basic trade-off of using a robo-advisor instead of a human
   advisor?
   A. No trade-off exists
   B. Lower fees and easier access, but typically less personalized
      guidance
   C. Robo-advisors guarantee higher returns
   D. Human advisors are always free

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A robo-advisor automates portfolio management using
algorithms.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Robo-advisors generally charge lower fees than
human advisors.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Lower cost and access trade off against personalized
guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- A robo-advisor automates portfolio building and management.
- It typically allocates investments based on goals and risk tolerance.
- It generally trades lower cost for less personalized guidance compared
  to a human advisor.
