# What Is Asset Allocation?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define asset allocation in plain terms.
- Distinguish it from diversification within one asset category.
- Explain why it's often described as a foundational portfolio decision.

## Core Content

### What Asset Allocation Is

Asset allocation refers to how an investor divides their overall
portfolio across different broad categories of assets — such as stocks,
bonds, and cash — rather than which specific investments to pick within
each category.

### How It Differs From Diversification

Diversification (covered earlier in this level) generally refers to
spreading investments within a category, like owning many different
stocks. Asset allocation operates one level up — deciding how much goes
into stocks overall versus bonds overall versus cash overall.

### Why It's Foundational

Asset allocation is often described as one of the most significant
decisions in building a portfolio, because the broad mix between asset
categories (not just which specific stocks are chosen) has a substantial
effect on overall risk and potential return.

## Worked Example

An investor might decide on an allocation of 70% stocks, 25% bonds, and
5% cash — a decision made before ever choosing which specific stocks or
bonds to hold within that structure.

## Practice Quiz

1. Asset allocation refers to:
   A. Picking specific individual stocks
   B. Dividing a portfolio across broad categories like stocks, bonds,
      and cash
   C. A type of dividend
   D. A government retirement program

2. True or False: Asset allocation and diversification within one
   category mean exactly the same thing.

3. Why is asset allocation often described as a foundational decision?
   A. It has little effect on a portfolio
   B. The broad mix between categories substantially affects overall
      risk and return
   C. It only matters for bonds
   D. It replaces the need for any other investment decisions

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Asset allocation is the broad category-level
division of a portfolio.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Allocation is category-level; diversification is
within-category.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. The broad category mix substantially affects overall
portfolio behavior.

## Key Takeaways

- Asset allocation is how a portfolio is divided across broad categories.
- It's a different, higher-level decision than diversification within a
  category.
- It's considered a foundational decision in building a portfolio.
