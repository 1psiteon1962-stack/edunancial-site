# What Is Rebalancing a Portfolio?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define rebalancing in plain terms.
- Explain why a portfolio's allocation drifts over time.
- Explain the basic mechanics of correcting that drift.

## Core Content

### What Rebalancing Is

Rebalancing means periodically adjusting a portfolio back to its
original target asset allocation (covered in the previous lesson), after
market movement has shifted it away from that target.

### Why Allocation Drifts Over Time

If stocks grow faster than bonds over a period, a portfolio that started
at 70% stocks / 30% bonds might drift to 80% stocks / 20% bonds purely
from market movement — even though the investor never actively changed
anything.

### The Basic Mechanics

Rebalancing generally involves selling some of what's grown to be
overweighted and buying more of what's become underweighted, bringing
the portfolio back toward its original target percentages.

## Worked Example

A portfolio drifts from a 70/30 stock-bond target to 80/20 after stocks
outperform. Rebalancing means selling enough stock and buying enough
bonds to bring the mix back closer to the original 70/30 target.

## Practice Quiz

1. Rebalancing means:
   A. Picking new individual stocks
   B. Adjusting a portfolio back to its original target allocation
   C. Closing an investment account
   D. Increasing overall risk permanently

2. True or False: A portfolio's allocation stays exactly the same over
   time without any action.

3. If stocks grow faster than bonds, what typically happens to a
   portfolio's stock allocation without rebalancing?
   A. It decreases automatically
   B. It increases, becoming overweighted relative to the original
      target
   C. It stays exactly the same
   D. Bonds automatically increase instead

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Rebalancing restores a portfolio to its original
target mix.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Market movement causes allocations to drift over
time.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Faster stock growth increases its share of the
portfolio without action.

## Key Takeaways

- Rebalancing restores a portfolio to its original target allocation.
- Allocations drift over time purely from market movement.
- Rebalancing involves selling overweighted assets and buying
  underweighted ones.
