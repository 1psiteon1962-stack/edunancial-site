# What Is a Target-Date Fund?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a target-date fund in plain terms.
- Explain how its allocation changes over time.
- Explain why this connects to the rebalancing concept.

## Core Content

### What a Target-Date Fund Is

A target-date fund is a fund built around a specific future date (often
a planned retirement year), automatically managing its own asset
allocation to fit an investor's time horizon.

### How Its Allocation Changes Over Time

A target-date fund typically starts with a higher allocation to stocks
(for growth potential, given the long time horizon) and gradually shifts
toward more bonds and cash as the target date approaches (for more
stability as the goal gets closer) — automatically, without the investor
having to make that adjustment manually.

### The Connection to Rebalancing

A target-date fund essentially automates both the rebalancing and the
gradually shifting allocation strategy in one product — an investor
doesn't need to periodically rebalance it themselves the way they might
with a portfolio they built manually.

## Worked Example

An investor planning to retire in 30 years might choose a target-date
fund for that year. Early on, it holds mostly stocks; as the target
year approaches, it automatically shifts toward a more conservative mix
of bonds and cash.

## Practice Quiz

1. A target-date fund is built around:
   A. A single company's stock
   B. A specific future date, often a planned retirement year
   C. A type of bond exclusively
   D. A government tax filing deadline

2. True or False: A target-date fund's allocation stays exactly the
   same from start to finish.

3. What generally happens to a target-date fund's allocation as the
   target date approaches?
   A. It shifts toward more stocks
   B. It shifts toward more bonds and cash, for stability
   C. It becomes entirely cash immediately
   D. Nothing changes at all

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A target-date fund is built around a specific future
date.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Its allocation shifts automatically over time.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. It gradually becomes more conservative as the date
nears.

## Key Takeaways

- A target-date fund is built around a specific future date.
- Its allocation automatically shifts from growth-focused to more
  conservative over time.
- It automates both allocation shifting and rebalancing in one product.
