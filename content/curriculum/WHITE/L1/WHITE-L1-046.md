# What Is a Stock Buyback? (Introductory Version — Deeper Look in Level 2)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a stock buyback in plain terms.
- Explain the basic mechanical effect on shares outstanding.
- Recognize that a deeper look at its effect on ratios comes in Level 2.

## Core Content

### What a Stock Buyback Is

A stock buyback happens when a company repurchases its own shares from
the open market, reducing the number of shares outstanding (a concept
introduced earlier in this level).

### The Basic Mechanical Effect

With fewer shares outstanding and the same total company earnings, each
remaining share represents a slightly larger slice of the company —
connecting back to EPS (earnings per share), covered earlier in this
level.

### A Preview of Level 2

*ASSUMPTION (for teaching purposes):* This introductory lesson covers
only the basic mechanics. Level 2 covers, in more depth, how buybacks
can make per-share numbers like EPS and P/E look better without the
underlying business actually improving.

## Worked Example

A company with 1,000,000 shares outstanding buys back 100,000 shares,
leaving 900,000. With the same total net income, EPS goes up simply
because that income is now divided among fewer shares.

## Practice Quiz

1. A stock buyback means:
   A. A company issuing brand new shares
   B. A company repurchasing its own shares from the market
   C. A stock split
   D. A dividend payment

2. True or False: A buyback increases the total number of shares
   outstanding.

3. With the same net income, what happens to EPS after a buyback reduces
   shares outstanding?
   A. EPS decreases
   B. EPS increases, since income is now divided among fewer shares
   C. EPS stays exactly the same
   D. EPS becomes zero

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A buyback is a company repurchasing its own shares.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A buyback reduces, not increases, shares
outstanding.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Fewer shares dividing the same income mechanically
raises EPS.

## Key Takeaways

- A buyback is a company repurchasing its own shares, reducing shares
  outstanding.
- This mechanically raises EPS, even without a change in total earnings.
- Level 2 covers, in more depth, how this can distort per-share ratios.
