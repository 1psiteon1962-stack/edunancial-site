# What Is a Bear Trap / Value Trap? (Why "Cheap" Can Be a Warning Sign)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a value trap in plain terms.
- Explain why a low price alone doesn't indicate a good deal.
- Connect this concept back to the P/E ratio lesson.

## Core Content

### What a Value Trap Is

A value trap is a stock that appears cheap based on simple metrics (like
a low P/E ratio, covered earlier in this level) but is actually cheap for
a real, lasting reason — declining business fundamentals, not a
temporary mispricing.

### Why Low Price Alone Isn't Enough

A stock's price or ratio being lower than it used to be doesn't
automatically mean it's a bargain. Sometimes a lower price accurately
reflects a real, ongoing decline in the company's business prospects —
the market correctly pricing in a genuine problem, not making a mistake.

### The Connection to P/E

A low P/E ratio can mean a stock is undervalued — or it can mean the
market expects declining future earnings, making the current low price
appropriate rather than a bargain. Distinguishing between these requires
looking deeper than the ratio alone.

## Worked Example

A stock's price drops significantly, and its P/E ratio looks
attractively low compared to its history. If that drop reflects a real,
ongoing decline in the company's core business rather than a temporary
overreaction, buying it as a "bargain" could be falling into a value
trap.

## Practice Quiz

1. A value trap is:
   A. A stock that is genuinely undervalued and a good deal
   B. A stock that looks cheap but is cheap for a real, lasting reason
   C. A type of bond
   D. A guaranteed profitable investment

2. True or False: A low P/E ratio always means a stock is a bargain.

3. Why might a low price accurately reflect a real problem rather than a
   bargain?
   A. Prices never reflect real problems
   B. The market may be correctly pricing in declining business
      prospects
   C. Low prices are always mistakes
   D. This never happens in real markets

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. A value trap looks cheap but reflects a real,
lasting problem.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. A low P/E can reflect a real, deserved decline,
not just a bargain.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Markets can correctly price in genuine, ongoing
problems.

## Key Takeaways

- A value trap looks cheap but is cheap for a real, lasting reason.
- Low price or a low ratio alone doesn't guarantee a bargain.
- A low P/E can reflect either undervaluation or a genuine, deserved
  decline.
