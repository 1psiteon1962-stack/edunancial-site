# Reading a Brokerage Statement: What Are You Actually Looking At?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Identify the core sections of a typical brokerage statement.
- Explain what each section actually tells you.
- Connect statement terms back to concepts from earlier in this level.

## Core Content

### Core Sections of a Statement

A typical brokerage statement generally includes: account holdings
(what's owned and how much), transaction history (recent buys, sells,
dividends), and a performance summary — though exact formatting varies
by brokerage.

### What Each Section Tells You

The holdings section shows current positions and their market value.
Transaction history shows recent activity, including dividend payments
(covered earlier in this level). The performance summary often reports
something like a time-weighted return (covered in the previous lesson),
not necessarily an investor's personal dollar-weighted experience.

### Connecting Back to This Level's Concepts

Reading a statement well means recognizing terms already covered:
shares owned, market value, dividends received, and the distinction
between the fund's reported return and an individual's actual personal
return based on their own contribution timing.

## Worked Example

An investor reviewing their statement sees they own shares in an ETF,
notices a recent dividend payment listed in the transaction history, and
sees a reported year-to-date return — recognizing that number likely
reflects time-weighted performance, not necessarily their own exact
personal return if they added money partway through the year.

## Practice Quiz

1. A brokerage statement's holdings section typically shows:
   A. Only the brokerage's fees
   B. Current positions and their market value
   C. Only the investor's tax return
   D. A list of unrelated companies

2. True or False: The performance summary on a statement always reflects
   an investor's exact personal dollar-weighted return.

3. What does the transaction history section typically include?
   A. Only future planned trades
   B. Recent buys, sells, and dividend payments
   C. A company's full financial statements
   D. Government tax filings

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. Holdings show current positions and market value.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Reported performance is often time-weighted, not
necessarily the investor's personal dollar-weighted return.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Transaction history covers recent account activity.

## Key Takeaways

- A brokerage statement typically includes holdings, transaction
  history, and a performance summary.
- Reported performance is often time-weighted, not automatically an
  investor's personal return.
- Reading a statement well draws on concepts covered throughout this
  level.
