# Capstone: Building Your First Simple Portfolio, Step by Step

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Apply core concepts from WHITE Level 1 to one connected scenario.
- Identify which concept from this level applies at each step.
- Explain how the pieces of building a portfolio fit together as a
  whole.

## Core Content

### The Scenario

James wants to start investing for the first time. He wants to
understand what he's actually doing at each step, not just follow
instructions blindly.

### Walking Through It

James first makes sure he has an emergency fund (Lesson 35) in a liquid,
low-risk place before investing anything else. He opens a brokerage
account (Lesson 21), understanding it's different from a retirement
account, and separately considers whether his employer offers a 401(k)
match (Lesson 18) worth taking advantage of first.

He decides to invest in an ETF (Lesson 9) rather than picking individual
stocks, valuing the diversification (Lesson 13) it provides. He checks
its expense ratio (Lesson 12), understanding that small percentage
differences compound over time. He decides to use dollar-cost averaging
(Lesson 22), investing a fixed amount monthly rather than trying to
time the market.

He sets an asset allocation (Lesson 43) of mostly stocks, given his long
time horizon, understanding he'll need to rebalance (Lesson 44)
periodically as the market shifts that mix. He knows his account
statement (Lesson 49) will show holdings, transactions, and a
performance figure that may not exactly match his personal
dollar-weighted return (Lesson 48), since he's contributing gradually
over time.

### Why This Matters

None of these decisions happen in isolation in a real portfolio — they
connect as one process, built on understanding, not just following
steps blindly.

## Worked Example

If James invests $300/month via dollar-cost averaging into a diversified
ETF with a low expense ratio, inside a standard brokerage account, after
already funding his emergency fund and capturing any available employer
match — he's applied nearly every major concept from this level in one
connected plan.

## Practice Quiz

1. Why did James make sure he had an emergency fund before investing
   further?
   A. It's not actually necessary
   B. It protects his other financial plans from being disrupted by
      unexpected expenses
   C. Emergency funds guarantee investment returns
   D. It has no connection to investing

2. True or False: James's reported account performance will always
   exactly match his personal dollar-weighted return.

3. Why did James choose an ETF over picking individual stocks?
   A. ETFs guarantee higher returns
   B. He valued the diversification it provides
   C. ETFs have no expense ratio
   D. Individual stocks are illegal for beginners

## Answer Key

1. B
2. False
3. B

## Answers and Explanations

### Question 1

Correct answer: B. An emergency fund protects longer-term plans from
short-term disruptions, as covered in Lesson 35.

### Question 2

Correct answer: False. Reported performance is often time-weighted, not
his exact personal dollar-weighted return, as covered in Lessons 48-49.

### Question 3

Correct answer: B. Diversification was James's stated reason, connecting
to Lesson 13.

## Key Takeaways

- Building a first portfolio connects nearly every concept from this
  level into one process.
- Order matters: emergency fund first, then account type, then
  investment choice, then ongoing management.
- This capstone marks the completion of WHITE Level 1 — Level 2 builds
  on this foundation with deeper valuation and investment philosophy
  concepts.
