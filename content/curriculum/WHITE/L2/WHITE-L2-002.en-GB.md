## WHITE-L2-002
### Investment Philosophies as Lenses: An Overview

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the concept of an "investment philosophy" as a lens for evaluating opportunities.
- Name the four philosophies covered in this level: value, growth, income, and passive/index.
- Explain why philosophies function as lenses rather than rigid categories.

**2. Core Content**
An investment philosophy is a general lens — a consistent set of questions and priorities — through which an investor evaluates opportunities. This level covers four widely discussed philosophies: value investing (Lesson 3), growth investing (Lesson 4), income investing (Lesson 5), and passive/index investing (Lesson 6).

Thinking of these as "lenses" rather than fixed categories is deliberate. Many real-world investors and funds blend elements of more than one lens, and the same underlying asset can look attractive or unattractive depending on which lens is applied to it. A stock trading at a low price relative to earnings might appeal to a value-oriented investor while being ignored by a growth-oriented investor focused on a company's future expansion potential, even though both are looking at the exact same company.

Understanding these lenses is not about picking "the right one" and discarding the others. It is about recognising which lens (or blend of lenses) fits an investor's own goals, risk tolerance, and time horizon — directly connecting back to Lesson 1's framework for narrowing choice.

**3. Worked Example**
Two investors look at the same company: a well-established utility with modest growth but a long history of steady dividend payments. An income-focused investor is drawn to the company specifically for its dividend history and cash flow potential. A growth-focused investor passes on the same company, viewing its modest growth prospects as unattractive relative to faster-expanding alternatives.

Neither investor is wrong — they are applying different lenses to the same underlying facts, based on their own goals. This illustrates why "the best investment" is not a fixed, universal answer; it depends significantly on the lens (and the investor) applying it.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An investment philosophy is a consistent lens or set of priorities used to evaluate opportunities.
2. Philosophies function as lenses because investors often blend them, and the same asset can appear differently depending on which lens is applied.
3. The same company can reasonably appeal differently to investors applying different lenses and priorities.
4. The two investors applied different lenses to the identical set of facts, reflecting their different goals.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An investment philosophy is a consistent lens for evaluating opportunities, not a guaranteed formula.
- This level covers four widely discussed lenses: value, growth, income, and passive/index.
- The same asset can look different depending on which lens is applied.
- Understanding these lenses helps an investor find the fit (or blend) that matches their own goals and time horizon.
