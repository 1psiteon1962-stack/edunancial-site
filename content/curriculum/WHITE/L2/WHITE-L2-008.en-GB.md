## WHITE-L2-008
### Stocks vs. Bonds: Two Pieces, Different Roles

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the fundamental legal and financial difference between owning a stock and owning a bond.
- Describe how stocks and bonds generally behave differently in a portfolio.
- Explain why investors often hold both, rather than only one.

**2. Core Content**
A stock represents partial ownership in a company, giving the holder a claim on future profits (potentially through dividends) and the company's residual value, with returns that can vary widely and are not guaranteed. A bond represents a loan made by the investor to the issuer (a company or government), with the issuer generally obligated to make specified interest payments and repay the principal at maturity, regardless of how profitable the issuer's underlying operations are in a given year — though the issuer's ability to actually make these payments is not guaranteed, which is why credit risk (Lesson 12) matters.

This distinction shapes how each tends to behave in a portfolio: bonds generally offer more predictable (though not risk-free) income and tend to be less volatile than stocks in normal market conditions, while stocks generally offer higher long-term growth potential alongside higher volatility and no guaranteed payments. Many investors hold both specifically because their different behaviours can help balance a portfolio's overall risk and return profile — a concept developed further in Lesson 16's discussion of asset allocation.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing his portfolio's behaviour during a period of stock market volatility, Felix notices his bond holdings experienced smaller price swings than his stock holdings during the same period, consistent with the general tendencies described in this lesson (though not a guarantee for every bond in every situation). This experience helps him understand, in a concrete way, why his portfolio blends both stocks and bonds rather than holding only one — each piece plays a different role, and their different typical behaviours work together rather than duplicating each other.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does owning a stock represent?
   a) A loan to the company
   b) Partial ownership in a company
   c) A government bond
   d) A savings account
2. What does owning a bond represent?
   a) Partial ownership in a company
   b) A loan made by the investor to the issuer, with specified payment obligations
   c) A type of real estate
   d) A guaranteed profit
3. True or False: Bonds are entirely risk-free investments with guaranteed payments in all circumstances.
4. Why do many investors hold both stocks and bonds?
   a) They behave identically, so it doesn't matter
   b) Their different typical behaviours can help balance a portfolio's overall risk and return profile
   c) It is legally required
   d) Bonds guarantee higher returns than stocks

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A stock represents partial ownership in a company, with returns tied to its performance.
2. A bond represents a loan to the issuer, with specified interest and principal repayment obligations.
3. Bond payments depend on the issuer's ability to pay, meaning bonds carry credit risk, not a guarantee.
4. Stocks and bonds tend to behave differently, and combining them can help balance a portfolio's overall risk and return.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A stock represents partial company ownership; a bond represents a loan with specified payment obligations.
- Bonds generally offer more predictable income and lower volatility; stocks generally offer higher growth potential and volatility.
- Neither is risk-free — bond payments depend on the issuer's ability to pay.
- Many investors hold both because their different typical behaviours can help balance overall portfolio risk and return.
