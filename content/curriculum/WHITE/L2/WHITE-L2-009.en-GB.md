## WHITE-L2-009
### Understanding Bond Basics: Face Value, Coupon, and Maturity

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define face value, coupon rate, and maturity date for a bond.
- Explain how these three elements combine to define a bond's basic terms.
- Calculate a simple annual coupon payment given face value and coupon rate.

**2. Core Content**
Every bond has three fundamental defining elements. Face value (also called par value) is the amount the issuer agrees to repay the bondholder at maturity — commonly a round number such as £1,000 per bond, though this varies. Coupon rate is the stated annual interest rate the issuer pays on the face value, usually distributed in periodic (often semiannual) payments. Maturity date is the specific date on which the issuer must repay the face value in full.

Together, these three elements define a bond's basic cash flow structure: an investor buying a bond generally knows, at purchase, exactly what interest payments to expect and when the principal will be repaid — assuming the issuer does not default (Lesson 12). This predictability is a key reason bonds are often associated with the income lens (Lesson 5) and with providing portfolio stability (Lesson 8).

**3. Worked Example**
An investor purchases a bond with a £1,000 face value, a 4% annual coupon rate, and a 10-year maturity. This means the bond will pay £40 per year in interest (commonly split into two £20 semiannual payments), and the issuer will repay the full £1,000 face value when the bond matures in 10 years — assuming no default occurs in the meantime.

Understanding these three numbers gives the investor a clear, upfront picture of the bond's expected cash flow over its life, distinct from a stock, where future dividend payments and eventual value are far less certain.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a bond's "face value"?
   a) The current trading price
   b) The amount the issuer repays the bondholder at maturity
   c) The dividend yield
   d) The stock's market capitalisation
2. What does the "coupon rate" represent?
   a) The bond's maturity date
   b) The stated annual interest rate paid on the face value
   c) The company's stock price
   d) A tax deduction
3. True or False: A bond's face value, coupon rate, and maturity date are unrelated to its expected cash flow.
4. In the worked example, how much annual interest does the bond pay?
   a) £4
   b) £40
   c) £400
   d) £1,000

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Face value is the amount the issuer agrees to repay at maturity.
2. Coupon rate is the stated annual interest rate paid on the bond's face value.
3. Together, these elements directly define a bond's expected cash flow structure over its life.
4. A 4% coupon rate on a £1,000 face value bond produces £40 in annual interest.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Face value is the amount repaid at maturity; coupon rate is the stated annual interest rate; maturity date is when principal is repaid.
- These three elements together define a bond's basic, upfront-knowable cash flow structure.
- This predictability (assuming no default) is a key reason bonds support the income lens and portfolio stability.
- Calculating a bond's coupon payment is a straightforward application of face value multiplied by coupon rate.
