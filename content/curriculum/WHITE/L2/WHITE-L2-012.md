## WHITE-L2-012
### Bond Ratings and Credit Risk

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of bond credit ratings.
- Distinguish between investment-grade and high-yield (speculative) bonds at a conceptual level.
- Explain how credit risk interacts with yield.

**2. Core Content**
Credit rating agencies assess and assign ratings to bond issuers (and sometimes specific bond issues), reflecting an estimate of the issuer's ability and willingness to meet its debt obligations. These ratings are generally grouped into two broad categories: investment-grade (indicating relatively lower assessed credit risk) and high-yield or "speculative-grade" (sometimes informally called "junk bonds," indicating relatively higher assessed credit risk).

Ratings are assessments, not guarantees — a highly rated issuer can still default, and a lower-rated issuer can still fully meet its obligations. Ratings are one input for evaluating credit risk, not a substitute for understanding an issuer's actual financial condition.

Credit risk interacts directly with yield: bonds perceived as carrying higher default risk generally must offer higher yields to attract investors willing to accept that risk, while bonds perceived as safer generally offer lower yields. This is a specific application of a broader risk/return relationship that recurs throughout paper assets.

**3. Worked Example**
Comparing two bonds, Teo notices one carries a high investment-grade rating and offers a modest yield, while another carries a high-yield (speculative-grade) rating and offers a significantly higher yield. Rather than assuming the higher-yield bond is simply a "better deal," Teo recognizes the higher yield exists specifically to compensate for higher assessed credit risk — and he researches the issuer's underlying financial condition further before deciding whether that added risk fits his own risk tolerance and goals.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What do bond credit ratings generally assess?
   a) The stock market's overall direction
   b) An issuer's ability and willingness to meet its debt obligations
   c) The bond's maturity date only
   d) Real estate values
2. What does a "high-yield" or speculative-grade bond generally indicate?
   a) Lower assessed credit risk
   b) Higher assessed credit risk
   c) Guaranteed repayment
   d) No risk of default
3. True or False: A bond credit rating is a guarantee that the issuer will never default.
4. Why did the high-yield bond in the worked example offer a significantly higher yield than the investment-grade bond?
   a) It was risk-free
   b) The higher yield compensates for higher assessed credit risk
   c) It had a shorter maturity
   d) It was backed by real estate

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Bond ratings assess an issuer's ability and willingness to meet its debt obligations.
2. High-yield/speculative-grade ratings indicate relatively higher assessed credit risk.
3. Ratings are assessments, not guarantees — even highly rated issuers can default.
4. The higher yield on the speculative-grade bond compensates investors for accepting higher assessed credit risk.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Bond credit ratings assess an issuer's ability and willingness to meet debt obligations.
- Ratings are generally grouped into investment-grade and high-yield (speculative-grade) categories.
- Ratings are assessments, not guarantees of repayment or default.
- Higher assessed credit risk generally corresponds to higher required yield, reflecting a core risk/return relationship.
