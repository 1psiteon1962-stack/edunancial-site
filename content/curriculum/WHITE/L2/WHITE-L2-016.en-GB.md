## WHITE-L2-016
### Asset Allocation: Combining Stocks, Bonds, and Cash

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define asset allocation at a conceptual level.
- Explain how asset allocation relates to risk tolerance and time horizon.
- Recognise that there is no single universal allocation formula.

**2. Core Content**
Asset allocation refers to how an investor divides their portfolio among broad asset categories — commonly stocks, bonds, and cash (or cash equivalents) — reflecting the Lesson 8 discussion of how these categories tend to behave differently. Widely discussed research suggests that asset allocation is one of the most significant drivers of a portfolio's overall risk and long-term return pattern, arguably more influential than the selection of specific individual securities within each category.

Generally, a higher allocation to stocks is associated with higher potential long-term returns alongside higher volatility, while a higher allocation to bonds and cash is associated with more stability and lower expected volatility, alongside typically lower long-term return potential. The "right" allocation depends on an investor's specific time horizon (Lesson 27) and risk tolerance (Lesson 26) — there is no single universal formula, despite some popular informal rules of thumb that oversimplify this deeply personal decision.

**3. Worked Example**
Two investors of different ages consider their asset allocation. A 25-year-old with decades until retirement and a stated high tolerance for short-term volatility allocates a large majority of her portfolio to stocks, accepting more volatility in exchange for higher long-term growth potential over her long time horizon. A 62-year-old approaching retirement within a few years allocates a more balanced mix between stocks and bonds, prioritising more stability given his shorter remaining time horizon before he expects to begin drawing on the portfolio for income.

Neither allocation is universally "correct" — each reflects the specific investor's own time horizon and risk tolerance.

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Asset allocation refers to how a portfolio is divided among broad categories like stocks, bonds, and cash.
2. Asset allocation is widely considered a significant driver of a portfolio's overall risk and long-term return pattern.
3. The right allocation depends on individual time horizon and risk tolerance — there is no single universal formula.
4. The two investors' differing time horizons and risk tolerances appropriately led to differing allocation choices.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Asset allocation is how a portfolio is divided among stocks, bonds, and cash.
- It is widely considered one of the most significant drivers of overall portfolio risk and return.
- Higher stock allocation generally means higher potential return and volatility; higher bond/cash allocation generally means more stability.
- The right allocation depends on individual time horizon and risk tolerance — there is no single universal formula.
