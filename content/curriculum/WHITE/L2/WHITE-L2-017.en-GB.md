## WHITE-L2-017
### Diversification Within Paper Assets

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Distinguish diversification within an asset class from asset allocation across classes.
- Explain the general purpose of diversification.
- Identify examples of diversification within stocks and within bonds.

**2. Core Content**
While asset allocation (Lesson 16) addresses how a portfolio divides across broad categories, diversification addresses how a portfolio spreads risk within a given category, echoing the RED-track discussion of property diversification (RED-L2-038). Within stocks, this can mean holding many different companies across different sectors (Lesson 24) and geographies (Lesson 25), rather than concentrating heavily in just a few individual companies. Within bonds, this can mean holding issuers of varying credit quality, maturity, and type, rather than concentrating in a single issuer or bond.

The general purpose of diversification is reducing the impact of any single holding's poor performance on the overall portfolio — if one company or bond issuer struggles, a well-diversified portfolio feels a smaller relative impact than a concentrated one would. This does not eliminate market-wide risk (a broad market downturn still affects a diversified portfolio), but it does reduce company-specific or issuer-specific risk.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing his stock holdings, Amara realises he holds twenty different companies, but eighteen of them are concentrated in a single industry sector. Recognising this as inadequate diversification despite the seemingly large number of holdings, he restructures his portfolio to spread his stock exposure across multiple sectors, reducing his vulnerability to a downturn affecting that one particular industry specifically, even though his total number of individual holdings does not need to change dramatically to achieve this improvement.

**4. Practice Quiz**

   a) They are identical concepts
   b) Asset allocation divides across broad categories; diversification spreads risk within a category
   c) Diversification only applies to real estate
   d) Asset allocation only applies to bonds
2. What is the general purpose of diversification?
   a) To guarantee profit
   b) To reduce the impact of any single holding's poor performance on the overall portfolio
   c) To eliminate all market risk
   d) To concentrate risk in one company
3. True or False: Holding twenty stocks automatically guarantees adequate diversification, regardless of sector concentration.
4. What problem did Amara identify in his own portfolio?
   a) Too few total holdings
   b) Heavy concentration in a single industry sector despite a seemingly large number of holdings
   c) Too much bond exposure
   d) No stocks at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Asset allocation addresses broad category division; diversification addresses spreading risk within a given category.
2. Diversification reduces the relative impact of any single holding's poor performance on the overall portfolio.
3. A large number of holdings concentrated in one sector does not achieve adequate diversification.
4. Amara's holdings, despite their number, were heavily concentrated in a single sector, undermining true diversification.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Diversification spreads risk within an asset class, distinct from asset allocation across broad categories.
- It reduces company-specific or issuer-specific risk, though it does not eliminate broad market risk.
- A large number of holdings does not guarantee diversification if they are concentrated in similar sectors or issuers.
- This concept echoes the RED-track diversification discussion, applied here within paper assets.
