## WHITE-L2-023
### Market Capitalization: Small, Mid, and Large Cap

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define market capitalization at a conceptual level.
- Distinguish small-cap, mid-cap, and large-cap companies generally.
- Explain general risk/return tendencies associated with each category.

**2. Core Content**
Market capitalization ("market cap") is calculated by multiplying a company's current share price by its total number of outstanding shares, providing a measure of the company's total market value as perceived by investors at a given moment. Companies are commonly grouped into broad categories — small-cap, mid-cap, and large-cap — based on ranges of market capitalization, though the exact dollar thresholds defining these categories vary by source and change over time, making specific cutoffs less important than understanding the general pattern.

Generally, large-cap companies tend to be more established, with longer operating histories, and often exhibit somewhat lower volatility than smaller companies, though not always lower long-term growth potential. Small-cap companies tend to be earlier in their growth trajectory, potentially offering higher long-term growth potential but generally with higher volatility and risk, including a higher likelihood of business failure for any individual small-cap company. Mid-cap companies generally sit between these two tendencies.

These are general tendencies, not fixed rules — individual companies within any category can behave differently from the broader pattern.

**3. Worked Example**
Building a diversified stock allocation, Yara deliberately includes exposure to large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap companies rather than concentrating only in one category, recognizing that this spread provides exposure to the generally different risk/return tendencies associated with each — some stability and established performance from large-cap holdings, alongside higher growth potential (and higher volatility) from smaller companies, rather than betting entirely on one category's general tendency playing out as expected.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. How is market capitalization generally calculated?
   a) Total company revenue
   b) Current share price multiplied by total outstanding shares
   c) The company's total debt
   d) The company's dividend yield
2. What is a general tendency associated with small-cap companies?
   a) Lower volatility and lower growth potential
   b) Higher potential growth alongside higher volatility and risk
   c) Guaranteed higher returns
   d) No risk of business failure
3. True or False: All small-cap companies always behave identically according to the general small-cap pattern.
4. Why did Yara include exposure to all three market cap categories?
   a) To eliminate diversification
   b) To gain exposure to the differing general risk/return tendencies of each category
   c) She was required to by regulation
   d) Market cap categories have no real differences

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Market capitalization is calculated as share price multiplied by total outstanding shares.
2. Small-cap companies generally exhibit higher growth potential alongside higher volatility and risk.
3. These are general tendencies; individual companies within any category can behave differently.
4. Yara's diversified approach across market cap categories aimed to capture their differing general risk/return characteristics.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Market capitalization measures a company's total market value based on share price and shares outstanding.
- Companies are commonly grouped into small-cap, mid-cap, and large-cap categories.
- Large-cap companies generally show more stability; small-cap companies generally show higher growth potential and risk.
- These are general tendencies, not fixed rules for any individual company.
