## WHITE-L2-024
### Sector and Industry Diversification

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define sector and industry as classification concepts.
- Explain the risk of sector concentration.
- Describe how sector diversification complements broader diversification (Lesson 17).

**2. Core Content**
Companies are commonly grouped into broad sectors (such as technology, healthcare, financials, energy, and others) and more specific industries within those sectors, reflecting the general area of the economy in which they operate. Building on Lesson 17's discussion of diversification, sector-level concentration is a specific risk worth watching directly: a portfolio that appears to hold many different companies can still be heavily exposed to a single sector's fortunes if those companies are drawn predominantly from the same area of the economy.

Different sectors can perform quite differently depending on broader economic conditions — some sectors tend to perform relatively better during economic expansions, while others may hold up relatively better during downturns, though these patterns are general tendencies rather than fixed rules. Sector diversification means deliberately spreading exposure across multiple sectors, reducing the portfolio's vulnerability to any single sector's specific challenges.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing his portfolio using a sector breakdown tool, Hassan discovers that despite holding shares in fifteen different companies, over 60% of his portfolio's value is concentrated in the technology sector alone, a concentration he had not previously recognized simply by counting his number of individual holdings. He rebalances his approach going forward, deliberately adding exposure to other sectors such as healthcare, financials, and consumer staples, reducing his reliance on any single sector's specific economic fortunes.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does "sector" generally refer to?
   a) A single company's stock price
   b) A broad area of the economy in which companies operate
   c) A type of bond
   d) A government agency
2. What risk does sector concentration create, even with many individual holdings?
   a) No risk at all
   b) Heavy exposure to a single sector's specific fortunes, despite an apparently diversified number of holdings
   c) Guaranteed higher returns
   d) Elimination of all portfolio risk
3. True or False: Counting the number of individual stock holdings alone is sufficient to confirm adequate diversification.
4. What did Hassan's sector breakdown reveal that a simple holdings count had not?
   a) He had too few holdings
   b) Over 60% of his portfolio was concentrated in a single sector
   c) He owned no stocks
   d) His portfolio was perfectly diversified

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Sector refers to a broad area of the economy in which companies operate.
2. Even many individual holdings can be heavily concentrated in a single sector, creating specific vulnerability.
3. A simple count of holdings does not confirm true diversification if those holdings cluster in one sector.
4. Hassan's sector analysis revealed a concentration his simple holdings count had obscured.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Sector and industry describe the broad and specific areas of the economy companies operate within.
- Sector concentration can exist even with a seemingly large number of individual holdings.
- Different sectors can perform differently under different economic conditions, as general tendencies.
- Deliberate sector diversification reduces vulnerability to any single sector's specific challenges.
