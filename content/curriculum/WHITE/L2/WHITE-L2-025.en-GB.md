## WHITE-L2-025
### International and Domestic Exposure

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general concept of international versus domestic investment exposure.
- Identify potential benefits and risks of international exposure.
- Recognise currency risk as a distinct consideration for international investments.

**2. Core Content**
Domestic exposure refers to investments in companies based in an investor's home country; international exposure refers to investments in companies based elsewhere. Adding international exposure can extend the diversification concepts from Lessons 17 and 24 across geographic borders, since different countries' economies do not always move in perfect sync, potentially smoothing overall portfolio performance, though this benefit is not guaranteed in every period, particularly during globally synchronised downturns.

International investing introduces additional considerations not present in purely domestic investing: currency risk (fluctuations in exchange rates can affect returns for an investor whose home currency differs from the investment's currency, independent of the underlying investment's actual performance), differing regulatory and accounting standards across countries, and sometimes differing political and economic stability considerations, which vary significantly by specific country or region.

**3. Worked Example**
Adding international stock exposure to her previously all-domestic portfolio, Camila notices that even when the underlying foreign companies perform well in their local currency, her actual returns (converted back to her home currency) can differ due to exchange rate fluctuations during the period she held the investment. She researches this currency risk dynamic before investing further, incorporating it as a distinct consideration in her overall risk assessment, separate from the underlying business performance of the foreign companies themselves.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is "domestic exposure"?
   a) Investments in foreign companies only
   b) Investments in companies based in an investor's home country
   c) A type of bond
   d) A government programme
2. What is "currency risk"?
   a) The risk of a company's stock price falling
   b) The risk that exchange rate fluctuations affect returns independent of the underlying investment's performance
   c) A type of dividend
   d) A tax on real estate
3. True or False: International diversification always guarantees smoother overall portfolio performance in every market period.
4. What did Camila discover about her international stock exposure?
   a) Currency fluctuations had no effect on her actual returns
   b) Exchange rate fluctuations could affect her returns independent of the foreign companies' actual business performance
   c) International investing eliminated all risk
   d) She could not invest internationally at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Domestic exposure refers to investments in companies based in an investor's home country.
2. Currency risk refers to exchange rate fluctuations affecting returns independent of the underlying investment's actual performance.
3. International diversification's smoothing benefit is not guaranteed in every period, especially during globally synchronised downturns.
4. Camila's actual returns were affected by currency fluctuations separate from the foreign companies' business performance.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Domestic exposure covers home-country investments; international exposure covers foreign investments.
- International exposure can extend diversification benefits across geographic borders, though not guaranteed in every period.
- Currency risk is a distinct consideration unique to international investing.
- Differing regulatory, accounting, and political/economic conditions also vary by specific country or region.
