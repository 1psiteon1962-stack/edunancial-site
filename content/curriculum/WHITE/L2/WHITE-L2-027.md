## WHITE-L2-027
### Time Horizon and Its Effect on Strategy

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define time horizon at a conceptual level.
- Explain how time horizon generally affects appropriate risk-taking.
- Recognize that an investor can have multiple, distinct time horizons for different goals.

**2. Core Content**
Time horizon refers to the length of time before an investor expects to need or use a given pool of invested money. This is one of the most significant factors shaping appropriate strategy (Lessons 16, 26): a longer time horizon generally allows an investor more capacity to endure short-term volatility, since there is more time available for a temporary decline to potentially recover before the money is actually needed. A shorter time horizon generally calls for more conservative positioning, since there is less time to recover from a poorly timed downturn before the funds are needed.

Importantly, an individual investor often has multiple, distinct time horizons for different financial goals simultaneously — retirement savings decades away, a home down payment fund needed in three years, and an emergency fund needed at any moment, without notice. Each of these pools of money may reasonably warrant a different strategy, since they have fundamentally different time horizons, even though they belong to the same individual.

**3. Worked Example**
Reviewing her overall finances, Georgia recognizes she is managing money with several very different time horizons at once: retirement savings she does not expect to touch for over thirty years, a house down payment fund she plans to use within two years, and an emergency fund she might need at any moment. Rather than applying one single strategy across all of this money, Georgia allocates her retirement savings aggressively toward stocks given the long horizon, keeps her house down payment fund in more conservative, stable investments given the short horizon, and keeps her emergency fund in readily accessible cash, recognizing that each pool's distinct time horizon calls for a distinctly different approach.

   a) The total amount of money invested

   c) A type of bond
   d) A tax filing deadline

   a) It generally calls for more conservative positioning
   b) It generally allows more capacity to endure short-term volatility
   c) It has no effect on strategy
   d) It requires avoiding all stocks
3. True or False: An individual investor can only ever have a single time horizon across all their money.

   a) She was required to by regulation
   b) Each pool of money had a fundamentally different time horizon warranting a different approach
   c) All three pools had identical time horizons
   d) She disliked diversification

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. Time horizon is the length of time before an investor expects to need or use invested money.
2. A longer time horizon generally provides more capacity to endure short-term volatility before funds are needed.
3. Individuals often have multiple, distinct time horizons for different financial goals simultaneously.
4. Georgia's differing time horizons for each financial goal appropriately warranted distinctly different strategies.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Time horizon is the length of time before invested money is expected to be needed.
- Longer horizons generally allow more capacity for volatility; shorter horizons generally call for more conservative positioning.
- An individual can have multiple, distinct time horizons for different goals simultaneously.
- Each distinct pool of money may reasonably warrant its own strategy based on its own specific time horizon.
