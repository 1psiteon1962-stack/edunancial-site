## WHITE-L2-031
### Capital Gains: Short-Term vs. Long-Term

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a capital gain at a conceptual level.
- Distinguish short-term from long-term capital gains conceptually.
- Explain why holding period can matter to an investor's decision-making.

**2. Core Content**
A capital gain generally refers to the profit realized when an investment is sold for more than its original purchase price (a capital loss occurs in the opposite scenario). Many jurisdictions, including the U.S., distinguish between short-term capital gains (generally on assets held for a shorter period, often taxed similarly to ordinary income) and long-term capital gains (generally on assets held longer than a specified threshold, often taxed at different, sometimes more favorable, rates) — though specific holding period thresholds and rates are jurisdiction-specific, change with legislation, and require current professional confirmation rather than general assumption.

Because of this general distinction (in jurisdictions where it applies), holding period can become a factor an investor considers, alongside their actual investment thesis — though this lesson emphasizes that tax considerations should generally inform, not override, sound investment decision-making. Selling a fundamentally weakening investment purely to avoid a short-term rate, or holding a fundamentally strong investment past the point it should be sold purely to reach a long-term threshold, both represent letting a tax consideration distort an otherwise sound investment decision.

**3. Worked Example**
Considering selling a stock holding, Julian notices he is just a few weeks away from crossing his jurisdiction's long-term holding threshold. Rather than making his decision purely based on the underlying investment thesis, he factors in this timing consideration as one input, discussing with his tax professional whether waiting those few weeks meaningfully changes his after-tax outcome, while confirming that the underlying fundamentals of the investment still support holding a bit longer regardless of the tax consideration — avoiding a scenario where tax timing alone would have justified holding a fundamentally deteriorating position.

   d) He had no underlying investment thesis at all

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A capital gain is the profit realized from selling an investment for more than its purchase price.
2. Holding period generally distinguishes short-term from long-term capital gains treatment, where this distinction applies.
3. Tax considerations should generally inform, not override, sound underlying investment decision-making.
4. Julian appropriately treated the holding period as one factor alongside, not a replacement for, his investment thesis.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A capital gain is the profit from selling an investment above its purchase price.
- Many jurisdictions distinguish short-term from long-term gains, often with different tax treatment, based on holding period.
- Specific rates and thresholds are jurisdiction-specific and require current professional confirmation.
- Tax considerations should inform, not override, sound underlying investment decision-making.
