## WHITE-L2-032
### Options Basics: Calls and Puts as Concepts

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define a call option and a put option at a conceptual level.
- Explain the general concept of a premium.
- Recognise options as a fundamentally different risk category from owning stock directly.

**2. Core Content**
An option is a contract giving its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset (commonly a stock) at a specified price (the "strike price") within a specified time period, in exchange for an upfront payment called a premium. A call option gives the buyer the right to buy the underlying asset at the strike price; a put option gives the buyer the right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price.

This lesson introduces options strictly as a conceptual building block, not as a recommended strategy. Options differ fundamentally from owning stock directly: a stock owner participates proportionally in a company's ownership and results over an unlimited time horizon, while an option buyer's position has a fixed expiration date and can expire completely worthless if the underlying asset's price does not move favorably relative to the strike price within that specific timeframe, meaning the entire premium paid can be lost — a risk profile meaningfully different from simply owning shares.

**3. Worked Example**
Learning about options for the first time, Priscilla studies a hypothetical example: a call option giving the buyer the right to purchase a stock at a specific strike price before a specific expiration date, in exchange for a premium paid upfront. She notes that if the stock's price stays below the strike price through expiration, the call option would expire worthless, and the buyer would lose the entire premium paid — a stark contrast to owning the stock directly, where a price that stays flat simply means no gain or loss, without a fixed deadline forcing an outcome.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a call option give its buyer?
   a) The obligation to sell an asset
   b) The right, but not the obligation, to buy an asset at a specified price within a specified time
   c) Guaranteed ownership of a company
   d) A fixed dividend payment
2. What is a "premium" in the context of an option?
   a) A type of dividend
   b) The upfront payment made to acquire the option contract
   c) A bond's face value
   d) A tax on stock ownership
3. True or False: An option, like a stock, has no expiration date and cannot become worthless.
4. What key risk distinguishes an option from owning stock directly, per this lesson?
   a) Options have no risk at all
   b) An option can expire worthless, losing the entire premium, due to its fixed time limit
   c) Stocks always expire worthless
   d) There is no difference between the two

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A call option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy an asset at a specified price within a specified time.
2. A premium is the upfront payment made by the option buyer to acquire the contract.
3. Unlike stock ownership, options have a fixed expiration date and can expire completely worthless.
4. The fixed expiration date creating the possibility of a total premium loss is a key risk distinguishing options from stock ownership.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) an asset at a specified price within a specified time.
- The premium is the upfront cost paid to acquire an option contract.
- Options have a fixed expiration date and can expire completely worthless, unlike direct stock ownership.
- This lesson introduces options as a concept only, not as a recommended strategy for any specific learner.
