## WHITE-L2-036
### Bear Markets and Corrections: What They Are

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define "correction" and "bear market" as commonly used terms.
- Explain why these terms describe magnitude, not necessarily cause or duration.
- Recognise the historical pattern of eventual recovery, without treating it as guaranteed for any specific future event.

**2. Core Content**
A "correction" is a commonly used term for a decline of roughly 10% or more from a recent peak in a broad market index; a "bear market" is a commonly used term for a decline of roughly 20% or more from a recent peak. These are descriptive labels for magnitude, not indicators of cause, and they say nothing on their own about how long a decline will last or how quickly (or whether) a recovery will follow — some corrections resolve within weeks, while some bear markets have historically persisted for extended periods.

Historically, broad market indices in many major economies have eventually recovered from corrections and bear markets, often reaching new highs over subsequent years, a pattern that connects to the earlier discussion of long-term time horizon (Lesson 27) and behavioural discipline (Lesson 28). This lesson presents this historical pattern factually, without guaranteeing it will repeat identically for any specific future decline, since every period has its own unique circumstances.

**3. Worked Example**
Experiencing his first genuine bear market as an investor, Cyrus researches historical examples of past bear markets and finds that, while each had unique causes and different recovery timelines, broad market indices had historically gone on to recover and reach new highs in the years following past declines. He does not treat this historical pattern as a guarantee for his current specific situation, but it does provide useful context, alongside his own long time horizon and previously established investment plan (Lesson 28), for maintaining his composure and sticking with his strategy rather than reacting purely to the current headlines.

**5. Answer Key**

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A correction commonly describes a decline of roughly 10% or more from a recent peak.
2. A bear market commonly describes a decline of roughly 20% or more from a recent peak.
3. These terms describe magnitude only; they say nothing about duration or the certainty of any specific future recovery timeline.
4. Cyrus appropriately used historical context as supportive information without treating it as a guarantee for his own specific situation.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A "correction" commonly describes a ~10%+ decline; a "bear market" commonly describes a ~20%+ decline from a recent peak.
- These terms describe magnitude, not cause or guaranteed duration.
- Broad market indices have historically recovered from past declines, though this is not a guarantee for any specific future event.
- This historical context, combined with a long time horizon and an established plan, supports behavioural discipline during downturns.
