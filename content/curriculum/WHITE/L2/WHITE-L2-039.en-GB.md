## WHITE-L2-039
### Target-Date and Lifecycle Funds

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general concept of a target-date or lifecycle fund.
- Describe how these funds automatically adjust allocation over time.
- Identify general considerations for evaluating whether such a fund fits an investor's situation.

**2. Core Content**
A target-date (or lifecycle) fund is a single fund designed to automatically adjust its asset allocation (Lesson 16) over time, generally becoming more conservative (shifting from a higher stock allocation toward a higher bond and cash allocation) as it approaches a specified target date, often associated with an investor's expected retirement year. This structure is designed to simplify the asset allocation and rebalancing (Lesson 18) decisions that would otherwise require ongoing active management by the investor.

General considerations when evaluating a target-date fund include reviewing its specific "glide path" (how its allocation actually shifts over time, since different funds with the same target date can have meaningfully different glide paths), its expense ratio (Lesson 15), and whether its underlying assumptions about risk tolerance and retirement spending needs actually match the specific investor's own circumstances, since a single fund cannot account for every individual's unique situation, additional income sources, or personal risk tolerance nuances.

**3. Worked Example**
Considering a target-date fund aligned with her expected retirement year, Simone researches its specific glide path and finds it becomes notably more conservative starting several years earlier than a competing target-date fund with the identical target year. Recognising that her personal risk tolerance and additional planned income sources in retirement might make a somewhat more aggressive glide path more appropriate for her specific situation, she compares several target-date fund options rather than assuming all funds sharing her target year are functionally identical.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What does a target-date fund generally do over time?
   a) Maintains a fixed allocation forever
   b) Automatically adjusts its allocation, generally becoming more conservative as the target date approaches
   c) Only invests in bonds from the start
   d) Eliminates the need for any diversification
2. What is a "glide path"?
   a) A type of bond
   b) How a target-date fund's allocation actually shifts over time
   c) A tax filing deadline
   d) A brokerage account type
3. True or False: All target-date funds sharing the same target year have identical glide paths and are functionally interchangeable.
4. Why did Simone compare several target-date fund options rather than assuming they were interchangeable?
   a) She had no retirement plans at all
   b) Different funds with the same target year can have meaningfully different glide paths suited to different investors
   c) Target-date funds do not actually differ from each other
   d) She disliked automatic rebalancing

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A target-date fund automatically adjusts its allocation, generally becoming more conservative as its target date approaches.
2. A glide path describes how a target-date fund's specific allocation shifts over time.
3. Different target-date funds sharing the same target year can have meaningfully different glide paths and expense ratios.
4. Simone appropriately recognised that differing glide paths could better or worse fit her own specific circumstances.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- Target-date (lifecycle) funds automatically adjust allocation over time, generally becoming more conservative near the target date.
- This simplifies ongoing allocation and rebalancing decisions for the investor.
- Glide paths and expense ratios can differ meaningfully between funds sharing the same target date.
- Evaluating whether a fund's underlying assumptions match an individual's actual circumstances remains important.
