## WHITE-L2-041
### Reading a Fund Prospectus

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Explain the general purpose of a fund prospectus.
- Identify key sections typically found in a prospectus.
- Apply this skill to evaluate a fund before investing.

**2. Core Content**
A prospectus is a formal legal document funds are generally required to provide, disclosing key information investors need to make an informed decision. Key sections typically include the fund's investment objective and strategy (does it pursue a value, growth, income, or passive approach, connecting to Lessons 2–7), its fee structure including the expense ratio (Lesson 15) and any other charges, its historical performance (presented with required disclaimers that past performance does not guarantee future results), its risk factors, and its portfolio holdings or general composition.

Reading a prospectus, even in summary form (many funds offer a shorter "summary prospectus"), is a practical Level 2 skill that turns abstract concepts from this entire level into a concrete evaluation exercise: an investor can directly verify a fund's stated strategy, fees, and risks rather than relying solely on marketing materials or a fund's name, which does not always fully capture its actual underlying strategy or risk profile.

**3. Worked Example**
Before investing in an unfamiliar fund recommended by a friend, Wendell reads its summary prospectus rather than relying only on its name and his friend's informal description. He discovers the fund's actual expense ratio is higher than he expected, and its stated investment strategy involves more concentrated, higher-risk positions than the fund's general marketing materials had implied. Armed with this more complete picture, Wendell decides to look for an alternative fund whose actual documented strategy and fees better align with his own goals and risk tolerance.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is a prospectus?
   a) An informal marketing brochure
   b) A formal legal document disclosing key fund information for investor decision-making
   c) A type of bond
   d) A tax form
2. What might reading a prospectus reveal that marketing materials or a fund's name alone might not?
   a) Nothing new
   b) Actual expense ratio, strategy, and risk details
   c) Only the fund's ticker symbol
   d) The fund manager's personal opinions
3. True or False: A fund's name always fully and accurately captures its actual underlying strategy and risk profile.
4. What did Wendell discover by reading the fund's summary prospectus?
   a) Nothing different from what he expected
   b) A higher expense ratio and a more concentrated, higher-risk strategy than marketing materials implied
   c) That the fund had no fees at all
   d) That the fund was risk-free

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. A prospectus is a formal legal document disclosing key information investors need for informed decision-making.
2. Reading a prospectus can reveal actual expense ratios, strategy details, and risks not fully captured by marketing or a fund's name.
3. A fund's name does not always fully or accurately reflect its actual underlying strategy or risk profile.
4. Wendell's prospectus review revealed a higher expense ratio and higher-risk strategy than he had initially assumed.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- A prospectus is a formal legal document disclosing a fund's strategy, fees, performance, and risk factors.
- Key sections include investment objective, fee structure, historical performance, and risk factors.
- Reading a prospectus turns abstract concepts from this level into a concrete, practical evaluation skill.
- A fund's name or marketing materials do not always fully capture its actual documented strategy and risks.
