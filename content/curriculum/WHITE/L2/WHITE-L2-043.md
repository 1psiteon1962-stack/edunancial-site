## WHITE-L2-043
### Building an Investment Policy Statement

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Define an Investment Policy Statement (IPS) at a conceptual level.
- Identify common elements included in a personal IPS.
- Explain how an IPS supports behavioral discipline (Lesson 28).

**2. Core Content**
An Investment Policy Statement (IPS) is a written document, commonly used by institutional investors but increasingly recommended for individual investors, that records an investor's goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, target asset allocation, and general rules for rebalancing or making changes — essentially consolidating many of this level's concepts (Lessons 16, 18, 26, 27) into a single, personal reference document created during a calm, rational period, specifically to be referred back to during emotionally charged market moments (Lesson 28).

Common elements of a personal IPS include a statement of financial goals and time horizon, target asset allocation ranges, rebalancing rules (calendar-based or threshold-based, Lesson 18), and explicit guidelines for how the investor intends to respond to significant market volatility, written in advance rather than decided reactively in the moment.

**3. Worked Example**
After completing this level's material, Bianca writes her own personal Investment Policy Statement, documenting her long-term retirement goal, her 30-year time horizon, her target 80/20 stock/bond allocation, her plan to rebalance annually, and an explicit note reminding her future self that market declines are a normal, expected part of long-term investing that her plan already accounts for. Months later, during a significant market downturn, Bianca refers back to this document, finding it provides exactly the calm, pre-decided anchor she needs to resist an emotional urge to deviate from her established plan.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is an Investment Policy Statement (IPS)?
   a) A government tax form
   b) A written document recording an investor's goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, and allocation rules
   c) A type of bond
   d) A brokerage account application
2. Why is an IPS created during a calm period rather than during market stress?
   a) It has no relationship to market stress
   b) To provide a stable, pre-decided anchor for later, more emotionally charged moments
   c) It must legally be created only during downturns
   d) Calm periods produce less accurate documents
3. True or False: An IPS is only useful for institutional investors and has no relevance to individuals.
4. How did Bianca's IPS help her during the later market downturn?
   a) It had no effect on her behavior
   b) It provided a calm, pre-decided anchor helping her resist an emotional urge to deviate from her plan
   c) It told her to sell all her holdings immediately
   d) She had never actually written one

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. b  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. An IPS is a written document recording an investor's goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, and allocation/rebalancing rules.
2. Creating an IPS during a calm period provides a stable anchor for navigating later, more emotionally charged market moments.
3. Individual investors are increasingly encouraged to create personal IPS documents, not just institutions.
4. Bianca's pre-established IPS gave her a stable reference point that helped her maintain discipline during the downturn.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- An Investment Policy Statement records goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, and allocation/rebalancing rules in one document.
- It consolidates many concepts from this level into a single, personal reference.
- Creating it during a calm period supports behavioral discipline during later, emotionally charged market moments.
- Individual investors, not just institutions, increasingly benefit from maintaining a personal IPS.
