## WHITE-L2-048
### Building a Personal Investment Framework

**1. Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:
- Assemble a structured set of questions drawn from across this level into a personal investment framework.
- Apply this framework to a hypothetical investment decision.
- Explain how this framework embodies Level 2 "chess piece" thinking.

**2. Core Content**
Echoing RED-L2-049's decision framework, this lesson consolidates WHITE-L2 into a practical personal investment framework, structured around questions drawn from earlier lessons: What is my time horizon and risk tolerance for this specific pool of money (Lessons 26–27)? Which lens or blend of lenses fits my goals (Lessons 2–7)? What asset allocation and diversification approach fits this profile (Lessons 16–17, 24–25)? What account type and asset location considerations apply (Lessons 29–30)? What is the full fee picture, beyond just the expense ratio (Lessons 15, 42)? And do I have a written plan (an IPS, Lesson 43) to refer back to during inevitable periods of market stress (Lessons 28, 36, 45)?

As with the RED-track framework, this is a habit of mind rather than a rigid checklist — the goal is deliberately tracing a decision's effects across time horizon, philosophy, allocation, taxes, fees, and behavioural planning together, rather than acting on a single attractive number or a friend's tip in isolation.

**3. Worked Example**
Considering a new investment opportunity a colleague mentioned, Farid runs through this framework deliberately rather than acting purely on the colleague's enthusiasm. He clarifies his own time horizon and risk tolerance for this specific money, considers which lens (if any) the opportunity fits, checks how it would affect his overall allocation and diversification, considers which account type would be most appropriate, investigates the full fee picture, and confirms it fits within his written Investment Policy Statement's guidelines — before deciding whether and how to proceed, rather than acting on enthusiasm alone.

**4. Practice Quiz**
1. What is the purpose of the personal investment framework introduced in this lesson?
   a) To replace all professional advice
   b) To provide a structured habit of tracing a decision's effects across multiple pieces from this level
   c) To guarantee a profitable outcome
   d) To eliminate the need for any research
2. Which of the following is part of the framework?
   a) Time horizon, lens fit, allocation, account type, fees, and a written plan
   b) Only the ticker symbol
   c) Only a friend's opinion
   d) Only the current price
3. True or False: This framework is meant to be applied mechanically without genuine analysis of the specific opportunity.
4. What did Farid do before proceeding with his colleague's suggested opportunity?
   a) He acted immediately based on enthusiasm alone
   b) He systematically ran the opportunity through his full personal investment framework
   c) He ignored his own risk tolerance entirely
   d) He skipped checking fees

**5. Answer Key**
1. b  2. a  3. False  4. b

**6. Answers and Explanations**
1. This framework builds the habit of comprehensively tracing an investment decision's effects across multiple pieces, not guaranteeing outcomes.
2. The framework spans time horizon, lens fit, allocation, account type, fees, and a written plan, drawn from across this level.
3. The framework is meant to prompt genuine, situation-specific analysis, not mechanical box-checking.
4. Farid's systematic application of the framework replaced acting purely on his colleague's enthusiasm.

**7. Key Takeaways**
- This lesson consolidates WHITE-L2 into a practical framework spanning time horizon, philosophy, allocation, accounts, fees, and behavioural planning.
- The framework is a habit of mind, echoing the RED-track framework's structure.
- Applying it systematically helps avoid acting on a single attractive detail or others' enthusiasm alone.
- This framework embodies the comprehensive "chess piece" thinking this entire level has built toward.
