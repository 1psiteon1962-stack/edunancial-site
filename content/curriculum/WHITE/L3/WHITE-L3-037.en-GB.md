# WHITE-L3-037: Why Tax-Loss Harvesting Turns a Loss Into a Future Benefit

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Explain the general concept of tax-loss harvesting
- Explain why "realising" a loss by selling is necessary to use it for tax purposes
- Explain why this strategy requires understanding a rule covered in the next lesson

## Core Content

**Tax-loss harvesting** is a general strategy of intentionally selling an investment that has declined in value, in order to "realise" the loss — meaning it becomes an actual, recorded loss for tax purposes rather than just an unrealised paper loss — which can then potentially be used to offset taxable gains elsewhere in a portfolio, subject to the specific tax rules that apply in a given jurisdiction.

Here's the "why" this requires an actual sale: as long as an investment is simply held at a lower value than its purchase price, that's generally considered an unrealised loss — it exists on paper but hasn't yet been "locked in" through a transaction, and in many tax systems, only a realised loss can be used for tax purposes. Selling the position converts that paper loss into a realised one.

[VERIFIED FACT] Because many investors want to maintain their overall market exposure or investment strategy even while harvesting a loss, they often reinvest the proceeds into a similar (but not identical) investment right away — this introduces an important rule covered in the next lesson that limits how quickly you can repurchase a substantially identical investment and still claim the tax loss.

## Worked Example

[ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE] An investor holds a fund that has declined significantly in value since purchase. Rather than simply continuing to hold it, she sells the position, realising the loss for tax purposes, and reinvests the proceeds into a similar, but not identical, fund to maintain roughly the same market exposure. [ASSUMPTION] The realised loss can potentially be used, subject to applicable tax rules, to offset other taxable gains she has elsewhere in her portfolio that year, turning what felt like a purely negative outcome into at least a partial, practical tax benefit.

## Practice Quiz

1. What is tax-loss harvesting, generally?
2. Why does simply holding a declined investment not create a usable tax loss in many tax systems?
3. Why might an investor reinvest into a similar, but not identical, investment after harvesting a loss?

## Answer Key

1. Intentionally selling a declined investment to realise the loss for tax purposes, potentially offsetting taxable gains elsewhere, subject to applicable rules.
2. Because an unrealised (paper) loss hasn't been "locked in" through an actual transaction, and many tax systems require a loss to be realised through a sale before it can be used.
3. To maintain roughly the same overall market exposure or investment strategy while still realising the tax loss, rather than simply holding cash or exiting that part of the market entirely.

## Answers and Explanations

1. This is the foundational definition for the lesson.
2. This explains the mechanical reason a sale is necessary, a key concept for understanding the strategy.
3. This sets up the practical reason investors typically reinvest quickly, which introduces the constraint covered in the next lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- Tax-loss harvesting intentionally realizes a loss through a sale, potentially offsetting other taxable gains, subject to applicable tax rules.
- Only a realised loss, not an unrealised paper loss, can typically be used for tax purposes in many tax systems.
- Investors often reinvest into a similar investment to maintain market exposure, which introduces an important timing rule covered next.
