# Sector Rotation Analysis: Why Different Sectors Lead at Different Cycle Stages

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation. As a central bank aggressively raises rates to fight inflation, certain sectors of the stock market start to underperform sharply while others hold up much better. This lesson explains the recurring pattern behind that divergence.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the concept of sector rotation across the economic cycle.
- Distinguish between cyclical and defensive sectors.
- Explain why certain sectors tend to lead during economic expansion and others during contraction.
- Apply sector rotation thinking to interpret current economic conditions.

## Core Content
Sector rotation refers to the tendency for different segments of the stock market — such as technology, financials, consumer discretionary, utilities, healthcare, and consumer staples — to outperform or underperform at different stages of the economic cycle. VERIFIED FACT: cyclical sectors (like consumer discretionary and financials) tend to be more sensitive to economic growth and interest rates, while defensive sectors (like utilities, healthcare, and consumer staples) tend to provide more stable demand regardless of economic conditions, since people still need electricity, medication, and basic household goods even during a downturn.

Here is the "why" this pattern recurs across cycles, connecting to the real estate market cycle analysis covered earlier in this curriculum: during economic expansion, investors favor cyclical sectors poised to benefit from strong consumer spending and business investment. As a central bank raises interest rates sharply to fight inflation (as in the scenario above), sectors sensitive to borrowing costs and discretionary consumer spending, such as certain technology and consumer discretionary companies, often underperform, while defensive sectors with stable, less rate-sensitive demand tend to hold up relatively better.

This is why understanding roughly where the economy sits in its cycle can inform, though never guarantee, an investor's sector positioning. ASSUMPTION/OPINION: sector rotation patterns are historically observed tendencies, not fixed rules, and unexpected developments (such as a major sector-specific technological breakthrough or regulatory shift) can override the typical cyclical pattern in any given period.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: During an aggressive interest-rate-hiking cycle, a technology-heavy sector index that had led the broader market during the prior low-rate expansion falls significantly more than the overall market, since many of its companies rely on future growth expectations that become less valuable at higher discount rates, and on cheap borrowing to fund expansion. Meanwhile, a utilities sector index, whose demand and cash flows are far less tied to discretionary spending or growth expectations, declines much less over the same period, illustrating the classic cyclical-versus-defensive divergence.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** Sector rotation describes the recurring pattern of leadership shifting among market sectors as economic conditions change.

**Q2.** The core distinction lies in how sensitive each sector's demand and profitability are to the broader economic cycle.

**Q3.** Higher rates reduce the present value of future growth and raise borrowing costs, both of which particularly affect growth-oriented cyclical sectors.

**Q4.** Essential, less discretionary demand for defensive sector products and services provides more stable performance during economic stress.

**Q5.** While the pattern has recurred historically, it is not guaranteed, and unusual events can disrupt the typical relationship between sectors and the economic cycle.

## Key Takeaways
- Sector rotation describes the tendency for different market sectors to lead or lag at different stages of the economic cycle.
- Cyclical sectors are more sensitive to growth and interest rates; defensive sectors offer more stable, less cycle-dependent demand.
- Rate-hiking cycles tend to pressure growth-oriented cyclical sectors more than defensive ones.
- Sector rotation is a historically observed tendency, not a guaranteed rule, and unexpected events can override the typical pattern.
