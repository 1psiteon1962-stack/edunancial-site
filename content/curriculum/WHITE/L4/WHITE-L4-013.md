# Inflation-Protected Securities Analysis: Why TIPS Aren't a Perfect Hedge

## Scenario Opener
ASSUMPTION/OPINION (hypothetical scenario for teaching purposes only): Picture a central bank raising its benchmark interest rate sharply and repeatedly over a short stretch of months to fight rising inflation. As inflation runs hot and a central bank responds aggressively, income-focused investors start asking whether inflation-protected bonds can fully shield their purchasing power. This lesson examines what these securities actually do — and where their protection runs out.

## Learning Objectives
- Explain how Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) adjust for inflation.
- Explain the difference between nominal yield and real yield.
- Identify the limitations of TIPS as an inflation hedge.
- Explain why TIPS still carry interest rate risk despite their inflation adjustment feature.

## Core Content
Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) are government bonds whose principal value adjusts based on changes in a designated inflation index (in the U.S., the Consumer Price Index). VERIFIED FACT: as the index rises, the bond's principal increases, and interest payments, calculated as a fixed rate applied to that adjusted principal, rise along with it — directly linking the bond's payments to measured inflation, unlike a standard nominal bond with a fixed principal and fixed coupon.

Here is the "why" TIPS are not a perfect or complete inflation hedge, despite this direct linkage: TIPS protect against changes in the specific inflation index they are tied to, but that index may not perfectly reflect any individual investor's actual personal cost-of-living increases, which can differ based on individual spending patterns (housing costs, healthcare, education, and other categories can inflate at different rates than the broad index). There can also be a reporting lag between when inflation actually occurs and when it is reflected in the published index used to adjust the bond.

This is why TIPS still carry real interest rate risk, distinct from inflation risk: TIPS pay a "real" yield (a return above and beyond inflation), and this real yield itself fluctuates with market conditions and can rise or fall independent of the inflation adjustment, meaning a TIPS bond's price can still decline if real yields rise sharply, even during a period of elevated inflation, similar to the duration risk any bond carries (Lesson 3). This is why TIPS address inflation risk specifically, but do not eliminate interest rate risk or guarantee a smooth, worry-free holding experience during a rate-hiking cycle like the one in the scenario above.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: An investor buys a TIPS bond during a period of high inflation, expecting full protection of purchasing power. Measured inflation does push up the bond's principal and interest payments as designed. However, over the same period, real yields rise sharply as the central bank tightens policy aggressively, and the TIPS bond's market price falls due to this real interest rate increase, even though its inflation adjustment mechanism functioned exactly as intended — illustrating that TIPS address inflation risk specifically but do not shield an investor from real interest rate risk.

## Answers and Explanations
**Q1.** TIPS are specifically designed to adjust their principal value in line with a measured inflation index.

**Q2.** Since the broad index averages across many spending categories, an individual's actual experienced inflation can differ from the published index.

**Q3.** Real yield reflects the additional return investors demand beyond compensation for inflation, and it can move independently of the inflation adjustment itself.

**Q4.** TIPS still carry real interest rate risk, similar to the duration risk of any bond, which can cause price declines separate from inflation-related adjustments.

**Q5.** TIPS are a targeted tool for a specific type of risk (measured inflation) but leave other risks, like real rate movements, unaddressed.

## Key Takeaways
- TIPS adjust their principal and interest payments based on a designated inflation index, unlike standard nominal bonds.
- This inflation index may not perfectly match an individual investor's actual personal cost-of-living changes.
- TIPS still carry real interest rate risk, since their real yield can rise or fall independent of the inflation adjustment.
- TIPS address inflation risk specifically, but they do not eliminate interest rate risk or guarantee complete protection.
