## Core Content
Synthesizing this level's earlier Lesson 2 discussion regarding orchestrating risk tolerance, time horizon, and account tax treatment, VERIFIED FACT, a fuller, more complete individual risk calibration framework requires distinguishing and then orchestrating together genuine risk tolerance (an investor's own psychological, emotional comfort with portfolio volatility) and genuine risk capacity (an investor's actual, objective financial ability to withstand a genuine loss without jeopardizing essential financial goals), mirroring this curriculum's parallel RED-L5 Lesson 44 discussion applied now to a fuller paper asset context.

Here is the "why" genuine risk tolerance and genuine risk capacity represent two genuinely distinct concepts that should be orchestrated together: risk tolerance concerns an investor's own subjective, psychological comfort level with experiencing portfolio volatility, while risk capacity concerns this same investor's objective, financial ability to actually absorb a genuine loss without jeopardizing essential goals such as near-term retirement income or an imminent major expense, meaning these two concepts, while related, are genuinely distinct and an investor should specifically orchestrate both together rather than assuming psychological comfort alone, or objective financial capacity alone, fully determines an appropriate overall allocation. This is why an investor whose genuine risk tolerance and genuine risk capacity diverge faces a genuine calibration challenge requiring honest resolution: OPINION and an important practical consideration, an investor who genuinely feels psychologically comfortable with considerably more risk than their objective financial situation can actually support (a genuinely limited risk capacity despite high risk tolerance) should honestly recognize that their objective risk capacity, rather than their subjective comfort level alone, should ultimately constrain their actual allocation, while an investor with genuine financial capacity to absorb considerable risk but genuine psychological discomfort with volatility (low risk tolerance despite high capacity) should honestly recognize that pursuing an allocation exceeding their genuine psychological comfort risks the very behavioral discipline failures this level's earlier Lesson 16 discussion described, meaning this honest reconciliation between tolerance and capacity represents a genuinely important, individual calibration challenge. This is why this lesson serves as a fitting closing synthesis point: drawing together this level's recurring risk-related themes — Lesson 2's initial risk tolerance and time horizon discussion, Lesson 3's position-sizing discipline, Lesson 16's behavioral discipline discussion, and this lesson's own risk tolerance-versus-capacity distinction — into a single, coherent individual risk framework, this lesson exemplifies precisely the kind of integrated orchestration this level's opening Lesson 1 discussion identified as its central, defining purpose, mirroring precisely the parallel closing structure this curriculum's RED-L5 batch established.

## Worked Example
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: An investor honestly recognizes that, while they genuinely feel psychologically comfortable with an aggressive, high-risk allocation, their actual objective financial situation — limited reserves and an imminent major expense — provides genuinely limited risk capacity to actually absorb a significant loss, and specifically orchestrates a more conservative allocation reflecting this objective capacity constraint rather than their subjective comfort level alone. A different investor, with genuine financial capacity to absorb considerable risk but genuine psychological discomfort with volatility, specifically orchestrates a more conservative allocation as well, recognizing that exceeding their genuine psychological comfort risks the behavioral discipline failures this level's earlier discussion described, illustrating precisely why both risk tolerance and risk capacity should be honestly orchestrated together.
